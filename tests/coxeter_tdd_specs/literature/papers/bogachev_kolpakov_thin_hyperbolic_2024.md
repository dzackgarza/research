# Thin Hyperbolic Reflection Groups

**Source**: arXiv:2112.14642v4 [math.GR]  
**Authors**: Nikolay Bogachev, Alexander Kolpakov  
**Retrieved**: 2025-07-26  
**Citation Key**: `bogachev_kolpakov_thin_2024`

## Abstract Summary

This paper studies Zariski dense finitely generated discrete subgroups of $\Isom(\mathbb{H}^d)$ containing at least one reflection. The main contribution is a general description of all thin hyperbolic reflection groups and proof that they are enumerable.

## Main Theorems

### Theorem 1 (Main Result)
Let $\Gamma < \Isom(\mathbb{H}^d)$ be a finitely generated Zariski dense discrete group containing at least one reflection. Then $\Gamma$ contains a discrete Zariski dense subgroup generated by finitely many reflections.

### Theorem 2 (Thin Groups from Vinberg Algorithm)  
Let $L$ be a non-reflective Lorentzian lattice over a totally real number field $k$, and assume that $\mathscr{R}(L)$ is non-trivial. Then for sufficiently large $m$ all subgroups $\mathscr{R}_m(L)$ are thin in $\mathbf{PO}(L)_{\mathcal{O}_k}$.

### Theorem 3 (Converse)
Every thin hyperbolic reflection group $\Lambda < \Isom(\mathbb{H}^d)$ is a subgroup of $\mathscr{R}_m(L) < \mathbf{PO}(L)_{\mathcal{O}_k}$, for some Lorentzian $\mathcal{O}_k$-lattice $L$ and natural $m \geq d+1$.

## Key Mathematical Concepts

### Thin Groups Definition
A group $\Lambda < \Gamma$ is thin if:
1. $\Lambda$ is finitely generated
2. $\Lambda$ has infinite index in $\Gamma$ 
3. $\Lambda$ is Zariski dense in $\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{R})$

### Lorentzian Lattices
- Freely generated $\mathcal{O}_k$-module with signature $(d,1)$ scalar product
- **Reflective**: If maximal reflection subgroup has finite index
- **Non-reflective**: Otherwise

### Vinberg Algorithm
- Constructs walls of fundamental Coxeter polyhedron
- Applied to Lorentzian lattices gives sequence $\mathscr{R}_m(L)$
- For non-reflective lattices, produces infinite sequences of thin groups

## Important Results

### Zariski Density Characterization
- Discrete group $\Gamma < \Isom(\mathbb{H}^d)$ is Zariski dense iff its limit set is not contained in a hypersphere of $\partial \mathbb{H}^d$

### Vinberg's Lemma Application
- If $\Gamma$ contains reflections, then $\Gamma = W \rtimes \Sym_\Gamma(\mathscr{P})$
- Where $W$ is maximal reflection subgroup, $\mathscr{P}$ is fundamental Coxeter polyhedron

### Classification by Eigenvalues
For Gram matrices of Coxeter polyhedra:
- **All positive eigenvalues**: Finite Coxeter group
- **All non-negative (≥1 zero)**: Affine Coxeter group  
- **Some negative**: Hyperbolic/indefinite Coxeter group
