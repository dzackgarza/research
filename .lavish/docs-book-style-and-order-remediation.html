Documentation book style and order remediation

Tier: workstream / organizational Parent: FEATURE-documentation-book / private agent-memory Public execution surface: none

Purpose / observable result

  • The rendered documentation has a chapter, section, and subsection order in which every load-bearing mathematical notion precedes its first use.
  • Each definition and ruling has one defining occurrence; later chapters cite it rather than restating or contradicting it.
  • Mathematical chapters contain mathematics. Rendered authoring and reference pages contain only mathematical conventions and information a human contributor needs. Agent audit instructions, terminology controls, and delegation policy remain in non-rendered agent-facing files; editorial history and implementation status use their own surfaces.
  • The current twenty-four rendered pages are accounted for through preservation, merge, split, relocation, or explicit retirement after semantic transfer.

The current book is insufficient because its order contains forward dependencies, several notions have incompatible definitions in different chapters, large chapters combine unrelated levels of the theory, and process prose interrupts mathematical exposition.

Completion is observable when the approved hierarchy is rendered, every source proposition has a disposition, all references resolve, independent auditors find no unresolved style or invented-language violations, and inspected page images show a coherent book at wide and narrow viewports.

Scope

  • Included:
    • docs/index.md, docs/Roadmap.md, and every rendered chapter named in docs/_quarto.yml;
    • the chapter/section/subsection hierarchy;
    • source-preserving merges, splits, relocations, and rewrites;
    • correction of the mathematical contradictions listed below;
    • cross-reference, citation, heading, navigation, and rendered-output verification;
    • agent handoffs needed to perform non-overlapping chapter work.
  • Excluded:
    • source code, tests, generated Sage inventories, Lean declarations, and the category diagram data;
    • new mathematical features not already required to make the current exposition correct;
    • redesign of the site theme or component system.
  • Preserved behavior:
    • source-backed mathematical statements, hypotheses, codomains, diagrams, examples, citations, warnings, and stable anchors;
    • the generated Sage catalogues and interactive category diagram as reference surfaces;
    • roadmap links to the live project state, without copying that state into the book.
  • Constraints and prohibitions:
    • every delegated agent reads docs/_writing-guide.md as agent-only audit policy and docs/contributing/Mathematical-Language-Style-Guide.md as the positive mathematical conventions for the rendered book;
    • never copy an agent pattern catalogue, terminology control, audit instruction, or delegation contract into a rendered page;
    • never use a summary in place of the source chapter being transferred;
    • do not resolve a contradiction by deleting one side without establishing the mathematical result and preserving any distinct content;
    • do not introduce a second definition to make a relocated section self-contained;
    • do not edit _quarto.yml until all target chapters and stable anchors exist;
    • documentation-only commits use the user-directed hook bypass and stage only the intended documentation files.

Invariants

  • The hierarchy is a topological order of mathematical prerequisites.
  • A forward reference is permitted only for a proof, example, implementation note, or elaboration that is not needed to understand the current statement.
  • Definitions have one anchored defining occurrence. Local reminders may repeat notation or a type signature, not the definition.
  • The same symbol has the same type and meaning everywhere.
  • Mathematical choices are supported by the declared literature; plausible reconstructions from memory are inadmissible.
  • A move or split preserves all source propositions until a source-to-destination comparison proves their disposition.
  • No rendered page contains agent threat-model rules, audit procedure, prompt contracts, or terminology blacklists. Human-facing contribution pages contain only positive mathematical and editorial conventions.
  • Existing unrelated worktree changes remain untouched.

Sources and current state

  • Governing policy is split by audience:
    • docs/_writing-guide.md is non-rendered agent writing and audit policy;
    • docs/contributing/Mathematical-Language-Style-Guide.md is the rendered mathematical authoring guide for human contributors;
    • .agents/references/mathematical-auditor-priming.md is agent-only audit procedure.
  • Book configuration: docs/_quarto.yml currently renders twenty-four pages: the home page, twelve framework chapters, two Sage chapters, two Lean chapters, six contributing chapters, and the roadmap.
  • Source history: merged PR #274 performed the four-part move and introduced the current framework chapter sequence. It is historical context, not an execution surface for this repair.
  • Relevant current contradictions:
    • framework/Ambient-Setting.md and framework/Categorical-Foundations.md do not name a model of higher categories and assign the right-adjoint role inconsistently between core, groupoid-completion, and homotopy-type constructions;
    • framework/Mathematical-Framework.md, framework/Categorical-Foundations.md, and framework/Truncation-and-Classifiers.md disagree on the variance and fibration/opfibration convention for the category of elements;
    • several chapters call arbitrary forgetful functors “axiom classifiers” without stating a represented functor or universal property, and the policy files previously called a family attached to every axiom a “universal fibration”;
    • framework/Mathematical-Definitions.md presents unitality as a subcategory inclusion while framework/Mathematical-Framework.md presents a unit as chosen structure with a faithful, non-full forgetful functor;

    • framework/Mathematical-Framework.md and framework/Distinguished-Functors.md use the project terms “Join”, “Meet”, and “least-common-category” before specifying the preorder or universal property in which those constructions exist.
  • Ordering failures include forward dependencies on the category of elements, the definition of a diagram as a functor from an indexing category, modules over a ring, and the El convention.
  • Repetition includes truncation, categories of elements, the phrase “axiom classifiers”, and the property/structure/stuff classification across several chapters.
  • Exact duplicate headings occur in the current framework chapter sequence and identification page; title case, emoji headings, self-narration, self-certification, and negative parallelism recur across the book.
  • The rendered mathematical authoring guide has been separated from the non-rendered agent writing and audit guide. Existing rendered pages still cite the retired P1–P6 labels, the Class A/B/C taxonomy, and the replacement dictionary. P1, P1b, the taxonomy, and the dictionary move to non-rendered agent policy; the genuine mathematical content formerly labeled P2–P6 now has descriptive anchors. Every dependent link must move to the appropriate mathematical anchor or leave the rendered book with its agent-facing passage; compatibility labels must not republish the old mixed policy.

The source-preservation map must inventory every occurrence before the corresponding pages are rewritten.

Proposed book hierarchy

This hierarchy is the first reviewable result. Section names are provisional; their mathematical purpose and prerequisite order are not.

Orientation

  • Home — index.md
    • mathematical scope of the project;
    • navigation to the theory, realizations, reference material, and contribution policy;
    • link to live execution state without reproducing its status.
  • Project roadmap — Roadmap.md
    • Sage-parity track;
    • fork and extension track;
    • relation between the tracks;
    • notebook-based user-story proof;
    • no emoji headings, live status tables, or claims of completeness.

Foundations

  • Higher categories and universes
    • universe and smallness;
    • the chosen model of higher categories and its source;
    • the category of higher categories supplied by that model;
    • internal hom, cells, and hom-objects as defined in that model;
    • mapping spaces as supplied by the chosen model;
    • ordinary categories and the exact relation to the higher-categorical model;
    • literal equality, isomorphisms in an ordinary category, and equivalences in the chosen higher-categorical model, with distinct types;
    • initial, terminal, and contractible objects;
    • truncation in the chosen model.
  • Categorical constructions
    • core, groupoid completion, and classifying space as distinct constructions, citing the equality and equivalence definitions;
    • arrow category and functor categories;
    • category of elements, with one variance convention and its corresponding fibration statement;
    • strict and pseudo-pullbacks, isofibrations, and the condition under which they coincide up to equivalence;
    • zero-truncation, connected components, and fiber sequences;
    • generalized elements as morphisms from A to X; representing objects for representable functors and corepresenting objects for corepresentable functors, with variance and sources stated;
    • the El notation as shorthand for the already-defined construction.
  • Loops and suspension
    • base change and cobase change;
    • the chosen interval object, its endpoint maps, and its stated axioms;
    • loops and hom-objects;
    • spheres and suspension;
    • deloopings;
    • every loop/suspension adjunction preserved from the source, each drawn;
    • the loop and suspension constructions needed by the following chapter, using the already-defined fiber-sequence construction.
  • Equivalences and witnesses
    • the space or groupoid of equivalences supplied by the chosen model, citing the mapping-space definition;
    • isomorphisms as data and the set or space of isomorphisms; when nonempty, its specified left, right, or bitorsor structure under the relevant automorphism groups;
    • contractibility of a specified witness space or groupoid only when established;
    • a specified isomorphism, equivalence, or natural isomorphism used for comparison;
    • the image of a witness after applying a functor;
    • consequences of the preceding loop and suspension constructions and the already-defined fiber-sequence construction;
    • any proposed universe U and maps into U only after a corpus-grounded definition;
    • Lean-specific reduction moved to the Lean realization chapter.

Properties, structures, and algebraic categories

  • Properties, structures, and classifying constructions
    • element projections by reference to the category-of-elements definition;
    • specified forgetful functors from categories of structured objects;
    • the property / structure / stuff classification, with fullness, faithfulness, and repleteness stated separately;
    • classifying objects or fibrations only where a represented functor or universal property has been established;
    • pullback of a specified family along a named functor;
    • in a category with finite products, n-ary operations on X as morphisms from its n-fold product to X, forming the cartesian endomorphism operad;
    • equations as equalities of parallel terms or commutative diagrams, with coherence 2-cells named separately;
    • the specified operad and its coherence axioms, or a source-defined tower of operads with every object and transition morphism named;
    • strict and finite cases as reductions, not definitions.
  • Algebraic categories from operations
    • the category of algebras for a signature with one n-ary operation;
    • magmas and binary operations;
    • associativity and commutativity as equations;
    • semigroups;
    • units as chosen structure and monoids;
    • inverses and groups;
    • rings as algebras for the signature with constants 0 and 1, addition, negation, and multiplication, subject to the ring identities;
    • the resulting forgetful diagram and named pullbacks.
  • Joins, meets, and closure
    • pullbacks of specified full-subcategory inclusions, with the diagram, hypotheses, and universal property;
    • the replete full subcategory generated by named source categories under an explicit list of closure operations, with its universal property; omit the construction if the sources do not establish those data;
    • connectivity and weak contractibility hypotheses;
    • join and meet only in a specified preorder, with their distinct universal properties and existence hypotheses; categorical limits or colimits are named separately;
    • consequences for the Lean and Sage realizations, with implementation-specific names confined to those realization pages.
  • Distinguished functors and comparison
    • specified factorizations F = G ∘ H, with the source and target of every functor;
    • parallel functors and comparison 2-cells;
    • comparison after factorization through a specified common target;
    • statements about where a construction lands versus the construction itself;
    • applications of the already-defined inverse-image and specified-family pullback constructions;
    • comparison through the meet only in the specified preorder, with its universal property and existence hypotheses; if a categorical limit or colimit is intended, name it separately.
  • Elements, subobjects, and hypotheses
    • the category-of-elements projection functors and generalized elements already defined in Foundations;
    • membership in the full subcategory spanned by objects satisfying the stated property, and the property/structure distinction;
    • subobjects and existence of a monomorphism representing the specified subobject;
    • specified subobject inclusions and factorizations between their named monomorphisms;
    • the functor sending a test object to the set or space of solutions over it, with domain, codomain, and its defining equalizer stated; a universal element appears only after representability is established;
    • hypotheses as explicit categorical data;
    • property disjunctions and case decomposition;
    • localization and descent.

Forms and lattices

  • Modules and form categories
    • module categories over a ring and the composable sequence of specified forgetful functors;
    • the full subcategories of finitely generated projective and free modules; any additional finiteness condition only after the source states whether it means finite as a set, finite length, or finitely generated;
    • the full subcategory of torsion modules only after fixing a base domain or a specified torsion theory;
    • bilinear and quadratic form presheaves;
    • their categories of elements, citing the definition in Foundations;
    • the full subcategory of the Grothendieck construction spanned by forms satisfying each stated property;
    • polarization and its functoriality;
    • orthogonal sums.
  • Lattices and discriminant forms
    • nondegenerate lattices;
    • unimodular and even lattices;
    • dual lattices;
    • the discriminant bilinear form with its codomain, and, for an even lattice, the discriminant quadratic form with its codomain and evenness hypothesis;
    • the discriminant construction and exact sequences;
    • nondegeneracy as vanishing of the radical and unimodularity as invertibility of the adjoint map to the dual module;
    • localization and the double complex where they are mathematically required.
  • Isometries and arithmetic invariants
    • the core of a form category, citing the defining construction in Foundations, and the resulting isometry groupoid;
    • automorphism groups as instances of the general automorphism construction;
    • the discriminant representation;
    • matrix realizations as chosen presentations;
    • index and its hypotheses;
    • the Miranda–Morrison sequence;
    • genus as the fiber of the induced map on connected components for the specified localization map, citing def-genus;
    • constructors relocated to the Sage realization pages.

Realizations

  • SageMath category framework
    • navigation-level explanation of the Sage category mechanism;
    • category, axiom, and functorial-construction inventories;
    • source-grounded defects and gaps;
    • provenance of generated data.
  • Generated SageMath category catalogue
    • source-linked category classes;
    • axiom-generated classes;
    • construction-generated classes;
    • import-time instances;
    • no manually maintained interpretation layered over generated facts.
  • Lean–Sage realization
    • mathematical declarations versus executable representations;
    • the A/B/C boundary in implementation language;
    • the ForMathlib boundary;
    • missing formalizations and their evidence;
    • the supported equality reduction, if the Foundations definition permits it.
  • Syntax and evaluation
    • concrete syntax;
    • abstract syntax trees or terms and their mathematical meaning;
    • evaluation;
    • documents;
    • status, provenance, and certification.
  • Category diagram
    • the interactive diagram;
    • the editable diagram source;
    • a legend that distinguishes categories, functors, and comparison 2-cells.

Authoring and mathematical reference

  • Mathematical authoring conventions
    • universes, domains, codomains, and chosen structure stated explicitly;
    • primitive constructions in the chosen higher-category model, followed by cited specialization or comparison theorems in spaces;
    • property, structure, and chosen-data language determined by the forgetful functor;
    • typed notation, universal properties, and diagram conventions;
    • no agent pattern catalogue, replacement blacklist, audit hook, internal-definition enforcement rule, or self-audit history.
  • Mathematical decisions and sources
    • mathematical choices whose rationale a reader or human contributor needs;
    • links from those decisions to current definitions and sources;
    • supersession history without restating definitions;
    • no agent threat model, design-hazard catalogue, audit history, remediation queue, or implementation status.

framework/Settled-Mathematical-Rulings.md is therefore decomposed: its mathematical claims move to their single defining occurrences, while rationale and supersession history move to the final reference chapter. framework/Mathematical-Framework.md splits across the three classifier/algebra chapters. framework/Mathematical-Definitions.md splits across the three forms-and-lattices chapters.
