# Finite automorphism groups and polynomial certificates

## Completeness as infeasibility

Let $G$ be a finite undirected graph with $n$ vertices, and let
$H=\langle a_1,\ldots,a_m\rangle\leq\operatorname{Aut}(G)$.

**Theorem (Drecun, Theorem 4).** A polynomial system
$\mathcal A(G,a_1,\ldots,a_m)$ over $\mathbb F_p$, for a prime $p>n$, is
infeasible exactly when $H=\operatorname{Aut}(G)$.

The variables form a permutation matrix commuting with the adjacency matrix.
Additional equations exclude the permutations in $H$. A solution therefore
represents an automorphism outside the proposed subgroup. A Nullstellensatz
certificate expresses $1$ in the ideal generated by the equations:

$$
1=\sum_i g_i f_i,\qquad f_i\in\mathcal A(G,a_1,\ldots,a_m).
$$

The paper provides a prototype with separate solver, prover, and verifier.

Source: Ivan Drecun, *An algorithmic construction of Nullstellensatz
certificates for graph automorphism groups*,
[arXiv:2610.28928v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.28928v1), 20 October 2026,
Theorem 4 and Section 4. The reduction is proved in the preprint; its software
is linked at [graph-aut-cert](https://github.com/idrecun/graph-aut-cert).

## What the polynomial identity establishes

At a common zero of the $f_i$, the displayed right side would be zero. The
left side is one. Thus the identity excludes common zeros over every extension
field of the coefficient field. This explains why polynomial infeasibility is
stronger than an unsuccessful search for solutions.

For exact arithmetic, checking the identity is finite polynomial arithmetic.
Constructing it may be much harder. The existence of a certificate does not
bound its size or make its production efficient. A compressed derivation also
requires sound rules for every step; accepting a file format is not the same
mathematical assertion as ideal membership.

## Transferring the result to a lattice

To use a graph computation for a lattice $L$, specify a finite configuration
$S\subseteq L$ preserved by the group of interest. A graph encoding must retain
the relevant relations, for example exact pairings and any markings. The
action induces a homomorphism
$\rho:\operatorname{O}(L)\to\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ when the full orthogonal
group preserves that configuration and its encoding.

For a proposed subgroup $J\leq\operatorname{O}(L)$, a sufficient route is

$$
\ker\rho=1,\qquad \rho(J)=\operatorname{Aut}(G).
$$

Indeed, any $g\in\operatorname{O}(L)$ then has the same image as some
$j\in J$, so $gj^{-1}\in\ker\rho$ and $g=j$. The graph computation can
address the image equality. The kernel assertion and the validity of the
lattice lifts remain separate mathematical obligations.

If $\rho$ has a kernel, image completeness leaves that kernel unresolved. If
$\operatorname{O}(L)$ is infinite, it cannot inject into the finite group
$\operatorname{Aut}(G)$. In that case a finite configuration can describe a
finite image or a restricted subgroup, not faithfully represent the entire
infinite orthogonal group.

## Role in this repository

The mathematical reduction can motivate formal statements about graph
automorphisms, polynomial ideals, and faithful actions. Under
[INTENT.md](../../INTENT.md), an implementation's own certificate or verifier
does not supply correctness evidence to the formal side. Leaf computations
remain subject to the independent `lean-cas-dsl` acceptance assertions.

A theorem formalizable in Lean belongs to the formalization surface, with
the declared mathematical inputs and hypotheses. It is not a route for a leaf
to certify its own standing. This distinction preserves the usefulness of the
polynomial reduction while retaining the programme's one-way evidence model.
