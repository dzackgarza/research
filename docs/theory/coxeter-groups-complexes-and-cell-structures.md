## Coxeter systems

- **Coxeter system.** `(W, S)` with Coxeter matrix `m`; word length `ℓ` with respect to `S`; reduced words.
- **Deletion and exchange conditions.** They characterize Coxeter systems among groups generated by involutions.
- **Root data.** A simple system `Δ`, positive roots `Φ⁺`, and the fundamental chamber `C = {x : (x, α) > 0 for α ∈ Δ}`.
- **Finite `W`.** There is a unique longest element `w₀`, with `ℓ(w₀) = |Φ⁺| = N`, the number of reflections.
- **Poincaré (growth) series.** `W(t) = Σ_w t^{ℓ(w)}`.
  - Finite `W`: `W(t) = ∏ (1 − t^{d_i})/(1 − t)`, where the `d_i` are the degrees of the basic invariants. Then `∏ d_i = |W|` and `Σ (d_i − 1) = N`. The degrees of `E_8` are 2, 8, 12, 14, 18, 20, 24, 30.
  - Parabolic factorization: `W(t) = W_J(t) · W^J(t)`.
  - Solomon's identity (finite `W`): `Σ_{J ⊆ S} (−1)^{|J|} W(t)/W_J(t) = t^N`.
  - Steinberg's formula (infinite `W`): `1/W(t^{-1}) = Σ_{J ⊆ S, W_J finite} (−1)^{|J|}/W_J(t)`. So `W(t)` is always rational.
  - Bott's formula (affine `W`): `W_aff(t) = W_0(t) ∏ 1/(1 − t^{d_i − 1})`.
  - Euler characteristic (Serre): `χ(W) = Σ_{J, W_J finite} (−1)^{|J|}/|W_J| = 1/W(1)`.
- **Growth of hyperbolic `W`.** Growth is exponential. The growth rate, the reciprocal of the radius of convergence, is often a Salem or a Perron number (Cannon–Wagreich, Floyd, Parry).
  - A Perron number is a real algebraic integer `> 1` that strictly dominates its other conjugates in absolute value.
  - A Salem number is a real algebraic integer `> 1` whose other conjugates lie in the closed unit disc, at least one of them on the circle.
- **Invariants.** The ring of invariants `k[V]^W` is a polynomial ring exactly when `W` is generated by pseudo-reflections (Chevalley–Shephard–Todd). Its generator degrees are the `d_i`. Molien's formula gives its Hilbert series. This leads to the GIT quotient `V // W = Spec k[V]^W`.
- **Crystallographic groups.** They are the stabilizers `GL(V)_L` of a lattice `L ⊂ V`, and a finite subgroup `W ≤ GL(V)` is crystallographic when it preserves some lattice. `W(L) ≤ O(L)` is crystallographic by construction.

## Complexes attached to a Coxeter system

- **Parabolic subgroups.** The subgroups `W_J` for `J ⊆ S`, as a poset; its finite members form the poset `𝒮^f`.
- **Coxeter complex `Σ(W, S)`.** The poset of cosets `wW_J` for `J ⊊ S`, ordered by reverse inclusion. It is the face poset of a simplicial complex whose vertices are the cosets of the `W_{S∖{s}}`. Its chambers are `W`, and it is thin. Its geometric realization is:
  - the sphere `S^{|S|−1}` for finite `W`;
  - Euclidean space tiled by alcoves for affine `W`;
  - in general, the interior of the Tits cone.
- **Tits cone.** `U = ⋃_w w C̄` in the dual of the geometric representation. `W` is finite if and only if `U` is the whole space.
- **Buildings.** A building is a chamber complex covered by apartments, each a Coxeter complex, subject to the building axioms. The Tits building of a reductive group arises from its BN-pair, and its apartments are Coxeter complexes of the Weyl group. "Tits complex" means this spherical building (owner, 2026-09-26).
- **Iwahori–Hecke algebra.** `H(W, S)` over `ZZ[q^{±1/2}]` has basis `T_w` for `w ∈ W`, the quadratic relations `(T_s − q)(T_s + 1) = 0` and the braid relations of `m`. At `q = 1` it specializes to `ZZ[W]`. It has the Kazhdan–Lusztig basis `C'_w` and polynomials `P_{y,w}`. For a finite group with BN-pair over `F_q`, `H(W, S)` at `q` is the convolution algebra of `B`-bi-invariant functions on `G`, which is `End_G` of the permutation module on the chambers of the building (Iwahori). The affine Hecke algebra plays the same role for `p`-adic groups and their Bruhat–Tits buildings (Iwahori–Matsumoto).
  Leads: Humphreys ch. 7; Geck–Pfeiffer, *Characters of Finite Coxeter Groups and Iwahori–Hecke Algebras*; Kazhdan–Lusztig, "Representations of Coxeter groups and Hecke algebras" (1979); Iwahori (1964); Iwahori–Matsumoto (1965); Sage `IwahoriHeckeAlgebra` and `KazhdanLusztigPolynomial`.
- **Davis complex.** The geometric realization of the poset `W 𝒮^f` of cosets of finite parabolic subgroups. It is contractible and CAT(0), and `W` acts on it properly and cocompactly.

## Cell structures

- **Complexes.** Simplicial complexes, Δ-complexes and simplicial sets, with face posets. The nerve of a poset is the order complex. The nerve of the category of simplices of a simplicial set is its barycentric subdivision.
- **CW complexes.**
  - Attaching maps `S^{n−1} → X^{n−1}`.
  - Regular CW complexes, whose attaching maps are embeddings; there the face poset determines the space up to homeomorphism.
  - Checks for being regular, simplicial, or a Δ-complex.
  - Regularization: induct up the skeleta, replacing each attaching map that is not an embedding with its mapping cylinder.
- **Maps of spheres.** Cells attached along maps whose homotopy classes lie in the known range of `π_{n+k}(S^n)`.
  - The 2-primary stable stems are known through about 90 (Isaksen–Wang–Xu). Check the current range.
  - Beyond that range, a map is given explicitly as a continuous or simplicial map. Deciding homotopy, for example of two attaching maps, or whether a map is a weak equivalence, then goes through simplicial approximation and effective homology.

Leads:
- Hatcher, *Algebraic Topology*: ch. 0, ch. 2.1 (Δ-complexes), ch. 4 (CW approximation, cellular approximation).
- Lundell–Weingram, *The Topology of CW Complexes* (regular complexes).
- Björner, "Posets, regular CW complexes and Bruhat order".
