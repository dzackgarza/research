Route I

Forms are the beginning.

Start with a form and its isometries. Then ask what changes when the coefficient ring has an involution, when a quadratic refinement matters, or when a boundary produces a finite linking pairing.

Core reference · algebra

Symmetric Bilinear Forms

John Milnor & Dale Husemoller · 1973

The natural bridge from lattice calculations to the general theory of forms. Start with “Basic Concepts” and “Symmetric Inner Product Spaces over Z”; then compare the field and discrete-valuation settings.

Bring back: orthogonal sums, isometries, isotropic subspaces, metabolic forms, and Witt equivalence as distinct constructions. A Gram matrix is a presentation; a Witt class has forgotten information that an isometry problem needs.

Focused companion · finite forms

Quadratic Forms on Finite Groups, and Related Topics. II

C. T. C. Wall · 1972

Read alongside the lattice theory, with finite bilinear pairings and quadratic refinements kept explicit. This is a useful route toward the algebra carried by the boundary of a manifold or by a non-unimodular integral form.

Bring back: the pairing, its value group, the refinement, and their isometries as separate data. For a nonsingular integral lattice, investigate the discriminant pairing on L* / L; an even lattice also gives its standard quadratic refinement. State the value convention before comparing formulas.

Foundation · bundles

Characteristic Classes

John Milnor & James Stasheff · 1974

Read the vector-bundle constructions before treating characteristic classes as a list of computable numbers. Follow Stiefel–Whitney, Euler, Chern, and Pontryagin classes with their coefficient groups and naturality maps.

Bring back: bundles over a specified base, bundle maps, pullbacks, orientations, Thom classes, and characteristic-class transformations. This is the prerequisite language for stable tangent and normal data, not an optional decoration on a manifold object.

Foundation · geometric moves

Lectures on the h-Cobordism Theorem

John Milnor · 1965

A compact source for the mechanism behind the high-dimensional story: Morse functions, handles, cancellation, and the geometric use of algebraic information.

Bring back: a cobordism with its incoming and outgoing boundary identifications, and the hypotheses under which handles can cancel. Track simple homotopy information when moving beyond the simply connected case; ordinary homotopy equivalence does not retain Whitehead torsion.

Route II

Obstructions come with maps.

L-theory is a source of reusable constructions: duality, quadratic structures, Poincaré pairs, bordism, and localization. Surgery explains which geometric problem an obstruction actually decides.

Start here · main spine

Algebraic and Geometric Surgery

Andrew Ranicki · 2002

The best first organizing text for this purpose. Read the algebra and geometry together, so that quadratic forms and formations arise from operations on manifolds rather than from an isolated API inventory.

Bring back: degree-one normal maps, surgery kernels, forms over group rings with involution, and obstruction classes with their dimension and decoration. Record the input problem and the theorem connecting vanishing to a geometric conclusion.

Classical reference · geometric surgery

Surgery on Compact Manifolds

C. T. C. Wall · 1970; second edition, edited by Andrew Ranicki, 1999

Use Wall to recover the original geometric contracts. Focus on the passage from surgery problems to algebraic obstructions and on the structure problem for a fixed homotopy type.

Bring back: the fundamental group and orientation character, the chosen category of manifolds, normal invariants, the structure set, and the action of an L-group. Treat “exactness” with its actual pointed-set and action meaning; do not silently replace the surgery sequence by a sequence of abelian groups.

Core reference · general machinery

Algebraic L-theory and Topological Manifolds

Andrew Ranicki · 1992

Read after the geometric introduction. The payoff is an algebraic language broad enough to express manifold recognition and classification through chain-level Poincaré structures and surgery.

Bring back: additive categories with chain duality; symmetric and quadratic Poincaré complexes; pairs, boundaries, and cobordism; assembly and the total surgery obstruction. Retain the chain data and structure maps before passing to an L-group class.

Second pass · modified surgery

Surgery and Duality

Matthias Kreck · Annals of Mathematics 149 · 1999

Read once the normal-map language is familiar. Modified surgery organizes classification around a normal k-type and bordism data, reducing the amount of homotopy information that must be fixed at the outset.

Bring back: a lift of the stable normal map to a specified fibration, its equivalences, and the corresponding bordism problem. This is especially useful inspiration for structured manifolds and moduli questions. Apply each classification result only with its stated dimension and arithmetic hypotheses.

Route III

Dimension four changes the rules.

Read the topological and smooth theories side by side. The intersection form is central to both, but its classification power depends on the category and the hypotheses.

Start here · concrete geometry

4-Manifolds and Kirby Calculus

Robert E. Gompf & András I. Stipsicz · 1999

The most direct source for turning handle diagrams into useful mathematical objects. Start with the four-manifold background and Kirby calculus, then follow the constructions involving complex, symplectic, and Stein surfaces.

Bring back: framed attaching links, handle attachments, boundary manifolds, handle slides, and intersection pairings. Distinguish a presentation move from a change in the represented manifold. Recover the lattice through the topology rather than treating the diagram as a labelled matrix.

Core reference · topological classification

Topology of 4-Manifolds

Michael H. Freedman & Frank Quinn · 1990

Study the disk-embedding and topological surgery machinery behind the classification results. Keep the simply connected case separate from extensions that impose conditions on the fundamental group.

Bring back: intersection forms together with the Kirby–Siebenmann invariant and the hypotheses of the realization and classification theorems. This is a model for specifying the exact domain on which an invariant becomes complete; it is not a smooth classification theorem.

Core reference · smooth geometry

The Geometry of Four-Manifolds

S. K. Donaldson & P. B. Kronheimer · 1990

Read “Four-Manifolds” and “Topology and Connections” as orientation, then follow the moduli-space construction and “Invariants of Smooth Four-Manifolds.” For algebraic surfaces, the chapters on stable holomorphic bundles and differential topology are the bridge.

Bring back: connections, gauge transformations, quotient moduli problems, orientations, and invariants derived from moduli spaces. A lattice is an essential invariant, but it does not retain the smooth, holomorphic, or Hodge-theoretic structures used here.

Concrete questions to keep beside the books
  1. Starting with an even nonsingular integral lattice, construct its discriminant pairing and quadratic refinement. How do isotropic subgroups describe integral overlattices?
  2. For a four-manifold built from a zero-handle and two-handles, recover the intersection matrix from the framed attaching link. Under which hypotheses does its cokernel give the first homology of the boundary?
  3. Express a boundary linking pairing from a presentation matrix, retaining the orientation and sign convention. Compare it with the lattice discriminant pairing.
  4. Follow one degree-one normal map from its normal data to its surgery obstruction. List every hypothesis needed to turn vanishing into the desired equivalence.
  5. Compare the information retained by an isometry class, a Witt class, a bordism class, a homeomorphism type, and a diffeomorphism type.
  6. Use a K3 surface to separate its underlying intersection lattice from its period, ample cone, and smooth or complex structures. Which forgetful maps are involved?

Distinctions that shape the interface

Bilinear and quadratic are different data. At the prime 2, a refinement is particularly consequential. Carry the involution, sign, and coefficient convention through every construction. Forms references ↑

A stable class is not an object with all its maps. Passing to a Witt or L-group class discards structure. Retain the source form or Poincaré complex when later operations need it. Ranicki ↑

High-dimensional surgery needs its dimension range. The standard surgery classification framework applies in dimensions at least five, with the appropriate category and other hypotheses. Four dimensions requires separate theorems. Wall and Ranicki ↑

TOP, PL, and DIFF are part of the problem. Freedman–Quinn’s topological theory and Donaldson–Kronheimer’s smooth theory answer different classification questions. Preserve this distinction in objects and theorem statements. Four-dimensional references ↑

Exactness can involve actions and pointed sets. A structure set is not automatically a group. Preserve the action and orbit statement that express the relevant exactness. Surgery references ↑

Topology does not supply a Hodge structure. A period and its compatibility with a pairing are additional data. The topological lattice is a useful common owner, not a substitute for the structured object. Geometry and algebraic surfaces ↑
