## §I The chamber determination and the (D2) reduction (narrowed per the owner's corrections)

**Lemma CH.1.** $\{x \in S_\Co : x^2 = -2,\ H.x = 0\} = \{\pm\alpha\}$. Hence the labels' own arrangement has exactly one wall through $H$, $\alpha^\perp$.
*Proof.* $x = ae+bf+w+c\alpha$, $w^2 = -4m \le 0$; $H.x = 2(a+b) = 0$ and $x^2 = 4ab-4m-2c^2 = -2$ give $2a^2+2m+c^2 = 1$, so $a=m=0$, $c^2=1$. (No $E_8$ classification used.) ∎

**Theorem CH.2 (pinning at a member is unconditional).** At a marked member $(X,\psi)$ with transported classes $H_b,\alpha_b$, the member's chamber arrangement has exactly one wall through $H_b$, namely $\alpha_b^\perp$; exactly two chambers have $H_b$ in closure; the $\alpha_b$-positive one is the chamber containing the Kähler cone. No accumulation hypothesis is ever needed: corpus :638's "walls can accumulate at $H_b$" cannot occur.
*Proof.* @lem:face-at-H(4): the only effective $(-2)$-class orthogonal to $H_b$ is $\pm\alpha_b$. Every other effective root $\delta$ has $H_b.\delta \in \mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}$ (integrality), hence $\operatorname{dist}(H_b,\delta^\perp) = |H_b.\delta|/|\delta| \ge 1/\sqrt 2$, uniformly. A ball of radius $< 1/(2\sqrt 2)$ around $H_b$ meets only $\alpha_b^\perp$; the two sides are the chambers; $\alpha_b$-positivity selects the Kähler-containing one ($H_b$ ample, $\mathrm{Kähler}.\alpha_b > 0$). ∎

**Lemma CH.0a.** $O(S_{dP})_{H,\alpha} = \{\mathrm{id}, s_{e-f}\}$, order 2, where $s_{e-f}$ swaps $e\leftrightarrow f$.
*Proof.* $P := \langle H,\alpha\rangle \subset S_{dP}$ is primitive rank 2 (script-verified: no halving), $P^\perp \cap S_{dP} = \mathbb Z(e-f)$ rank 1; an isometry fixing $H,\alpha$ preserves both, acts on the rank-one lattice by $\pm 1$; both values occur and determine the isometry on $P + \mathbb Z(e-f) = S_{dP}$. ∎

**Theorem CH.0 (structure).** With $\Gamma_L = \operatorname{im}(Z_{O(L_{K3})}(I_\Co,I_{dP})_{H,\alpha} \to O(T_\Co))$ and $\Gamma^{dir}_{\Co,2}$ the chamber-refined analogue:

## §II (X2), closed negatively: no $A$-ample realization has $\alpha$ reducible

Standing hypotheses of the section: a marked pair $(X,\psi)$ with $\psi(S_\Co) \subset \mathrm{NS}(X)$ and $\psi(A)$ ample. Established inputs at their scopes: (E1) $H$ nef; $e,f$ base-point-free pencils with $H.C \ge 0$ for effective $C$ (Lemma 4b + Lemma 5, `:286-336`); (E2) the double cover $\phi = (\phi_e,\phi_f) : X \to Y = \mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$ branched along $B \in |O_Y(4,4)|$, fibre components over singular points of $B$ are smooth rational $(-2)$-curves with $H.E_i = 0$, and every $(-2)$-class in $H^\perp$ is effective up to sign over a single singular point (Section QC Q3-Q4); (E3) $\alpha$ effective with $A.\alpha = 2$ (RR: $\chi = 1$; the sign $-\alpha$ is killed by $A$ ample); (E4) $I_\Co, I_{dP}$ commute and are automorphisms fixing $A$; $I_{dP}$ is the deck involution, $I_\Co$ covers $\tau = (\tau_1,\tau_2) \in \operatorname{Aut} Y$, each $\tau_i$ a nontrivial involution of $\mathbb P^1$; (E5) $S_\Co$ has no $H$-null $(-2)$-class other than $\pm\alpha$ (CH.1). Textbook facts: (S1) Cartan linearization; (S2) the Milnor number depends on the ideal, not the equation.

**Lemma 1 (fibre count).** Let $q_1,\dots,q_k$ be the singular points of $B$, $F_i = \phi^{-1}(q_i)$ with components $D_{i,1},\dots,D_{i,r_i}$. Then (a) $k = 1$; (b) every component of $F_1$ lies in $\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)$ with multiplicity $\ge 1$; hence $A.\alpha = \sum m_j (A.D_j) = 2$ with all terms positive integers, so $r_1 \le 2$; (c) $r_1 = 1$ forces $\alpha = D_1$, a single smooth rational $(-2)$-curve, $|\alpha| = \{D_1\}$; (d) $r_1 = 2$ forces $m_1 = m_2 = 1$, $D_1.D_2 = 1$, $D_i^2 = -2$, $A.D_i = 1$, an $A_2$ rational double point, $\alpha = D_1 + D_2$.
*Proof.* (i) $\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)$ is a union of fibre components: each component $C$ of $\alpha$ has $C.H = 0$ (E3,E1), so $C.e = C.f = 0$, so it lies over a single point, a singular point of $B$ by (E2). (ii) No fibre component is outside $\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)$: if $D$ had multiplicity 0 in $\alpha$, then $A.D = -\alpha.D = -\sum m_{i,l} (D_{i,l}.D) \le 0$ (distinct curves meet non-negatively, and $D^2$ does not occur since $m_D = 0$), contradicting $A.D \ge 1$ ($A$ ample, $D$ effective). (iii) The count $A.\alpha = 2$ with positive-integer terms allows at most two components total, so $k = 1$, $r_1 \le 2$. (c) $\alpha = mD_1$, $-2 = m^2 D_1^2$ forces $m = 1$, $D_1^2 = -2$; (E5) then gives $D_1 = \alpha$; the exact sequence $0 \to O \to O(\alpha) \to O_{\mathbb P^1}(-2) \to 0$ gives $h^0(\alpha) = 1$. (d) The count gives multiplicities and $A$-degrees; $D_1.D_2 = 2m_1 - A.D_1 = 1$; $-2 = \alpha^2 = D_1^2 + D_2^2 + 2$ with $D_i^2 \ge -2$ (adjunction on a K3) forces $-2,-2$; Artin contractibility gives the $A_2$ RDP on the contracted model. ∎

**Lemma 2 (an $A_2$ fibre forces a cusp).** In case (d), with $b$ the branch equation at $p$: $\operatorname{mult}_p b = 2$, $b_2 = c\ell^2$ ($c \ne 0$), and the coefficient of $x^3$ in $b_3$ (for $\ell = y$) is nonzero; hence $b \sim y^2 + x^3$.
*Proof.* The local model $X^\sharp = \{z^2 = b\}$ with $X$ its minimal resolution: the $A_2$ singularity has tangent cone two *distinct* lines, so $\operatorname{mult}_p b = 2$ and $b_2 = c\ell^2$. Blow up $p$ in the chart $y = xY$: the proper transform is $\tilde b = Y^2 + x\,b_3(1,Y) + x^2 b_4(1,Y) + \cdots$ and the pulled-back double cover restricts over the exceptional $e$ to the degree-2 cover branched along $\tilde B \cap e$ counted with multiplicity 2. *If the coefficient $c_3$ of $x^3$ in $b_3$ vanishes*: then $Y \mid b_3(1,Y)$, and both partials of $\tilde b$ vanish at the origin of $e$, so $\tilde B$ is singular there, the cover is singular above, and the resolution needs a further blow-up — a third fibre component, contradicting $r_1 = 2$. *If $c_3 \ne 0$*: $\tilde B$ is smooth and tangent to $e$ at the point over $p$, the cover $\{Z^2 = \tilde b\}$ is smooth there, and $\pi^*e$ splits as $D_1 + D_2$ with $D_1.D_2 = 1$ — the $A_2$ fibre. The weighted-leading part (weights $x:2$, $y:3$) is $y^2 + c_3 x^3$ of weight 6, all other terms of weight $\ge 7$, so by the semi-quasihomogeneous normal form $b \sim y^2 + x^3$. ∎

**Lemma 3 (no cusp at a $\tau$-fixed point).** If $p$ is $\tau$-fixed and $B$ is a $\tau$-invariant reduced curve through $p$, $B$ is not analytically a cusp at $p$.
*Proof.* At a fixed point $d\tau_p = -\mathrm{id}$ (each factor's derivative is $-1$ at its fixed points); by (S1) choose coordinates with $\tau = -\mathrm{id}$. The equation satisfies $b\circ(-\mathrm{id}) = u b$ for a unit $u$ with $(u\circ(-\mathrm{id}))u = 1$; put $b' := u^{1/2}b$; then $b' \circ (-\mathrm{id}) = b'$, so $b'$ has only even-degree terms and defines the same germ, hence the same Milnor number (S2). For an even germ: if $b'_2$ is non-degenerate, $\mu = 1$; if $b'_2 = c y^2$ ($c\ne0$) then $(b')_y \in 2cy + \mathfrak m^3$ and the Jacobian ideal lies in $(y) + \mathfrak m^3$, so $\mu \ge \dim \mathcal O/((y) + \mathfrak m^3) = \dim \mathbb C\{x\}/(x^3) = 3$; if $b'_2 = 0$ then both partials lie in $\mathfrak m^3$, so $\mu \ge \dim \mathcal O/\mathfrak m^3 = 6$ *(referee's numeral: 6, not 3 as in the member's draft — the bound only strengthens)* — hence $\mu(b') \in \{1\} \cup [3, 4, \dots)$ up from 3, never 2. The cusp has $\mu = \dim \mathbb C\{x,y\}/(3x^2, 2y) = 2$. Since $\mu$ is a right-equivalence invariant, $b$ is not a cusp. ∎
*(Machine corroboration, exploratory, tier-labelled: the localized Milnor numbers of twelve even germs all lie in $\{1,3,5,9\}$; the cusp alone attains 2; the multiplicity system over all Dynkin and affine graphs returns exactly the $A_1,(1)$ and $A_2,(1,1)$ solutions — m3's `fibre-count-check.py`, `even-mu-local.py` logs.)*
