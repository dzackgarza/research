VERDICT.  The data do not determine the limit at this cusp, and the reason is a count.

Fix the 1-cusp J.  Its Baily-Borel boundary component is a modular curve [AEGS25, Def. 2.9,
printed p. 588], that is a quotient of the upper half plane by a finite-index group, on which
the j-invariant is a non-constant holomorphic function; AET23 records the finite morphism to
the j-line in the parallel case [AET23, section 3B, printed p. 16].  So the limits over J
carry uncountably many values of j.  The points over the cusp are KSBA stable limits of
degenerations in F_En,2 [AEGS25, section 5.3, printed p. 611], and the preimage of the
component in the compactification is a family over it [AET23, section 3C, printed p. 17].

Every such degeneration has a monodromy invariant lam, and since the cusp is the plane
(Z d + Z lam)_sat [AE22, section 6A, printed p. 28] a lam at a FIXED cusp ranges over J/Ze,
a rank 1 lattice: countably many, computed above as the coordinate b, which is unbounded.
J is totally isotropic, so each of them satisfies lam^2 = 0 and the Type II condition
excludes none of them.

Now count.  If each lam occurred at a single j, the j-values realised over the cusp would
be at most countable, one per lam.  They are uncountable.  So one lam occurs at two
different j.  At a fixed transform the data can be chosen to be the same for all
degenerations [AEGS25, section 4.1, printed p. 599], and a limit carries the j of its double
curve as an invariant of the pair [AEGS25, proof of Theorem 5.9, printed p. 612].  Those two
limits are therefore non-isomorphic with identical (Gamma, R_IA): the conjecture is false,
and no elliptic curve had to be constructed to see it.
