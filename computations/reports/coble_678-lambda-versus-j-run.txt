VERDICT.  The data do not determine the limit, and the reason is a count.

Fix a 1-cusp of F_En,2.  By Sterk, section 4.1, printed p. 15, a rational boundary component
of the period domain with sgn L_- = (2,10) is a singleton for a rank 1 isotropic I and a
HALF-PLANE for a rank 2 one; and section 4.3.16, printed p. 27, computes the one-dimensional
components as H/Gamma^1(2) with Gamma^1(2) a congruence subgroup of SL2(Z), while section
4.3.17, printed p. 27, has Gamma' containing the level 2 subgroup Gamma(2) in the remaining
cases.  So the boundary component of a 1-cusp is H/Gamma' with Gamma' a congruence subgroup
of SL2(Z): uncountable, and the classical j is a non-constant holomorphic function on it,
hence its image is uncountable.  The points of the KSBA compactification over it are the
KSBA stable limits of degenerations in F_En,2 [AEGS25, section 5.3, printed p. 611], and the
j-invariant of such a boundary point is recovered from the stable pair [AEGS25, proof of
Theorem 5.9, printed p. 612].

On the other side, a degeneration has a monodromy invariant lam, and since the cusp is the
plane (Z d + Z lam)_sat [AE22, section 6A, printed p. 28], a lam at a FIXED cusp is a class in
J/Ze: rank one by the computation above, hence countably many, and each is admissible because
J is totally isotropic.

Count: were each lam attached to one value of j, the j realised over the cusp would be
countable, one per lam.  Uncountably many j against countably many lam leaves one lam at two
different j.  At a fixed transform the data can be chosen the same for all degenerations
[AEGS25, section 4.1, printed p. 599], so the two limits carry one (Gamma, R_IA) and, having
different j, are non-isomorphic.  The conjecture is false.
