# Two-elementary lattice specimens from Alexeev–Engel and Nikulin

Sources: V. Alexeev and P. Engel, *Compactifications of moduli spaces of K3
surfaces with a nonsymplectic involution*, [arXiv:2208.10383](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.10383),
Figure 1, Table 2 and the theorem and notation immediately following Table 2
(local source [2el.tex](../../../writing/coble/papers/AE22/2el.tex),
`tab:neg-def`, `thm:2-elem-negdef`); V. V. Nikulin, *Factor groups of groups
of automorphisms of hyperbolic forms with respect to subgroups generated by
2-reflections*, J. Soviet Math. 22 (1983), 1401–1475,
[DOI 10.1007/BF01094757](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01094757), Table 1 and
Theorem 4.3.2.

The original fixtures are preserved in
[`source-main/tests/fixtures/`](../../../archives/provenance/lattice-research/source-main/tests/fixtures/).
Their `verified: true` fields are historical claims, not evidence.
The corrected reference data here use the paper's notation and the
bilinear/quadric discriminant convention in
[`two-elementary-lattices.md`](../../../writing/lattice-theory/classification/two-elementary-lattices.md).
They are mathematical specimens, not an alternative executable category or
catalogue API.

- `table-2-source-rows.json`: all 128 rows, preserving the exact TeX lattice
  labels and whether there are zero, one, or two stars. A single star means a
  proper finite-index extension of the displayed root lattice. Two stars
  mean the root lattice has infinite index.
- `negative-definite-direct-sums.json`: all 82 unstarred rows, including the
  46 old specimens. `summands` lists the actual direct summands.
- `negative-definite-glued-specimens.json`: all 16 originally attempted
  starred constructions, with corrected glue vectors in the basis specified
  below. These are not an enumeration of every starred row of Table 2.
- `nikulin-k3-invariant-lattices.json`: all 75 triples of the original
  invariant-lattice table, with explicit constructions of signature
  `[1,r-1]`. The rank-one member is positive definite; the rest are
  hyperbolic. The recipes are taken from the named source revision of
  sage-extensions and checked mathematically against the prescribed triples.

The table identifies an individual definite lattice by its root sublattice
and invariants, not by its triple alone. Table 2 includes distinct rows with
identical triples. No identification of two such lattices is inferred merely
from the triple.

## Relation to the maintained catalogue

At sage-extensions revision [`c457e6b4`](https://github.com/dzackgarza/sage-extensions/blob/c457e6b4aa087d447711b6cc374a212b72ef5051/src/sage_extensions/catalogue.py), `TwoElementary` implements the 75 K3 invariant triples and `NegativeDefTwoElementary` stores 128 recipes grouped under 51 invariant triples. Comparing the number of recipes for every triple with `table-2-source-rows.json` gives exact agreement. This establishes that the table already has a maintained representation; it is not a proof of row-by-row isometry or of the prescribed root sublattices. In particular 51 is a count of keys, not a count of definite isometry classes. The independently derived specimens in this directory supply mathematical evidence for checking the catalogue rather than an alternative implementation. The root inclusions, glue indices and pairwise distinction of repeated triples require their own mathematical acceptance checks.

## Basis and discriminant conventions

All root lattices here are negative definite. Write $e_i$ for orthonormal
vectors for the positive Euclidean product; the Gram form is the negative
of that product. For $A_1$ use a root $a$ with $(a,a)=-2$. For $D_n$ use
$\alpha_i=e_i-e_{i+1}$ for $1\le i<n$ and
$\alpha_n=e_{n-1}+e_n$. For $E_7$ use the first seven roots of the following
$E_8$ basis:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\alpha_1&=(e_1-e_2-e_3-e_4-e_5-e_6-e_7+e_8)/2,\
\alpha_2&=e_1+e_2,\
\alpha_i&=e_{i-1}-e_{i-2}\qquad(3\le i\le 8).
\end{aligned}
$$

The last six roots in this list run from $e_2-e_1$ to $e_7-e_6$.
An orthogonal sum concatenates the summand bases in the listed order.
The suffix `_2` means multiplication of the pairing by 2, not a change of
underlying group. The symbols `Z_2` and `Z_m2` mean rank-one Gram matrices
$[2]$ and $[-2]$ respectively; `U` has Gram matrix
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$;
`E10` means $U\perp E_8$.

The discriminant group is $A_R=R^\vee/R$, with
$q_R(x+R)=(x,x)\bmod 2\mathbb Z$ and
$b_R(x+R,y+R)=(x,y)\bmod\mathbb Z$.
The following table describes the representatives used in the glue data.
The displayed lengths are positive Euclidean squared lengths (negative
of the lattice square).

| Summand | Class label | Representative | Minimum squared length |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $A_1$ | `half` | $a/2$ | $1/2$ |
| $D_n$ | `vector` | $e_1$ | $1$ |
| $D_n$ | `spinor` | $(e_1+\cdots+e_n)/2$ | $n/4$ |
| $E_7$ | `weight` | $e_6+(e_7-e_8)/2$ | $3/2$ |
| any | `zero` | $0$ | $0$ |

For $D_n$, the vector and spinor classes have coordinates
$(1,\ldots,1,1/2,1/2)$ and
$(1/2,1,\ldots,(n-2)/2,(n-2)/4,n/4)$, respectively.
For $E_7$, the weight has coordinates
$(-1,-1/2,-1,-1,-1/2,0,1/2)$ in the above basis.
The minimum for the $A_1$ coset follows from its half-integer coefficient.
For $D_n$, the vector coset consists of integral vectors of odd coordinate
sum, and the spinor coset consists of vectors with every coordinate
half-integral; the exhibited representatives attain the stated bounds.
For $E_7$, the displayed weight pairs integrally with all seven simple
roots, has squared Euclidean length $3/2$ and is not in $E_7$ (the lattice is
even). The Cartan determinant is 2. The squared lengths in its coset are
congruent to $3/2$ modulo 2, so none can be smaller.

## Verification of the direct sums

For an orthogonal sum of even 2-elementary lattices, ranks, signatures and
discriminant lengths add; coparity is the maximum of the coparities of the
summands, since the discriminant quadratic module is their orthogonal sum.
The blocks used in the data have the following invariants:

| Block | Rank | $a$ | $\delta$ | Positive index |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $[2]$ | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| $A_1=[-2]$ | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 |
| $U$ | 2 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| $U(2)$ | 2 | 2 | 0 | 1 |
| $D_n$, $n$ even | $n$ | 2 | $0$ if $4\mid n$, otherwise $1$ | 0 |
| $E_7$ | 7 | 1 | 1 | 0 |
| $E_8$ | 8 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| $E_8(2)$ | 8 | 8 | 0 | 0 |
| $E_{10}$ | 10 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| $E_{10}(2)$ | 10 | 10 | 0 | 1 |

The determinant and evenness of the displayed Gram matrices give these
values: the four classes of $D_n^\vee/D_n$ are zero, the vector class and
the two spinor classes, with squares $0,-1,-n/4,-n/4$ modulo 2. The
nonzero class of $E_7^\vee/E_7$ has square $-3/2$. The lattices $U$ and
$E_8$ are even unimodular (their Gram determinants are $-1$ and $1$).
For any even unimodular lattice $M$, its twist $M(2)$ has dual
$\frac12M$, so $A_{M(2)}\cong M/2M$ and its discriminant squares are
integers. This proves the twisted rows. The zero lattice has rank and
discriminant length zero and coparity zero.

For each of the 75 invariant lattices the prescribed positive index is
one; in particular an old spelling with only negative roots or two
hyperbolic planes is not a valid construction just because its triple
agrees. The recipes in the corrected data satisfy the signature as well
as the discriminant invariants.

## Verification of the sixteen finite-index extensions

Let $R$ be the displayed negative-definite direct sum and let $g$ be the
listed rational vector. For each row, $g\in R^\vee$, $2g\in R$, and
$g\notin R$. Its Euclidean square is 4, so
$H=\langle g+R\rangle\subset A_R$ is isotropic. Thus
$T=R+\mathbb Zg$ is an even lattice, $[T:R]=2$, and its discriminant
quadratic module is $H^\perp/H$ (Nikulin, *Integral symmetric bilinear
forms and some of their applications*, Math. USSR Izv. 14 (1980),
Proposition 1.4.1). In particular the exponent divides 2 and
$a(T)=a(R)-2$.

To compute $\delta(T)$, use the characteristic class $w\in A_R$ defined
by $b_R(w,x)=q_R(x)\bmod\mathbb Z$ for all $x$. It exists uniquely since
$q_R\bmod\mathbb Z$ is additive on a 2-elementary discriminant group
and $b_R$ is nondegenerate. Its components are the nonzero class for
$A_1$ and $E_7$, the vector class for $D_n$ when $n\equiv2\pmod4$, and
zero for the other blocks. The claim for $D_n$ follows by pairing the
vector and spinor representatives: their pairing is $-1/2$ while
the vector's self-pairing is zero modulo $\mathbb Z$.
Then $\delta(T)=0$ if and only if $w\in H$, since $q_R$ is integral on
$H^\perp$ precisely when $w\in(H^\perp)^\perp=H$. This computes the
prescribed coparity in every row.

It remains to check the *root sublattice*, rather than only the triple.
Each glue coset has minimum Euclidean squared length exactly 4, by the
componentwise minima above. Thus there are no new vectors of square
$-2$. There are also no vectors of square $-4$ and divisibility 2
outside $R$: if $x\in g+R$ paired evenly with $T$, then $x/2\in
R^\vee$, and $x+R=2(x/2+R)=0$ in the 2-elementary group $A_R$, a
contradiction. The displayed root lattice $R$ is generated by its
square-$-2$ roots; it is consequently exactly the root sublattice for
the full reflection group described by Alexeev–Engel. The theorem
immediately after Table 2 now identifies each $T$ with the listed
isometry class, not merely some lattice with the same triple.

For example, the historical $A_1^9$ specimen glued only the last two
half-roots, producing square $-1$ and an odd extension. The corrected
vector is the half-sum of eight roots; it has square $-4$, produces
$(9,7,1)$, and does not introduce any new roots. The same correction
principle applies to the other recorded witnesses.

## Independent arithmetic check

For the integral basis Gram matrix $G$, the conditions checked were
$\lvert\det G\rvert=2^a$, $2G^{-1}$ integral, and $\delta=0$
if and only if the diagonal of $G^{-1}$ is integral. The latter test
suffices because the mixed terms in $x^{\mathsf T}G^{-1}x$ have
coefficients $2(G^{-1})_{ij}$. For the glued rows, reduce the coordinates
of $g$ modulo $\mathbb Z$, choose an entry equal to $1/2$, and
replace that standard basis vector by the reduced $g$; these vectors
form a basis of $T$, since twice the new vector minus the other
integral basis vectors recovers the omitted vector. Congruence by this
basis gives the Gram matrix to check.

On 2026-11-27 exact rational row reduction on the JSON constructions
checked these conditions, integrality, evenness and the prescribed
signature for all 82 unstarred rows, 16 recovered glued rows and 75
K3 invariant lattice rows. The root-coset arguments above establish
the remaining root-sublattice assertion without trusting a CAS's
classification. This is a check of mathematical reference data, not
acceptance evidence about a computational leaf.
