Let $\Lambda = \mathrm{II}_{3,19}$ be the K3 lattice and $G \subset O(\Lambda)$ a finite group of symplectic isometries.
Row 61 of Table 10.3 of Hashimoto gives the invariant lattice $\Lambda^G$ for the groups $\mathfrak{G}_{61} \cong \mathfrak{A}_{4,3}$, $\mathfrak{G}_{19} \cong C_2 \times C_6$, $\mathfrak{G}_{20} \cong Q_{12}$, $\mathfrak{G}_{36} \cong C_3 \rtimes D_8$, $\mathfrak{G}_{47} \cong C_3 \times \mathfrak{A}_4$ of Table 10.2, and this record is $\Lambda^G(-1)$; $\Lambda^G$ has signature $(3, 1)$ and the corpus records the sign with $n_+ \leq n_-$.

Roots: write $r = a e_{1} + c e_{2} + w$ with $w$ in the span $W$ of $e_{3}$ and $e_{4}$.
$b(e_{1}, e_{2}) = -3$ is odd, $W$ is orthogonal to $e_{1}$ and $e_{2}$, and $b(w, w) \in 4\mathbb{Z}$ for $w$ in $W$, because the Gram tensor of $W$ has its diagonal components in $4\mathbb{Z}$ and the others in $2\mathbb{Z}$.
So $b(r, e_{2}) = -3 a$, $b(r, e_{1}) = -3 c$ and $b(r, r) = -6 a c + b(w, w)$.
If $a$ is odd and $c$ is even, then $b(r, r) \equiv 0 \bmod 4$, and $b(r, r)$ divides $2 b(r, e_{2}) = -6 a \equiv 2 \bmod 4$, which is impossible; the same holds with $a$ and $c$ exchanged.
Each root is therefore in $L' = \{a \equiv c \bmod 2\}$, which has index 2 in $L$.
$e_{1} + e_{2}$ has $b(r, r) = -6$ and $b(r, L) = 3\mathbb{Z}$, and $-6$ divides $6$.
$e_{1} - e_{2}$ has $b(r, r) = 6$ and $b(r, L) = 3\mathbb{Z}$, and $6$ divides $6$.
$e_{3}$ and $e_{4}$ have $b(r, r) = -8$ and $b(r, L) = 4\mathbb{Z}$, and $-8$ divides $8$.
These roots are in $L'$, and their invariant factors in $L$ are $1, \dots, 1, 2$, so they generate $L'$: $\mathbb{Z}\Phi(L) = L'$, of index 2 in $L$.
