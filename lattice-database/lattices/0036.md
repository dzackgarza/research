Roots: let $e, f$ be the basis of $U$ and $g, h$ the basis of $U(2)$, and let $r = a e + c f + p g + q h$ be a root.
$b(r, r) = 2ac + 4pq$, and $b(r, L) = \delta\mathbb{Z}$ with $\delta = \gcd(a, c, 2p, 2q)$.
$r$ is primitive, so $\gcd(a, c, p, q) = 1$, and $\delta \in \{1, 2\}$.
$b(r, r)$ divides $2 b(r, x)$ for each $x$ in $L$, so it divides $2\delta$.
When $\delta = 2$, $a$ and $c$ are even.
When $\delta = 1$, $b(r, r)$ divides 2 and is even, so $ac + 2pq = \pm 1$, and $a$ and $c$ are odd.
Each root is therefore in $M = \{a \equiv c \bmod 2\}$, which has index 2 in $L$ and the basis $e + f$, $e - f$, $g$, $h$.
$e + f$, $e - f$, $e + f + g$ and $e + f + h$ are roots, and they generate $M$: $\mathbb{Z}\Phi(L) = M = \mathbb{Z}(e + f) \oplus \mathbb{Z}(e - f) \oplus U(2)$, an orthogonal sum.
