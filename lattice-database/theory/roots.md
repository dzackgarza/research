Let $L$ be a [lattice](lattices.html#lattices): the form $b$ can be indefinite or degenerate, and its values need not be integers.

## Roots {#roots}

A *root* of $L$ is a primitive $r \in L$ with $b(r, r) \neq 0$ and $s_r \in O(L)$, where $s_r(x) = x - \frac{2 b(x, r)}{b(r, r)} r$; equivalently, $2 b(r, L) \subseteq b(r, r) \mathbb{Z}$.
$\Phi(L)$ is the set of roots of $L$, and $\Phi_S(L) := \{r \in \Phi(L) : b(r, r) \in S\}$ for $S \subseteq \mathbb{Q}$.

$L$ is an *$S$-root lattice* when $L = \mathbb{Z}\Phi_S(L)$, and a *root lattice* when $L = \mathbb{Z}\Phi(L)$.

For the [twist](lattices.html#twists) $L(k)$, $\Phi(L(k)) = \Phi(L)$ and $\Phi_{kS}(L(k)) = \Phi_S(L)$: $E_8(2)$ is a $\{4\}$-root lattice.

Let $L$ be [integral](lattices.html#integral) and nondegenerate, and $r \in \Phi(L)$ with $b(r, L) = d\mathbb{Z}$, $d > 0$.
Then $b(r, r) \in \{\pm d, \pm 2d\}$, and $d$ divides the exponent of the [discriminant group](discriminant-forms.html#discriminant-group).
Each $r \in L$ with $b(r, r) \in \{\pm 1, \pm 2\}$ is a root.

## Root sublattices {#root-sublattices}

$\mathbb{Z}\Phi_S(L)$ is the sublattice that $\Phi_S(L)$ generates.
It is one sublattice of $L$, and it is stable under $O(L)$.
$\mathbb{Z}\Phi(L)$ is a root lattice: a root $r$ of $L$ is primitive in $\mathbb{Z}\Phi(L)$, and $s_r(x) \in x + \mathbb{Z}r$ for each $x \in L$, so $r \in \Phi(\mathbb{Z}\Phi(L))$.
$\mathbb{Z}\Phi(L)$ contains each sublattice of $L$ that roots of $L$ generate.
$L$ is a root lattice exactly when $[L : \mathbb{Z}\Phi(L)] = 1$, and the index is finite exactly when $\mathbb{Z}\Phi(L)$ has the rank of $L$.
A sublattice of $L$ that is a root lattice is a different notion: $\mathbb{Z}v$ is a root lattice for each $v \in L$ with $b(v, v) \neq 0$, and $v$ is not a root of $L$ in general.

$\mathbb{Z}\Phi_S(L)$ is not primitive in $L$ in general: $\mathbb{Z}\Phi_{\{2\}}(\mathbb{Z}^n) = \{x : \sum_i x_i \in 2\mathbb{Z}\}$ has index 2 for $n \geq 2$.

The *root sublattice* of $L$ with norms in $S$ is $R_S(L) := \mathbb{Z}\Phi_S(L)$, and $R(L) := R_{\mathbb{Q}}(L) = \mathbb{Z}\Phi(L)$.
Let $Q_S(L)$ be the set of the sublattices $M \subseteq L$ with $M = \mathbb{Z}(M \cap \Phi_S(L))$, the sublattices that roots of $L$ with norms in $S$ generate, ordered by inclusion.
$R_S(L)$ is the greatest element of $Q_S(L)$, so it is a function of $L$: $R_S(L) \in Q_S(L)$, and $M \subseteq \mathbb{Z}\Phi_S(L)$ for each $M \in Q_S(L)$.
Each $M \in Q_S(L)$ is an $S$-root lattice, because $M \cap \Phi(L) \subseteq \Phi(M)$.
$L$ is a root lattice exactly when $R(L) = L$, and $R(L) \hookrightarrow L$ is then an isomorphism of lattices.
$R(L)$ is stable under $O(L)$.

The embedding $R(L) \hookrightarrow L$ is not primitive in general, and the page of a lattice states the index $[L : R(L)]$ and the quotient.
[$D_{12}^+$](../tag/0151.html) is generated by $D_{12}$ and $h = (\tfrac{1}{2}, \dots, \tfrac{1}{2})$.
$D_{12}^+$ is unimodular, so each root $r$ has $b(r, r) \in \{1, 2\}$, and each vector with coordinates in $\tfrac{1}{2} + \mathbb{Z}$ has $b(x, x) \geq 3$, so $\Phi(D_{12}^+)$ is the set of the 264 vectors $\pm e_i \pm e_j$, $i \neq j$, and $[D_{12}^+ : R(D_{12}^+)] = [D_{12}^+ : D_{12}] = 2$.
$R([U](../tag/0016.html)) = \mathbb{Z}(e + f) \oplus \mathbb{Z}(e - f) \cong \langle 2 \rangle \oplus \langle -2 \rangle$ has index $2$ in $U$.
$R(\mathbb{Z}^n) = \mathbb{Z}^n$, and $R_{\{2\}}(\mathbb{Z}^n) = D_n$ has index $2$ in $\mathbb{Z}^n$ for $n \geq 2$.

Primitivity is not part of the definition, because it breaks uniqueness.
Let $P_S(L) \subseteq Q_S(L)$ be the subset of the primitive sublattices.
$P_S(L)$ has a maximum exactly when $\mathbb{Z}\Phi_S(L)$ is primitive in $L$, and it has maximal elements that are not isometric otherwise.
$M_1 = \{x \in D_{12}^+ : x_1 = 0\}$ and $M_2 = \{x \in D_{12}^+ : x_1 = x_2 + \dots + x_{12}\}$ are primitive of rank 11, the roots of $D_{12}^+$ in each one generate it, and each one is maximal in $P(D_{12}^+)$: the only primitive sublattice of rank 12 is $D_{12}^+$, which is not in $P(D_{12}^+)$.
$M_1 \cong D_{11}$ has determinant 4, and $M_2 \cong A_{11}$ has determinant 12: a vector of $D_{12}^+$ with coordinates in $\tfrac{1}{2} + \mathbb{Z}$ has an even sum of coordinates, and $x_1 = x_2 + \dots + x_{12}$ makes the sum $2x_1$ odd, so $M_2 \subseteq \mathbb{Z}^{12}$.
The sublattices $M \subseteq L$ with $M = \mathbb{Z}\Phi(M)$, the sublattices that are root lattices, are not the right set either: $\mathbb{Z}h \oplus A_{11} \subseteq D_{12}^+$, with $A_{11} = \{x \in D_{12} : \sum_i x_i = 0\}$, is a root lattice of rank 12 and determinant 36 that is not isometric to $D_{12}$, and $h$ is a root of it but not of $D_{12}^+$.

## Hyperbolic lattices {#hyperbolic}

Let $L$ have signature $(1, n)$ and $S \subseteq \mathbb{Q}_{<0}$.
$W_S(L) := \langle s_r : r \in \Phi_S(L) \rangle$ is a discrete group generated by reflections of the hyperbolic space $\mathbb{H}^n$ of $L \otimes \mathbb{R}$.
Let $P$ be a fundamental polyhedron of $W_S(L)$ with walls $\alpha_i^\perp$, $\alpha_i \in \Phi_S(L)$.
Each mirror of $W_S(L)$ is $w(\alpha_i^\perp)$ for some $w \in W_S(L)$ and some $i$, so $\Phi_S(L) = \{\pm w \alpha_i\}$.
$W_S(L)$ is generated by the $s_{\alpha_j}$, and $s_{\alpha_j}(x) \in x + \mathbb{Z}\alpha_j$, so $\mathbb{Z}\Phi_S(L) = \mathbb{Z}\langle \alpha_i \rangle$.
$L$ is an $S$-root lattice exactly when the $\alpha_i$ generate $L$.

## Definite lattices {#definite}

Witt's theorem (Conway and Sloane, *Sphere Packings, Lattices and Groups*, 3rd edition, Chapter 4, §3): a positive definite $\{2\}$-root lattice is an orthogonal sum of lattices $A_n$, $D_n$, $E_6$, $E_7$ and $E_8$.
The field `root_system` of a definite integral record states the ADE type of $\Phi_{\{\pm 2\}}(L)$.

Let $L$ be definite.
$\Phi(L)$ is finite, $O(L)$ permutes it, $2 b(r, r') / b(r, r) \in \mathbb{Z}$ for $r, r' \in \Phi(L)$, and the multiples of a root $r$ in $\Phi(L)$ are $\pm r$, because a root is primitive.
$\Phi(L)$ is therefore a reduced root system in $\mathbb{Z}\Phi(L) \otimes \mathbb{R}$, and it is the union of irreducible components of the types $A_n$ to $G_2$, which are orthogonal in pairs.
The components need not be of type ADE: $\Phi(\mathbb{Z}^n)$ is of type $B_n$ for $n \geq 2$, with the roots $\pm e_i$ and $\pm e_i \pm e_j$, and $\Phi(A_2)$ is of type $G_2$.

The field `definite.roots` states each component $\Phi_c$ with its type, its scale $k$ and its simple roots in the basis of the record.
A short root $r$ of the component has $b(r, r) = 2k$.
The build lists $\Phi(L)$, and it rejects a record whose `definite.roots` is not $\Phi(L)$.
$\mathbb{Z}\Phi(L)$ is the orthogonal sum of the sublattices $Q(\Phi_c)$ that the components generate, and the simple roots are a basis of it.
$Q(\Phi_c)$ is isometric to $X_n(k)$ for a type $X_n$ among $A_n$, $D_n$, $E_n$; to $\mathbb{Z}^n(2k)$ for $B_n$; to $D_n(k)$ for $C_n$, with $D_3 = A_3$; to $D_4(k)$ for $F_4$; and to $A_2(k)$ for $G_2$.

## Lattices that are not definite {#not-definite}

$\Phi(L)$ can be infinite, and the build does not list it.
The field `root_span.roots` states roots that generate $\mathbb{Z}\Phi(L)$, and the build checks that each one is a root.
When they generate $L$, that proves $L = \mathbb{Z}\Phi(L)$.
When they do not, the notes of the record prove that each root of $L$ is in the sublattice that they generate.
The fields `root_span.summands` and `root_span.embedding` then state records $M_1, M_2, \dots$ and the matrix of an isometric embedding $M_1 \oplus M_2 \oplus \cdots \to L$ with image $\mathbb{Z}\Phi(L)$.

## What a record states {#record}

The label `root lattice` means that the record proves $L = \mathbb{Z}\Phi(L)$.
The label `not a root lattice` means that the record proves $L \neq \mathbb{Z}\Phi(L)$.
A lattice with neither label is not decided.
The page of a lattice states $R(L) = \mathbb{Z}\Phi(L)$ as a lattice that is isometric to it, its rank, the index $[L : R(L)]$ and whether $R(L)$ is primitive in $L$, with the quotient when it is not.
Each row says `not decided` when the record does not state $\mathbb{Z}\Phi(L)$.
For a root lattice the page states a set $S$ with $L = \mathbb{Z}\Phi_S(L)$.
$S$ is not unique: $\mathbb{Z}^n = \mathbb{Z}\Phi_{\{1\}}(\mathbb{Z}^n)$, and $\mathbb{Z}^n = \mathbb{Z}\Phi_S(\mathbb{Z}^n)$ for each $S \ni 1$.
