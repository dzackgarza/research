## How an affine diagram arises

The affine Coxeter groups are infinite, and each contains a normal abelian subgroup whose quotient is a finite Coxeter group.
The affine graph is obtained from the graph of that quotient by adding one vertex.

Concretely: let $R$ be an irreducible root system of rank $r > 1$ with simple roots $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_r$, and let $\alpha_{r+1}$ be the **highest root**. The affine Coxeter group is generated by the linear reflections in the hyperplanes perpendicular to $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_r$ together with the affine reflection in the hyperplane $\{x : \langle x, \alpha_{r+1}\rangle = 1\}$.

Equivalently it is the group generated by all reflections in the hyperplanes of the Stiefel diagram of $R$ — the hyperplanes orthogonal to the roots together with certain translates of them.

## One correction the corpus needs

$\tilde A_1 = I_2(\infty)$, the infinite dihedral group, is **irreducible**: its diagram is two nodes joined by an edge labelled $\infty$, which is connected, and its Gram matrix $[[-2,2],[2,-2]]$ is indecomposable.
The row above is part of this same list of irreducible affine groups.
A comment in `tests/coxeter_tdd_specs/system/test_classification_examples.py` (`test_affine_type_negative_semidefinite_criterion`) excludes it as "reducible", which is false — it is the rank-2 euclidean diagram and belongs in the affine roster.
