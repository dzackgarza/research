# Root systems and root lattices

::: {.convention #conv:root-sign title="Root-lattice sign convention"}
The symbols $A_n,D_n,E_n$ denote negative-definite root lattices. Coordinate realizations below use the negative standard form $\beta(e_i,e_j)=-\delta_{ij}$ on $\bZ^n$ and $\bQ^n$. Their positive-definite Euclidean realizations are obtained by the twist $(-)(-1)$ of @def:form-twist. This twist preserves the underlying module, so $L[k]=L(-1)[-k]$ as sets of vectors and $\Orth(L)=\Orth(L(-1))$ as subgroups of $\Aut_\bZ(L)$.
:::

## General root lattices

::: {.definition #def:root-lattice title="Simply-laced root lattices"}
Let $L$ be a negative-definite integral lattice. Its **norm-$(-2)$ roots** are $R(L)\definedas L[-2]$ in the norm-shell notation of @def:lattice-basic-invariants. A **simple system** $\Phi(L)$ is a basis of the rational span of $R(L)$ consisting of roots such that every root is an integral combination of $\Phi(L)$ with coefficients either all nonnegative or all nonpositive. The **root-span morphism** is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_L\colon\bZ^{(R(L))}&\longrightarrow L,\\
e_\alpha&\longmapsto\alpha.
\end{aligned}
$$
A **simply-laced root lattice** is one for which $\sigma_L$ is epic, equivalently its module cokernel vanishes.
:::

::: {.definition #def:weyl-group title="The Weyl group"}
For a chosen simple system $\Phi(L)$, the Weyl group is the subgroup
$$
W(L)\definedas\generators{s_\alpha\mid\alpha\in\Phi(L)}
\injects\Orth(L),
$$
generated by the integral reflections of @prop:integrality-root-reflections.
:::

::: {.proposition #prop:integrality-root-reflections title="Integral root reflections"}
If $\alpha\in L$ has norm $\alpha^2=\pm2$ in an integral lattice, its rational reflection preserves $L$ and is an involution in $\Orth(L)$. For a negative norm-$2$ root the formula is
$$
s_\alpha(x)=x+\beta_L(x,\alpha)\alpha
\qquad(x\in L).
$$
:::

::: {.proof}
The reflection of @def:reflection-in-a-vector is $s_\alpha(x)=x-\frac{2\beta_L(x,\alpha)}{\alpha^2}\alpha$, which has integral coefficients for $\alpha^2=\pm2$. It is an involutive isometry over $\bQ$ by @prop:reflection-rational-isometry, hence an integral one.
:::

## Cartan matrices of root data {#sec-cartan-matrices}

::: {.proposition #prop:cartan-symmetrization title="Cartan–Gram relations for root systems"}
For crystallographic simple roots $(\alpha_i)$, let $A$ be their Cartan matrix of @def:cartan-matrix and let $G_{ij}\definedas\beta(\alpha_i,\alpha_j)$. With $d_j\definedas\alpha_j^2/2$, one has
$$
G=A\diag(d_1,\ldots,d_\ell),
\qquad d_jA_{ij}=d_iA_{ji}.
$$
If every simple root has norm $-2$, then $A=-G$.
:::

::: {.proof}
The defining Cartan identity $A_{ij}\alpha_j^2=2G_{ij}$ and the symmetry of $G$ give the assertions.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:ADE-root-lattice-classification title="Classification of negative-definite simply-laced root lattices"}
Every nonzero negative-definite simply-laced root lattice of @def:root-lattice is an orthogonal sum of indecomposable root lattices, uniquely determined up to permutation of summands. The indecomposable factors are precisely
$$
A_n\quad(n\ge1),\qquad
D_n\quad(n\ge4),\qquad
E_6,\ E_7,\ E_8.
$$
In a simple-root basis the matrix of the restricted form is the negative of its simply-laced Cartan matrix; the connected components of the Dynkin diagram determine the orthogonal decomposition. Each indecomposable factor is uniquely determined up to isometry by its connected Dynkin diagram [@Bou08, Ch. VI; @Hum90, Chs. 1–2].
:::

## The $ADE$ lattices

The simply-laced root lattices $A_n$, $D_n$, $E_n$ have a single root length.

::: {.definition #def:lattice-An title="The root lattice $A_n$"}
For $n\ge1$ let $\ZZ^{n+1}\isoto\generators{-1}^{\perp(n+1)}$ denote the negative standard diagonal lattice with orthogonal basis $e_1,\ldots,e_{n+1}$ of square $-1$, and put $v_{n+1}\definedas\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}e_i$. The root lattice $A_n$ is the primitive orthogonal-complement sublattice
$$
A_n\definedas v_{n+1}^{\perp\,\ZZ^{n+1}}
=\theset{x\in\ZZ^{n+1}\st \sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_i=0}.
$$
Its $n(n+1)$ norm-$(-2)$ roots are
$$
R(A_n)=\theset{e_i-e_j\st1\le i\ne j\le n+1},
$$
with simple system $\alpha_i\definedas e_i-e_{i+1}$ for $1\le i\le n$.
:::

::: {.definition #def:lattice-Dn}

The root lattice $D_n$ is the sublattice of the negative standard lattice $\ZZ^n$ cut out by the even-coordinate-sum condition, i.e. the kernel of the reduction $\varepsilon_2: \ZZ^n \to \ZZ/2\ZZ$, $x\mapsto \Sum_i x_i \bmod 2$:
$$
D_n \definedas \ZZ^n_{\mathrm{ev}}
= \theset{ x = (x_1,\dots,x_n)\in \ZZ^n \mid \textstyle\Sum_{i=1}^n x_i \equiv 0 \pmod{2} }
\injects \ZZ^n
.
$$
Its roots are
$$
R(D_n) = \theset{ \pm e_i \pm e_j \mid 1\leq i < j\leq n }
,
$$
of which there are $2n(n-1)$.
A simple system is given by
$$
\alpha_i \definedas e_i - e_{i+1} \ \ (1\leq i\leq n-1),
\qquad
\alpha_n \definedas e_{n-1} + e_n
.
$$
:::

::: {.definition #def:lattice-En}

The root lattice $E_8$ is realized inside $\QQ^8$ with the negative standard pairing as
$$
E_8 \definedas \theset{ x\in \ZZ^8 \union (\ZZ^8 + \tfrac12 v_8) \mid
\textstyle\Sum_{i=1}^8 x_i \equiv 0 \pmod 2 },
\qquad v_8 = (1,\dots,1)
,
$$
Thus $E_8$ consists of the integer vectors of even coordinate sum together with the half-integer vectors of even coordinate sum.
The lattices $E_7$ and $E_6$ are obtained as orthogonal complements inside $E_8$:
$$
E_7 \definedas L_1^{\perp\, E_8}
\quad\text{for any } L_1\leq E_8 \text{ isometric to } A_1,
\qquad
E_6 \definedas L_2^{\perp\, E_8}
\quad\text{for any } L_2\leq E_8 \text{ isometric to } A_2
.
$$
(Here $A_1 = \generators{-2}$ is generated by a single root and $A_2$ by two roots meeting at the usual angle; the isometry type of the complement is independent of the chosen sublattice.)
:::

::: {.example #ex:E8-roots}

The lattice $E_8$ contains exactly $2^4\cdot 3\cdot 5 = 240$ roots, of two kinds:

1. the $112$ signed-pair vectors $\pm e_i \pm e_j$ for $1\leq i < j\leq 8$ (these are the roots of the $D_8$ sublattice), and

2. the $128$ half-sum vectors $\tfrac12(\pm e_1 \pm \cdots \pm e_8)$ with an even number of minus signs.

Indeed $\binom{8}{2}\cdot 4 = 112$ and $2^{8-1} = 128$, giving $240$ in total.
There are $8$ simple roots comprising $\Phi(E_8)$, and the Weyl group $W(E_8)$ acts transitively on the root set $R(E_8)$.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:E8-characterization title="$E_8$ in the house sign convention"}

$E_8$ is the unique even unimodular negative-definite lattice of rank $8$.
Equivalently, $E_8(-1)$ is the unique even unimodular positive-definite lattice of rank $8$.
In particular $E_8\isoto E_8^\#$.
:::

::: {.proof}

The positive-definite classification is classical [@CS10].
Applying the twist involution of @conv:root-sign gives the negative-definite statement.
The construction of @def:lattice-En gives $E_8\isoto D_8^+$ via @def:plus-construction and @thm:plus-construction.
:::

## Non-simply-laced root systems

The following root systems carry two root lengths. Their ambient lattices are generated by the short-root subsystem specified below, while the full reflection root set contains both lengths.

::: {.proposition #prop:finite-crystallographic-coxeter-classification title="Crystallographic finite Coxeter systems"}

A finite Coxeter system is crystallographic in the sense of @def:crystallographic-coxeter if and only if every Coxeter exponent satisfies $m_{ij}\in\theset{2,3,4,6}$.
The remaining finite reflection groups $H_3$, $H_4$ and $I_2(p)$ for $p\notin\theset{2,3,4,6}$ preserve no lattice of full rank (@thm:crystallographic-lattice), and so are finite Coxeter systems that are the Weyl group of no root system in a lattice; their Gram forms are defined over the real number fields of @ex:noncrystallographic-base-rings rather than over $\ZZ$.
:::

::: {.definition #def:root-system-Bn-Cn}

The root set $R(B_n)$ is equipped with the monomorphism $\iota_{B_n}\colon R(B_n)\injects U(\ZZ^n)$ in $\mathbf{Set}$ whose image consists of the **short roots** $\pm e_i$ for $1\leq i\leq n$ together with the **long roots** $\pm e_i\pm e_j$ for $1\leq i<j\leq n$.
Thus $\cardinality{R(B_n)}=2n^2$, with $2n$ short and $2n(n-1)$ long roots.
A simple system is
$$
\alpha_i \definedas e_i - e_{i+1} \ \ (1\leq i\leq n-1),
\qquad \alpha_n \definedas e_n
.
$$

The root system $R(C_n)$ is dual to $B_n$: it consists of the **long roots** $2r_i = \pm 2e_i$ together with the **short roots** $r_{ij} = \pm e_i \pm e_j$, again $2n^2$ roots in total ($2n$ long, $2n(n-1)$ short).
A simple system is
$$
\alpha_i \definedas e_i - e_{i+1} \ \ (1\leq i\leq n-1),
\qquad \alpha_n \definedas 2e_n
.
$$
:::

::: {.definition #def:root-system-F4 title="The root system $F_4$"}
Equip $\bQ^4$ with the negative standard form. The root system $F_4$ consists of the $24$ long roots
$$
\theset{\pm e_i\pm e_j\st1\le i<j\le4}
$$
of square $-2$ and the $24$ short roots
$$
\theset{\pm e_i\st1\le i\le4}
\cup
\theset{\tfrac12(\pm e_1\pm e_2\pm e_3\pm e_4)}
$$
of square $-1$, with all $16$ sign choices in the latter set. A simple system is
$$
\alpha_1=e_2-e_3,\quad
\alpha_2=e_3-e_4,\quad
\alpha_3=e_4,\quad
\alpha_4=\tfrac12(e_1-e_2-e_3-e_4).
$$
:::

::: {.definition #def:root-system-G2 title="The root system $G_2$"}
Equip $\bZ^3$ with the negative standard form and let $\Sigma\colon\bZ^3\too\bZ$ be the coordinate-sum morphism. Its kernel is the trace-zero lattice in the exact sequence
$$
0\too\bZ^3_\Delta\too\bZ^3\xrightarrow{\Sigma}\bZ\too0.
$$
The $G_2$ roots in $\bZ^3_\Delta$ are the six short roots $\pm(e_i-e_j)$ and the six long roots $\pm(2e_i-e_j-e_k)$, where $\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}$. A simple system is
$$
\alpha_1=e_1-e_2,\qquad
\alpha_2=-e_1+2e_2-e_3.
$$
:::

## The plus construction and $D_8^+ \cong E_8$

The realization of $E_8$ in the $E_n$ lattice definition (@def:lattice-En) is an instance of a general overlattice construction for the $D_n$ family.

::: {.definition #def:plus-construction title="The plus construction"}
For the negative standard lattice $D_n$ of @def:lattice-Dn, put
$$
v_+\definedas\tfrac12(1,\ldots,1),
\qquad v_-\definedas\tfrac12(-1,1,\ldots,1)
$$
inside $\bQ^n$. Define the subsets
$$
D_n^+\definedas D_n\union(v_++D_n),
\qquad D_n^-\definedas D_n\union(v_-+D_n).
$$
Their closure under addition and their induced integral lattice structures are addressed by @thm:plus-construction.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:plus-construction title="Integral and even plus lattices"}
For $n\ge2$, the set $D_n^+$ is an additive subgroup if and only if $n$ is even. For even $n$ its index over $D_n$ is $2$ and its rational Gram determinant is $1$. It is an integral unimodular lattice if and only if $4\mid n$, and even if and only if $8\mid n$. In the negative-definite convention, $D_8^+\isoto E_8$.
:::

::: {.proof}
Closure is equivalent to $2v_+=(1,\ldots,1)\in D_n$, hence to even $n$. Its index is then $2$ and the determinant-index formula of @prop:embedding-index gives determinant $1$. The pairing $\beta(x,v_+)=-\tfrac12\sum_i x_i$ is integral for every $x\in D_n$, while $v_+^2=-n/4$. Thus the integral and even conditions are $4\mid n$ and $8\mid n$, respectively. The $n=8$ coordinate model equals @def:lattice-En.
:::
