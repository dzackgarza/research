# Lattice Theory {#sec:lattice-theory}


## Basic Theory


::: {.definition #def:lattice-basic-invariants title="Basic lattice invariants"}

By a **lattice**, we mean a free $\ZZ$-module $L$ of finite rank equipped with a symmetric integral bilinear form
$$
\beta_L\colon L\tensor_\ZZ L\too\ZZ
$$
whose adjoint
$$
\beta_L^\sharp\colon L\too\Hom_\ZZ(L,\ZZ),
\qquad
x\longmapsto\beta_L(x,-),
$$
is injective, as in @def:polarization and @def:lattice.
We abbreviate $vw\definedas\beta_L(v,w)$ and $v^2\definedas\beta_L(v,v)$.
Let
$$
q\colon L\too\ZZ,
\qquad
q(v)\definedas v^2
$$
be the norm map.
For $v\in L$ let
$$
i_v\colon\ZZ\too L,
\qquad
i_v(1)=v.
$$
For $n\in\ZZ$ define
$$
L[n]\definedas q^{-1}(n),
\qquad
L^{\mathrm{prim}}[n]\definedas
\theset{v\in L[n]\st i_v\text{ is a primitive embedding}}.
$$
Define the divisibility $\div_L(v)\geq0$ by the image factorization
$$
\operatorname{im}\bigl(\beta_L(v,-)\colon L\to\ZZ\bigr)
=
\div_L(v)\ZZ.
$$
For nonzero primitive $v$, the vector $v/\div_L(v)$ belongs to $L^\#$; with
$\pi_L\colon L^\#\twoheadrightarrow A_L$ from @def:discriminant, define
$$
v^*\definedas\pi_L\!\left(v/\div_L(v)\right)\in A_L.
$$
For a scalar extension $\ZZ\to R$, write
$L_R\definedas L\tensor_\ZZ R$ and $\beta_{L_R}$ for the induced $R$-valued form.

The value module of $\beta_L$ is $\ZZ$.
By @def:form-axioms, $L$ is **even** precisely when $q$ factors through the canonical monomorphism $2\ZZ\injects\ZZ$; otherwise it is **odd**.
A morphism $\iota\colon M\injects L$ exhibits $M$ as a **sublattice** when the form on $M$ is the pullback $\iota^*\beta_L$.
A vector $v\in L$ is **isotropic** when $v\in L[0]$, and a lattice morphism
$\iota\colon M\injects L$ is **totally isotropic** when $\iota^*\beta_L=0$.
Base change along $\ZZ\to\RR$ gives $L_\RR$; Sylvester's theorem diagonalizes $\beta_{L_\RR}$ with diagonal entries in $\theset{1,-1}$.
We write $n_+$ and $n_-$ respectively for the multiplicities of $1$ and $-1$.
The **signature** of $L$ is the pair $(n_+, n_-)$, and the **index** is $n_+ - n_-$.
We say $L$ is **definite** if either $n_+$ or $n_-$ is zero, and **indefinite** otherwise.
More precisely, if $n_- = 0$ we say $L$ is **positive definite**, and similarly if $n_+ = 0$, we say $L$ is **negative definite**. A negative definite lattice generated by elements of norm $-2$ is said to be a **root lattice**, and such elements are referred to as **roots**.

The **rank** $r$ of a lattice is its rank as a free $\ZZ$-module and is given by $r=n_+ + n_- = \dim_\QQ L_\QQ$.
An indefinite lattice of signature $(1, r-1)$ is said to be **hyperbolic**. Fixing a generating set $e_i$ of $L$, we define the **Gram matrix** of $L$ as the matrix $G_L \definedas (\beta_L(e_i, e_j))_{ij}$, and the **discriminant** as $\disc L \definedas \det G_L$.
The discriminant is independent of the choice of generating set.
:::

::: {.proposition #prop:definite-finiteness title="Finiteness in the definite and indefinite cases"}


If $L$ is definite then $L[k]$ is finite for every $k$, since the real extension of $\pm\beta_L$ is a positive definite quadratic form on $L_\RR$ and $L$ is discrete, so a bounded region contains finitely many lattice points.
For the same reason $\Orth(L)$ is finite: an isometry is determined by the images of a basis $e_1,\dots,e_r$, and it carries $e_i$ into the finite set $L[e_i^2]$.

Neither statement survives indefiniteness.
In $U = \ZZ e\oplus\ZZ f$ one has $U[0] = \ZZ e\union\ZZ f$, which is infinite.
In $L = \generators{1}\oplus\generators{-2}$ the set $L[1]$ is the solution set of the Pell equation $x^2 - 2y^2 = 1$, again infinite, and
$$
M = \matt{3}{4}{2}{3}
\qquad\text{satisfies}\qquad
M^{\mathsf T} G_L M = G_L,
\qquad G_L = \matt{1}{0}{0}{-2}
,
$$
with $M$ of infinite order, so $\Orth(L)$ is infinite.
Thus enumeration of a level set or of the entire orthogonal group is not, by itself, an available operation for an indefinite lattice.
:::

## Definite lattices

### Minima, minimal shells, and regularity

::: {.definition #def:definite-minimum-shell title="Minimum, minimal vectors, and kissing number"}

Let $L$ be a definite lattice and put $\varepsilon_L=1$ when $L$ is positive definite and $\varepsilon_L=-1$ when $L$ is negative definite.

The **minimum** of $L$ is

$$
\mu(L)
\definedas
\min_{0\neq x\in L}\varepsilon_L x^2
=
\min_{0\neq x\in L}\abs{x^2}
.
$$
The **minimal shell**, or set of **minimal vectors**, is
$$
\operatorname{Min}(L)
\definedas
\theset{x\in L\sm\theset{0}\st \varepsilon_L x^2=\mu(L)}
,
$$
and the **kissing number** of $L$ is
$$
\tau(L)\definedas\abs{\operatorname{Min}(L)}.
$$
Thus both $x$ and $-x$ are counted.
For a negative-definite lattice these are, by convention, the corresponding invariants of $L(-1)$.
:::


::: {.definition #def:voronoi-perfect-lattice title="Voronoi-perfect lattices"}

Let $L$ be definite of rank $r$, and identify $L_\QQ$ with $\QQ^r$ after choosing a basis.
The lattice is **perfect in the sense of Voronoi** if
$$
\spanof_\QQ\theset{vv^{\mathsf T}\st v\in\operatorname{Min}(L)}
=
\operatorname{Sym}_r(\QQ)
.
$$
Equivalently, the values of the quadratic form on its minimal vectors determine the form up to scale.
This is the standard notion of a perfect Euclidean lattice [@Mar03, Ch. 4].
:::

::: {.observation #obs:perfect-lattice-vs-form title="Perfect lattice versus perfect bilinear form"}

The Voronoi-perfect condition in @def:voronoi-perfect-lattice is a spanning condition on the rank-one tensors of minimal vectors of a definite lattice.
It is distinct from a **perfect bilinear form**, for which the adjoint $L\to L^\vee$ is an isomorphism.
:::

::: {.definition #def:regular-ternary-lattice title="Regular and spinor-regular ternary lattices"}

Let $L$ be a positive-definite integral ternary lattice.
It is **regular** if every positive integer represented by some lattice in the genus of $L$ is represented by $L$ itself.
It is **spinor regular** if every positive integer represented by some lattice in the spinor genus of $L$ is represented by $L$ itself.
For a negative-definite ternary lattice these adjectives refer to $L(-1)$.
:::


## Level, modularity, and the discriminant character

::: {.definition #def:lattice-level title="Arithmetic level"}

Let $L$ be a nondegenerate integral lattice.
Its **level** is the least positive integer $N$ such that
$$
N x^2\in 2\ZZ
\qquad\text{for every }x\in\dualof{L}.
$$
Equivalently, in a basis with Gram matrix $G_L$, the matrix $N G_L^{-1}$ is integral with even diagonal.
This is the arithmetic level of an integral lattice [@Han13].
For an even integral lattice it is the level used in the theory of theta series and modular lattices, and it equals the exponent of $A_L$ or twice that exponent [@SSP99].
:::

::: {.definition #def:modular-lattice-global title="$N$-modular lattices"}

Let $L$ be a nondegenerate integral lattice and $N>0$.
Following the modular-lattice literature, $L$ is **$N$-modular** if there is a similarity of the rational quadratic space $L_\QQ$ with multiplier $N$ carrying $\dualof{L}$ onto $L$.
Equivalently,
$$
L\iso\dualof{L}(N)
$$
as lattices.
The case $N=1$ is unimodularity.
:::

::: {.observation #obs:homothetic-modularity title="Relation with homothetic modularity"}

The similarity-to-the-dual definition above is Quebbemann's notion of an $N$-modular lattice [@Que95].
The stronger equality $N\dualof{L}=L$ inside a chosen rational realization is the special homothetic case.
The similarity factor $N$ is distinct from the local scale ideal $\mathfrak{s}(L)$.
:::

::: {.definition #def:lattice-bad-primes title="The bad-prime set attached to an integral lattice"}

For a nondegenerate integral lattice $L$, set
$$
\Sigma_L
\definedas
\theset{p\text{ prime}\st p\mid 2\disc L}.
$$
For an odd prime $p\notin\Sigma_L$, the reduction of $\beta_L$ modulo $p$ is nondegenerate.
If
$$
X_n\definedas\theset{x\in\mathbb{A}^r_\ZZ\st x^{\mathsf T}G_Lx=n},
$$
then $X_n$ is smooth at every prime $p\nmid 2n\disc L$: its gradient is $2G_Lx$, which can vanish modulo such a $p$ only at $x=0$, and $0\notin X_n(\FF_p)$ when $p\nmid n$.
Thus $\Sigma_L$ is the set of primes at which the lattice form itself can have bad reduction; the fiber $X_n$ has the additional bad primes dividing $n$.
:::

::: {.definition #def:lattice-discriminant-character title="The discriminant quadratic character"}

Let $L$ be an even integral lattice of rank $2m$ and put
$$
D_L\definedas(-1)^m\disc L.
$$
Let $d_L$ be the fundamental discriminant of the quadratic field $\QQ(\sqrt{D_L})$, and set $d_L=1$ when $D_L$ is a square.
The associated quadratic Dirichlet character is
$$
\chi_L(a)
\definedas
\left(\frac{d_L}{a}\right),
$$
the Kronecker symbol.
For every odd $p\notin\Sigma_L$, the nondegenerate quadratic space $L\tensor\FF_p$ is split, i.e. an orthogonal sum of $m$ hyperbolic planes, exactly when $\chi_L(p)=1$.
:::

::: {.proposition #prop:discriminant-character-finite-field title="Finite-field characterization"}

For every odd $p\nmid 2\disc L$, the value $\chi_L(p)$ is the square-class invariant
distinguishing the two isometry classes of nondegenerate quadratic spaces of dimension
$2m$ over $\FF_p$; equivalently, $\chi_L(p)=1$ exactly in the split case
[@CS10, Ch. 15].
:::

## Integral hyperbolic index

::: {.definition #def:integral-hyperbolic-index title="Integral hyperbolic index"}

Let $U$ be the unimodular hyperbolic plane.
For a nondegenerate integral lattice $L$, define
$$
h_\ZZ(L)
\definedas
\max\theset{n\geq0\st \exists L\iso U^{\oplus n}\oplus L'\text{ for some lattice }L'}.
$$
The **integral hyperbolic index** of $L$ is $h_\ZZ(L)$.
:::

::: {.proposition #prop:integral-hyperbolic-vs-witt title="Comparison with the Witt index"}

The standard **Witt index** $i_W(L_\QQ)$ is the number of hyperbolic planes split off by the rational quadratic space $L_\QQ$.
The invariant $h_\ZZ(L)$ is an integral refinement:
$$
h_\ZZ(L)\leq i_W(L_\QQ)\leq\min(n_+,n_-),
$$
and equality with the rational Witt index need not hold.
For integral $L$, $h_\ZZ(L)$ is also the largest $n$ for which
$$
\operatorname{PrimEmb}(U^{\oplus n},L)\neq\varnothing.
$$
For any such embedding, @prop:unimodular-splits gives
$$
U^{\oplus n}\perp(U^{\oplus n})^{\perp L}\isoto L.
$$
The rational Witt index is standard quadratic-form terminology [@OM00]; $h_\ZZ(L)$ is the integral direct-summand refinement above.
:::


::: {.definition #def:spinor-norm title="Determinant and spinor-norm exact sequences"}

Let $R$ be a field of characteristic different from $2$, and let
$(V,\beta)$ be a finite-dimensional nondegenerate symmetric $R$-bilinear space.
For every anisotropic $w\in V$ the reflection
$$
s_w(x)\definedas x-2\frac{\beta(x,w)}{\beta(w,w)}w
$$
lies in $\Orth(V)$.
By the Cartan--Dieudonne theorem every $g\in\Orth(V)$ is a product
$g=s_{w_1}\cdots s_{w_m}$ of such reflections.
The normalized **spinor norm**
$$
\spinornorm_R\colon\Orth(V)\too\unitsof{R}/(\unitsof{R})^2
$$
is the homomorphism determined by
$$
\spinornorm_R(s_w)
=
\left[-\frac{\beta(w,w)}2\right].
$$
Equivalently,
$$
\spinornorm_R(g)
=
\prod_{i=1}^m
\left[-\frac{\beta(w_i,w_i)}2\right],
$$
independently of the reflection factorization [@GHS08].

The determinant and spinor norm determine short exact sequences
$$
1\too\SO(V)\too\Orth(V)
\xrightarrow{\det}\operatorname{im}(\det)\too1
$$
and
$$
1\too\Theta_R(V)\too\Orth(V)
\xrightarrow{\spinornorm_R}\operatorname{im}(\spinornorm_R)\too1.
$$

For a lattice $L$ and a field extension $\ZZ\to R$, restriction along
$\Orth(L)\to\Orth(L_R)$ gives the corresponding determinant and spinor-norm
homomorphisms on $\Orth(L)$ and hence the exact sequences
$$
1\too\SO(L)\too\Orth(L)
\xrightarrow{\det}\operatorname{im}(\det|_{\Orth(L)})\too1
$$
and
$$
1\too\Theta_R(L)\too\Orth(L)
\xrightarrow{\spinornorm_R}\operatorname{im}(\spinornorm_R|_{\Orth(L)})\too1.
$$
For a lattice of signature $(2,n)$ set
$$
\Orth^+(L)\definedas\Theta_\RR(L),
\qquad
\widetilde\Orth^+(L)
\definedas
\widetilde\Orth(L)\times_{\Orth(L)}\Orth^+(L).
$$
:::

::: {.definition #def:orthogonal-direct-sum-complement title="Orthogonal direct sums and orthogonal complements"}

For lattices $L_1$ and $L_2$, the **orthogonal direct sum** $L_1\oplus L_2$ has underlying module direct sum and bilinear form
$$
\beta_{L_1\oplus L_2}(v_1+v_2,w_1+w_2)
\definedas
\beta_{L_1}(v_1,w_1)+\beta_{L_2}(v_2,w_2).
$$
Write $L^{\oplus n}$ for the $n$-fold orthogonal direct sum.

Let $\eta\colon M\injects L$ be a lattice morphism.
Pairing with $\eta$ defines
$$
\lambda_\eta\colon L\too M^\vee,
\qquad
x\longmapsto\bigl(m\mapsto\beta_L(x,\eta(m))\bigr).
$$
The **orthogonal complement** of $\eta$ is defined by the exact sequence
$$
0\too M^{\perp L}\xrightarrow{\kappa_\eta}L
\xrightarrow{\lambda_\eta}\im(\lambda_\eta)\too0.
$$
When the ambient lattice is fixed, write $M^\perp$ for $M^{\perp L}$.

Since $M$ is nondegenerate, the induced orthogonal-sum morphism
$$
j_\eta\colon M\oplus M^{\perp L}\injects L
$$
is a monomorphism of full rank and therefore has finite cokernel.
It need not be an isomorphism.
For orthogonal direct sums, the discriminant functor gives
$$
A_{L_1\oplus\cdots\oplus L_n}
\cong
A_{L_1}\oplus\cdots\oplus A_{L_n}.
$$
:::

## Indefinite even lattices

### 2-elementary lattices

::: {.definition #def:two-elementary-isotropic-types title="Isotropic-vector types in a $2$-elementary lattice"}

Let $L$ be an even $2$-elementary lattice. For a primitive vector one has
$\div_L(v)\in\theset{1,2}$.
Let
$$
r\colon\QQ/2\ZZ\twoheadrightarrow\QQ/\ZZ
$$
be the quotient morphism. For $v$ with $\div_L(v)=2$, call the class
$v^*=\pi_L(v/2)\in A_L$ **characteristic** when
$$
r\bigl(q_L(x)\bigr)=\bar\beta_L(v^*,x)
\qquad
\text{for every }x\in A_L,
$$
where
$$
\bar\beta_L\colon A_L\tensor_\ZZ A_L\too\QQ/\ZZ
$$
is the discriminant bilinear form; otherwise call $v^*$ **ordinary**.
For $e\in L^{\mathrm{prim}}[0]$ define its type by

1. **odd** if $\div_L(e)=1$;

2. **even ordinary** if $\div_L(e)=2$ and $e^*$ is ordinary;

3. **even characteristic** if $\div_L(e)=2$ and $e^*$ is characteristic.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:two-elementary-classification title="Nikulin's classification of indefinite even $2$-elementary lattices"}

An indefinite even $2$-elementary lattice is determined up to isometry by its signature and the invariants
$$
r\definedas\rank_\ZZ L,
\qquad
a\definedas\dim_{\bF_2}A_L,
\qquad
\delta\in\theset{0,1},
$$
where $\delta=0$ precisely when the discriminant quadratic form has integral values modulo $2\ZZ$, and $\delta=1$ otherwise.
The admissible triples and the uniqueness statement are those of [@Nik80, Thm. 3.6.2].
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:isotropic-trichotomy title="Classification of primitive isotropic vectors by type"}

Let $S$ be an even hyperbolic 2-elementary lattice with invariants $(r, a, \delta)$, let $v\in S$ be a primitive isotropic vector, and write $\oS\definedas v^{\perp S}/v$.
Then exactly one of the following holds, according to the type of $v$ in the sense above, and in each case $S$ splits off the indicated rank-two summand containing $v$:

1. **odd**: $S \cong U\oplus\oS$, with $a_{\oS} = a$ and $\delta_{\oS} = \delta$;

2. **even ordinary**: $S \cong U(2)\oplus\oS$, with $a_{\oS} = a - 2$ and $\delta_{\oS} = \delta$;

3. **even characteristic**: $S \cong \latI_{1,1}(2)\oplus\oS$, with $a_{\oS} = a - 2$; this case forces $\delta = 1$ and gives $\delta_{\oS} = 0$.

In all three cases $\oS$ is even negative definite of rank $r - 2$.
Classifying the primitive isotropic vectors of $S$ up to isometry is therefore equivalent to classifying the even negative definite lattices with invariants $(r-2, a, \delta)$, $(r-2, a-2, \delta)$ and $(r-2, a-2, 0)$.
:::

::: {.proof}

This is [@AE22 Prop. 5.5].
:::


## Standard examples

::: {.example #ex:rank-one-lattice title="Rank-one lattices"}

For $n\in\ZZ$, let $\generators{n}$ be the rank-one lattice on a generator $v$
with $\beta_{\generators{n}}(v,v)=n$.
Its Gram matrix in the basis $v$ is $[n]$.
:::

::: {.example #ex:integral-hyperbolic-plane title="The hyperbolic plane"}

In rank 2, there are two unimodular hyperbolic lattices: the odd $\latI_{1, 1} \definedas \generators{1} \oplus \generators{-1}$, and the even $U\definedas \latII_{1, 1}$.
We refer to the latter as the **hyperbolic lattice**, which can be realized as $U \definedas \ZZ e \oplus \ZZ f$ with $e^2=f^2 = 0$ and $ef = 1$, and thus the following Gram matrix:
$$
G_U = \matt{0}{1}{1}{0}
.
$$
:::

Any Dynkin diagram of type $A_n, D_n, E_6, E_7, E_8$ corresponds to a root lattice of the respective type.
By convention, we take the negative definite twists of these lattices.

## Classification of unimodular lattices

::: {.theorem #thm:indefinite-unimodular-classification title="Classification of indefinite unimodular lattices"}

Let $L$ be an indefinite unimodular lattice of signature $(p,q)$.
If $L$ is odd, then
$$
L\cong\latI_{p,q}
\definedas
\generators{1}^{\oplus p}\oplus\generators{-1}^{\oplus q}.
$$
If $L$ is even, then $p-q\equiv0\pmod 8$ and
$$
L\cong\latII_{p,q},
$$
where
$$
\latII_{p,q}\definedas
\begin{cases}
U^{\oplus p}\oplus E_8^{\oplus (q-p)/8}, & p<q,\\
U^{\oplus q}\oplus E_8(-1)^{\oplus (p-q)/8}, & p>q.
\end{cases}
$$
Thus an indefinite unimodular lattice is determined up to isometry by its
signature and parity [@Ser73, Ch. V; @MH73, Ch. II].
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:small-unimodular-classification title="Unimodular lattices of rank at most four"}

Let $L$ be a unimodular lattice with $\rank_\ZZ L\leq4$.
If $L$ is odd, then $L\cong\latI_{p,q}$ for its signature $(p,q)$.
If $L$ is even and nonzero, then
$$
L\cong U
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
L\cong U^{\oplus2},
$$
according as $\rank_\ZZ L=2$ or $4$ [@MH73, Ch. II; @CS10, Ch. 15].
:::
