# Invariant and coinvariant lattices

::: {.remark}

We record the lattice-theoretic constructions attached to a group of isometries of a lattice, and specialize them to the case of an involution.
:::

::: {.definition #def:invariant_coinvariant_lattices}

Let $G\injects \Orth(L)$ be a faithful representation realizing a group $G$ acting on a lattice $L$ by isometries.
The **invariant sublattice** and the **coinvariant sublattice** of the action are defined as
$$
L^G \definedas \theset{v\in L \mid g.v = v \text{ for all } g\in G},
\qquad
L_G \definedas (L^G)^{\perp L}
.
$$
Thus $L^G$ is the sublattice of vectors fixed by every element of $G$, and $L_G$ is its orthogonal complement in $L$.
:::

::: {.proposition #prop:involution_eigenspaces}

Suppose $G = \generators{I}$ is generated by an involution $I\in\Orth(L)$, so that $I^2 = \id$.
Then, for the induced action of $I$ on $L_\QQ$, the rational spans $(L^G)_\QQ$ and $(L_G)_\QQ$ are precisely the eigenspaces of $I$ for the eigenvalues $+1$ and $-1$ respectively, and
$$
L_\QQ = (L^G)_\QQ \operatorname{\perp} (L_G)_\QQ
.
$$
:::

::: {.proof}

Since $I^2 = \id$, the induced isometry of $L_\QQ$ is diagonalizable with all eigenvalues in $\theset{+1, -1}$, so $L_\QQ = V_+ \oplus V_-$ where $V_\pm$ denotes the $\pm 1$ eigenspace.
The fixed space $V_+$ is by definition $(L^G)_\QQ$.
For $v\in V_+$ and $w\in V_-$, using that $I$ is an isometry,
$$
\beta_{L_\QQ}(v, w) = \beta_{L_\QQ}(I v, I w) = \beta_{L_\QQ}(v, -w) = -\beta_{L_\QQ}(v, w)
,
$$
so $\beta_{L_\QQ}(v, w) = 0$.
Hence $V_-\iscontainedin V_+^{\perp}$, and comparing dimensions gives $V_- = V_+^{\perp} = (L_G)_\QQ$.
:::

::: {.proposition #prop:invariant_coinvariant_primitive}

Let $G\injects\Orth(L)$ be a faithful representation of a finite cyclic group $G$.
Then $L^G$ and $L_G$ are primitive sublattices of $L$, and the orthogonal direct sum $L^G \oplus L_G$ is a finite-index sublattice of $L$ [@Nik80].
:::

::: {.proposition #prop:involution_decomposition}

Let $L$ be an even nondegenerate lattice and let $G = \generators{I}\cong \ZZ/2\ZZ$ be generated by an involution $I\in\Orth(L)$.
The invariant and coinvariant sublattices $L^G$ and $L_G$ are primitive and mutually orthogonal.
Moreover, each $v\in L$ decomposes orthogonally, up to a factor of $2$, as
$$
2\cdot v = v_+ + v_-,
\qquad
v_+ \definedas v + I(v)\in L^G,
\qquad
v_- \definedas v - I(v)\in L_G
.
$$
:::

::: {.proof}

Orthogonality and primitivity are the case $G = \generators{I}$ of the primitivity proposition (@prop:invariant_coinvariant_primitive), and $L_G = (L^G)^{\perp L}$ is orthogonal to $L^G$ by definition.
For the decomposition, fix $v\in L$ and set $v_+ \definedas v + I(v)$ and $v_- \definedas v - I(v)$, both of which lie in $L$.
Applying $I$ and using $I^2 = \id$,
$$
I(v_+) = I(v) + I^2(v) = I(v) + v = v_+,
\qquad
I(v_-) = I(v) - v = -v_-
,
$$
so $v_+$ is fixed by $I$ and hence $v_+\in L^G$, while $v_-$ lies in the $-1$ eigenspace of $I$ and hence, by the eigenspace proposition (@prop:involution_eigenspaces), in $(L_G)_\QQ\intersect L = L_G$.
Adding the two expressions gives $v_+ + v_- = 2v$, and the summands are orthogonal since $v_+\in L^G$ and $v_-\in L_G = (L^G)^{\perp L}$.
:::

::: {.proposition #prop:coinvariant_involution_2elementary}

Let $L$ be a $2$-elementary lattice and let $I\in\OStab(L)$ be an involution lying in the stable orthogonal group $\OStab(L) = \tilde\Orth(L)$.
Then both the invariant lattice $L^I$ and the coinvariant lattice $L_I$ are again $2$-elementary.
:::

::: {.proof}

Write $\pi_\pm\colon L\tensor_\ZZ\QQ \to L^{\pm}\tensor_\ZZ\QQ$ for the projections $\pi_\pm(v) = \tfrac12(v \pm I v)$, where $L^{+} = L^I$ and $L^{-} = L_I$.

Both $L^{\pm}$ are primitive in $L$: if $nv \in L^I$ for some $v \in L$ and $n \neq 0$ then $nIv = nv$, so $Iv = v$; and $L_I = (L^I)^{\perp}$ is primitive because an orthogonal complement always is.
A primitive sublattice is a direct summand as a $\ZZ$-module, so restriction $\dualof{L} \to \dualof{(L^{\pm})}$ is surjective, and under the identifications made by the form it is $\pi_{\pm}$.
Hence every $x \in \dualof{(L^{\pm})}$ is $x = \pi_{\pm}(w)$ for some $w \in \dualof{L}$, and
$$
2x = w \pm Iw .
$$

Now use the two hypotheses.
Since $I$ lies in the stable orthogonal group it acts trivially on $A_L = \dualof{L}/L$, so $Iw - w \in L$; and since $L$ is $2$-elementary, $2\dualof{L} \iscontainedin L$, so $2w \in L$.
Therefore
$$
2x = w - Iw = -(Iw - w) \in L
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
2x = w + Iw = 2w + (Iw - w) \in L
$$
in the two cases respectively.
In each case $2x$ lies in $L^{\pm}\tensor_\ZZ\QQ$ as well as in $L$, and $L^{\pm}$ is primitive, so $2x \in L^{\pm}$.

Thus $2\dualof{(L^{\pm})} \iscontainedin L^{\pm}$, which is $2$-elementarity.
:::
