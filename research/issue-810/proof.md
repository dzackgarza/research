# Clean proof under review: research#810

The result relied on is the following theorem, written exactly as on the page. Clause 1 is also used for a connected complex Lie group and for an arbitrary atlas of the Coble stack; clause 2 is only used in its stated discrete case.

::: {.theorem #thm:quotient-local-system-descent title="Descent and monodromy of local systems along an atlas"}
Let $p\colon U\to X$ be an atlas of a topological stack, and let $A$ be an abelian group. A *local system of $A$-modules on $X$* means a covering of stacks in the sense of [@Noo05, Definition 18.10], with an $A$-module structure on its fibres; equivalently, it is a locally constant sheaf of $A$-modules with its comparisons along all $2$-morphisms and their cocycle conditions. Let $R$ be the fibre product shown by the square
$$\begin{tikzcd}
R \ar[r,"t"] \ar[d,"s"'] \ar[dr,phantom,"\lrcorner",very near start] & U \ar[d,"p"]\\
U \ar[r,"p"'] & X ,
\end{tikzcd}$$
so that $R=U\times_XU\rightrightarrows U$. Then:

1. Pullback along $p$ is an equivalence from local systems of $A$-modules on $X$ to $R$-equivariant local systems of $A$-modules on $U$. The $R$-action is precisely the comparison along $2$-morphisms and its cocycle condition. If $X=[U/G]$ for a topological group $G$ and $p$ is the quotient atlas, then $R\cong G\times U$, so this is an equivalence between local systems on $[U/G]$ and $G$-equivariant local systems on $U$, with pullback inverse to descent. The equivalence is natural in the coefficients and preserves lattice forms preserved by the action.
2. Suppose in addition that $G$ is discrete and acts properly discontinuously with finite stabilizers (@def:group-orbit), and that $U$ is connected, locally path connected and locally simply connected. For $\tilde x\in U$, the monodromy of a descended system is the Noohi fibre action on $\pi_1([U/G],[\tilde x])$. On an orbifold loop represented by $(g,\widetilde\gamma)$, where $\widetilde\gamma$ runs from $\tilde x$ to $g\tilde x$, it is the transport computed on the pullback to the interval, namely $\varphi(g)^{-1}\circ\widetilde\gamma^*$ when the equivariant structure is written $\varphi$.
:::

::: {.proof}
*Atlas descent.* If $E\to X$ is a covering of stacks, representability in [@Noo05, Definition 18.10], makes $p^{-1}E$ an ordinary covering space of $U$. Its two pullbacks to $R$ are identified by the canonical $2$-morphism in the fibre square; on triple fibre products the three identifications satisfy the cocycle identity by functoriality of $2$-composition. This is exactly an $R$-action on $p^{-1}E$. Conversely, an $R$-equivariant covering space $E\to U$ has a quotient stack $[E/R]$ over $[U/R]$, and [@Noo05, Proposition 3.4] identifies $[U/R]$ with $X$. The projection $[E/R]\to X$ is representable by [@Noo05, Lemma 6.3] and a covering by [@Noo05, Definition 4.5 and Example 4.6(1)], since both properties are checked after pullback to the atlas $U$, where the projection is the original covering $E\to U$. Its pullback to $U$ is canonically $E$. Morphisms descend by the same quotient universal property, and equivariant morphisms are precisely morphisms compatible with the descent data, so the two functors are quasi-inverse. The construction uses every $2$-morphism comparison and its cocycle, not only strict maps of spaces. This is the covering-space case of the $G$-space and $G$-sheaf formalism of [@Moe02, §§5.1, 5.3 and 5.6, and 6.1]; independently, [@MoePro97, §3, Remarks 2--3] states that pullback of equivariant sheaves along a weak equivalence of groupoids is an equivalence of topoi. Restricting these equivalences to covering projections gives the stated local-system equivalence. The lattice assertion is naturality applied to the form.

*The quotient action groupoid.* For the quotient atlas, the torsor identity $R=U\times_{[U/G]}U\cong G\times U$ identifies the descent groupoid with the action groupoid. Thus $R$-equivariance is $G$-equivariance, including the action on each fibre and the cocycle $\varphi_{gh}=\varphi_g\circ\varphi_h$. The same conclusion also follows directly from [@Moe02, §§5.1 and 5.3]: pullback of $G$-spaces is an equivalence along an equivalence of groupoids, and restricts to an equivalence of covering spaces. The argument is stated for topological groupoids, with the smooth case being [@Moe02, §§5.1 and 5.3]. Properness and étaleness are hypotheses of orbifold classification statements such as [@MoePro97, Theorem 4.1], not of this descent equivalence, and are not needed in clause 1.

*Monodromy.* The quotient atlas is a covering of topological stacks, so $X$ inherits connectedness, local path connectedness and semilocal $1$-connectedness from $U$ ([@Noo05, Lemmas 18.8--18.9 and Definition 18.2]). [@Noo05, Definition 18.13 and Theorem 18.19] therefore apply: the fibre functor is an equivalence from covering stacks to $\pi_1(X,[\tilde x])$-sets. Enriching the fibres and the action by $A$-module structures gives the corresponding statement for local systems of $A$-modules. [@Noo05, Definition 18.13] computes the fibre action by pulling the covering back along a loop and transporting between the two endpoint fibres. To evaluate it, write $P=\lambda^{-1}U$. The path $\widetilde\gamma$ trivializes $P|_I$ as $I\times G$, and if the two endpoints are identified by $(0,h)\sim(1,hg^{-1})$, the covering associated to the equivariant system has endpoint identification $(0,t)\sim(1,\varphi(g)t)$. Transport around $S^1$, referred to the fibre at $0$, is therefore $\varphi(g)^{-1}\circ\widetilde\gamma^*$, the displayed formula.

It remains to compare the loop presentations. [@Noo05, Definitions 17.2 and 17.5] define paths, homotopies and $\pi_1$ by pointed maps of stacks. [@Col09, Definitions 3.2--3.3] define homotopy of Haefliger $G$-paths and the groupoid of their homotopy classes; [@Col09, Remark 3.1] identifies $G$-paths up to equivalence with generalized maps from the interval to the groupoid up to isomorphism. On the atlas groupoid these are the same maps and homotopies as Noohi's, with the same endpoint identifications. Quotients by the chart have the effect recorded in [@Noo05, Proposition 3.4], so the Noohi loop and the Haefliger loop in clause 2 agree. The inversion in the displayed representation is the product convention of @def:transport-and-monodromy, not a different fundamental group.
:::
