# Kimi item 2: principal-bundle loop representation and transport

*Principal bundles over intervals and squares.* The discreteness in the preceding two paragraphs is used only to make the endpoint group element locally constant; the existence of the sections uses just local triviality of a principal bundle. Suppose therefore that $p$ is a principal $G$-bundle and $\lambda$ a loop, with pullback $Q=\lambda^{-1}U$. Local triviality of the base-changed bundle and compactness of $I$ give a global section $q$ after matching on finitely many overlaps. The section projects to $\widetilde\gamma=pq$, and the endpoint identification supplies $g$. Conversely, pullback of the descended system along $\lambda$ is the associated local system on $Q$. In the section $q$, it is a local system on $I$; its endpoint gluing is the action of $g$ on the fibre of $\widetilde\gamma$. Hence its transport is $\varphi(g)^{-1}\circ\widetilde\gamma^*$, without using Theorem 18.19 or discreteness.

A Noohi homotopy pulls back to a principal bundle on $I^2$, which again has a global section by finite-grid trivializations. The endpoint element is now a continuous function $u\mapsto g(u)$, and this varying arrow is exactly what the deformation of [@Col09, Definitions 3.2--3.3] permits. If $G$ is path connected, choose $\beta$ from $e$ to $g=g(0)$ and use the $G$-action on $Q$ to replace the section by $(u,t)\mapsto q(t)\beta(1-ut)^{-1}$. At $u=1$ the endpoint element is $e$, and the projected path joins the two basepoints of the original pair. This homotopy is precisely the statement of clause 3; no Noohi covering hypothesis is asserted for the principal bundle.
