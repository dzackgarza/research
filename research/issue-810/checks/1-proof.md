# Clean proof under review: research#810

The theorem contains only atlas descent (including base and coefficient naturality) and the discrete-group monodromy statement. The connected-group extension is withdrawn.

::: {.theorem #thm:quotient-local-system-descent title="Descent and monodromy of local systems along an atlas"}
Let $p\colon U\to X$ be an atlas of a topological stack, and let $A$ be an abelian group. A *local system of $A$-modules on $X$* means a covering of stacks in the sense of [@Noo05, Definition 18.10], with an $A$-module structure on its fibres; equivalently, it is a locally constant sheaf of $A$-modules with its comparisons along all $2$-morphisms and their cocycle conditions. Let $R$ be the fibre product shown by the square
$$\begin{tikzcd}
R \ar[r,"t"] \ar[d,"s"'] \ar[dr,phantom,"\lrcorner",very near start] & U \ar[d,"p"]\\
U \ar[r,"p"'] & X ,
\end{tikzcd}$$
so that $R=U\times_XU\rightrightarrows U$. Then:

1. Pullback along $p$ is an equivalence from local systems of $A$-modules on $X$ to $R$-equivariant local systems of $A$-modules on $U$. The $R$-action is precisely the comparison along $2$-morphisms and its cocycle condition. If $X=[U/G]$ for a topological group $G$ and $p$ is the quotient atlas, then $R\cong G\times U$, so this is an equivalence between local systems on $[U/G]$ and $G$-equivariant local systems on $U$, with pullback inverse to descent. The equivalence is natural in the coefficients and in base: for every map $f\colon Y\to X$, the pullback $f^*[E/R]$ is canonically the descended system of $f^{-1}E$ along the pullback groupoid $R_f=Y\times_XR\rightrightarrows Y\times_XU$. It preserves lattice forms preserved by the action.
2. Suppose in addition that $G$ is discrete and acts properly discontinuously with finite stabilizers (@def:group-orbit), and that $U$ is connected, locally path connected and locally simply connected. For $\tilde x\in U$, the monodromy of a descended system is the action of $\pi_1([U/G],[\tilde x])$ on the fibre of its covering, constructed immediately after [@Noo05, Definition 18.13]. On an orbifold loop represented by $(g,\widetilde\gamma)$, where $\widetilde\gamma$ runs from $\tilde x$ to $g\tilde x$, it is the transport computed on the pullback to the interval, namely $\varphi(g)^{-1}\circ\widetilde\gamma^*$ when the equivariant structure is written $\varphi$.
:::

::: {.proof}
*Atlas descent.* If $E\to X$ is a covering of stacks, representability in [@Noo05, Definition 18.10], makes $p^{-1}E$ an ordinary covering space of $U$. Its two pullbacks to $R$ are identified by the canonical $2$-morphism in the fibre square; on triple fibre products the three identifications satisfy the cocycle identity by functoriality of $2$-composition. This is exactly an $R$-action on $p^{-1}E$. Conversely, an $R$-equivariant covering space $E\to U$ has a quotient stack $[E/R]$ over $[U/R]$, and [@Noo05, Proposition 3.4] identifies $[U/R]$ with $X$. The projection $[E/R]\to X$ is representable by [@Noo05, Lemma 6.3] and a covering by [@Noo05, Definition 4.5 and Example 4.6(1)], since both properties are checked after pullback to the atlas $U$, where the projection is the original covering $E\to U$. Its pullback to $U$ is canonically $E$. Morphisms descend by the same quotient universal property, and equivariant morphisms are precisely morphisms compatible with the descent data, so the two functors are quasi-inverse. The construction uses every $2$-morphism comparison and its cocycle, not only strict maps of spaces. The covering-space case of the $G$-space and $G$-sheaf formalism of [@Moe02, §§5.1, 5.3 and 5.6, and 6.1] gives the same statement in its smooth setting. The quotient construction above, however, is used directly and needs no smoothness hypothesis. The lattice assertion is naturality applied to the form.

*Base naturality.* For a map $f\colon Y\to X$, putting $R_f=Y\times_XR$ gives the pullback groupoid with objects $Y\times_XU$. Iterating $2$-fibre products canonically identifies $f^*[E/R]$ with $[f^{-1}E/R_f]$, where $f^{-1}E$ is pulled back along the projection $Y\times_XU\to U$. A map compatible with the descent data on $Y$ is exactly a map compatible with the descent data on $X$ after pullback along $f$. These canonical identifications respect composition in $f$: their triple-product cocycles are the original cocycles on $R$ pulled back to $R_f$. Every family-wise system, not only its coefficients, is recovered on $Y$.

*The quotient action groupoid.* For the quotient atlas, the torsor identity $R=U\times_{[U/G]}U\cong G\times U$ identifies the descent groupoid with the action groupoid. Thus $R$-equivariance is $G$-equivariance, including the action on each fibre and the cocycle $\varphi_{gh}=\varphi_g\circ\varphi_h$. The same conclusion also follows directly from [@Moe02, §§5.1 and 5.3]: pullback of $G$-spaces is an equivalence along an equivalence of groupoids, and restricts to an equivalence of covering spaces. The argument is stated for topological groupoids, as noted at the beginning of [@Moe02, §5.1]. Properness and étaleness are hypotheses of orbifold classification statements such as [@MoePro97, Theorem 4.1], not of this descent equivalence, and are not needed in clause 1.

*Monodromy.* The quotient atlas is a covering of topological stacks, so $X$ inherits connectedness, local path connectedness and semilocal $1$-connectedness from $U$ ([@Noo05, Lemmas 18.8--18.9 and Definition 18.2]). [@Noo05, Definition 18.13 and Theorem 18.19] therefore apply: the fibre functor is an equivalence from covering stacks to $\pi_1(X,[\tilde x])$-sets. Enriching the fibres and the action by $A$-module structures gives the corresponding statement for local systems of $A$-modules. The construction immediately following [@Noo05, Definition 18.13] computes the fibre action by pulling the covering back along a loop and transporting between the two endpoint fibres. To evaluate it, write $P=\lambda^{-1}U$. The path $\widetilde\gamma$ trivializes $P|_I$ as $I\times G$, and if the two endpoints are identified by $(0,h)\sim(1,hg^{-1})$, the covering associated to the equivariant system has endpoint identification $(0,t)\sim(1,\varphi(g)t)$. Transport around $S^1$, referred to the fibre at $0$, is therefore $\varphi(g)^{-1}\circ\widetilde\gamma^*$, the displayed formula.

*The comparison of loop presentations, by torsor trivialization.* A loop $\lambda$ in the sense of [@Noo05, Definitions 17.2 and 17.5] pulls the quotient atlas back to a $\Gamma$-torsor $Q=\lambda^{-1}U$ on the interval. It is locally trivial by pullback of the chart. Choose finitely many trivializing intervals and use $\Gamma$-equivariance to match their sections on the overlaps; thus $Q\to I$ has a section $q$. Writing $\pi_Q\colon Q\to U$ for the projection, put $\widetilde\gamma=\pi_Q\circ q$. Using the endpoint identification of the loop, choose $q(0)$ over $\tilde x$; there is then $g\in\Gamma$ with $q(1)=g\tilde x$, so $\widetilde\gamma$ runs from $\tilde x$ to $g\tilde x$. This constructs the required pair for the actual loop of the stack, not only for a loop assumed at the outset to have that form. Conversely, a pair and its path project to a stack path by the construction preceding this paragraph.

The same construction applies to a homotopy of loops of the stack in the sense of [@Noo05, Definition 17.2], $H\colon I^2\to X$. Pulling the chart back gives a $\Gamma$-torsor over the square. Trivialize it on a finite grid and glue compatible sections to obtain $Q_H\cong I^2\times\Gamma$. Its section gives the paths $\widetilde\gamma_u$. Since $\Gamma$ is discrete and $I^2$ is connected, the endpoint element $g$ is independent of $u$; the endpoint identifications of the homotopy make the initial and final section agree with the chosen sections on the two loops. This is precisely the homotopy of pairs in [@Col09, Definitions 3.2--3.3]: paths in $U$ move continuously, the group element remains fixed, and endpoint identifications are respected. Colman's statement is used only for the pair terminology, so no manifold hypothesis on $U$ is needed. Concatenation of intervals gives the same composition on the two sides, with the group law of the action groupoid. The inversion in the displayed representation is the product convention of @def:transport-and-monodromy, not a different fundamental group.
