# Categorical Foundations Authority Document

This document defines the categorical and homotopical foundations for the Coxeter project. It consolidates literal extractions from model structure research, spectral theory, and suspension logic archives.

## 1. Derived and Infinity-Category Structure

The project models the category of bilinear modules through the lens of modern stable homotopy theory.

- **Foundational Equivalence**: The derived category $D(\text{Bil}R\text{-Mod})$ is the homotopy category of a stable $\infty$-category $\text{Bil}R\text{-Mod}^\infty$.
- **Adjunctions**: Every object in $\text{Bil}R\text{-Mod}^\infty$ satisfies the tensor-hom adjunction:
  $$Map(M \otimes^L N, P) \simeq Map(M, RHom(N, P))$$
- **t-Structure**: The category possesses a standard t-structure whose heart is equivalent to the abelian category $\text{Bil}R\text{-Mod}$.

## 2. Model Category Presentation

A projective model structure is defined on the category of chain complexes $Ch(\text{Bil}R\text{-Mod})$ to provide a computational platform for the $\infty$-category.

- **Weak Equivalences**: Quasi-isomorphisms.
- **Cofibrations**: Inclusions generated by hyperbolic "disks" $D^n(H)$ where $H$ is the hyperbolic plane. Homotopy-free replacements are built from cells $D^n \otimes H$.
- **Homotopical Freeness**: Cofibrant objects serve as "homotopy-free" replacements for modules that lack a classical free basis. Projective resolutions correspond to the bar construction.

## 3. Categorical Resolutions

### Bar Construction
Explicit resolutions for bilinear modules are provided by the bar construction with hyperbolic planes:
$$\dots \to H \otimes H \otimes M \to H \otimes M \to M \to 0$$
These resolutions enable the computation of derived functors without requiring a classical projectivity.

### Simplicial Spectral Sequence
The spectral sequence for Ext groups is defined via the bar construction:
$$E_1^{p,q} = Ext^q(B_p(M), N) \implies Ext^{p+q}(M, N)$$
where $B_p(M) = H^{\otimes p} \otimes M$.

## 3. Stabilization and Spectral Theory

### Suspension Functor
The stabilization of $\text{Bil}R\text{-Mod}$ is driven by the suspension functor $\Sigma$:
- **Definition**: $\Sigma M = H \oplus M \oplus H$, adding hyperbolic summands to the original module.
- **Traceability**: Stabilization is the $\infty$-categorical colimit of the sequence:
  $$\text{Bil}R\text{-Mod} \xrightarrow{\Sigma} \text{Bil}R\text{-Mod} \xrightarrow{\Sigma} \dots$$

### Bilinear Module Spectra
A spectrum $E$ in $Sp(\text{Bil}R\text{-Mod})$ consists of a sequence $\{E_n\}$ and structure maps $\sigma_n: \Sigma E_n \to E_{n+1}$.
- **Connective Spectra**: $E$ is connective if $\pi_{i}(E) = 0 for $i < 0$, corresponding to the suspension spectrum $\Sigma^\infty M$.
### Stable Dold-Kan Correspondence
The stable Dold-Kan equivalence extends the classical result to the spectral setting:
$$Sp^\Sigma(\text{Bil}R\text{-Mod}) \simeq \text{Fun}(Fin_*, \text{Bil}R\text{-Mod})$$
This identifies bilinear module spectra with functors from pointed finite sets.

### Infinite Loop Space
The functor $\Omega^\infty E$ recovers the underlying bilinear module with coherent higher group structure.

## 4. Derived Operations

- **Derived Tensor Product**: $M \otimes^L N$ is computed via cofibrant replacement (projective resolutions).
- **Homotopy Groups**: For $n \geq 0$, $\pi_n(E) = \text{colim}_k [ \Sigma^k R, E_{n+k} ]_{\text{Bil}R\text{-Mod}}$.
- **Smash Product**: The smash product of spectra is defined via the colimit:
  $$E \wedge F = \text{colim}_{i+j \to n} E_i \otimes_{\text{Bil}R} F_j$$
- **THH for Bilinear Ring Spectra**: $THH(R) = R \wedge_{R \wedge R^{op}} R$, enabling the study of cyclic homology in the bilinear context.

## 5. Summary Principle: Homotopy freeness

The core resolution to the "rigidity problem" (lack of free objects) in bilinear modules is the transition to the stable setting:
1. Every object has a **cofibrant replacement** (built from free cells/hyperbolic planes).
2. Every object becomes **free up to homotopy** after sufficient suspensions.
3. This enabling of universal properties allows for the rigorous definition of functors that are otherwise ill-defined in the classical abelian category.
