# Historical Development: Evolution of Coxeter Theory

This document traces the historical development of Coxeter group theory, providing context for the mathematical foundations and computational approaches used in this project.

## Origins and Early Development

### H.S.M. Coxeter's Foundational Work (1930s-1940s)

**"Discrete Groups Generated by Reflections" (1934)**:
- First systematic study of reflection groups
- Introduced Coxeter diagrams and matrices
- Established connection between combinatorial data and geometric groups

**Key Innovations**:
- **Coxeter Matrix**: Encoding reflection orders in combinatorial form
- **Diagram Notation**: Visual representation of group structure
- **Classification Method**: Systematic approach to finite reflection groups

**Historical Context**: Built on Klein's work on regular polytopes and crystallographic groups, but provided general framework.

### Crystallographic Roots

**19th Century Crystallography**:
- Study of crystal symmetries led to finite reflection groups
- Root systems emerged from Lie theory (Killing, Cartan)
- Connection between algebra and geometry was recognized

**Weyl's Contribution (1920s)**:
- Weyl groups as symmetries of root systems
- Classification of simple Lie algebras via root systems
- Established ADE classification for simply-laced types

**Integration**: Coxeter generalized these crystallographic and Lie-theoretic results to arbitrary reflection groups.

## Mathematical Development Phases

### Phase 1: Finite Type Classification (1930s-1950s)

**Achievement**: Complete classification of finite Coxeter groups.

**Method**: 
- Eigenvalue analysis of Coxeter matrices
- Connection to positive definiteness
- Enumeration of irreducible types: A_n, B_n, D_n, E_6, E_7, E_8, F_4, G_2, H_3, H_4, I_2(p)

**Significance**: Unified crystallographic and non-crystallographic cases.

### Phase 2: Affine Extension (1950s-1960s)

**Motivation**: Extend finite groups to Euclidean reflection groups.

**Key Results**:
- Classification of affine Weyl groups
- Extended Dynkin diagrams
- Connection to loop groups and Kac-Moody algebras

**Mathematical Framework**: 
- Affine root systems
- Euclidean tilings
- Connection to modular forms and theta functions

### Phase 3: Hyperbolic Theory (1960s-1980s)

**Pioneers**: 
- **Vinberg (1960s-1970s)**: Hyperbolic reflection groups, volume finiteness
- **Coxeter himself**: Continued work on hyperbolic polytopes
- **Allcock, Carlson, Toledo**: Modern arithmetic theory

**Breakthrough Results**:
- Vinberg's volume finiteness criterion
- Classification of Lannér groups (compact hyperbolic)
- Connection to arithmetic groups and hyperbolic manifolds

**Computational Aspects**: Led to algorithmic problems requiring computer assistance.

## Computational Era (1980s-Present)

### Early Computer Classifications

**1980s-1990s**: First systematic computer enumerations.
- Complete enumeration of small hyperbolic Coxeter groups
- Discovery of sporadic examples
- Verification of theoretical predictions

**Key Tools**:
- Symbolic computation systems
- Exact arithmetic libraries
- Specialized algorithms for eigenvalue problems

### Modern Computational Tools

**CoxIter (Guglielmetti, 2010s)**:
- Systematic enumeration of maximal parabolic subdiagrams
- Implementation of Vinberg's algorithms
- Validation of theoretical results

**SageMath Integration**:
- Standardized implementations of root systems
- Integration with broader mathematical software ecosystem
- Reproducible research capabilities

**GAP System**:
- Group-theoretic computations
- Verification of classification results
- Connection to finite group theory

### Current Research Directions

**Computational Complexity**:
- Efficient algorithms for large systems
- Pruning strategies for exponential searches
- Parallel computation approaches

**Arithmetic Properties**:
- Discriminants and field extensions
- Galois actions on classification results
- Connection to number theory

**Higher Dimensional Cases**:
- Extension to higher dimensions
- Connections to higher-dimensional manifolds

## Key Historical Insights

### Evolution of Classification Methods

**Early Approach**: Case-by-case analysis of small examples.

**Coxeter's Innovation**: Systematic method using matrices and diagrams.

**Modern Approach**: Definiteness-based classification with computational verification.

**Lesson**: Mathematical abstraction enabled systematic treatment of infinite families.

### Interplay Between Theory and Computation

**Theoretical Predictions**: Vinberg's finiteness conditions predicted computational results.

**Computational Discoveries**: Computer enumerations revealed unexpected patterns.

**Verification Cycle**: Theory guides computation, computation validates theory.

**Modern Synthesis**: Combination of exact symbolic computation with theoretical frameworks.

### Field Extension Requirements

**Historical Recognition**: Early work noticed special properties of H_3, H_4.

**Galois Theory Connection**: Understanding of why certain types require field extensions.

**Computational Impact**: Development of exact arithmetic over number fields.

**Modern Perspective**: Natural from algebraic geometry and arithmetic viewpoint.

**Klein's "Icosahedron" (1884)**:
- Regular polytopes and their symmetries
- Connection between algebra and geometry
- Non-crystallographic cases (icosahedral symmetry)

**Lie Theory (Killing, Cartan, Weyl)**:
- Root systems and Weyl groups
- Classification methods
- Connection to representation theory

**Crystallography (19th century)**:
- Space groups and symmetry
- Finite reflection groups
- Practical computational problems

**Geometric Group Theory**: Coxeter groups as fundamental examples.

**Hyperbolic Geometry**: Revival through Coxeter group theory.

**Algebraic Topology**: Connections through aspherical manifolds and K(π,1) spaces.
