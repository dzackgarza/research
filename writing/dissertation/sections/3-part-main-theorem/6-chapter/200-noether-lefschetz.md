### $\fent$ as a Noether–Lefschetz Locus in $\fttz$ {#section-7-2}

In this section, we establish that the moduli space $\fent$ of degree-2 polarized Enriques surfaces is realized as a locally closed, irreducible sublocus of a Noether–Lefschetz locus inside $\fttz$, the moduli space of degree-4 polarized K3 surfaces with a nonsymplectic involution of fixed type.
Recalling that if $(Z, [L])$ is a smooth Enriques surface equipped with a numerical class of an ample line bundle $L$ having self-intersection $L^2=2$. The canonical étale double cover $\pi: X \longrightarrow Z$ is uniquely determined up to isomorphism.
The deck transformations of $X/Z$ are generated by a fixed-point-free involution $\ien: X \to X$ -- the **Enriques involution**. The class $H \da \pi^*L$ is a nef and big divisor on $X$ with $H^2 = 4$.
There is a unique involution $\idp: X \to X$ distinct from $\ien$, commuting with $\ien$ and fixing $H$. According to Nikulin's classification, $\idp$ is a nonsymplectic involution of type $(2,2,0)$.

:::{.definition
    title="{The Canonical Map $j$}"
    #def:canonical-map-j
}
The association

\begin{align*}
(Z, L) \mapsto (X, H = \pi^*L, \idp)
.\end{align*}

defines a functorial morphism

\begin{align*}
j: \fent \to \fttz,
.\end{align*}

where $\fent$ is the moduli of degree-2 polarized Enriques surfaces and $\fttz$ the moduli of degree-4 polarized K3 surfaces with a nonsymplectic involution of type $(2,2,0)$. This construction is compatible with base change and extends to families.
:::

:::{.definition
    title="{Noether–Lefschetz Locus $\mathrm{NL}_{\ten}$}"
    #def:noether-lefschetz-locus
}
Let $\Lambda = H^2(X, \ZZ) \cong \mathrm{II}_{3,19}$, and let $\ten = U(2) \oplus E_8(-2)$ denote the anti-invariant lattice under the Enriques involution, reflecting both the required polarization and involution structure. 
Define the **Noether–Lefschetz locus**

\begin{align*}
\mathrm{NL}_{\ten} = \left\{ [X] \in \fttz \st \ten \injects \Pic(X),\,\,[\idp,\ien] = 0 \text{ for some } \ien \right\}
,\end{align*}

where we require the embedding to be primitive.
This locus parameterize polarized quartic hyperelliptic K3 surfaces $(X, H)$ with involutions so that $(X, H, \idp, \ien)$ descends to data of a (possibly degenerate) degree-2 polarized Enriques surface.
:::

:::{.proposition
    title="{Lattice-Theoretic Description of the Image of $j$}"
    #prop:lattice-theoretic-image-j
}
The image $j(\fent)$ consists precisely of those $(X, H, \idp) \in \fttz$ for which:

- $X$ admits a commuting involution $\ien$,
- $\ien$ is fixed-point-free,
- The anti-invariant lattice is $\Pic(X)^{\ien} = \ten$.

Equivalently,

\begin{align*}
j(\fent) = \left\{ (X, H, \idp) \in \fttz \st \exists \ien, [\ien,\idp]=0, \, \Pic(X)^{\Ien = -1} \iso  \ten \right\}.
\end{align*}

:::

:::{.proof}
For $X = \pi^{-1}(Z)$ as above, the involution $\ien$ is fixed-point-free, $\idp$ arises canonically from the polarization, and the anti-invariant lattice is explicitly computed as $\ten$ using the cohomology of double covers and the configuration of the involutions. The presence of these two commuting involutions together with the polarization forces the Picard lattice to contain $\ten$ primitively. Conversely, any $(X, H, \idp)$ with such a primitive embedding and compatible involutions arises as the canonical cover of some Enriques surface with polarization of degree $2$.
:::

:::{.proposition
    title="{Period Compatibility and Linear Subdomain}"
    #prop:period-compatibility
}
Let $\sdp$ be the invariant lattice of $\idp$ and $\sen$ that of $\ien$. Then

\begin{align*}
\tdp \oplus \sdp, T_{\mathrm{En}} \oplus \sen \injects H^2(X, \ZZ),
\end{align*}

with $\sdp \cong U(2)$ and $S_{\mathrm{en}} \cong U(2) \oplus E_8(2)$. For any $(Z,L) \in \fent$, the period point for $X$ lies in the orthogonal complement of $\sen$ in the K3 period domain. Thus, the image $j(\fent)$ is described as the subset of the moduli space where the periods are orthogonal to the fixed lattice $\sen$.
:::

:::{.proof}
This follows directly from the construction: the double cover, involutions, and polarization together prescribe the lattice embedding up to equivalence, forcing the period to be in the locus cut out by the orthogonality to $\sen$.
:::

:::{.definition
    title="{Zariski Closure $B$}"
    #def:zariski-closure-b
}
Define

\begin{align*}
B := \overline{j(\fent)} \subset \ksbacpt{\fttz}
.\end{align*}

as the Zariski closure of $j(\fent)$ in the KSBA compactification. This $B$ forms a closed Noether–Lefschetz locus, parameterizing all limits (in the KSBA sense) of K3 surfaces that are double covers of Enriques surfaces, possibly with semi-log canonical singularities and compatible involution structure.
:::

:::{.proposition
    title="{Realization and Dimension}"
    #prop:realization-dimension
}
$\fent$ is realized as a locally closed, irreducible component of the Noether–Lefschetz locus $\mathrm{NL}_{\ten} \subset \fttz$. The Zariski closure $B$ in $\ksbacpt{\fttz}$ parametrizes exactly all (possibly degenerate) degree-4 K3 surfaces $(X, H)$ with a compatible pair of involutions $(\ien, \idp)$ arising from a numerically polarized Enriques surface (or its stable degeneration).
:::

:::{.proof}
By the arguments above, the construction $j$ embeds $\fent$ into $\mathrm{NL}_{\ten}$ defined by the period and lattice conditions. Local closedness follows because the period subdomain is defined by a linear orthogonality condition. The irreducibility and dimension count come from the identification of the period domain for Enriques surfaces as a 10-dimensional linear section inside the ambient 20-dimensional K3 period domain by the global Torelli theorem.
:::

Both $B$ and $\fent$ are 10-dimensional, as is determined by their period domains. The period domain for numerically degree-2 polarized Enriques surfaces is a linear subdomain (cut out by the orthogonality to $\sen$) of the 20-dimensional period domain for degree-4 K3s with involution, and its dimension is 10 by classical results.
Every Enriques surface in $\fent$ and every KSBA degeneration thereof in $B$ is canonically covered by a K3 surface $X$ (possibly degenerate). The full modular datum of the point corresponding to $Z$, including its possible degenerations, is encoded by the triple $(X, \idp, \ien)$: the K3 cover $X$ and its two commuting involutions.

:::{.proposition
    title="{Compatibility of Period Map}"
    #prop:compatibility-period-map
}
For any $(Z, L) \in \fent$, the Hodge structure on its canonical K3 cover $X$ lies in the orthogonal complement of $\sen$ in the K3 period domain. The period map for $\fent$ factors through the period map for $\fttz$ as a linear subspace cut out by the linear condition of being orthogonal to $\sen$.
:::

:::{.proof}
The characterization of the period domain for Enriques surfaces (with fixed degree-2 polarization) as a linear section of the K3 period domain follows immediately from the splitting of the full lattice into invariant and anti-invariant parts for the involutions, and the polarization data forces a unique class in the orthogonal complement.
:::
