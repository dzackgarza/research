# The Main Theorem {#sec:part-3}

## Statement and Outline {#sec:chapter-6}

This chapter is devoted to establishing the isomorphism $\semitorcpt{\fent}\iso \ksbacpt{\fent}$:

:::{.theorem
    title="Main Theorem [@AEGS25, Theorem 1.1, Thm. 5.9]"
    #thm:main-theorem
}
Let $\ksbacpt{\fent}$ denote the KSBA compactification of the moduli space $\fent$ of numerically polarized Enriques surfaces of degree $2$, and let $\semifans{F} = \ts{ \semifan{F}_k }_{k=1,2,3,4,5}$ be the explicit collection of semifans, one for each $0$-cusp as classified in @sec:fent-zero-cusps.

Then:

1. The normalization $\ksbacpt{\fent}$ is isomorphic to the semitoroidal compactification $\semitorcpt{\fent}$ for these semifans,

2. This compactification is toroidal over the $0$-cusps $2$ and $4$, their adjacent $1$-cusps, and cusp $35$, and strictly semitoroidal over all other cusps as described in @sec:five-cusps.

3. There exists a toroidal compactification $\coxcpt{\fent}$ associated to the collection of Coxeter fans $\coxfan = \ts{ \coxfan_k }_{k=1,2,3,4,5}$, such that there is a chain of morphisms
$$
\coxcpt{\fent}
\to
\semitorcpt{\fent}
\to
\ksbacpt{\fent}
$$
where the first morphism contracts cones corresponding to faces defined by irrelevant roots (see [@AEGS25, §5.2]), and the second morphism is the normalization.
:::

We summarize this entire situation in the following diagram:

\begin{tikzcd}
\coxcpt{\fent} \arrow[d] &   & \coxcpt{\fttz} \arrow[d] \\
\semitorcpt{\fent} \arrow[equal, "\therefore"']{d} \ar[d] & 
\semitorcpt{B} 
\ar[equal]{d} \ar[r, hook]   \ar[equal, ":="']{l} 
& \semitorcpt{\fttz} \ar[equal]{d} \ar[d] \\
\normksbacpt{\fent} \arrow[d, "\nu"'] & 
\normalize{B} \ar[d, "\nu"] 
\ar[equal, dashed,"\exists \tilde \Phi"']{l}
\ar[dl, "\Phi"'] 
\ar[r, hook] 
& \normksbacpt{\fttz} \arrow[d, "\nu"] \\
\ksbacpt{\fent} \arrow[d] & 
B := \closure{ j(\fent) } \ar[r, hook]
& \ksbacpt{\fttz} \arrow[d] \\
\fent \arrow[rr, "j", hook] \arrow[rd, ->>] & \phantom{B} & \fttz \\
\phantom{X} & j(\fent) \ar[ru, hook] \ar[uu, hook, bend right] & x 
\end{tikzcd}

We define the embedding $j$, restrict to its image, take the Zariski closure, and then normalize to produce $B^\eta$.
Functoriality of normalization and the KSBA compactification gives am embedding of $B^\eta$ into $\normksbacpt{\fttz}$, which is identified with a specific semitoroidal compactification $\semitorcpt{\fttz}$ for the ramification divisor. We pull back that semifan data to obtain an identification of $B^\nu$ with a semitoroidal compactification $\semitorcpt{B}$ by restriction, which we define to be $\semitorcpt{\fent}$. We then construct a morphism $\Phi: \normalize{B}\to \ksbacpt{\fent}$ by constructing a universal family, lifting it to an isomorphism to $\normksbacpt{\fent}$ using the universal property of normalization, and obtain an isomorphism $B^\nu \cong \normksbacpt{\fent}$ by Zariski's main theorem. Thus $\normalize{B}$ is used as an intermediate space which is isomorphic to both $\semitorcpt{\fent}$ and $\normksbacpt{\fent}$.
The proof proceeds in the following steps:

1. Defining $\fent$ and $\ksbacpt{\fent}$:

    The moduli stack $\fent$ is defined to parametrize numerically polarized Enriques surfaces of degree two and their KSBA stable limits. The compactification $\ksbacpt{\fent}$ is constructed using the theory of stable surface pairs with boundary divisor weighted by $\eps \ll 1$. Each point corresponds to a pair $(Z, \eps R_Z)$ where $K_Z + \eps R_Z$ is ample and the total pair is semi-log-canonical.

2. Realization of $\fent$ as a Noether–Lefschetz locus inside $\fttz$:

    By @lem:fent-to-fttz-closed-immersion, there is an immersion of $\fent$ into the moduli stack $\fttz$ of K3 surfaces with a del Pezzo involution $\idp$ of type $(2,2,0)$. The image consists of K3 surfaces admitting an additional fixed-point-free involution $\ien$, and is cut out by period-theoretic conditions. Its Zariski closure $B \subset \ksbacpt{\fttz}$ in the KSBA compactification is the Noether–Lefschetz locus parameterizing K3 surfaces $X$ whose quotients $Z \da X/\ien$ define Enriques surfaces.

3. Extension of $\ien$ to the universal family of K3 surfaces over $B$ inherited from $\fttz$:

    The moduli stack $\ksbacpt{\fttz}$ admits a universal family $(\mcx, \eps \mcr)$, where $\mcr$ is the normalized ramification divisor of $\idp$. This family pulls back to a family over $B$ whose general fiber admits an Enriques involution $\ien$ commuting with $\idp$. By the uniqueness of KSBA limits, $\ien$ extends over the total space.

4. Quotient to produce a family of stable Enriques pairs over $B$:

    After extending $\ien$, the quotient $(\mcx_B, \eps \mcr_B)/\ien$ defines a family of stable Enriques pairs $(\mcz, \eps \mcr_Z) \to B$, extending the universal family over the interior. The quotient preserves KSBA stability and compatible degeneration data.

5. Pass to $\normalize{B}$ and descend the semifans from $\ksbacpt{\fttz}$:

    The normalization $\normalize{B} \to B$ is a proper, normal stack supporting the pullback of the universal family. The semifans associated to $\ksbacpt{\fent}$, governed by the ramification semifan, restricts to induce a semifan on $\normalize{B}$. The resulting combinatorics define a collection of folded semifans $\mcf = \{ \mcf_k \}$ determining the boundary stratification.

6. Show finiteness of the classifying map using the geometry of Kulikov models of K3 surfaces:

    The classifying morphism $\phi: \normalize{B} \to \ksbacpt{\fent}$ is birational. To prove finiteness, one must exclude the existence of distinct K3 pairs giving the same Enriques quotient. This is achieved via explicit analysis of degenerations (see Sections 4 and 5): the Enriques quotient of a K3 degeneration cannot have a more singular $\mcx_0$ than its cover. Any strict coarsening of the semifans would contradict this.

7. Zariski’s Main Theorem and identification of compactifications:

    Since $\phi$ is finite and birational, it is an isomorphism. The normalization $\ksbacpt{\fent}$ is thus identified with the semitoroidal compactification $\semitorcpt{\fent}$, as constructed via the restricted and folded ramification semifans for $\fttz$.
