# Compactifications and Integral Affine Structures {#sec:part-2}


Let $D$ be a Hermitian symmetric domain of type IV, such as the domains $\halfpd{T}$ arising from indefinite lattices of signature $(2, n)$, and and $\Gamma$ be an arithmetic subgroup of $\Aut(D)$, e.g. an arithmetic subgroup of $\Orth(T)$. The typical compactifications of $\FG = \Gamma \backslash D$ include the Baily–Borel compactification $\bbcpt{\FG}$, toroidal compactifications $\torcpt{\FG}$, semitoroidal compactifications $\semitorcpt{\FG}$, and the KSBA compactification $\ksbacpt{\FG}$. Discussions of the following material can be found in [@Sca87; @AMRT10; @BB66; @Kol13; @KM98].

The Baily Borel compactification $\bbcpt{\FG}$ is defined as $\operatorname{Proj}\,A(\Gamma)$, where $A(\Gamma)$ is the graded $\CC$-algebra of automorphic forms for $\Gamma$. It is a projective, normal variety, and is canonical in the sense that it does not depend on any auxiliary choices.
It is the unique *minimal* geometric compactification in the following sense: for any other normal, projective compactification $X$ of $\FG$ admitting an extension of the period map, there exists a unique, regular, projective morphism $X \to \bbcpt{\FG}$ restricting to the identity on $\FG$.
The boundary $\partial\bbcpt{\FG}$ consists of finitely many strata, each itself a quotient of a Hermitian symmetric domain by an arithmetic group, and is described via rational boundary components indexed by maximal rational parabolics subgroups of $\Aut(D)$. However, the boundary is generally of high codimension and almost never divisorial for Type $\IV$ domains, for which it is generically comprised of only points and modular curves. The analytic structure at the boundary is highly singular and generally not even $\QQ$-factorial.
Moreover, boundary points typically do not correspond to moduli of degenerate surfaces. However, $\bbcpt{\FG}$ is functorial and canonical, and the representation-theoretic nature of the boundary components often reduces their study to combinatorics and lattice theory.

Toroidal compactifications $\torcpt{\FG}$ are defined by refining the structure near the cusps of $\bbcpt{\FG}$. For each cusp, one chooses an admissible rational polyhedral fan in a particular cone at that cusp. The result is a proper algebraic variety with a divisorial boundary which is locally toric.
The construction is highly non-canonical, and depends on the choice of fans, of which there are typically infinitely many. Different choices give different, though birationally equivalent, compactifications, all mapping to $\bbcpt{\FG}$.
The boundary $\bd \torcpt{\FG}$, after potentially a refinement of fans and a finite cover, is a snc divisor. Each boundary component is constructed from tori associated to unipotent radicals $U_P$ of rational parabolic subgroups $P$ of $\Gamma \leq \Aut(D)$.
The fibers of the canonical morphism $\torcpt{\FG}\to \bbcpt{\FG}$ over boundary strata are toric.
The boundary singularities are typically milder than in $\bbcpt{\FG}$, and for well-chosen admissible decompositions, is is possible to construct smooth compactifications.
Moreover, toroidal compactifications often allow the extension of period maps and universal families over boundary strata, yielding a much stronger modular interpretation than $\bbcpt{\FG}$.

Semitoroidal compactifications, introduced by @Loo85, are useful when $\bbcpt{\FG}$ is insufficient, but a full toroidal compactification $\torcpt{\FG}$ is unnecessary or obstructed, or there are no particularly canonical choice of the necessary toric fan data.
While $\bbcpt{\FG}$ has boundary components of codimension typically at least 2 and toroidal compactifications $\torcpt{\FG}$ *fully* refine all cusps to divisorial components, semitoroidal compactifications only partially "toroidalize" along a subset of boundary strata. They thus allow individual cusps to be differentiated, some being fully toroidal, and others being only partially toroidal, hence the name *semitoroidal*. 
Formally, choose a subset $\mcs$ of boundary strata in $\bbcpt{\FG}$ and assign, for each $F \in \mcs$, an admissible rational polyhedral decomposition (i.e. a *semifan*) in the relevant cone at that cusp. One then performs a blowup up only along these loci, glues in toroidal models over $\mcs$, and leave other strata untouched.
The boundary is divisorial along those strata $F \in \mcs$, but retains higher codimension strata elsewhere.
The construction is not canonical, since there are still infinitely many choices of both semitoric data at each cusp.
There are still natural projective morphisms

\begin{align*}
\torcpt{\FG} \longrightarrow \semitorcpt{\FG} \longrightarrow \bbcpt{\FG}
.\end{align*}

restricting to the identity on the open locus $\FG$, yielding a compactification birationally equivalent to all toroidal compactifications. By [@AE23a, Thm. 5.14] the semitoroidal compactifications are *precisely* the compactifications fitting into such a tower.
Such compactifications are particularly effective when compactifying Type $\IV$domains, and often admit extensions of period maps and (partial) universal families over the cusps.

The KSBA compactification $\ksbacpt{\FG}$ arises from the (log) minimal model program (MMP), for varieties of log general type. For a moduli problem, e.g., polarized varieties or stable pairs, one seeks a modular compactification where points correspond to geometric degenerations: namely, stable slc pairs $(X,D)$ with prescribed numerical invariants with $K_X + D$ ample.
Forming the moduli stack of such pairs, by properness of the moduli functor (see [@KS88; @Ale96a] ), one obtains a proper algebraic space that compactifies the stack of smooth pairs.
The resulting **KSBA moduli space** $\ksbacpt{\FG}$ includes boundary divisors corresponding to *all* possible stable degenerations, including those not accessible via general methods for locally symmetric spaces.
The boundary is divisorial, though possibly reducible and singular, and every 1-parameter family over a DVR of smooth surfaces admits, after base-change, a unique stable limit. Universal families exist over open subsets, and a coarse moduli interpretation is valid everywhere.
When a period map exists (e.g. by a Torelli theorem), there is a regular  but not generally finite morphism
$\ksbacpt{\FG} \to\bbcpt{\FG}$ and $\ksbacpt{\FG}$ is generally the finest among all of the compactifications discussed. 
In general, these maps contract or identify boundary components, and the typical situation is shown in \Cref{tor-semitor-bb-ksba}.
However, in situations such as $\fent$, the morphism $\cpt{\FG}\to \bbcpt{\FG}$ lifts to an isomorphism $\ksbacpt{\FG} \iso \semitorcpt{\FG}$ for a proper choice of semifans $\mcf$.
