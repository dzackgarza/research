title: "Compact Moduli of Numerically Polarized Degree Two Enriques Surfaces"
author:
  - D. Zack Garza
abstract: |
  We construct and describe a modular compactification $\ksbacpt{\fent}$ of the moduli space of degree $2$ numerically polarized Enriques surfaces by KSBA stable pairs compactifications. Our approach blends explicit lattice-theoretic methods -- rooted in Nikulin’s classification of 2-elementary lattices and the arithmetic of the transcendental polarizing lattice $\ten$ -- with the Hodge-theoretic theory of period maps and the analytic theory of compactification for Type $\IV$ locally symmetric spaces of orthogonal type.
  We provide a detailed classification of $\bd{\cpt{\fent}}$ via orbits of primitive isotropic sublattices in $\ten$, connecting the five $0$-cusps of the Baily–Borel compactification $\bbcpt{\fent}$ to explicit degenerations of Enriques surfaces. Combining combinatorial, geometric, and computational techniques, we construct explicit divisorial log terminal (dlt) models for all stable limits in $\bd \ksbacpt{\fent}$, relating them to semitoroidal compactifications $\semitorcpt{\fent}$ defined by semifans at each boundary component in the Baily-Borel compactification $\bbcpt{\fent}$.
  The key innocating is the use of "folding" procedures, arising from involutions of the K3 lattice $\lkt$ -- as the primary source of combinatorial data to construct models for all strata in $\bd\ksbacpt{\fent}$. The irreducible components appearing include *$\ADE$ surfaces*, as well as new, non-simply-laced types $B$ and $C$.

  Our main theorem establishes an isomorphism between the normalization of the KSBA compactification and an explicit semitoroidal compactification $\semitorcpt{\fent}$ determined by the arithmetic of $\ten$. This provides the first explicit and functorial identification of these two approaches for Enriques surfaces, yielding an interesting class of techniques that may be applicable to other surfaces whose geometry can be closely tied to that of K3 surfaces.

# Foundations and Geometric Setup {#sec:part-1}

Throughout this text, we work over $k=\CC$.
A **K3 surface** is a smooth, projective, geometrically integral surface $X$ such that $\omega_X\cong \OO_X$ is trivial, and $q(X) \da h^{0, 1}(X) \da H^0(\OO_X) = 0$.
An **Enriques surface** is a non-rational algebraic surface $Z$ of Kodaira dimension $\kappa(Z) = 0$ for which $h^1(\OO_Z) = h^2(\OO_Z) = 0$ and $K_Z$ is nontrivial $2$-torsion in $\Pic(Z)$.
Equivalently, they are quotients $Z = X/\iota$ of K3 surfaces $X$ by fixed-point-free involutions $\iota$, satisfying $2K_Z \sim 0$ and $q(Z) = 0$.
Enriques surfaces were among the first examples of non-rational surfaces with $p_g = 0$.
They have Kodaira dimension $\kappa(Z) = 0$ and are  minimal.
We are interested in the study of moduli spaces of such surfaces, and in particular compactifications of $\fent$, the moduli space of degree 2 numerically polarized Enriques surfaces, which we will describe shortly.

For typical moduli spaces $\mcm$, one often has many choices for what a compactification $\cpt{\mcm}$ should mean: GIT quotients $\gitcpt{\mcm}$, Baily-Borel compactifications $\bbcpt{\mcm}$, toroidal compactifications $\torcpt{\mcm}$, the semitoroidal compactifications $\semitorcpt{\mcm}$ of @Loo85 which simultaneously generalize $\bbcpt{\mcm}$ and $\torcpt{\mcm}$, KSBA compactifications $\ksbacpt{\mcm}$, quotient stack constructions, derived quotient stacks, and so on.
A disadvantage of many naively constructed compactifications $\cpt{\mcm}$ is that they do not a priori have modular interpretations, i.e. $\bd\cpt{\mcm}$ may not parameterize singular limits of smooth interior points in any canonically meaningful way.
However, the theory developed by KSBA (see [@Kol23]) provides a functorial, geometrically meaningful compactification which can be defined in a canonical way, provided one can make a natural choice of divisor on the varieties in question (e.g. a polarization or a ramification divisor), and $\bd\cpt{\mcm}$ can be understood as stable pairs $(X, R)$: a variety $X$ with slc singularities and an ample divisor $R$.
Our goal for @sec:part-1 is thus to establish the setting of the main theorem, along with the lattice-theoretic background and computational tools needed to contextualize and prove it.
