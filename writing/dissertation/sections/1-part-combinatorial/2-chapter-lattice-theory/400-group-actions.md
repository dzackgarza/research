### Group Actions and Isometries {#section-2-4}

Define the space of **isometries** between two lattices $(L_1, \beta_1)$ and $(L_2, \beta_2)$ as
$$\Isom(L_1, L_2) \da  \{f \in \Hom_{\Lat_{\ZZ}}(L_1, L_2) \st f \text{ is an isomorphism of } \ZZ\text{-modules}\}$$
Two lattices are **isometric** ($L_1 \cong L_2$) if and only if this set is non-empty.

#### Reflections and Transvections

Let $(L,\beta)$ be a nondegenerate integral lattice. For an anisotropic vector $\alpha \in L$ satisfying the condition $2\beta(\alpha, L) \containedin \beta(\alpha,\alpha)\ZZ$, the **reflection in $\alpha$** is the isometry:
$$s_\alpha(x) \da  x - 2\frac{\beta(\alpha,x)}{\beta(\alpha,\alpha)}\alpha$$
Reflections have determinant -1 and act as the identity on the orthogonal complement $\gens{\alpha}^{\perp L}$. The group generated by all such reflections is the **Weyl group** $W(L) \leq \Orth(L)$.
Letting $e \in L_\QQ$ be an isotropic vector and let $a \in \gens{e}^{\perp_{L_\QQ}}$ be any vector satisfying $a\cdot e=0$. 
The **Siegel-Eichler transformation** associated to the pair $(e, a)$ is the isometry $E_{e,a} \in \Orth(L_\QQ)$ defined by:
$$ 
E_{e,a}(v) = v + \beta(e, v)a - \left( \beta(a, v) + {1\over 2}\beta(a, a)\beta(e, v) \right) e 
$$
If $a$ is also isotropic, this simplifies to 
$$
E_{e,a}(v) = v + \beta(e, v)a - \beta(a, v)e
.$$
The **Eichler transvection group** $E(L)$ is generated by all such transvections where $e \in L$ is a primitive isotropic vector with $\div_L(e) = 1$, and $a \in \gens{e}^{\perp_L}$. If $L$ is even, then $E(L) \containedin \OStab(L)$.

#### Orbits of Isotropic Vectors

For a nondegenerate indefinite lattice $L$ and any integer $k$, the set of $\Orth(L)$-orbits of primitive vectors of norm $k$, denoted $L[k]/\Orth(L)$, is finite.
In particular, let $T$ be an even lattice of signature $(2,n)$ and let $\Gamma \containedin \Orth(T)$ be an arithmetic subgroup (e.g., a subgroup of finite index like $\OStab(T)$). The 0-cusps of the Baily-Borel compactification of the associated arithmetic quotient $\FG \da \dmodgamma{ \halfpd{T} }{\Gamma}$ are in one-to-one correspondence with $\Gamma$-orbits of primitive rank 1 isotropic sublattices of $T$, i.e.
$$ 
\bd_0\bbcpt{\FG}\da 
\bd_0\bbcpt{
    \dmodgamma
    { \halfpd{T} }
    { \Gamma }
} 
    \mapstofrom L[0]/\Gamma 
    = \ts{v \in L \st v \text{ is primitive and } v^2 = 0} / \Gamma 
.$$
For $T \da \ten$, the appropriate arithmetic group $\gent$ has exactly 5 orbits of primitive isotropic vectors, yielding 5 cusps of $\bbcpt{\fent}$. They are partitioned by divisibility: there is one orbit of divisibility 1 vectors, and 4 orbits of divisibility 2 vectors.
We are thus interested in classifying orbits of primitive isotropic vectors.
For lattices containing a double hyperbolic plane $U^2$, we recount the following useful theorems:

:::{.theorem    #thm:eichler-siegel-transformations}

There is an isometry 
$$
\phi: U \oplus U \to (\Mat_{2 \times 2}(\ZZ), 2\cdot \det)
.$$ 
Under $\phi$, the group generated by Eichler-Siegel transformations corresponds to the image of $\SL_2(\ZZ) \times \SL_2(\ZZ)$ acting on a matrix $X$ by $(A, B)\actson X \da A X B\inv$.
:::

:::{.proof}
Write $L = U \oplus U'$ as two distinct copies of $U$.
The map $\phi: L \to \Mat_{2 \times 2}(\ZZ)$ defined by

\begin{align*}
ae + df + be' + cf' \mapsto \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ -c & d \end{pmatrix}
.\end{align*}

is a $\ZZ$-module isomorphism. To check that $\phi$ is an isometry for the quadratic form, observe that

\begin{align*}
(ae + df + be' + cf')^2 = 2ad + 2bc.
,
.\end{align*}

which is precisely twice the determinant of the corresponding matrix.
The group $\SL_2(\ZZ) \times \SL_2(\ZZ)$ acts on $\Mat_{2 \times 2}(\ZZ)$ by

\begin{align*}
(A, B): X \mapsto A X B\inv,
.\end{align*}

which preserves the determinant and thus the bilinear form.
The action of $\SL_2(\ZZ)$ by left multiplication corresponds to elementary row operations (acting on the basis $\{e, f\}$), and the action on the right corresponds to elementary column operations (acting on the basis $\{e', f'\}$).
We conclude using the fact that $\SL_2(\ZZ)$ is generated by elementary matrices.
:::

:::{.theorem title="[@Sca87, Prop. 3.7.3]"}
Let $L$ be an even lattice that decomposes as $L \iso U^{2} \oplus M$ where $M$ is any lattice, and suppose $v, w \in L[k]$ for some fixed $k$. Then
$$
v \sim_{E(L)} w \Longleftrightarrow\left[v^{*}\right]=\left[w^{*}\right] \in A_{L} .
$$
:::

:::{.proof title="Sketch"}
Suppose that $\phi(v)=w$ for some $\phi \in E(L)$. Then necessarily $\phi^{*}\left(\left[v^{*}\right]\right)=\left[w^{*}\right]$ in $A_{L}$ by restriction. However, $E(L) \containedin \OStab(L)$, so $\phi$ must induce the identity on $A_{L}$ and thus $\left[v^{*}\right]=\left[w^{*}\right]$.
Conversely, suppose that $\left[v^{*}\right]=\left[w^{*}\right]$ in $A_{L}$. Write $L=U_{1} \oplus U_{2} \oplus M$; then by the above proposition we may assume that $v, w \in L^{\prime}\da M \oplus U_{1}$ up to $E\left(U_{1} \oplus U_{2}\right)$. Since $\left[v^{*}\right]=\left[w^{*}\right]$, in particular these elements have the same order in $A_{L}$, and so $\div_{L}(v)=\div_{L}(w)=d$ is a fixed positive integer. Thus there exist $x, y \in L^{\prime}$ such that $\beta(x, v)=d$ and $\beta(y, w)=d$. 
We now claim that $z\da \frac{v}{d}-\frac{w}{d}=\frac{v-w}{d}$ is an element of $M \oplus U_{1}$. This follows from the fact that $v^{*} \equiv w^{*}\left(\bmod L^{\prime}\right)$, so $\frac{v}{d} \equiv \frac{w}{d}$ $\left(\bmod L^{\prime}\right)$. Thus $z\da \frac{v}{d}-\frac{w}{d} \equiv 0 \bmod L$ is an element of $L^{\prime}$.
Using the fact that $d z^{2}=2 v z$, a direct calculation shows that the following composition maps $v$ to $w$:
$$
E_{e, y} \circ E_{f,-z} \circ E_{e,-x}
.$$

:::

#### Group Actions

For a faithful representation $G \injects \Orth(L)$ realizing a group $G$ acting on $L$ by isometries, the sublattice of **invariants** and **coinvariants** are defined as
$$
L^G \da  \ts{ v \in L \st g.v = v \,\,\forall\,\, g \in G }, 
\qquad 
L_G \da  (L^G)^{\perp L}
.$$
If $I$ is an involution ($I^2=\id$), these correspond to the eigenspaces for eigenvalues +1 and -1, respectively, for the induced action on $L_\QQ$.
More generally, if $G \injects \Orth(L)$ is a faithful representation of a finite cyclic group $G$, then $L^G$ and $L_G$ are primitive sublattices, and $L^G \oplus L_G$ has finite index in $L$. If $I \in \OStab(L)$ is an involution and $L$ is 2-elementary, then both $L^I$ and $L_I$ are again 2-elementary lattices.


#### Applications

:::{.definition title="{Overlattices and Gluing Theory}" #def:overlattices-gluing}
An **overlattice** $L$ of a lattice $S$ is a lattice containing $S$ as a finite-index sublattice. Nikulin's theory provides a classification of even overlattices of $S$ in terms of isotropic subgroups of $A_S$ up to $\Orth(S)$.
:::
