# Calculus on Schemes {#sec-calculus-on-schemes}

Differentiation on a scheme begins with a derivation of its structure sheaf. Connections extend that derivation to modules; curvature measures the failure of the extension to square to zero. Tangent endomorphisms and bilinear pairings then express the algebraic parts of complex and metric geometry. On a smooth manifold, the corresponding constructions use the sheaf of smooth functions and its smooth differential forms. Integrability and positivity supply the additional conditions for complex and Kähler geometry.

The algebraic and differential-geometric settings will remain explicit. A smooth manifold with its sheaf of smooth functions is a locally ringed space; an algebraic scheme is locally the spectrum of a commutative ring. Complexifying the coefficients of smooth functions does not change the underlying manifold. Base change of an algebraic scheme can change its underlying space. These distinctions determine which of the constructions below can be identified.

The exposition follows the sheaf-theoretic treatment in [@Ram04, Chapters 1, 2, 5–7], the calculus of complex manifolds in [@Huy05, §§1.2, 2.6, 3.1, 4.2 and Appendix 4.A], and the relative differentials of [@stacks-08RL]. All schemes and sheaves of rings in this chapter are classical, and all tensor products are ordinary tensor products. Smooth manifolds are Hausdorff and second countable.

## Differentials and tangent sheaves {#sec-calculus-differentials}

Let $f:X\to S$ be a morphism of schemes. Write $\mathcal A=\mathcal O_X$ and $\mathcal R=f^{-1}\mathcal O_S$, with its structural homomorphism to $\mathcal A$. Sections in a formula are taken over a common open subset of $X$. For an $\mathcal A$-module $\mathcal E$, write $\mathcal E_x$ for its stalk and $\mathcal E(x)=\mathcal E_x\otimes_{\mathcal O_{X,x}}\kappa(x)$ for its fiber.

:::{.definition #def:calculus-derivation title="Relative derivation"}
An $\mathcal R$-*derivation* into an $\mathcal A$-module $\mathcal E$ is an additive sheaf map $D:\mathcal A\to\mathcal E$ that vanishes on the image of $\mathcal R$ and satisfies $D(ab)=aD(b)+bD(a)$.
:::

:::{.definition #def:calculus-differentials title="Relative differentials"}
The *sheaf of relative differentials* is the $\mathcal A$-module $\Omega^1_{X/S}$ with a derivation $d:\mathcal A\to\Omega^1_{X/S}$ representing derivations:

\begin{align*}
\mathcal H om_{\mathcal A}(\Omega^1_{X/S},\mathcal E)
&\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\mathcal D er_{\mathcal R}(\mathcal A,\mathcal E),\qquad \varphi\longmapsto\varphi\circ d.
\end{align*}

The isomorphism is natural in $\mathcal E$. Here the right-hand side denotes the sheaf of derivations, obtained by applying the definition on open subsets.
:::

:::{.proposition #prop:calculus-universal-differentials title="Construction of relative differentials"}
The object in @def:calculus-differentials exists. Locally it is generated by symbols $da$, for sections $a$ of $\mathcal A$, with relations $d(a+b)=da+db$, $d(ab)=a\,db+b\,da$, and $dr=0$ for sections in the image of $\mathcal R$ [@stacks-08RL, Lemma 17.28.2].
:::

:::{.proof title="Universal property"}
**Assume:** $\mathcal E$ is an $\mathcal A$-module. **Prove:** the stated presentation represents its derivations.

1. Form the free $\mathcal A$-module sheaf on the sheaf of sections of $\mathcal A$, and quotient by the indicated relations. The assignment $a\mapsto da$ is a derivation by those relations.
2. A derivation $D$ determines an $\mathcal A$-linear map by $da\mapsto D(a)$. The derivation identities make this assignment descend through the quotient.
3. This map is unique because the sections $da$ generate locally. Restriction and composition commute with the assignment, giving the sheaf isomorphism and its naturality. $\square$
:::

:::{.definition #def:calculus-tangent title="Tangent sheaf"}
The *relative tangent sheaf* is $\mathcal T_{X/S}=\mathcal H om_{\mathcal A}(\Omega^1_{X/S},\mathcal A)$. A vector field on an open subset $U$ is a section $v\in\mathcal T_{X/S}(U)$. Its derivation is $a\mapsto v(a):=da(v)$.
:::

:::{.remark #rmk:calculus-duality title="Duality hypotheses"}
If $X\to S$ is smooth, $\Omega^1_{X/S}$ is finite locally free [@stacks-02G1]. Consequently evaluation identifies $\Omega^1_{X/S}$ with $\mathcal T_{X/S}^{\vee}$, where $\mathcal F^{\vee}=\mathcal H om_{\mathcal A}(\mathcal F,\mathcal A)$. For finite locally free $\mathcal E,\mathcal F$, the map $\lambda\otimes t\mapsto(s\mapsto\lambda(s)t)$ identifies $\mathcal E^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal F$ with $\mathcal H om_{\mathcal A}(\mathcal E,\mathcal F)$. In a local basis these maps send dual basis tensors to matrix units, which proves the identifications. They are used below only under these hypotheses.
:::

:::{.definition #def:calculus-de-rham title="Relative de Rham forms"}
Set $\Omega^p_{X/S}=\bigwedge^p_{\mathcal A}\Omega^1_{X/S}$ for $p\geq0$, with $\Omega^0_{X/S}=\mathcal A$. The *relative de Rham differential* extends $d$ by

\begin{align*}
d(a_0\,da_1\wedge\cdots\wedge da_p)
&=da_0\wedge da_1\wedge\cdots\wedge da_p.
\end{align*}

It is an $\mathcal R$-linear graded derivation with $d^2=0$ [@stacks-0FKM].
:::

:::{.definition #def:calculus-valued-forms title="Module-valued forms"}
For an $\mathcal A$-module $\mathcal E$, its sheaf of *$\mathcal E$-valued $p$-forms* is $\Omega^p(\mathcal E)=\Omega^p_{X/S}\otimes_{\mathcal A}\mathcal E$. If $\Omega^1_{X/S}$ is finite locally free, evaluation gives $\Omega^p(\mathcal E)\cong\mathcal H om_{\mathcal A}(\bigwedge^p\mathcal T_{X/S},\mathcal E)$, by the local dual-basis calculation of @rmk:calculus-duality.
:::

### Smooth and holomorphic differentials

For a smooth real manifold $M$, put $\mathcal A_M=C^\infty_M(\mathbb R)$ and let $\mathcal A_M^p$ denote its sheaf of smooth real differential $p$-forms. For a complex manifold $Z$, write $\mathcal O_Z$ for holomorphic functions and $\Omega_Z^p$ for holomorphic differential forms. Their local differentials satisfy the smooth or holomorphic chain rule, respectively [@Ram04, Chapter 2, §§1–2; @Huy05, §§1.3, 2.2].

The calculus below applies in each setting with its specified differential forms. In the smooth setting, these are smooth differentials, with the smooth chain-rule relations; they are not defined by merely forgetting to sheaves of abstract $\mathbb R$-algebras and taking unrestricted algebraic Kähler differentials. This choice fixes what a differential and a vector field mean.

:::{.remark #rmk:calculus-directional-derivative title="Directional derivatives"}
Concretely, in smooth local coordinates $x^1,\ldots,x^n$, write $V=\sum_j V(x^j)\partial_{x^j}$. Then

\begin{align*}
df(V)&=\left(\sum_j\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^j}\,dx^j\right)(V)
=\sum_j V(x^j)\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^j},\\
df_x(V_x)&=\sum_j v^j\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^j}(x),\qquad v^j=V(x^j)(x).
\end{align*}

Thus we recover the directional derivative of $f$ in the direction $V_x$. Here $V$ acts on the coordinate function before evaluation at $x$.
:::

## Connections as sheaf maps {#sec-calculus-connections}

For this section, $(\mathcal A,\Omega^\bullet,d)$ denotes the algebraic relative de Rham algebra above, the smooth de Rham algebra, or the holomorphic de Rham algebra. The base scalar sheaf $\mathcal R$ is $f^{-1}\mathcal O_S$ in the algebraic setting and the constant real or complex scalars in the manifold settings. Its image in $\mathcal A$ is annihilated by $d$.

:::{.definition #def:calculus-connection title="Connection"}
A *connection* on an $\mathcal A$-module $\mathcal E$ is an $\mathcal R$-linear sheaf map $\nabla:\mathcal E\to\Omega^1\otimes_{\mathcal A}\mathcal E$ satisfying

\begin{align*}
\nabla(as)&=da\otimes s+a\nabla s.
\end{align*}

This is the Leibniz formulation of [@Ram04, Chapter 5, §1; @Huy05, Definition 4.2.1].
:::

:::{.definition #def:calculus-horizontal-morphism title="Morphisms of connections"}
A morphism $(\mathcal E,\nabla^{\mathcal E})\to(\mathcal F,\nabla^{\mathcal F})$ is an $\mathcal A$-linear sheaf map $u:\mathcal E\to\mathcal F$ satisfying $\nabla^{\mathcal F}\circ u=(1\otimes u)\circ\nabla^{\mathcal E}$. Such objects and morphisms form the category of modules with connection for the chosen differential algebra; composition is composition of sheaf maps.
:::

:::{.definition #def:calculus-horizontal-section title="Horizontal sections"}
The sheaf of *horizontal sections* is $\mathcal E^\nabla=\ker(\nabla)$, with the kernel taken in sheaves of $\mathcal R$-modules. Its sections are also called parallel or covariantly constant. It is a module over the sheaf of rings $\ker(d:\mathcal A\to\Omega^1)$, since $\nabla(as)=0$ whenever $da=0$ and $\nabla s=0$.
:::

:::{.proposition #prop:calculus-covariant title="Covariant derivatives"}
Suppose $\Omega^1$ is finite locally free and put $\mathcal T=(\Omega^1)^\vee$. A connection determines operators $\nabla_v s=(\operatorname{ev}_v\otimes1)(\nabla s)$ satisfying

\begin{align*}
\nabla_{av+bw}s&=a\nabla_v s+b\nabla_w s,\\
\nabla_v(as)&=v(a)s+a\nabla_v s.
\end{align*}

Conversely, a family of $\mathcal R$-linear operators satisfying these identities and commuting with restriction determines a connection. A section is horizontal exactly when all its covariant derivatives vanish.
:::

:::{.proof title="Evaluation and duality"}
**Assume:** $\Omega^1$ is finite locally free. **Prove:** the two descriptions agree.

1. Contracting the Leibniz identity in @def:calculus-connection with $v$ gives the second identity. Linearity of contraction gives the first.
2. Conversely, $v\mapsto\nabla_v s$ is $\mathcal A$-linear. The isomorphism $\Omega^1\otimes\mathcal E\cong\mathcal H om_{\mathcal A}(\mathcal T,\mathcal E)$ therefore recovers a unique $\nabla s$.
3. Evaluation detects equality under this isomorphism. It proves the Leibniz identity for the recovered map, the inverse relationship, and the assertion about horizontal sections. $\square$
:::

:::{.proposition #prop:calculus-connection-difference title="Differences of connections"}
If $\nabla$ and $\nabla'$ are connections on $\mathcal E$, then $\nabla'-\nabla$ is $\mathcal A$-linear. Conversely, adding any $\mathcal A$-linear map $B:\mathcal E\to\Omega^1\otimes\mathcal E$ to a connection gives a connection. Thus, when nonempty, the set of connections on $\mathcal E$ is a torsor under $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal A}(\mathcal E,\Omega^1\otimes\mathcal E)$ [@Huy05, Proposition 4.2.3].
:::

:::{.proof title="Cancellation of the symbol"}
Subtracting the two Leibniz identities cancels $da\otimes s$ and gives $(\nabla'-\nabla)(as)=a(\nabla'-\nabla)(s)$. Adding an $\mathcal A$-linear map preserves that identity. The action is free and transitive because the difference is uniquely determined. $\square$
:::

:::{.construction #cons:calculus-induced-connections title="Tensor, dual, and pullback connections"}
For finite locally free modules with connection, the induced connections are determined by

\begin{align*}
\nabla^{\mathcal E\otimes\mathcal F}(s\otimes t)
&=\nabla^{\mathcal E}s\otimes t+s\otimes\nabla^{\mathcal F}t,\\
(\nabla^{\mathcal E^\vee}_v\lambda)(s)
&=v(\lambda(s))-\lambda(\nabla^{\mathcal E}_v s),\\
(\nabla^{\mathcal H om(\mathcal E,\mathcal F)}_v u)(s)
&=\nabla^{\mathcal F}_v(u(s))-u(\nabla^{\mathcal E}_v s).
\end{align*}

For a morphism $q:Y\to X$ in the chosen geometric category, over the same base in the relative case, use $dq:q^*\Omega_X^1\to\Omega_Y^1$ to define

\begin{align*}
(q^*\nabla)(a\otimes s)&=da\otimes s+a\,(dq\otimes1)(q^*(\nabla s)).
\end{align*}

Here $q^*\mathcal E=\mathcal O_Y\otimes_{q^{-1}\mathcal O_X}q^{-1}\mathcal E$. These formulas descend through the tensor-product relations because the Leibniz terms on both representatives of $as\otimes t=s\otimes at$ agree. They commute with restriction and satisfy the connection Leibniz rule [@Huy05, Examples 4.2.6; @Ram04, Chapter 5, §1].
:::

## Curvature and flatness {#sec-calculus-curvature}

:::{.construction #cons:calculus-exterior-connection title="Exterior covariant derivative"}
A connection extends to $\mathcal R$-linear maps $d_\nabla:\Omega^p(\mathcal E)\to\Omega^{p+1}(\mathcal E)$ by

\begin{align*}
d_\nabla(\alpha\otimes s)&=d\alpha\otimes s+(-1)^p\alpha\wedge\nabla s.
\end{align*}

To check balancing, expand $d(a\alpha)=da\wedge\alpha+a\,d\alpha$ and $\nabla(as)=da\otimes s+a\nabla s$. The relation $da\wedge\alpha=(-1)^p\alpha\wedge da$ identifies the two expansions. The extension is therefore well-defined [@Ram04, Chapter 5, §5].
:::

:::{.definition #def:calculus-curvature title="Curvature"}
The *curvature* of $\nabla$ is $F_\nabla=d_\nabla\circ\nabla:\mathcal E\to\Omega^2\otimes_{\mathcal A}\mathcal E$.
:::

:::{.proposition #prop:calculus-curvature-tensor title="Tensoriality and the curvature formula"}
The map $F_\nabla$ is $\mathcal A$-linear. When $\mathcal E$ is finite locally free, it is a global section of $\Omega^2\otimes\mathcal E nd_{\mathcal A}(\mathcal E)$. When $\Omega^1$ is also finite locally free, its evaluation on vector fields is

\begin{align*}
F_\nabla(v,w)s&=\nabla_v\nabla_w s-\nabla_w\nabla_v s-\nabla_{[v,w]}s.
\end{align*}

Here $[v,w](a)=v(w(a))-w(v(a))$ is the commutator derivation.
:::

:::{.proof title="Cancellation and evaluation"}
**Assume:** $\nabla$ is a connection. **Prove:** the assertions of the proposition.

1. Applying $d_\nabla$ to $\nabla(as)$ gives

   \begin{align*}
   F_\nabla(as)&=-da\wedge\nabla s+da\wedge\nabla s+aF_\nabla(s)=aF_\nabla(s).
   \end{align*}

   Thus $F_\nabla$ is $\mathcal A$-linear.
2. The identification with an endomorphism-valued form follows by the local dual-basis map of @rmk:calculus-duality.
3. For a one-form $\alpha$, $d\alpha(v,w)=v(\alpha(w))-w(\alpha(v))-\alpha([v,w])$. Apply this formula to the one-form component of each local term in $\nabla s$ and use @cons:calculus-exterior-connection. The resulting terms are the displayed commutator formula. $\square$
:::

:::{.proposition #prop:calculus-local-curvature title="Connection and curvature matrices"}
Choose a local frame $e=(e_1,\ldots,e_r)$ and write a section as $s=e\,u$, with $u$ a column. There is a matrix $A$ of one-forms such that $\nabla(eu)=e(du+Au)$. In this frame

\begin{align*}
F_\nabla&=dA+A\wedge A.
\end{align*}

Under the frame change $e'=eG$, the matrices transform as $A'=G^{-1}AG+G^{-1}dG$ and $F'=G^{-1}FG$.
:::

:::{.proof title="Change of frame"}
**Assume:** the indicated frame and column convention. **Prove:** the formulas.

1. The columns of $A$ are the coefficients of $\nabla e_j$. Expanding $d_\nabla^2(eu)$ cancels the terms involving $du$ and leaves $e(dA+A\wedge A)u$.
2. Apply $\nabla$ to $eGu'$ and use the Leibniz rule. Its coefficients in $e'$ are $du'+(G^{-1}AG+G^{-1}dG)u'$.
3. Since $F_\nabla$ is $\mathcal A$-linear, its matrix in $eG$ is $G^{-1}FG$. $\square$
:::

:::{.definition #def:calculus-flat title="Flat connection"}
A connection is *flat*, or integrable, if $F_\nabla=0$. Flatness here is a property of the connection, distinct from flatness of its underlying module over a ring.
:::

:::{.proposition #prop:calculus-flat-complex title="The de Rham complex with coefficients"}
For $\alpha\in\Omega^p$ and $s\in\mathcal E$, one has $d_\nabla^2(\alpha\otimes s)=\alpha\wedge F_\nabla(s)$. Hence $d_\nabla$ defines a complex $\mathcal E\to\Omega^1(\mathcal E)\to\Omega^2(\mathcal E)\to\cdots$ precisely when $\nabla$ is flat.
:::

:::{.proof title="The square of the extension"}
Apply @cons:calculus-exterior-connection twice. The term $d^2\alpha$ is zero; the coefficients of $d\alpha\wedge\nabla s$ are $(-1)^{p+1}$ and $(-1)^p$, so they cancel. The remaining term is $\alpha\wedge F_\nabla(s)$. Taking $p=0$ proves the converse. $\square$
:::

:::{.example #ex:calculus-line-curvature title="A connection on the trivial line"}
On $X=\operatorname{Spec}k[x,y]$, the connection $\nabla=d+x\,dy$ on $\mathcal O_X$ has curvature $dx\wedge dy$, by @prop:calculus-local-curvature. It is not flat. On the same trivial line, $d$ is flat. Thus the underlying sheaf alone does not determine the curvature.
:::

## Tangent connections and metrics {#sec-calculus-metrics}

For this section assume that $\Omega^1$ is finite locally free and that $2$ is invertible. Write $\mathcal T=(\Omega^1)^\vee$. The bracket of vector fields is linear over the base scalars and satisfies $[v,aw]=v(a)w+a[v,w]$.

:::{.definition #def:calculus-torsion title="Torsion"}
A connection on $\mathcal T$ is an affine connection. Its *torsion* is

\begin{align*}
T_\nabla(v,w)&=\nabla_vw-\nabla_wv-[v,w].
\end{align*}

It is torsion-free if $T_\nabla=0$ [@Huy05, Definition 4.A.1].
:::

:::{.proposition #prop:calculus-torsion-tensor title="Torsion is a tensor"}
Torsion defines an $\mathcal A$-linear map $\bigwedge^2\mathcal T\to\mathcal T$.
:::

:::{.proof title="Cancellation of the derivative terms"}
The identities $\nabla_w(av)=w(a)v+a\nabla_wv$ and $[av,w]=a[v,w]-w(a)v$ give $T_\nabla(av,w)=aT_\nabla(v,w)$. Skew-symmetry gives linearity in the other variable, and $T_\nabla(v,v)=0$ gives the factorization through the exterior square. $\square$
:::

:::{.definition #def:calculus-perfect-metric title="Perfect symmetric pairing"}
For a finite locally free $\mathcal A$-module $\mathcal E$, a symmetric pairing $g:\mathcal E\otimes\mathcal E\to\mathcal A$ is *perfect* if its adjoint $g^\flat:\mathcal E\to\mathcal E^\vee$, $s\mapsto g(s,-)$, is an isomorphism.
:::

:::{.remark #rmk:calculus-pairing-adjoints title="The two adjoints of a pairing"}
A pairing $b:\mathcal E\otimes\mathcal F\to\mathcal A$ induces maps

\begin{align*}
b_1:\mathcal E&\longrightarrow\mathcal F^\vee,&s&\longmapsto b(s,-),\\
b_2:\mathcal F&\longrightarrow\mathcal E^\vee,&t&\longmapsto b(-,t).
\end{align*}

For $\mathcal E=\mathcal F$, symmetry means $b_1=b_2$. For finite locally free modules, the tensor–Hom identifications also give

\begin{align*}
\mathcal E^\vee\otimes\mathcal F
&\cong\mathcal H om(\mathcal E,\mathcal F)
\cong\mathcal H om(\mathcal F^\vee,\mathcal E^\vee),\\
\mathcal E^\vee\otimes\mathcal F^\vee
&\cong\mathcal H om(\mathcal E,\mathcal F^\vee)
\cong\mathcal H om(\mathcal F,\mathcal E^\vee)
\cong(\mathcal E\otimes\mathcal F)^\vee.
\end{align*}

The first line uses the dual of a morphism; the second uses $b\mapsto(s\mapsto b(s,-))$ and interchange of the arguments. All maps are over $\mathcal A$.
:::

:::{.definition #def:calculus-real-positivity title="Positivity at real points"}
Let $X$ be an $\mathbb R$-scheme, $\mathcal E$ finite locally free, and $g$ a symmetric pairing on $\mathcal E$. At a real point $p:\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb R\to X$, the pairing is *positive semidefinite* if $(p^*g)(v,v)\geq0$ for every $v\in p^*\mathcal E$, and *positive definite* if the inequality is strict for every nonzero $v$. It is positive semidefinite or positive definite on the real locus if the corresponding condition holds at every real point. Nondegeneracy at $p$ means that the adjoint of $p^*g$ is an isomorphism.
:::

:::{.definition #def:calculus-isometric-endomorphism title="Endomorphisms compatible with a metric"}
Let $(\mathcal E,g)$ be a finite locally free module with perfect symmetric pairing. An endomorphism $F$ is *compatible with $g$*, or *isometric*, if $g(Fs,Ft)=g(s,t)$ for all local sections. This identity makes $F$ an automorphism: in a frame, $F^{\mathsf t}GF=G$ implies that $\det F$ is a unit. In particular, $F$ induces isometries on all fibers. On a nonreduced scheme, the fiber identities alone need not imply the sheaf identity, since residue fields do not detect nilpotents.
:::

:::{.definition #def:calculus-riemannian title="Riemannian metric"}
On a smooth real manifold $M$, a *Riemannian metric* is a smooth symmetric pairing $g$ on $\mathcal T_M$ whose value on each real tangent space is positive definite. A smooth perfect symmetric pairing without this positivity requirement is a pseudo-Riemannian metric.
:::

On an algebraic scheme, perfectness is expressed by $g^\flat$ being an isomorphism. Positivity requires ordered real fibers. In particular, positivity at real points of an $\mathbb R$-scheme does not replace perfectness on the scheme: the real locus can be empty. A fiberwise test for perfectness must concern every point, or must use hypotheses that justify a smaller test set.

:::{.definition #def:calculus-metric-connection title="Metric compatibility"}
A connection on $\mathcal E$ is *compatible with $g$* if

\begin{align*}
v(g(s,t))&=g(\nabla_v s,t)+g(s,\nabla_v t)
\end{align*}

for all local sections. Equivalently, the induced connection on $\mathcal E^\vee\otimes\mathcal E^\vee$ annihilates $g$, by the dual and tensor formulas of @cons:calculus-induced-connections.
:::

:::{.theorem #thm:calculus-levi-civita title="Levi-Civita connection"}
A perfect symmetric pairing $g$ on $\mathcal T$ determines a unique torsion-free connection compatible with $g$. In the smooth real case this is the Levi-Civita connection [@Huy05, Theorem 4.A.3; @Ram04, Chapter 7, §2]. Its defining formula is

\begin{align*}
2g(\nabla_u v,w)
&=u(g(v,w))+v(g(w,u))-w(g(u,v))\\
&\quad-g(u,[v,w])+g(v,[w,u])+g(w,[u,v]).
\end{align*}
:::

:::{.proof title="Koszul formula"}
**Assume:** $g^\flat$ is an isomorphism and $2$ is invertible. **Prove:** existence and uniqueness.

1. Let $K(u,v,w)$ denote the right-hand side. Expanding brackets and derivatives gives

   \begin{align*}
   K(u,v,aw)&=aK(u,v,w),\\
   K(au,v,w)&=aK(u,v,w),\\
   K(u,av,w)&=aK(u,v,w)+2u(a)g(v,w).
   \end{align*}

   Thus $K(u,v,-)/2$ is a section of $\mathcal T^\vee$. Apply $(g^\flat)^{-1}$ to define $\nabla_u v$. The other two identities are its connection axioms.
2. Subtracting $K(v,u,w)$ from $K(u,v,w)$ yields $2g([u,v],w)$. Adding $K(u,w,v)$ to $K(u,v,w)$ yields $2u(g(v,w))$. Perfectness now gives torsion-freeness and compatibility.
3. Conversely, write compatibility for the triples $(u,v,w)$, $(v,w,u)$, and $(w,u,v)$. Add the first two equations and subtract the third; replacing differences of covariant derivatives by brackets gives the Koszul formula. Perfectness makes its solution unique. The local solutions therefore agree on overlaps. $\square$
:::

### Parallel transport and Riemannian curvature

Here $M$ is a smooth manifold and $E$ is a finite-rank smooth vector bundle with sheaf of sections $\mathcal E$ and connection $\nabla$.

:::{.construction #cons:calculus-transport title="Parallel transport"}
For a piecewise smooth path $\gamma:[a,b]\to M$, a section $s$ of $\gamma^*E$ is parallel if $(\gamma^*\nabla)_{\partial_t}s=0$. In a frame this is the linear ordinary differential equation $u'(t)+A(\dot\gamma(t))u(t)=0$. Existence and uniqueness for linear ordinary differential equations give an isomorphism $P_\gamma:E_{\gamma(a)}\to E_{\gamma(b)}$, sending the initial value to the final value [@Ram04, Chapter 5, §1]. Reversing the path gives its inverse; solving consecutively gives composition under concatenation.
:::

:::{.theorem #thm:calculus-flat-local-system title="Flat connections and local systems"}
For a smooth real or complex vector bundle, flatness of $\nabla$ is equivalent to the existence of local parallel frames. It is also equivalent to invariance of $P_\gamma$ under homotopies with fixed endpoints. In this case $\mathcal E^\nabla$ is a local system, and multiplication induces an isomorphism $\mathcal A_M\otimes_{\underline{k}}\mathcal E^\nabla\cong\mathcal E$, with $k=\mathbb R$ or $\mathbb C$ as appropriate [@Ram04, Chapter 5, §5].
:::

:::{.proof title="Horizontal integral manifolds"}
**Assume:** $E$ and $\nabla$ are smooth. **Prove:** the stated equivalences.

1. On the frame bundle, curvature is the vertical component, up to sign, of the bracket of horizontal lifts. Its vanishing makes the horizontal distribution involutive. Frobenius gives integral manifolds transverse to the fibers; their local sections provide parallel frames [@Ram04, Chapter 5, §5, flatness criterion]. Conversely, a parallel frame has connection matrix zero and hence curvature zero.
2. Transition matrices between parallel frames have derivative zero and are locally constant. Parallel transport in these frames is constant continuation. Subdividing a homotopy into frame neighborhoods proves homotopy invariance.
3. Conversely, on a sufficiently small coordinate ball, homotopy invariance makes transport from a fixed point independent of the path. Transporting a basis gives a parallel frame. In such a frame $\ker\nabla=\underline{k}^{r}$ and the asserted multiplication map is the identity on $\mathcal A_M^r$. These local descriptions prove the local-system and reconstruction statements. $\square$
:::

:::{.corollary #cor:calculus-monodromy title="Monodromy representation"}
Let $M$ be connected, let $x\in M$, and let $\nabla$ be flat. Parallel transport defines $\rho_x:\pi_1(M,x)\to\operatorname{GL}_k(E_x)$, $[\gamma]\mapsto P_\gamma$, when the product $[\gamma_2][\gamma_1]$ means traversing $\gamma_1$ first and then $\gamma_2$. Changing a basis of $E_x$ conjugates its matrix representation.
:::

:::{.proof title="Composition of parallel transports"}
Theorem @thm:calculus-flat-local-system makes $P_\gamma$ depend only on $[\gamma]$. Uniqueness of the parallel solution gives $P_{\gamma_2\gamma_1}=P_{\gamma_2}P_{\gamma_1}$ and sends the constant loop to the identity. Changing a basis conjugates each linear endomorphism by the same matrix. $\square$
:::

:::{.definition #def:calculus-geodesic title="Geodesic"}
For an affine connection on $M$, a smooth curve $\gamma$ is a *geodesic* if $(\gamma^*\nabla)_{\partial_t}\dot\gamma=0$.
:::

:::{.definition #def:calculus-sectional title="Sectional curvature"}
For a Riemannian metric $g$ with Levi-Civita connection and the curvature sign convention of @prop:calculus-curvature-tensor, the *sectional curvature* of the plane spanned by linearly independent $u,v\in T_xM$ is

\begin{align*}
K_x(\langle u,v\rangle)&=\frac{g(F_\nabla(u,v)v,u)}{g(u,u)g(v,v)-g(u,v)^2}.
\end{align*}

Alternation in $u,v$ and skew-adjointness of $F_\nabla(u,v)$ make both numerator and denominator transform by the square of the determinant under a change of basis of the plane. Skew-adjointness follows by applying the induced curvature to the parallel tensor $g$. Hence the quotient is well-defined. This convention makes the unit round sphere have curvature $+1$ [@Ram04, Chapter 7, §6, with the curvature sign fixed here].
:::

:::{.definition #def:calculus-constant-curvature title="Constant sectional curvature"}
A Riemannian manifold has *constant sectional curvature* $\kappa$ if $K_x(P)=\kappa$ for every point $x$ and every two-plane $P\subset T_xM$.
:::

:::{.theorem #thm:calculus-space-forms title="Complete constant-curvature manifolds"}
Let $(M,g)$ be connected, complete, and of dimension $n\geq2$, with constant sectional curvature $\kappa$. Its universal cover, with the pullback metric, is isometric to the following model [@Car92, Chapter 8, Theorem 4.1]:

| Curvature | Simply connected model |
| --- | --- |
| $\kappa=0$ | $\mathbb R^n$ with the Euclidean metric. |
| $\kappa>0$ | $S^n$ with metric $\kappa^{-1}g_{\mathrm{round}}$, equivalently the round sphere of radius $\kappa^{-1/2}$. |
| $\kappa<0$ | $\mathbb H^n$ with metric $(-\kappa)^{-1}g_{\mathrm{hyp}}$, where $g_{\mathrm{hyp}}$ has curvature $-1$. |

The original manifold is the quotient of its model by its deck transformations, acting freely and properly discontinuously by isometries [@Car92, Chapter 8, Proposition 4.3].
:::

:::{.proof title="Normalization and the complete classification"}
**Assume:** the hypotheses of the theorem. **Prove:** the models with the stated scale.

1. A constant rescaling $g'=cg$, $c>0$, has the same Levi-Civita connection by the Koszul formula. In @def:calculus-sectional the numerator scales by $c$ and the denominator by $c^2$, so $K_{g'}=c^{-1}K_g$.
2. For $\kappa\ne0$, take $c=|\kappa|$. The complete classification at curvature $+1$, $0$, and $-1$ is [@Car92, Chapter 8, Theorem 4.1], proved there using Cartan's local-isometry theorem and completeness. Applying it to the complete universal cover and undoing the rescaling gives the displayed models.
3. Deck transformations preserve the pulled-back metric. The quotient recovers $M$ and its metric by the covering construction [@Car92, Chapter 8, Proposition 4.3]. $\square$
:::

An open Euclidean ball has curvature zero but is incomplete, so its universal cover is that ball rather than all of Euclidean space. Completeness is essential to @thm:calculus-space-forms.

## Complex structures on modules {#sec-calculus-complex-modules}

Let $\mathcal A$ be a sheaf of commutative $\mathbb R$-algebras on a fixed space and put $\mathcal A_{\mathbb C}=\mathcal A\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C$. All constructions in this section take place on that same space.

:::{.definition #def:calculus-module-complex-structure title="Complex structure on a module"}
A *complex structure* on an $\mathcal A$-module $\mathcal E$ is an endomorphism $J\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal A}(\mathcal E)$ satisfying $J^2=-1$.
:::

:::{.proposition #prop:calculus-complex-scalar-action title="Complex scalar multiplication"}
A complex structure $J$ is equivalent to an $\mathcal A_{\mathbb C}$-module structure on the underlying $\mathcal A$-module $\mathcal E$, via $(a+ib)s=as+bJs$. Denote this module by $\mathcal E_J$. Morphisms are exactly the $\mathcal A$-linear maps commuting with $J$.
:::

:::{.proof title="The relation for multiplication by i"}
The presentation $\mathcal A_{\mathbb C}=\mathcal A[t]/(t^2+1)$ identifies an action with an endomorphism satisfying $J^2+1=0$. A module map respects the action precisely when it commutes with the image of $t$. $\square$
:::

:::{.definition #def:calculus-conjugate title="Conjugate module"}
For an $\mathcal A_{\mathbb C}$-module $\mathcal F$, its *conjugate module* $\overline{\mathcal F}$ has sections written $\bar s$ and scalar action $a\cdot\bar s=\overline{\bar a s}$. The map $s\mapsto\bar s$ is conjugate-linear; double conjugation gives a canonical linear isomorphism. In the description of @prop:calculus-complex-scalar-action, conjugation replaces $(\mathcal E,J)$ by $(\mathcal E,-J)$ [@Huy05, Remark 2.6.6].
:::

:::{.proposition #prop:calculus-eigensheaves title="Complexification and eigensheaves"}
For $(\mathcal E,J)$, set $\mathcal E_{\mathbb C}=\mathcal E\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C$ and extend $J$ complex-linearly. The endomorphisms

\begin{align*}
P^{1,0}&=\tfrac12(1-iJ),& P^{0,1}&=\tfrac12(1+iJ)
\end{align*}

give a decomposition $\mathcal E_{\mathbb C}=\mathcal E^{1,0}\oplus\mathcal E^{0,1}$ into the $i$- and $-i$-eigensheaves. The maps $s\mapsto P^{1,0}s$ and $s\mapsto P^{0,1}s$ identify these with $\mathcal E_J$ and $\mathcal E_{-J}$, respectively. Conjugation interchanges the two eigensheaves [@Huy05, §1.2 and Proposition 2.6.4].
:::

:::{.proof title="Complementary projectors"}
**Assume:** $J^2=-1$. **Prove:** the decomposition and identifications.

1. Multiplication gives $(P^{1,0})^2=P^{1,0}$, $(P^{0,1})^2=P^{0,1}$, $P^{1,0}P^{0,1}=0$, and $P^{1,0}+P^{0,1}=1$. Also $JP^{1,0}=iP^{1,0}$ and $JP^{0,1}=-iP^{0,1}$.
2. If $z=a+ib$ is an $i$-eigenvector, comparison of real and imaginary parts gives $b=-Ja$, so $z=P^{1,0}(2a)$. Moreover $P^{1,0}(Js)=iP^{1,0}s$, proving the first linear identification. For a $-i$-eigenvector the equations give $b=Ja$ and $P^{0,1}(-Js)=iP^{0,1}s$, proving the second.
3. Coefficientwise conjugation sends $P^{1,0}$ to $P^{0,1}$, so it interchanges their images. $\square$
:::

### Real structures and algebraic base change

:::{.definition #def:calculus-real-structure title="Real structure on a complex module"}
A *real structure* on $\mathcal F$ is a conjugate-linear involution $\rho:\mathcal F\to\mathcal F$. Write $\mathcal F^\rho=\ker(\rho-1)$ as a sheaf of $\mathcal A$-modules.
:::

:::{.proposition #prop:calculus-module-descent title="Reconstruction from real sections"}
Multiplication gives an isomorphism $\mathcal A_{\mathbb C}\otimes_{\mathcal A}\mathcal F^\rho\to\mathcal F$. Its inverse sends a local section $s$ to

\begin{align*}
1\otimes\frac{s+\rho(s)}2+i\otimes\frac{s-\rho(s)}{2i}.
\end{align*}
:::

:::{.proof title="Real and imaginary parts"}
Both displayed components are fixed by $\rho$, by conjugate-linearity and $\rho^2=1$. Multiplying them in $\mathcal F$ gives $s$. Conversely, the same real and imaginary part formulas recover a tensor $1\otimes a+i\otimes b$ with $a,b\in\mathcal F^\rho$. These assignments commute with restriction. $\square$
:::

For an algebraic $\mathbb R$-scheme $X$, the base change $p:X_{\mathbb C}\to X$ is instead defined by the Cartesian square

```{.tikz}
%%| filename: calculus-complex-base-change
%%| additionalPackages: \usepackage{amsmath,amssymb,tikz-cd}
\begin{tikzcd}
X_{\mathbb C} \arrow[r] \arrow[d,"p"'] \arrow[dr,phantom,"\lrcorner",very near start] & \operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C \arrow[d] \\
X \arrow[r] & \operatorname{Spec}\mathbb R.
\end{tikzcd}
```

Its conjugation $\sigma$ is an involution over $X$. Quasicoherent sheaves on $X$ correspond to quasicoherent sheaves $\mathcal F$ on $X_{\mathbb C}$ with descent isomorphism $\theta:\sigma^*\mathcal F\to\mathcal F$ satisfying $\theta\circ\sigma^*\theta=1$ [@stacks-023T]. Morphisms commute with $\theta$. This is descent along $p$. It is not reconstruction from a fixed-point subscheme: fixed complex-valued points recover $X(\mathbb R)$, while descent recovers the scheme and its sheaves. The fixed-section calculation in @prop:calculus-module-descent is the corresponding algebra calculation on affine opens.

:::{.definition #def:calculus-conjugate-scheme title="Conjugate scheme"}
For a complex scheme $Y\to\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C$, its *conjugate scheme* is

\begin{align*}
\overline Y&=Y\times_{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C,c}\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C,
\end{align*}

where $c$ is induced by complex conjugation, and the second projection is the complex structure morphism. This is the fiber-product construction of [@stacks-01JO]. For $Y=\operatorname{Spec}B$, its coordinate algebra is $B\otimes_{\mathbb C,c}\mathbb C$; for a module $N$, conjugate base change is $N\otimes_{\mathbb C,c}\mathbb C$. Double conjugation recovers $Y$ and $N$ canonically. A real structure supplies an identification between $Y$ and $\overline Y$ satisfying the involution condition; conjugate base change itself does not require a chosen real structure.
:::

:::{.remark #rmk:calculus-conjugate-descent title="Descent of a module and its conjugate"}
Given a quasicoherent module $\mathcal F$ on $X_{\mathbb C}$, set $\mathcal G=\mathcal F\oplus\sigma^*\mathcal F$. There is a descent isomorphism

\begin{align*}
\theta:\sigma^*\mathcal G
=\sigma^*\mathcal F\oplus\sigma^{*2}\mathcal F
&\longrightarrow\mathcal F\oplus\sigma^*\mathcal F,\\
(u,v)&\longmapsto(v,u),
\end{align*}

using the canonical identification $\sigma^{*2}\mathcal F\cong\mathcal F$. Swapping twice gives $\theta\circ\sigma^*\theta=1$. Thus $\mathcal G$ descends to a quasicoherent module $\mathcal F_{\mathbb R}$ on $X$, with $p^*\mathcal F_{\mathbb R}\cong\mathcal G$. On an affine open this is restriction of scalars along $A\to A\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C$, followed by the splitting after extension back to $\mathbb C$.
:::

## Almost complex structures and integrability {#sec-calculus-integrability}

Let $M$ be a smooth real manifold. Put $\mathcal B=C^\infty_M(\mathbb C)$, $\mathcal T_{\mathbb C}=\mathcal T_M\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C$, and $\mathcal A^p_{M,\mathbb C}=\mathcal A_M^p\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C$.

:::{.definition #def:calculus-almost-complex title="Almost complex structure"}
An *almost complex structure* on $M$ is an $\mathcal A_M$-linear endomorphism $J$ of $\mathcal T_M$ with $J^2=-1$ [@Huy05, Definition 2.6.1].
:::

:::{.construction #cons:calculus-types title="Forms of type (p,q)"}
Apply @prop:calculus-eigensheaves to $\mathcal T_M$ to obtain $\mathcal T^{1,0}\oplus\mathcal T^{0,1}$. Let $\mathcal A^{1,0}$ be the annihilator of $\mathcal T^{0,1}$ in $\mathcal A^1_{M,\mathbb C}$, and let $\mathcal A^{0,1}$ be the annihilator of $\mathcal T^{1,0}$. Set

\begin{align*}
\mathcal A^{p,q}&=\bigwedge^p_{\mathcal B}\mathcal A^{1,0}\otimes_{\mathcal B}\bigwedge^q_{\mathcal B}\mathcal A^{0,1},\\
\mathcal A^r_{M,\mathbb C}&=\bigoplus_{p+q=r}\mathcal A^{p,q}.
\end{align*}

The exterior-power decomposition follows by expanding wedges of the two summands. Define $\partial$ and $\bar\partial$ to be the $(p+1,q)$- and $(p,q+1)$-projections of $d$ on $\mathcal A^{p,q}$ [@Huy05, Definitions 2.6.7 and 2.6.9].
:::

:::{.remark #rmk:calculus-polyvector-types title="Types of multivector fields"}
Similarly, exterior powers of the complexified tangent sheaf split as

\begin{align*}
\bigwedge^r\mathcal T_{\mathbb C}
&=\bigoplus_{p+q=r}\mathcal T^{p,q},&
\mathcal T^{p,q}&=\bigwedge^p\mathcal T^{1,0}\otimes_{\mathcal B}\bigwedge^q\mathcal T^{0,1}.
\end{align*}

Conjugation interchanges $\mathcal T^{p,q}$ and $\mathcal T^{q,p}$, and likewise the form sheaves $\mathcal A^{p,q}$ and $\mathcal A^{q,p}$. These decompositions require only $J^2=-1$.
:::

:::{.definition #def:calculus-integrable title="Integrability"}
An almost complex structure is *integrable* if $[\mathcal T^{0,1},\mathcal T^{0,1}]\subseteq\mathcal T^{0,1}$. The bracket is that of smooth complex vector fields [@Huy05, Proposition 2.6.17].
:::

:::{.proposition #prop:calculus-integrability-differential title="Integrability and the exterior differential"}
Integrability is equivalent to $d=\partial+\bar\partial$ on all complex differential forms. Under these conditions $\partial^2=\bar\partial^2=0$ and $\partial\bar\partial+\bar\partial\partial=0$ [@Huy05, Propositions 2.6.15–2.6.17 and Corollary 2.6.18].
:::

:::{.proof title="The annihilator of the distribution"}
**Assume:** $J$ is almost complex. **Prove:** the equivalence and the identities.

1. For $\alpha\in\mathcal A^{1,0}$ and $v,w\in\mathcal T^{0,1}$, the exterior derivative formula gives $d\alpha(v,w)=-\alpha([v,w])$. Thus bracket closure is equivalent to the vanishing of the $(0,2)$-component of $d\alpha$ for all such $\alpha$.
2. Complex conjugation gives the corresponding vanishing of the $(2,0)$-component on $\mathcal A^{0,1}$. Functions and one-forms generate the exterior algebra locally, so the graded Leibniz rule gives $d=\partial+\bar\partial$ in every degree. The converse follows by applying this equality to one-forms.
3. Separate the distinct bidegrees in $0=d^2=\partial^2+\partial\bar\partial+\bar\partial\partial+\bar\partial^2$. Each component vanishes. $\square$
:::

:::{.remark #rmk:calculus-nonintegrable-types title="The differential before integrability"}
For an arbitrary almost complex structure, $d$ can also have components of bidegrees $(2,-1)$ and $(-1,2)$. On functions these components vanish, so $df=\partial f+\bar\partial f$ always holds. On one-forms they are exactly the components tested in the proof of @prop:calculus-integrability-differential. It is therefore essential to impose integrability before using a Dolbeault complex.
:::

:::{.theorem #thm:calculus-newlander-nirenberg title="Newlander–Nirenberg"}
An integrable smooth almost complex structure is induced by a complex atlas [@Huy05, Theorem 2.6.19]. This is the analytic existence theorem used here.
:::

:::{.construction #cons:calculus-holomorphic-sheaves title="Holomorphic functions and forms"}
For an integrable $J$, write $Z=(M,J)$ for the resulting complex manifold. Its sheaves of holomorphic functions and forms are

\begin{align*}
\mathcal O_Z&=\ker(\bar\partial:\mathcal B\to\mathcal A^{0,1}),\\
\Omega_Z^p&=\ker(\bar\partial:\mathcal A^{p,0}\to\mathcal A^{p,1}).
\end{align*}

Indeed, in complex coordinates $z^1,\ldots,z^n$, the equation $\bar\partial f=0$ is the Cauchy–Riemann equation, and $\bar\partial(\sum f_I\,dz^I)=0$ says that every coefficient $f_I$ is holomorphic [@Huy05, Proposition 2.6.11]. The smooth sheaf $\mathcal T^{1,0}$ is the extension of scalars of the holomorphic tangent sheaf $\mathcal T_Z$: $\mathcal B\otimes_{\mathcal O_Z}\mathcal T_Z\cong\mathcal T^{1,0}$. In coordinates, both sides have the frame $\partial/\partial z^j$ with smooth complex coefficients.
:::

:::{.definition #def:calculus-holomorphic-map title="Maps preserving complex structures"}
A smooth map $q:(M,J_M)\to(N,J_N)$ is *pseudoholomorphic* if $dq\circ J_M=(q^*J_N)\circ dq$. If the structures are integrable, this is the coordinate Cauchy–Riemann condition for a holomorphic map. Pullback then gives $q^{-1}\mathcal O_{(N,J_N)}\to\mathcal O_{(M,J_M)}$.
:::

### The algebraic analogue

On a smooth $\mathbb R$-scheme $X$, one can likewise specify $J\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal O_X}(\mathcal T_{X/\mathbb R})$ with $J^2=-1$. On $X_{\mathbb C}$ its pullback has the two eigensubbundles of @prop:calculus-eigensheaves, and bracket closure is an algebraic condition on these distributions. Smooth base change identifies the pulled-back relative tangent sheaf with $\mathcal T_{X_{\mathbb C}/\mathbb C}$ [@stacks-02G1; @stacks-01V0].

These data are an endomorphism and involutive distributions on an algebraic tangent sheaf. The Newlander–Nirenberg conclusion concerns smooth manifolds. In particular, replacing $\mathcal O_{X_{\mathbb C}}$ by a kernel of a projected derivation does not, by that theorem, produce an algebraic scheme. Algebraic base change and the recovery of holomorphic functions on a smooth manifold are different constructions.

## Holomorphic bundles and their connections {#sec-calculus-holomorphic-connections}

Let $Z$ be a complex manifold, $\mathcal B=C^\infty_Z(\mathbb C)$, and $\mathcal E$ a finite locally free $\mathcal B$-module. Write $\mathcal A^{p,q}(\mathcal E)=\mathcal A^{p,q}\otimes_{\mathcal B}\mathcal E$.

:::{.definition #def:calculus-dolbeault-operator title="Dolbeault operator"}
A *Dolbeault operator* on $\mathcal E$ is a $\mathbb C$-linear sheaf map $\bar\partial_{\mathcal E}:\mathcal E\to\mathcal A^{0,1}(\mathcal E)$ satisfying $\bar\partial_{\mathcal E}(as)=\bar\partial a\otimes s+a\bar\partial_{\mathcal E}s$. Extend it to forms by the graded Leibniz rule. It is integrable if $\bar\partial_{\mathcal E}^2=0$.
:::

:::{.theorem #thm:calculus-koszul-malgrange title="Holomorphic structures from Dolbeault operators"}
An integrable Dolbeault operator determines a holomorphic vector bundle with sheaf of holomorphic sections $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{hol}}=\ker\bar\partial_{\mathcal E}$. This sheaf is locally free over $\mathcal O_Z$, and multiplication identifies $\mathcal B\otimes_{\mathcal O_Z}\mathcal E^{\mathrm{hol}}$ with $\mathcal E$. Conversely, each holomorphic vector bundle determines such an operator [@Huy05, Theorem 2.6.26]. The analytic local existence of holomorphic frames is the content of the theorem.
:::

:::{.definition #def:calculus-compatible-holomorphic title="Compatibility with a holomorphic structure"}
A smooth complex connection $\nabla$ is *compatible with the holomorphic structure* if its $(0,1)$-part is $\bar\partial_{\mathcal E}$ [@Huy05, Definition 4.2.12].
:::

:::{.definition #def:calculus-holomorphic-connection title="Holomorphic connection"}
A *holomorphic connection* on $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{hol}}$ is a $\mathbb C$-linear sheaf map $D:\mathcal E^{\mathrm{hol}}\to\Omega_Z^1\otimes_{\mathcal O_Z}\mathcal E^{\mathrm{hol}}$ satisfying $D(as)=da\otimes s+aDs$ for holomorphic $a,s$ [@Huy05, Definition 4.2.17].
:::

:::{.proposition #prop:calculus-curvature-types title="Curvature and the Dolbeault operator"}
For a smooth complex connection, $(\nabla^{0,1})^2=F_\nabla^{0,2}$. Thus $F_\nabla^{0,2}=0$ makes $\nabla^{0,1}$ a holomorphic structure by @thm:calculus-koszul-malgrange. If $\nabla$ is compatible with a fixed holomorphic structure, its $(1,0)$-part restricts to a holomorphic connection exactly when $F_\nabla^{1,1}=0$.
:::

:::{.proof title="Calculation in a holomorphic frame"}
**Assume:** $Z$ is complex. **Prove:** the curvature criteria.

1. In the expansion of $d_\nabla^2$, the $(0,2)$-component on sections is $(\nabla^{0,1})^2$, since $d$ has only the two integrable bidegrees.
2. For a compatible connection, choose a local holomorphic frame. Its connection matrix $A$ has type $(1,0)$, so $F^{1,1}=\bar\partial A$ by @prop:calculus-local-curvature.
3. Its $(1,0)$-part preserves holomorphic sections exactly when the coefficients of $A$ are holomorphic, which is equivalent to $\bar\partial A=0$. $\square$
:::

:::{.remark #rmk:calculus-holomorphic-versus-smooth title="Extension and flatness"}
Locally $D=\partial+A$ with $A$ a holomorphic matrix of one-forms. Its smooth extension, together with $\bar\partial_{\mathcal E}$, gives $\nabla=D+\bar\partial_{\mathcal E}$. It has curvature of type $(2,0)$, namely $\partial A+A\wedge A$. Thus a holomorphic connection need not be flat. Conversely, a flat smooth complex connection induces both a holomorphic structure and a holomorphic connection, by @prop:calculus-curvature-types. A real connection on a real bundle with $J$ must preserve $J$ before this conclusion can be applied to the associated complex bundle.
:::

## Hermitian metrics and the Chern connection {#sec-calculus-hermitian}

:::{.definition #def:calculus-sesquilinear title="Sesquilinear pairings"}
For smooth complex bundles $\mathcal E,\mathcal F$, a *sesquilinear pairing* is a $\mathcal B$-linear map $b:\mathcal E\otimes_{\mathcal B}\overline{\mathcal F}\to\mathcal B$. We write $b(s,t)$ for $b(s,\bar t)$. Thus $b(as,ct)=a\bar c\,b(s,t)$.

Given complex connections on the bundles, the pairing is *parallel* if

\begin{align*}
db(s,t)&=b(\nabla^{\mathcal E}s,t)+b(s,\nabla^{\mathcal F}t).
\end{align*}

Here forms in the second argument are conjugated. If the base is a complex manifold, the two type components of this identity are

\begin{align*}
\partial b(s,t)&=b(\nabla^{\mathcal E,1,0}s,t)+b(s,\nabla^{\mathcal F,0,1}t),\\
\bar\partial b(s,t)&=b(\nabla^{\mathcal E,0,1}s,t)+b(s,\nabla^{\mathcal F,1,0}t).
\end{align*}

This says that the induced connection on $(\mathcal E\otimes\overline{\mathcal F})^\vee$ annihilates $b$. For flat bundles one also calls $b$ a *flat pairing*. Neither positivity nor nondegeneracy is required.
:::

:::{.definition #def:calculus-hermitian-metric title="Hermitian metric"}
A *Hermitian metric* on a smooth complex vector bundle is a $\mathcal B$-linear pairing $h:\mathcal E\otimes_{\mathcal B}\overline{\mathcal E}\to\mathcal B$ with $h(s,\bar t)=\overline{h(t,\bar s)}$ and positive-definite values on each fiber. We abbreviate it to $h(s,t)$; it is linear in the first argument and conjugate-linear in the second.
:::

:::{.remark #rmk:calculus-hermitian-adjoints title="Hermitian symmetry as an equality of sheaf maps"}
The pairing $h$ induces two adjoints

\begin{align*}
h_1:\mathcal E&\longrightarrow(\overline{\mathcal E})^\vee,&
h_2:\overline{\mathcal E}&\longrightarrow\mathcal E^\vee.
\end{align*}

Conjugating $h_2$ and using double conjugation and the natural identification $\overline{\mathcal E^\vee}\cong(\overline{\mathcal E})^\vee$ gives another map with the source and target of $h_1$. Explicitly,

\begin{align*}
(\overline{h_2}(s))(\bar t)&=\overline{h(t,\bar s)}.
\end{align*}

Thus Hermitian symmetry is exactly $h_1=\overline{h_2}$. In the algebraic base-change setting, replace conjugation of modules by $\sigma^*$ and use the canonical identifications $\sigma^{*2}\mathcal E\cong\mathcal E$ and $\sigma^*(\mathcal E^\vee)\cong(\sigma^*\mathcal E)^\vee$. The same equality expresses conjugate symmetry of a pairing $\mathcal E\otimes\sigma^*\mathcal E\to\mathcal O_{X_{\mathbb C}}$.
:::

:::{.definition #def:calculus-hermitian-connection title="Hermitian connection"}
A smooth complex connection is *Hermitian* if $dh(s,t)=h(\nabla s,t)+h(s,\nabla t)$. Forms in the second argument are conjugated: $h(s,\alpha\otimes t)=\bar\alpha h(s,t)$ [@Huy05, Definition 4.2.9].
:::

:::{.theorem #thm:calculus-chern title="Chern connection"}
For a holomorphic vector bundle with Hermitian metric $h$, there is a unique Hermitian connection $\nabla^{\mathrm{Ch}}$ whose $(0,1)$-part is $\bar\partial_{\mathcal E}$. Its curvature has type $(1,1)$ [@Huy05, Proposition 4.2.14 and §4.3].
:::

:::{.proof title="The metric matrix in a holomorphic frame"}
**Assume:** the frame convention of @prop:calculus-local-curvature and $H_{ij}=h(e_i,e_j)$. **Prove:** existence, uniqueness, and the curvature type.

1. Compatibility with $\bar\partial_{\mathcal E}$ forces $A$ to have type $(1,0)$. Metric compatibility is $dH=A^{\mathsf t}H+H\bar A$. Its $(1,0)$-part gives $A^{\mathsf t}=(\partial H)H^{-1}$, or $A=(H^{\mathsf t})^{-1}\partial H^{\mathsf t}$.
2. This formula defines a local connection satisfying both compatibility conditions: the $(0,1)$-part of the metric equation is the conjugate transpose of the $(1,0)$-part. Uniqueness makes these local connections agree on overlaps.
3. Put $K=H^{\mathsf t}$. Differentiating $K^{-1}K=1$ gives $\partial K^{-1}=-K^{-1}(\partial K)K^{-1}$, hence $\partial A+A\wedge A=0$. There is no $(0,2)$-component, and $F^{\mathrm{Ch}}=\bar\partial A$ has type $(1,1)$. $\square$
:::

:::{.example #ex:calculus-chern-line title="Chern curvature of a Hermitian line bundle"}
If $e$ is a holomorphic frame with $h(e,e)=e^{-\varphi}$ for a smooth real function $\varphi$, then $A=-\partial\varphi$ and $F^{\mathrm{Ch}}=\partial\bar\partial\varphi$. This follows directly from the proof of @thm:calculus-chern. The Chern connection is always compatible with the holomorphic structure; it is a holomorphic connection only if this curvature vanishes, by @prop:calculus-curvature-types.
:::

## Kähler geometry {#sec-calculus-kahler}

:::{.definition #def:calculus-almost-hermitian title="Almost Hermitian structure"}
An *almost Hermitian structure* on a smooth real manifold is a pair $(J,g)$ consisting of an almost complex structure and a Riemannian metric with $g(Ju,Jv)=g(u,v)$. It is Hermitian when $J$ is integrable [@Huy05, Definition 3.1.1].
:::

:::{.proposition #prop:calculus-compatible-metric title="Constructing a compatible metric"}
For an almost complex structure $J$ and any Riemannian metric $g_0$, the formula $g(u,v)=\tfrac12(g_0(u,v)+g_0(Ju,Jv))$ defines a $J$-compatible Riemannian metric [@Huy05, Exercise 3.1.1].
:::

:::{.proof title="Averaging"}
The pairing is smooth and symmetric, and $g(u,u)>0$ for nonzero $u$ because $g_0(u,u)>0$. Substitution of $J^2=-1$ gives $g(Ju,Jv)=g(u,v)$. $\square$
:::

:::{.construction #cons:calculus-fundamental-form title="Fundamental two-form"}
For an almost Hermitian structure define $\omega(u,v)=g(Ju,v)$ and $h(u,v)=g(u,v)-i\omega(u,v)$ on $(\mathcal T_M)_J$. Then

\begin{align*}
g(u,v)&=\omega(u,Jv),& g&=\operatorname{Re}h,& \omega&=-\operatorname{Im}h.
\end{align*}

The identity $g(Ju,v)=-g(u,Jv)$ proves that $\omega$ is alternating. Perfectness of $g$ and invertibility of $J$ prove that $\omega$ is nondegenerate. Also $h(Ju,v)=ih(u,v)$, $h(v,u)=\overline{h(u,v)}$, and $h(u,u)=g(u,u)>0$ for nonzero tangent vectors. Thus $h$ is a Hermitian metric with the convention of @def:calculus-hermitian-metric. Closure of $\omega$ is an additional differential condition [@Huy05, §1.2 and Definition 3.1.1].
:::

:::{.remark #rmk:calculus-hermitian-reconstruction title="Equivalent forms of a Hermitian metric"}
The same construction works for any real vector bundle with complex structure $J$. Conversely, a Hermitian metric $h$ on $\mathcal E_J$ gives the $J$-invariant positive symmetric pairing $g=\operatorname{Re}h$ and the alternating pairing $\omega=-\operatorname{Im}h$. Starting instead with an alternating pairing satisfying

\begin{align*}
\omega(Ju,Jv)&=\omega(u,v),&\omega(u,Ju)&>0\quad(u\ne0),
\end{align*}

we recover $g(u,v)=\omega(u,Jv)$ and $h=g-i\omega$. Indeed,
$\omega(v,Ju)=-\omega(Ju,v)=\omega(u,Jv)$ proves symmetry, and the displayed conditions prove positivity and $J$-invariance. These constructions are inverse. On the tangent bundle, $\omega$ is symplectic precisely when it is also closed; compatibility and positivity alone give an almost Hermitian structure.
:::

:::{.remark #rmk:calculus-hermitian-normalization title="Normalization on the i-eigensheaf"}
Let $g_{\mathbb C}$ be the complex-bilinear extension of $g$ and $\xi(u)=\tfrac12(u-iJu)$. Expanding gives $g_{\mathbb C}(\xi(u),\overline{\xi(v)})=\tfrac12h(u,v)$. Thus $h$ on $(\mathcal T_M)_J$ corresponds to twice this restricted pairing on $\mathcal T^{1,0}$. The factor does not change its Chern connection, because it is a positive constant [@Huy05, Appendix 4.A].
:::

:::{.definition #def:calculus-symplectic title="Symplectic form"}
A *symplectic form* is a closed two-form $\omega$ whose adjoint $\omega^\flat:\mathcal T\to\mathcal T^\vee$ is an isomorphism. This definition applies to smooth manifolds and to relative differential forms on smooth schemes.
:::

:::{.definition #def:calculus-almost-kahler title="Almost Kähler structure"}
An almost Hermitian structure is *almost Kähler* if its fundamental form is closed.
:::

:::{.definition #def:calculus-kahler title="Kähler structure"}
A *Kähler structure* is an integrable almost Hermitian structure with $d\omega=0$ [@Huy05, Definition 3.1.6].
:::

:::{.theorem #thm:calculus-kahler-equivalences title="Parallel complex structures"}
For an almost Hermitian manifold $(M,J,g)$ with Levi-Civita connection $\nabla$, the following are equivalent:

1. $(M,J,g)$ is Kähler.
2. $\nabla^{\operatorname{End}(\mathcal T_M)}J=0$.
3. $\nabla\omega=0$.

If these conditions hold, $\nabla$ induces the Chern connection on $\mathcal T^{1,0}$ [@Huy05, Propositions 4.A.7–4.A.9].
:::

:::{.proof title="Torsion, type, and the metric"}
**Assume:** $(M,J,g)$ is almost Hermitian. **Prove:** the equivalences and the Chern identification.

1. Metric compatibility gives $(\nabla_u\omega)(v,w)=g((\nabla_uJ)v,w)$. Perfectness of $g$ proves the equivalence of conditions 2 and 3.
2. Assume condition 2. Complexifying $\nabla$ preserves both eigensubbundles. If $v,w$ have type $(0,1)$, torsion-freeness gives $[v,w]=\nabla_vw-\nabla_wv$ of type $(0,1)$, so $J$ is integrable. Moreover a parallel two-form for a torsion-free connection is closed: expanding the exterior differential gives $d\omega(u,v,w)=\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\nabla_u\omega)(v,w)=0$. Thus condition 1 holds.
3. Assume condition 1 and choose holomorphic coordinates. Write $g_{j\bar k}=g_{\mathbb C}(\partial_{z^j},\partial_{\bar z^k})$. Closure of $\omega=i\sum g_{j\bar k}\,dz^j\wedge d\bar z^k$ gives $\partial_\ell g_{j\bar k}=\partial_j g_{\ell\bar k}$ and its conjugate. The Koszul formula then gives

   \begin{align*}
   g_{\mathbb C}(\nabla_{\partial_\ell}\partial_j,\partial_k)&=0,\\
   2g_{\mathbb C}(\nabla_{\partial_{\bar\ell}}\partial_j,\partial_{\bar k})
   &=\partial_{\bar\ell}g_{j\bar k}-\partial_{\bar k}g_{j\bar\ell}=0,\\
   2g_{\mathbb C}(\nabla_{\partial_{\bar\ell}}\partial_j,\partial_k)
   &=\partial_jg_{k\bar\ell}-\partial_kg_{j\bar\ell}=0.
   \end{align*}

   The pairing between the two tangent types is perfect. The first equation makes $\nabla_{\partial_\ell}\partial_j$ of type $(1,0)$; the last two give $\nabla_{\partial_{\bar\ell}}\partial_j=0$. Conjugating proves preservation of the other type. Hence condition 2 holds.
4. The last computation gives $\nabla^{0,1}=\bar\partial$ on $\mathcal T^{1,0}$. Preservation of $g$ and $J$ gives Hermitian compatibility. The uniqueness in @thm:calculus-chern identifies the induced connection. Steps 1–3 prove all equivalences. $\square$
:::

:::{.remark #rmk:calculus-parallel-J title="A parallel endomorphism on a general bundle"}
For a real bundle with connection and complex structure $J_{\mathcal E}$, the condition of preserving complex scalar multiplication is

\begin{align*}
\nabla^{\operatorname{End}(\mathcal E)}J_{\mathcal E}
&=\nabla\circ J_{\mathcal E}-(1\otimes J_{\mathcal E})\circ\nabla=0.
\end{align*}

The composite $\nabla\circ J_{\mathcal E}$ alone is not this induced covariant derivative. The Kähler criterion in @thm:calculus-kahler-equivalences uses the tangent bundle, the Levi-Civita connection, and its torsion-freeness. For a general bundle, the displayed equation means that its complex structure is parallel.
:::

:::{.example #ex:calculus-euclidean-kahler title="The standard affine example"}
On $\mathbb R^{2n}$, with coordinates $x^j,y^j$, set $J\partial_{x^j}=\partial_{y^j}$, $J\partial_{y^j}=-\partial_{x^j}$, and $g=\sum_j(dx^j\otimes dx^j+dy^j\otimes dy^j)$. Then $\omega=\sum_jdx^j\wedge dy^j$, the connection matrix in the coordinate frame is zero, and $\nabla J=0$. Theorem @thm:calculus-kahler-equivalences recovers the standard Kähler structure with coordinates $z^j=x^j+iy^j$. The same polynomial tensors exist on $\mathbb A^{2n}_{\mathbb R}$; their real-point realization is this manifold calculation.
:::

## Comparing the geometric settings {#sec-calculus-comparison}

| Construction | Algebraic setting | Smooth or complex-analytic setting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Differentials | $\Omega^1_{X/S}$ represents relative algebraic derivations. | Smooth or holomorphic differentials satisfy the corresponding chain rule. |
| Vector bundles | Finite locally free $\mathcal O_X$-modules. | Finite locally free modules over $C^\infty_M$ or $\mathcal O_Z$. |
| Connection | A relative sheaf map satisfying the Leibniz rule. | The same formula in the chosen smooth or holomorphic differential algebra. |
| Curvature | $F_\nabla=d_\nabla\nabla$ is $\mathcal O_X$-linear. | The same tensor also controls local parallel transport. |
| Complexification | Base change along $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C\to\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb R$. | Complexification of functions and bundles on a fixed manifold. |
| Integrability of $J$ | Bracket closure of the eigendistributions is an algebraic condition. | Newlander–Nirenberg yields complex charts and $\mathcal O_Z=\ker\bar\partial$. |
| Metric | A perfect symmetric pairing; positivity needs specified ordered fibers. | A Riemannian or Hermitian metric includes positivity on every real or complex tangent fiber. |
| Kähler condition | One can state the tensor equations for algebraic $J,g,\omega$. | The Kähler structure combines integrability, positivity, and $d\omega=0$. |

For a smooth separated finite-type $\mathbb R$-scheme, algebraic tangent tensors restrict to smooth tensors on its real-point manifold. Smooth local coordinates identify the algebraic and smooth tangent fibers there; the coordinate formulas for brackets and differentials agree. Thus an algebraic perfect symmetric pairing that is positive definite on real tangent fibers, together with a compatible algebraic $J$ satisfying $J^2=-1$ and bracket integrability, gives a Kähler structure on the real locus if its fundamental form is closed. This applies @thm:calculus-newlander-nirenberg and @def:calculus-kahler to the restricted tensors; it does not identify the algebraic scheme with that locus.

For a smooth complex algebraic variety, analytification gives a complex manifold with its canonical complex structure. An algebraic bundle or connection gives a holomorphic bundle or connection there. A Hermitian metric is additional smooth data. In particular, the existence of an algebraic or holomorphic connection and the existence of a Chern connection are different assertions.
