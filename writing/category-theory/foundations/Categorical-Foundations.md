# Categorical constructions {#sec-constructions}

::: {.definition #def:injective-map title="Injective maps and fibres" defines="injective map; injective; fibre of a map"}

Let $f\colon A\to B$ be a map of sets.
The *fibre* of $f$ over $b\in B$ is the preimage $f^{-1}(\{b\})$ [@DF04, §0.1].
The map $f$ is *injective* if $f(a_1)\neq f(a_2)$ whenever $a_1\neq a_2$ [@DF04, §0.1]; equivalently, every fibre of $f$ has at most one element.
The injective maps are exactly the monomorphisms of $\mathbf{Set}$ [@Rie16, Example 1.2.8].
:::

::: {.definition #def:codomain-of-a-map title="Domain, codomain and image of a map" defines="codomain of a map; codomain; domain of a map"}

A map $f\colon A\to B$ has a *domain* $A$ and a *codomain* $B$, both specified as part of the data of $f$ [@Rie16, Definition 1.1.1; @DF04, §0.1].
The *image* of $f$ is the subset $f(A)=\{b\in B\mid b=f(a)\text{ for some }a\in A\}$ of the codomain [@DF04, §0.1].
Whether $f$ is surjective depends on the codomain: $f$ is surjective when $f(A)=B$ [@DF04, §0.1].
:::

::: {.definition #def:equivalence-relation title="Equivalence relations" defines="equivalence relation; equivalence class; equivalence relation generated by a relation"}

A relation $\sim$ on a set $A$ is an *equivalence relation* if it is reflexive ($a\sim a$), symmetric ($a\sim b$ implies $b\sim a$) and transitive ($a\sim b$ and $b\sim c$ imply $a\sim c$) for all $a,b,c\in A$ [@DF04, §0.1].
The *equivalence class* of $a$ is $\{x\in A\mid x\sim a\}$, and the equivalence classes partition $A$ [@DF04, §0.1].
Reflexivity, symmetry and transitivity are each preserved by intersections of relations, so for any relation $R$ on $A$ the intersection of all equivalence relations on $A$ containing $R$ is an equivalence relation.
It is the *equivalence relation generated by* $R$.
:::

::: {.definition #def:closed-under-composition title="Subsets closed under composition" defines="subset closed under composition; closed under composition"}

Let $\mathcal C$ be a category (@def:subcategory).
A class $S$ of morphisms of $\mathcal C$ is *closed under composition* if $g\circ f\in S$ for every composable pair $f,g\in S$; this is the condition that a subcategory contain the composite of each pair of composable arrows [@Mac98, §I.3].
It is the instance, for the partial operation of composition, of the condition on a subset $H$ of a set with a binary operation $*$ that $a*b\in H$ for all $a,b\in H$ [@DF04, §1.1].
:::

::: {.definition #def:group-orbit title="Group actions, orbits and stabilizers" defines="group action; orbit of a group action; orbit; stabilizer"}

A *group action* of a group (@def:group) $G$ on a set $A$ is a map $G\times A\to A$, written $(g,a)\mapsto g\cdot a$, with $g_1\cdot(g_2\cdot a)=(g_1g_2)\cdot a$ and $1\cdot a=a$ for all $g_1,g_2\in G$ and $a\in A$ [@DF04, §1.7].
The *orbit* of $G$ containing $a$ is $\{g\cdot a\mid g\in G\}$; the orbits partition $A$ [@DF04, §1.7].
The *stabilizer* of $a$ in $G$ is the subgroup $\{g\in G\mid g\cdot a=a\}$ [@DF04, §1.7].
:::

::: {.definition #def:free-group-action title="Free actions" defines="free action of a group; free action"}

An action of $G$ on $A$ (@def:group-orbit) is *free* if every stabilizer is trivial [@Rie16, Example 2.3.6], that is, if $g\cdot a=a$ for some $a\in A$ implies $g=1$.
:::

::: {.definition #def:permutation-representation title="Permutation representations" defines="permutation representation of a group; permutation representation"}

The action of $G$ on $A$ (@def:group-orbit) defines a homomorphism from $G$ to the symmetric group (@def:group) $S_A$ of $A$, the *permutation representation* associated to the action.
Conversely, a homomorphism $\varphi\colon G\to S_A$ defines the action $g\cdot a=\varphi(g)(a)$, and the two constructions are inverse bijections between actions of $G$ on $A$ and homomorphisms $G\to S_A$ [@DF04, §1.7].
:::

::: {.definition #def:commutative-diagram title="Commutative diagrams" defines="commutative diagram; commutes"}

A diagram (@def:diagram) presented as a directed graph (@def:free-category) of morphisms in a category *commutes* if any two paths (@def:path-connectedness) of composable arrows in the graph with the same source (@def:globular-boundary) and the same target (@def:globular-boundary) have the same composite; the diagram is then a *commutative diagram* [@Rie16, §1.6].
:::

::: {.definition #def:diagonal-morphism title="The diagonal morphism" defines="diagonal morphism; diagonal map"}

Let $X$ be an object of a category in which the product $X\times X$ exists.
The *diagonal morphism* $\delta_X\colon X\to X\times X$ is defined by $p_1\delta_X=1_X=p_2\delta_X$ for the two projections $p_1,p_2$ [@Mac98, §III.5].
In $\mathbf{Set}$ it is the map $x\mapsto(x,x)$, and precomposition with $\delta_X$ restricts a morphism $X\times X\to Y$ to the diagonal.
:::

::: {.definition #def:terminal-category title="The terminal category" defines="terminal category"}

The *terminal category* $\mathbf 1$ has one object and only its identity morphism.
It is the terminal object (@def:initial-terminal) of $\mathbf{Cat}$ [@Rie16, Example 3.1.12].
:::

::: {.definition #def:graded-object title="Graded objects" defines="graded object; degree"}

Let $\mathcal A$ be an additive category (@def:preadditive).
A *graded object* of $\mathcal A$ is a family $A=(A^i)_{i\in\bZ}$ of objects of $\mathcal A$; the index (@def:index) $i$ is the *degree* of the component $A^i$.
A morphism $A\to B$ of graded objects is a family of morphisms $f^i\colon A^i\to B^i$ of $\mathcal A$ [@The25, Tag 0125].
:::

::: {.definition #def:stalk title="Stalks" defines="stalk of a sheaf; stalk"}

Let $X$ be a topological space, $x\in X$, and $\mathcal F$ a presheaf of sets on $X$.
The *stalk* of $\mathcal F$ at $x$ is
$$
\mathcal F_x=\operatorname{colim}_{x\in U}\mathcal F(U),
$$
where the colimit (@def:limit) is over the open neighbourhoods $U$ of $x$, partially ordered by reverse inclusion, with transition maps the restriction maps of $\mathcal F$ [@The25, Tag 0078].
A sheaf, and in particular a local system, has a stalk at each point by this definition.
:::

::: {.definition #def:algebraic-signature title="Signatures of one-sorted algebraic theories" defines="algebraic signature; signature of an algebraic theory; operation symbol"}

A *signature (@def:signature)* is a pair $\Omega=(|\Omega|,\operatorname{ari})$ where $|\Omega|$ is a set of *operation symbols* and $\operatorname{ari}$ assigns to each $\alpha\in|\Omega|$ a nonnegative integer, the arity of $\alpha$.
An $\Omega$-algebra is a set $|A|$ with, for each $\alpha\in|\Omega|$, an $\operatorname{ari}(\alpha)$-ary operation $\alpha_A$ on $|A|$ [@Ber15, §2.7].
The signature is *one-sorted* because all operations act on a single set.
:::

::: {.definition #def:groupoid-cardinality title="Groupoid cardinality" defines="essentially finite; groupoid cardinality"}

An ordinary groupoid $\mathcal G$ is **essentially finite** when $\pi_0(\mathcal G)$ is finite and $\Aut_{\mathcal G}(x)$ is finite for every $x\in\mathcal G$.
Its **groupoid cardinality** is
$$
\cardinality{\mathcal G}
\definedas
\sum_{[x]\in\pi_0(\mathcal G)}
\frac{1}{\cardinality{\Aut_{\mathcal G}(x)}}
\in\bQ_{\geq0}.
$$
:::

::: {.definition #def:category-of-elements title="Category of elements" defines="category of elements"}

Let $F\colon C^{\opop}\to\mathbf{Set}$ be a presheaf.
Its *category of elements* $\int_C F$ has objects $(c,x)$ with $x\in F(c)$.
A morphism $(c,x)\to(d,y)$ is a morphism $f\colon c\to d$ satisfying $F(f)(y)=x$.
The projection
$$
p_F\colon\int_C F\too C,\qquad(c,x)\longmapsto c,
$$
is a discrete fibration [@nlab:category_of_elements; @Rie16, §2.4].
For a covariant functor $C\to\mathbf{Set}$ the corresponding projection is a discrete opfibration.
:::

## Grothendieck construction

For a pseudofunctor $F\colon C^{\opop}\to\mathbf{Cat}$, the Grothendieck construction $\int_C F\to C$ is a fibration.
The category of elements is its $\mathbf{Set}$-valued special case [@nlab:grothendieck_construction].
A general forgetful functor need not arise from a category of elements.

::: {.definition #def:subcategory title="Subobjects, full and replete subcategories" defines="subobject; full subcategory; replete"}

A *subobject* of $Y\in\mcc$ is a monomorphism $m:X\to Y$ in $\mcc$; the datum is the arrow $m$, not merely the domain $X$ [@Lur18c, Tag 04VD].
Equivalently, $m$ is a subterminal object of the slice $\mcc_{/Y}$, and if the relevant pullback (@def:pullback-square) exists, $m$ is monic exactly when the diagonal $\Delta_m:X\to X\times_Y X$ is an equivalence (@def:equivalence-of-objects).

For ordinary $\mathbf{Cat}$, a functor $F:\mca\to\mathcal B$ is monic if and only if it is injective (@def:injective-map) on objects and faithful; hence subobjects in $\mathbf{Cat}$ are ordinary subcategories, up to isomorphism (@def:isomorphism-of-objects) over $\mathcal B$.
In $\operatorname{Cat}_{\infty,\infty}$, a monomorphism must also contain every equivalence between objects in its image, so not every ordinary subcategory remains a subobject after passing to $\operatorname{Cat}_{\infty,\infty}$.

A *full subcategory* $\mcd\iscontainedin\mcc$ is the special case where $m$ is fully faithful: a collection of objects of $\mcc$ with mapping spaces inherited from $\mcc$,
$$
\operatorname{Map}_{\mcd}(x,y)\simeq\operatorname{Map}_{\mcc}(x,y).
$$
It is *replete* if every object of $\mcc$ equivalent to an object of $\mcd$ also belongs to $\mcd$.
An isomorphism-invariant property (@def:property-structure-stuff) of objects of $\mcc$ defines a replete full subcategory (@def:property-structure-stuff) [@The25, Tag 001D; @nlab:replete_subcategory].
:::

::: {.definition #def:concrete-category title="Concrete categories" defines="concrete category; underlying set"}

A *concrete category* is a category $C$ equipped with a faithful functor $U\colon C\to\mathbf{Set}$.
The set $U(X)$ is the underlying set of $X$.
Any further underlying-set construction is stated as a factorization through this functor.
:::

For a concrete category, a morphism whose underlying function is injective is monic, and one whose underlying function is surjective is epic; each converse is a separate claim about $U$.
When $U$ is corepresented by an object $P$ as in @def:element-functor, $U(f)=\Hom_C(P,f)$ and $f$ is monic exactly when $U(f)$ is injective: one direction applies the definition of a monomorphism to $P$, the other uses faithfulness.
The corresponding claim for epimorphisms fails: the inclusion $\bZ\hookrightarrow\bQ$ is monic and epic in $\mathbf{Ring}$, and it is neither surjective nor invertible [@Rie16, Example 1.2.10].
Invertibility is asked of the morphism, by exhibiting a two-sided inverse in $C$.

## Comma categories and universal arrows {#sec-comma}

::: {.definition #def:comma-category title="Comma categories" defines="comma category"}

Let $T\colon\mathcal E\to\mcc$ and $S\colon\mcd\to\mcc$ be functors.
The *comma category* $(T\downarrow S)$ has as objects the triples $\langle e,d,f\rangle$ with $e\in\mathcal E$, $d\in\mcd$, and $f\colon Te\to Sd$ in $\mcc$, and as morphisms $\langle e,d,f\rangle\to\langle e',d',f'\rangle$ the pairs $\langle k\colon e\to e',\,h\colon d\to d'\rangle$ with
$$
f'\circ Tk=Sh\circ f.
$$
The two projections send $\langle e,d,f\rangle$ to $e$ and to $d$ [@Mac98, §II.6].

Taking $T$ or $S$ to be a functor $\mathbf 1\to\mcc$, that is an object of $\mcc$, gives the coslice $(b\downarrow S)$ and the slice $(T\downarrow a)$; taking both to be $\id_{\mcc}$ gives the arrow category $(\mcc\downarrow\mcc)$, whose objects are the morphisms of $\mcc$; taking both to be objects $a,b$ gives the discrete category $\Hom_{\mcc}(b,a)$.
:::

For a $1$-category $C$, the arrow category is also $[[1],C]$, and evaluation at the source and target gives a functor to $C\times C$.

::: {.definition #def:universal-arrow title="Universal arrows" defines="universal from $c$ to $S$; couniversal from $S$ to $c$"}

Let $S\colon\mcd\to\mcc$ and $c\in\mcc$.
A morphism $u\colon c\to Sr$ is *universal from $c$ to $S$* when, for every $d\in\mcd$ and every $f\colon c\to Sd$, there is a unique $g\colon r\to d$ with $Sg\circ u=f$.
Equivalently, $\langle r,u\rangle$ is an initial object (@def:initial-terminal) of the comma category (@def:comma-category) $(c\downarrow S)$, so it is unique up to a unique isomorphism (@def:isomorphism-of-objects) of $(c\downarrow S)$ and $r$ is unique up to isomorphism in $\mcd$ [@Mac98, §III.1].
Dually, a morphism $v\colon Sr\to c$ is couniversal when $\langle r,v\rangle$ is terminal (@def:terminal-coble-surface) in $(S\downarrow c)$.
:::

Free constructions are of this form: the insertion of a graph into the underlying graph of the free category on it is universal from the graph to the forgetful functor $U\colon\mathbf{Cat}\to\mathbf{Grph}$ (@def:free-category).
The corepresenting objects listed in @def:element-functor are the values of the corresponding free constructions on a one-element set, and the universal arrow is the choice of that element.

## Additive and abelian categories {#sec-abelian}

::: {.definition #def:preadditive title="Preadditive and additive categories" defines="preadditive category; zero morphisms; zero object; biproduct diagram; biproduct; $a\oplus b$; additive category"}

A *preadditive category*, or $\mathbf{Ab}$-category, is a category $\mca$ in which each $\Hom_{\mca}(b,c)$ is an abelian group (@def:group) and composition is bilinear.
Its zero elements are the *zero morphisms* $0\colon b\to c$, and a composite with a zero morphism is zero.

In a preadditive category an object $z$ is initial exactly when it is terminal (@def:terminal-coble-surface), exactly when $1_z=0$, and exactly when $\Hom_{\mca}(z,z)$ is the zero group; such a $z$ is a *zero object* [@Mac98, §VIII.2, Proposition 1].

A *biproduct diagram* for $a,b\in\mca$ consists of an object $c$ and morphisms
$$
i_1\colon a\to c,\quad i_2\colon b\to c,\quad p_1\colon c\to a,\quad p_2\colon c\to b
$$
satisfying
$$
p_1i_1=1_a,\qquad p_2i_2=1_b,\qquad i_1p_1+i_2p_2=1_c.
$$
Two objects of $\mca$ have a product exactly when they have a biproduct, exactly when they have a coproduct; the biproduct object with $p_1,p_2$ is their product and with $i_1,i_2$ their coproduct, and it is written $a\oplus b$ [@Mac98, §VIII.2, Theorem 2].

An *additive category* is a preadditive category with a zero object and a biproduct for each pair of objects [@Mac98, §VIII.2].
:::

::: {.definition #def:kernel-cokernel title="Kernels and cokernels" defines="kernel; cokernel"}

In a category with a zero object (@def:preadditive), the *kernel* of $f\colon a\to b$ is the equalizer of the pair $f,0\colon a\to b$: a morphism $k\colon s\to a$ with $fk=0$ through which every $h$ with $fh=0$ factors uniquely.
The *cokernel* is the coequalizer of the same pair [@Mac98, §VIII.1].
:::

::: {.definition #def:abelian-category title="Abelian categories" defines="abelian category"}

An *abelian category* is an additive category (@def:preadditive) in which every morphism has a kernel (@def:kernel-cokernel) and a cokernel (@def:kernel-cokernel) and, for every morphism $f$, the canonical map
$$
\operatorname{Coim}(f)\too\operatorname{Im}(f)
$$
is an isomorphism (@def:isomorphism-of-objects) [@The25, Tag 0109].
In an abelian category, a morphism is monic exactly when its kernel is zero and epic exactly when its cokernel is zero.

An equivalent axiomatization asks for a zero object (@def:preadditive), binary biproducts (@def:preadditive), kernels and cokernels of every morphism, and that every monomorphism be a kernel and every epimorphism a cokernel [@Mac98, §VIII.3].
Kernels and cokernels then supply the remaining finite limits and colimits (@def:limit): the equalizer of $f,g$ is the kernel of $f-g$, and biproducts are the finite products, so an abelian category is finitely complete (@def:complete) and finitely cocomplete (@def:complete) [@Mac98, §VIII.3].
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:canonical-factorization title="The canonical factorization"}

In an abelian category (@def:abelian-category) every morphism $f$ factors as $f=me$ with $m$ monic and $e$ epic, where
$$
m=\ker(\coker f),
\qquad
e=\coker(\ker f),
$$
and the factorization is functorial: a commutative square from $f$ to $f'$ induces a unique morphism between the two factorizations commuting with both halves.
The object $\operatorname{Im}(f)$ is the domain of $m$ and $\operatorname{Coim}(f)$ the codomain (@def:codomain-of-a-map) of $e$ [@Mac98, §VIII.3, Proposition 1].
:::

::: {.proof}

This is [@Mac98, §VIII.3, Proposition 1].
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:diagram-lemmas title="Diagram lemmas"}

Let $\mca$ be abelian.
For a morphism $\langle f,g,h\rangle$ of short exact sequences (@def:exact-complex): if $f$ and $h$ are monic then $g$ is monic, and if $f$ and $h$ are epic then $g$ is epic (short five lemma); there is a connecting morphism $\delta\colon\ker h\to\coker f$ making
$$
\ker f\to\ker g\to\ker h
\xrightarrow{\;\delta\;}
\coker f\to\coker g\to\coker h
$$
exact (@def:exact-complex) (snake lemma).
In a commutative (@def:operation-axioms) diagram with exact rows and five vertical morphisms, if the four outer ones are isomorphisms (@def:isomorphism-of-objects) then so is the middle one (five lemma) [@Mac98, §VIII.4, Lemmas 1, 4, and 5].
:::

::: {.proof}

This is [@Mac98, §VIII.4, Lemmas 1, 4 and 5].
:::

Exactness is a property of a composable pair: $a\xrightarrow{f}b\xrightarrow{g}c$ with $gf=0$ is exact at $b$ when the monomorphism $\operatorname{Im}(f)\to b$ and the kernel of $g$ represent the same subobject of $b$ (@def:subobject-relation).

## Endomorphisms and automorphisms {#sec-endomorphisms}

::: {.definition #def:endomorphism-monoid title="The endomorphism monoid and the automorphism group" defines="endomorphism monoid; automorphism group"}

For an object $X$ of a category $\mcc$, composition makes $\Hom_{\mcc}(X,X)$ a monoid (@def:semigroup-monoid) with unit $\id_X$.
Its group (@def:group) of invertible elements is $\Aut_{\mcc}(X)$, the automorphism group of $X$, which is $\operatorname{Isom}_{\mcc}(X,X)$ in the notation of @sec-equivalence-spaces.
When $\mcc$ is preadditive, $\Hom_{\mcc}(X,X)$ is a ring under composition and the group addition, and $\Aut_{\mcc}(X)$ is its group of units.
:::

The arity-$1$ term of the endomorphism operad of @sec-operations is this monoid; the higher arities record the operations $X^{\times n}\to X$ and are not composition.

## Pseudo-pullbacks {#sec-pullback-cat}

Given functors $F\colon A\to C$ and $G\colon B\to C$, their pseudo-pullback has objects $(a,b,\alpha)$ with $\alpha\colon F(a)\isoto G(b)$ and the evident compatible morphisms.
It is the 2-categorical pullback up to equivalence [@nlab:2-pullback].

If one leg is an isofibration, its strict pullback presents the pseudo-pullback up to equivalence.
A replete full inclusion is an isofibration [@nlab:isofibration].
Thus strict pullbacks suffice for replete full subcategories, while forgetful functors from chosen structures generally require pseudo-pullbacks.

## Connected components and fibers {#sec-pi0-fiber}

The functor $\pi_0\colon\mcs\to\mathbf{Set}$ sends a space to its set of components.
It does not preserve arbitrary homotopy pullbacks.
For a fiber sequence $F\to E\to B$, the associated exact sequence of pointed sets contains
$$
\pi_1(B,b)\too\pi_0(F)\too\pi_0(E)
\too\pi_0(B).
$$
Consequently, the fiber of $\pi_0(E)\to\pi_0(B)$ need not equal $\pi_0(F)$ [@May99, Ch. 9 §3, last display].
A set-valued invariant formed from this sequence must specify whether it uses the fiber after $\pi_0$ or the components of the homotopy fiber.

::: {.definition #def:generalized-element title="Generalized elements" defines="generalized element"}

A *generalized element* of $X\in C$ with domain $T$ is a morphism $T\to X$.
For a presheaf $F\colon C^{\opop}\to\mathbf{Set}$, an element $x\in F(T)$ is an object $(T,x)$ of $\int_C F$.
:::

::: {.definition #def:representable-presheaf title="Representable presheaves" defines="representation; universal element; representable"}

Let $C$ be locally small and let $F\colon C^{\opop}\to\mathbf{Set}$ be a presheaf.
A **representation** of $F$ is a pair $(X,u)$ with $X\in C$ and $u\in F(X)$ for which the maps
$$
\theta^u_T\colon\Hom_C(T,X)\too F(T),\qquad f\longmapsto F(f)(u)
$$
are bijections for every $T\in C$; equivalently, $\theta^u\colon\Hom_C(-,X)\isoto F$ is a natural isomorphism (@def:isomorphism-of-objects).
The element $u$ is the **universal element** of the representation.
The presheaf $F$ is **representable** if it admits a representation.
The dual convention applies to corepresentable covariant functors.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:yoneda title="The Yoneda lemma"}

Let $C$ be locally small, $F\colon C\to\mathbf{Set}$ a functor, and $c\in C$.
Evaluation (@def:dualizable-object) of a natural transformation at $\id_c$ is a bijection
$$
\operatorname{Nat}\bigl(\Hom_C(c,-),\,F\bigr)
\;\cong\;
F(c),
\qquad
\alpha\longmapsto\alpha_c(\id_c),
$$
natural in $c$ and in $F$ [@Rie16, Theorem 2.2.4; @Mac98, §III.2].
Consequently the Yoneda embeddings
$$
C\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Set}^{C^{\opop}},
\qquad
C^{\opop}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Set}^{C}
$$
are fully faithful: natural transformations between represented functors (@def:classifying-object) correspond to morphisms between the representing objects [@Rie16, Corollary 2.2.8].
:::

::: {.proof}

This is [@Rie16, Theorem 2.2.4; @Mac98, §III.2].
:::

::: {.corollary #cor:representations-unique title="Uniqueness of representations"}

Let $F\colon C^{\opop}\to\mathbf{Set}$ be a presheaf. For any two representations (@def:representable-presheaf) $(X,u)$ and $(Y,v)$ of $F$, there is a unique isomorphism (@def:isomorphism-of-objects) $\varphi\colon X\isoto Y$ satisfying $F(\varphi)(v)=u$.
:::

::: {.proof}
The natural isomorphism $(\theta^v)^{-1}\circ\theta^u\colon\Hom_C(-,X)\isoto\Hom_C(-,Y)$ corresponds by @thm:yoneda to a unique isomorphism $\varphi\colon X\isoto Y$.
Evaluating at $\id_X$ gives $F(\varphi)(v)=u$.
:::

::: {.definition #def:el-convention title="The $\int_C F$ convention" defines="$\int_C F$"}
[]{#sec-el}

For every presheaf $F\colon C^{\opop}\to\mathbf{Set}$, use the category of elements and discrete-fibration convention of @def:category-of-elements.
Thus the projection $\int_C F\to C$ is a discrete fibration; no additional opposite category is taken after forming $\int_C F$.
:::

::: {.definition #def:smooth-manifold title="Smooth manifolds as locally ringed spaces" defines="$\mathsf{LRS}_{\bR}$; standard local model; $M_n$; smooth atlas; smooth manifold"}

Let $\mathsf{LRS}_{\bR}$ denote the category of locally $\bR$-ringed spaces: topological spaces equipped with a sheaf of $\bR$-algebras whose stalks (@def:stalk) are local rings [@nlab:locally_ringed_space].
The standard local model of dimension $n$ is
$$
M_n := (\bR^n,\, C^\infty_{\bR^n}),
$$
the Euclidean space (@def:euclidean-structure) with its sheaf of smooth real-valued functions.

A *smooth atlas* for an object $X \in \mathsf{LRS}_{\bR}$ is a morphism $p\colon U \to X$ such that:

1. $U$ is isomorphic to a coproduct of local models: $U \cong \coprod_{i \in I} M_{n_i}$.

2. For every component inclusion $\iota_i\colon M_{n_i} \hookrightarrow U$, the composite $p \circ \iota_i\colon M_{n_i} \to X$ is an open immersion.

3. $p$ is an effective epimorphism: it is the coequalizer of its kernel (@def:kernel-cokernel) pair $$ U \times_X U \;\rightrightarrows\; U \;\xrightarrow{p}\; X.
$$

A *smooth manifold* is an object $X \in \mathsf{LRS}_{\bR}$ that admits a smooth atlas [@nlab:effective_epimorphism; @nlab:smooth_manifold].

The data encode the classical construction as follows.
The object $U$ is the disjoint union of charts.
The fibered product $U \times_X U$ represents the pairwise intersections of charts in $X$.
The two projections $\pi_1, \pi_2\colon U \times_X U \to U$ encode the transition functions.
The coequalizer condition forces $X$ to be the colimit obtained from charts glued along their transitions: the classical manifold, recovered without point-set intersections.
:::

::: {.definition #def:scheme-as-lrs title="Schemes as locally ringed spaces" defines="$\mathsf{LRS}$; $M_A$; Zariski atlas; scheme"}

The smooth manifold definition of @def:smooth-manifold transfers to algebraic geometry by substituting the ambient category and local models.

Let $\mathsf{LRS}$ denote the category of all locally ringed spaces.
The local models are affine schemes: for a commutative (@def:operation-axioms) ring $A$, write
$$
M_A := (\Spec(A),\, \mathcal{O}_{\Spec(A)}).
$$
A *Zariski atlas* for an object $X \in \mathsf{LRS}$ is a morphism $p\colon U \to X$ such that:

1. $U$ is isomorphic to a coproduct of affine schemes: $U \cong \coprod_{i \in I} M_{A_i}$.

2. For every component inclusion $\iota_i\colon M_{A_i} \hookrightarrow U$, the composite $p \circ \iota_i\colon M_{A_i} \to X$ is an open immersion.

3. $p$ is an effective epimorphism: it is the coequalizer of its kernel (@def:kernel-cokernel) pair $$ U \times_X U \;\rightrightarrows\; U \;\xrightarrow{p}\; X.
$$

A *scheme* is an object $X \in \mathsf{LRS}$ that admits a Zariski atlas.

The coequalizer is essential in algebraic geometry because the intersection of two affine open subschemes need not be affine.
The fibered product $U \times_X U$ encodes the scheme-theoretic intersections of the affine covers without requiring the intersections themselves to be local models.
:::

::: {.definition #def:grothendieck-topology-generalization title="Generalization by Grothendieck topology" defines="admissible morphism; $\mca$-atlas"}

The formulation of @def:smooth-manifold and @def:scheme-as-lrs admits a uniform generalization.
Fix a category $\mcc$ of locally ringed spaces, a class $\mcl$ of local models, and a class $\mca$ of admissible morphisms.
A *$\mca$-atlas* for $X \in \mcc$ is an effective epimorphism $p\colon \coprod_{i \in I} L_i \to X$ with each $L_i \in \mcl$ and each component an $\mca$-morphism.
Different choices of $\mca$ yield different Grothendieck topologies and geometric objects:

| Admissible morphisms $\mca$ | Topology | Geometric objects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open immersions | Zariski | Schemes |
| Étale morphisms | Étale | Algebraic spaces, Deligne–Mumford stacks |
| Faithfully flat morphisms of finite presentation | fppf | Artin stacks |
| Faithfully flat quasi-compact morphisms | fpqc | fpqc sheaves |

The smooth manifold case uses $\mcl = \{M_n\}_{n \geq 0}$ and $\mca =$ open immersions in $\mathsf{LRS}_{\bR}$.
:::
