# K3 covers

::: {.remark #rmk:k3-cover-invariants title="On relation to K3s"}

Let $\mcl \definedas \OO_S(-K_S) \in \Pic(S)$.
By [@DK25 Prop. 9.1.1], taking a section $s\in H^0(\mcl ^{\tensor_{\OO_S} 2})$ with $Z(s) = C$ yields a branched double cover $f: X\to S$ where $X$ is a smooth K3 surface.
Let $\sigma$ be the involution generating the deck transformations of this cover.
Then the fixed locus $\Fix(\sigma)$ is a union of $n$ smooth rational curves which are precisely the reduced preimages $(\inverseof{f}(C_i))_{\mathrm{red}}$, where $C_i$ are the irreducible components of $C$ in $S$.
By [@CDL25 Def. 5.4.3], the preimages $\inverseof{f}(C_i)$ are disjoint $(-2)$-curves.
Two lattices are at issue and the source separates them: the sublattice $M \definedas \Pic(X)_0$ generated by $f^*\Pic(S)$ and the classes of the $f^{-1}(C_i)$ is $2$-elementary [@CDL25 §5.4], and it is $M$ that the table of @sec-coble-lattice-table lists, with invariants
$$
(r,a,\delta)_1 = (10+n, 12-n, \delta)_1
.
$$
For a general Coble surface $\Pic(X)$ itself is $2$-elementary with these invariants, so the two agree generically, while a special surface can carry more classes.
The distinction matters wherever a statement is made about one of them: $M$ is defined by the cover and needs no genericity, whereas the identification $\Pic(X) = M$ is a statement about the general member.

Three lattices of the same rank $10+n$ are therefore in play, in a chain of inclusions,
$$
f^*\Pic(S) \;=\; \latI_{1, N}(2) \;\iscontainedin\; M \;=\; \Pic(X)_0 \;\iscontainedin\; \Pic(X),
$$
and each inclusion can be strict: the first because $M$ adds the classes of the $f^{-1}(C_i)$, the second because a special Coble surface may have Picard number above $10+n$.
Only the outer lattice is the one a lattice-polarized moduli problem sees; the table of @sec-coble-lattice-table lists the middle one, and every statement about it is a statement about the cover, valid without genericity.
By [@CDL25 Def. 5.4.3, Eqn. 5.3.1], the ramification divisor $R$ is explicitly of one of the following forms:

1. $R=\emptyset$ if $(r,a,\delta) = \EnriquesInvariants$,

2. $R$ is a sum of two elliptic curves if $(r,a,\delta) = (10, 8, 0)$,

3. $R$ is the sum of a single rational curve and $n-1$ other disjoint $(-2)$-curves otherwise.

The first two cases cannot occur for a cover of a Coble surface: both carry $r = 10$, whereas $M$ has $r = 10+n$ with $1\leq n\leq 10$, so the third case is the only one, and the ramification divisor is never empty.
The case distinction is quoted because it is the source's, taken from its statement for a general involution of a K3 surface, and reading the first two cases as available here would suggest that a Coble cover can be unramified.

The discriminant length of $M$ is derived rather than tabulated: with $k$ the number of boundary components, so that $k = n$,
$$
a(M) \;=\; a(\latI_{1, N}(2)) - 2(k-1) \;=\; 21 - N \;=\; 12 - n
\qquad (N = 9+n)
$$
[@CDL25 §5.4], which is where the second entry of the table comes from.
The source uses $k$ twice in this passage and not for the same thing: in the ramification description (5.3.1) it is the number of $(-2)$-curves, which is $n-1$, while in the formula above it is the number of components, which is $n$; the value $21-N$ fixes the second reading, and $21-N = 12-n$ only for $N = 9+n$.

For the parity invariant, $\delta = 1$ for every $n\neq 8$; at $n=8$ both values occur, [@CDL25] Table 5.1 carrying two rows there, $(18,4,0)$ with $M = \mathrm{E}_8\oplus \mathrm{D}_8\oplus \mathrm{U}(2)$ and $(18,4,1)$ with $M = \mathrm{E}_{10}\oplus \mathrm{D}_6\oplus \mathrm{A}_1^{\oplus 2}$.
So for $n\neq 8$ every Coble surface of K3 type has $\delta = 1$, and for $n=8$ the two families differ exactly in $\delta$.
Thus if $S$ is a terminal Coble surface of K3 type with $n=1$, the ramification locus of the K3 cover is a single smooth rational curve, and we obtain a lattice with invariants
$$
S_{\Co} \definedas (11, 11, 1)_1 \cong \generators{-2} \oplus E_{10}(2)
$$
with orthogonal complement
$$
T_{\Co} \definedas S_{\Co}^{\perp \lkt} = (11, 11, 1)_2 \cong \latI_{2, 9}(2) \cong \generators{2} \oplus E_{10}(2)
.
$$
Both displayed isomorphisms are checks rather than conventions: $\mathrm{I}_{2,9}(2)$ and $\generators{2}\oplus E_{10}(2)$ are both $2$-elementary of rank $11$, determinant $-2^{11}$, signature $(2,9)$ and $\delta = 1$ — the first because $\mathrm{I}_{2,9}$ is odd unimodular of rank $11$, the second because $\generators{2}$ contributes the odd type and the even unimodular summand of $E_{10}(2)$ does not — and an indefinite $2$-elementary lattice is determined by these invariants [@Nik80].
Similarly $S_{\Co} = \mathrm{E}_{10}(2)\oplus\generators{-2}$ has rank $11$, determinant $2^{11}$, signature $(1,10)$ and $\delta = 1$, which is row $n=1$ of the table of @sec-coble-lattice-table with $N = \mathrm{I}_{2,9}(2)$ as its complement.
The lattices $S_{\Co}$ and $T_{\Co}$ will be used to construct the Hodge-theoretic period domain for Coble surfaces, yielding a coarse space for the corresponding moduli space.
:::

::: {.proposition #prop:double-cover-is-k3 title="The branched double cover is a K3 surface"}

Let $S$ be a smooth rational surface, let $\mcl\definedas\OO_S(-K_S)$, and let $s\in H^0(S, \mcl^{\tensor_{\OO_S} 2})$ have smooth divisor $B\in\abs{-2K_S}$.
Let
$$
\pi\colon X \definedas \Spec_S\bigl(\OO_S\oplus\inverseof{\mcl}\bigr)\to S
$$
be the double cover determined by $s$, branched along $B$.
Then $X$ is a K3 surface, and for divisors $D, D'$ on $S$,
$$
\pi^{*}D\cdot\pi^{*}D' = 2\,(D\cdot D')
.
$$
:::

::: {.proof}

The canonical bundle formula for a cyclic double cover branched along $B\in\abs{\mcl^{\tensor_{\OO_S} 2}}$ gives
$$
K_X = \pi^{*}(K_S + \mcl) = \pi^{*}(K_S - K_S) = 0
,
$$
so $\omega_X\cong\OO_X$.
Pushing forward, $\pi_*\OO_X = \OO_S\oplus\inverseof{\mcl} = \OO_S\oplus\OO_S(K_S)$, and $\pi$ is finite, so
$$
H^i(X, \OO_X) = H^i(S, \OO_S)\oplus H^i(S, K_S)
\qquad\text{for all } i
.
$$
Since $S$ is rational, $h^1(\OO_S) = 0$, and Serre duality on the surface $S$ gives $h^1(K_S) = h^1(\OO_S) = 0$; hence $h^1(\OO_X) = 0$.
Again by Serre duality $\chi(K_S) = \chi(\OO_S) = 1$, so
$$
\chi(\OO_X) = \chi(\OO_S) + \chi(K_S) = 2
,
$$
and combining $\chi(\OO_X) = 1 - h^1(\OO_X) + h^2(\OO_X)$ with $h^1(\OO_X) = 0$ gives $p_g(X) = h^2(\OO_X) = 1$.
A smooth projective surface with $\omega_X\cong\OO_X$ and $h^1(\OO_X) = 0$ is a K3 surface, which is the first claim.

For the intersection numbers, $\pi$ is finite of degree $2$, so $\pi_*\pi^{*} = 2$ on divisor classes, and the projection formula gives
$$
\pi^{*}D\cdot\pi^{*}D' = D\cdot\pi_*\pi^{*}D' = D\cdot 2D' = 2(D\cdot D')
.
$$
:::

::: {.remark}

Applied to a Coble surface $S$ with $n = 1$, where $B = C$ is the smooth rational curve of $\abs{-2K_S}$ recorded in @lem:rational_sextic_ten_nodes and the surrounding discussion, @prop:double-cover-is-k3 is the assertion cited from [@DK25 Prop. 9.1.1] in @rmk:k3-cover-invariants, and its intersection-number statement is the twist by $2$ used in @prop:coble-invariant-lattice.
:::

::: {.proposition #prop:coble-invariant-lattice}

Let $f: X\to S$ be the K3 double cover of a terminal Coble surface of K3 type with $n = 1$, and let $\sigma$ be the covering involution.
Write $\Pic(S) = \generators{H, E_1, \ldots, E_{10}} \cong \latI_{1, 10}$, where $H$ is the pullback of a line and the $E_i$ are the exceptional curves of $S = \Bl_{p_1, \ldots, p_{10}} \PP^2$, so that $H^2 = 1$, $E_i^2 = -1$, and $H\cdot E_i = E_i\cdot E_j = 0$ for $i\neq j$.
Then $f^*: \Pic(S)\to \Pic(X)$ is injective, its image
$$
S_\Co \definedas f^*\Pic(S) = \generators{f^*H, f^*E_1, \ldots, f^*E_{10}}
$$
is the invariant lattice $H^2(X, \bZ)^\sigma$, and
$$
S_\Co \cong \generators{2}\oplus\generators{-2}^{10} = \latI_{1, 10}(2) = (11, 11, 1)_1
.
$$
:::

::: {.proof}

First, If $f^*D = 0$ then $2D = f_* f^* D = 0$, and $\Pic(S)\cong\latI_{1, 10}$ is torsion-free, so $D = 0$; thus $f^*$ is injective.
As $f$ has degree $2$, one has $f_* f^* = 2$, and the projection formula gives, for all $D, D'\in\Pic(S)$,
$$
\qty{f^*D \cdot f^*D'} = D \cdot f_* f^* D' = D\cdot 2D' = 2\qty(D\cdot D')
.
$$
Applied to the orthogonal basis $H, E_1, \ldots, E_{10}$, this shows that $f^*H, f^*E_1, \ldots, f^*E_{10}$ have Gram matrix $\diag(2, -2, \ldots, -2)$, so that $S_\Co \cong \generators{2}\oplus\generators{-2}^{10} = \latI_{1, 10}(2)$, of signature $(1, 10)$.
This recovers, by the projection formula, the twist-by-$2$ described geometrically in @rmk:k3-cover-twist.

Next, The lattice $S_\Co$ is $\sigma$-invariant, so $S_\Co\iscontainedin H^2(X, \bZ)^\sigma$.
The fixed locus $\Fix(\sigma) = R$ is a single smooth rational curve (the case $n = 1$), so $\chi(R) = 2$.
On the K3 surface $X$, both $H^0$ and $H^4$ are $\sigma$-invariant of rank $1$ and $H^1 = H^3 = 0$, so the topological Lefschetz fixed-point formula reads
$$
\chi(R) = \Sum_i (-1)^i \trace\qty(\sigma^* \mid H^i(X, \bZ)) = 2 + \trace\qty(\sigma^* \mid H^2(X, \bZ))
.
$$
Since $\chi(R) = 2$, we obtain $\trace(\sigma^* \mid H^2(X, \bZ)) = 0$.
As $H^2(X, \bZ)$ has rank $22$ and $\sigma^*$ is an involution, the invariant lattice has rank $\tfrac{1}{2}(22 + 0) = 11$ and the coinvariant lattice rank $\tfrac{1}{2}(22 - 0) = 11$, so that $11 + 11 = 22$; cf. @prop:involution_eigenspaces. Hence $S_\Co$, of rank $11$, is a finite-index sublattice of $H^2(X, \bZ)^\sigma$.

Finally, The lattice $S_\Co \cong \generators{2}\oplus\generators{-2}^{10}$ has
$$
\abs{\det S_\Co} = 2\cdot 2^{10} = 2^{11},
\qquad
A_{S_\Co} \cong (\bZ/2\bZ)^{11},
\qquad
q_{S_\Co} \cong \generators{\tfrac{1}{2}}\oplus\generators{-\tfrac{1}{2}}^{10}
,
$$
so $S_\Co$ is $2$-elementary of rank $r = 11$ and length $a = 11$.
Because $q_{S_\Co}$ takes the value $\tfrac{1}{2}\notin\bZ$, one has $\delta = 1$, giving $(r, a, \delta) = (11, 11, 1)$.
For a nonsymplectic involution whose fixed locus is a single rational curve, the fixed-locus formula assigns the invariant lattice these same invariants $(r, a, \delta) = (11, 11, 1)$ [@CDL25 Def. 5.4.3, Eqn. 5.3.1], so $\abs{\det H^2(X, \bZ)^\sigma} = 2^{11}$.
Since $S_\Co \iscontainedin H^2(X, \bZ)^\sigma$ have equal rank $11$ and equal absolute determinant $2^{11}$, the index $[H^2(X, \bZ)^\sigma : S_\Co]$ is $1$: the two coincide, and by @thm:invariants-coinvariants-integral the invariant lattice -- hence $S_\Co$ -- is primitive in $H^2(X, \bZ)$.
That the invariants $(11, 11, 1)_1$ determine the isometry class $\generators{2}\oplus\generators{-2}^{10}\cong\generators{-2}\oplus E_{10}(2)$ is Nikulin's classification of indefinite even $2$-elementary lattices [@Nik80].
This derivation makes explicit the invariants stated by citation in @rmk:k3-cover-invariants.
:::

::: {.remark #rmk:k3-cover-twist}

Following [@CD12], we note that this computation is a special case of a general construction.
Let $S$ be any basic rational surface and write $S$ as the blowup of $\PP^2$ at $N$ points $p_1,\cdots, p_N$ with $N\geq 9$.
It is a fact that $\Pic(S) \cong \latI_{1, N}$, since one can construct a **geometric basis** in the following way: let $e_0$ be the class of the total transform of a hyperplane class in $\PP^2$ and for $1\leq i\leq N$, let $e_i$ be the class of the total transform of the exceptional divisor over $p_i$.
Then $\Pic(S) = \generators{e_0,e_1,\cdots, e_N}$ and $\rho(S) = N+1$; one verifies that $e_0^2 = 1$, and for $i\geq 1$, that $e_i^2 = -1$.
Moreover $e_ie_j = 0$ for $i\neq j$, making this an orthogonal basis with respect to the intersection pairing, yielding $\latI_{1, N}$.
In the case of Coble surfaces, the effect of taking the K3 double cover is to twist this lattice by $2$, so that the pullback $f^*\Pic(S) \iscontainedin \Pic(X)$ is $\latI_{1, N}(2)$, generated by the preimages of the $e_i$.
That pullback is $\Pic(X)$ itself when $n=1$ and is a proper sublattice in general, the extra classes being those of the ramification curves $f^{-1}(C_i)$ [@CDL25 §5.4]; the generic $\Pic(X)$ is the lattice $M = \Pic(X)_0$ of @rmk:k3-cover-invariants.
We remark that
$$
K_S = -3e_0 + e_1 + \cdots + e_N
.
$$
:::
