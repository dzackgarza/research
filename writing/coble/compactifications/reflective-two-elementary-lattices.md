# Reflective two-elementary lattices {#sec-reflective-two-elementary}

::: {.notation title="Sign convention"}

Hyperbolic means signature $(1, r-1)$, root lattices are negative definite, and the roots of an even $2$-elementary lattice are the short roots $v^2 = -2$ and the long roots $v^2 = -4$ with $\div_L(v) = 2$ (@def:2elementary-roots).
:::

## Reflective and two-reflective lattices

::: {.definition #def:reflective-lattice title="Reflective and two-reflective"}

Let $S$ be an even hyperbolic lattice, and let $W_2(S)\leq W_r(S)\leq\Orth(S)$ be the subgroups generated by the reflections in the short roots, respectively in all roots, in the notation of @def:k-root.
Both are normal in $\Orth(S)$ and in its index-two subgroup $\Orth^+(S)$ preserving the positive cone [@Ale22, §2].
The lattice $S$ is **two-reflective** if $W_2(S)$ has finite index in $\Orth(S)$, and **reflective** if $W_r(S)$ has finite index in $\Orth(S)$.
Write $\Gamma_2$ and $\Gamma_r$ for the Coxeter--Vinberg diagrams (@def:coxeter-vinberg-diagram) of the two reflection groups, and $\Gamma_4\iscontainedin\Gamma_r$ for the subdiagram spanned by the long roots.
:::

::: {.corollary #cor:vinberg-terminates-reflective title="Termination of Vinberg's algorithm"}

Let $S$ be an even hyperbolic lattice.
Vinberg's algorithm (@thm:vinberg-algorithm) terminates on $S$ for the group $W_r(S)$ if and only if $S$ is reflective, and for the group $W_2(S)$ if and only if $S$ is two-reflective.
:::

::: {.proof}

By @thm:vinberg-algorithm, the algorithm for a reflection subgroup $\Gamma\iscontainedin\Orth(S)$ terminates if and only if $\Gamma$ has finite covolume in $\Orth(S)$, that is finite index.
For $\Gamma = W_r(S)$ and $\Gamma = W_2(S)$ these are the conditions of @def:reflective-lattice.
:::

::: {.proposition #prop:reflective-two-elementary-finite title="Finiteness of reflective two-elementary lattices"}

Let $S$ be an even hyperbolic $2$-elementary lattice with invariants $(r,a,\delta)$.
Then $a\leq r$, and if $S$ is reflective then $r\leq 20$ or $r = 22$.
In particular there are only finitely many reflective even hyperbolic $2$-elementary lattices.
:::

::: {.proof}

This is [@Ale22, §1], where the bound $r\leq 20$ or $r = 22$ is attributed to Esselmann.
Finiteness follows because an indefinite even $2$-elementary lattice is determined by its signature and $(r,a,\delta)$ (@thm:two-elementary-classification), and $\delta\in\theset{0,1}$.
:::

::: {.definition #def:coeven-coodd title="Coeven and coodd lattices"}

For a $2$-elementary lattice $S$, the **doubled dual** is $S^{\dagger}\definedas \dualof{S}(2)$, the metric dual of @def:metric-dual with its form scaled by $2$.
The lattice $S$ is **coeven** if $S^{\dagger}$ is even and **coodd** if $S^{\dagger}$ is odd; these are the cases $\delta = 0$ and $\delta = 1$ of the coparity $\delta$ of @def:p-elementary [@Ale22, §2].
:::

::: {.proposition #prop:coeven-direct-sum title="Coparity of a direct sum"}

An orthogonal direct sum $S_1\oplus S_2$ of $2$-elementary lattices is coeven if and only if $S_1$ and $S_2$ are coeven.
:::

::: {.proof}

The metric dual of an orthogonal direct sum is the orthogonal direct sum of the metric duals (@def:metric-dual), and scaling the form by $2$ commutes with the sum, so $(S_1\oplus S_2)^{\dagger} = S_1^{\dagger}\oplus S_2^{\dagger}$.
For $x = x_1 + x_2$ in an orthogonal direct sum, $x^2 = x_1^2 + x_2^2$, so the sum is even if and only if both summands are even.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:coeven-duality title="Duality for coeven lattices"}

If $S$ is even hyperbolic $2$-elementary and coeven with invariants $(r, a, 0)$, then $S^{\dagger} = \dualof{S}(2)$ is again even hyperbolic and coeven, with invariants $(r, r - a, 0)$.
The Coxeter diagrams $\Gamma_r(S)$ and $\Gamma_r(S^{\dagger})$ correspond to each other with the short and long roots interchanged, and there is a bijection between the maximal parabolic subdiagrams of the two.
:::

::: {.proof}

This is [@Ale22, §3, paragraph "Other coeven lattices"].
:::

## The decomposition of even hyperbolic two-elementary lattices

::: {.theorem #thm:two-elementary-hyperbolic-decomposition title="Elementary summands"}

Every even hyperbolic $2$-elementary lattice is an orthogonal direct sum of exactly one of the **elementary hyperbolic summands**
$$
\generators{2} = (1,1,1),
\qquad
U = (2,0,0),
\qquad
U(2) = (2,2,0)
,
$$
together with a direct sum of negative definite root lattices drawn from the list
$$
B_n(2) = (n, n, 1),
\quad
C_{4n} = (4n, 2, 0)\ (4n\geq 8),
\quad
C_{4n+2} = (4n+2, 2, 1)\ (4n+2\geq 6),
$$
$$
F_4 = (4, 2, 0),
\qquad
E_7 = (7,1,1),
\qquad
E_8 = (8,0,0),
\qquad
E_8(2) = (8,8,0)
,
$$
each listed with its invariants $(r, a, \delta)$.
Here the non-simply-laced names refer to the full reflection group of the summand; as $(-2)$-root lattices in the sense of @def:root-lattice one has
$$
B_n(2) = A_1^{\oplus n},
\qquad
C_n = D_n,
\qquad
F_4 = D_4
.
$$
:::

::: {.proof}

This is [@Ale22, §2].
:::

::: {.example #ex:coble-picard-decomposition title="The decomposition of $S_\Co$"}

The lattice of the row $n = 1$ of @tbl-coble-lattices is $S_\Co = E_{10}(2)\oplus A_1$.
As $E_{10} = U\oplus E_8$ (@def:enriques-lattice), its decomposition in @thm:two-elementary-hyperbolic-decomposition is
$$
S_\Co = U(2)\oplus\bigl(A_1\oplus E_8(2)\bigr)
,
$$
with hyperbolic summand $U(2)$ and negative definite part $A_1\oplus E_8(2) = B_1(2)\oplus E_8(2)$.
:::

## Main roots and diagrams built on a graph

::: {.definition #def:main-roots title="Main roots"}

Let $L$ be an even $2$-elementary lattice.
The **main roots** of $L$ are the short roots $\alpha$, $\alpha^2 = -2$, of divisibility $\div_L(\alpha) = 1$, that is those satisfying $\beta_L(\alpha, L) = \ZZ$.
:::

::: {.definition #def:edge-n-fold-graph title="The edge $n$-fold graph"}

Let $G$ be a graph and $n\geq 2$ an integer.
The **edge $n$-fold graph** $G^{(n)}$ is obtained from $G$ by subdividing each edge into $n$ edges, inserting $n-1$ new vertices on each.
Thus $G^{(n)}$ has $n\abs{E_G}$ edges and $\abs{V_G} + (n-1)\abs{E_G}$ vertices, where $E_G$ and $V_G$ are the edge and vertex sets of $G$.
The vertices of $G^{(2)}$ are in natural bijection with the vertices and edges of $G$ together.
:::

::: {.definition #def:built-on-top-of title="Built on top of a graph"}

A Coxeter--Vinberg diagram $\Gamma$ is **built on top of** a simple graph $H$ if $\Gamma$ contains a subdiagram of main roots isomorphic to $H$ and the remaining roots of $\Gamma$ are determined by the main roots by explicit rules.
For the lattices of @thm:coxeter-built-on-complete-graphs the rule is that a main root $\alpha$ and an additional root $\beta$ are joined exactly when $\alpha\cdot\beta = 2$.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:coxeter-built-on-complete-graphs title="Coxeter diagrams on the line $r + a = 22$"}

For $n = 3, 4, 5, 6$ the Coxeter diagram $\Gamma_r$ of the even $2$-elementary lattice $(10 + n,\, 12 - n,\, 1)$ is built on top of $K_n^{(2)}$, where $K_n$ is the complete graph on $n$ vertices.
The diagram of $(18, 4, 0)$ is built on top of $K_{4,4}^{(2)}$, the complete bipartite graph on $4 + 4$ vertices, and that of $(14, 8, 0)$ on top of $D_4^{(2)}$.
In every case
$$
\Aut\Gamma_r = \Aut G,
\qquad
\Orth^+(S) = \Aut G \ltimes W_r(S)
,
$$
where $G$ is the graph in question.
:::

::: {.proof}

This is [@Ale22 Thm. 1.4].
:::

::: {.corollary #cor:coble-table-complete-graphs title="Coble lattices built on complete graphs"}

For $n = 3, 4, 5, 6$, let $M$ be the lattice of the row $n$ of @tbl-coble-lattices.
Then $\Gamma_r(M)$ is built on top of $K_n^{(2)}$, and $\Orth^+(M) = S_n\ltimes W_r(M)$.
:::

::: {.proof}

By @prop:coble-table-invariants and @tbl-coble-lattices, $M$ has invariants $(10+n, 12-n, 1)$.
@thm:coxeter-built-on-complete-graphs then gives the diagram and $\Orth^+(M) = \Aut K_n\ltimes W_r(M)$, and $\Aut K_n = S_n$.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:nonreflective-r-plus-a-22 title="Non-reflective lattices on the line $r + a = 22$"}

The even $2$-elementary lattices $(17, 5, 1)$, $(18, 4, 1)$ and $(19, 3, 1)$ are not reflective.
Every other even $2$-elementary lattice on the line $r + a = 22$ is reflective.
:::

::: {.proof}

This is [@Ale22 Thm. 1.1].
:::

::: {.corollary #cor:coble-table-reflectivity title="Reflectivity of the Coble lattices"}

The lattices $(17,5,1)$, $(18,4,1)$ and $(19,3,1)$ of the rows $n = 7, 8, 9$ of @tbl-coble-lattices are not reflective, and Vinberg's algorithm for their groups $W_r$ does not terminate.
The lattices of the other eight rows are reflective.
:::

::: {.proof}

Every row of @tbl-coble-lattices has $r + a = (10+n) + (12-n) = 22$ by @prop:coble-table-invariants.
The first assertion is @thm:nonreflective-r-plus-a-22 together with @cor:vinberg-terminates-reflective, and the second is @thm:nonreflective-r-plus-a-22.
:::

## Maximal parabolic subdiagrams and cusps

::: {.notation #not:reflective-fundamental-polytope title="The fundamental polytope of $W_r$"}

Let $S$ be a reflective even hyperbolic $2$-elementary lattice, let $C_S^+$ be the component of its positive cone preserved by $\Orth^+(S)$, and let $P_r\iscontainedin\HH^{r-1}_S$ be a fundamental Coxeter polytope of $W_r(S)$, with walls indexed by the vertex set $I$ of $\Gamma_r$.
For $s\in I$ let $\alpha_s\in S$ be the root orthogonal to the wall $H_s$, with sign chosen so that $x\cdot\alpha_s\geq 0$ on $P_r$ [@Ale22, §2].
A root of $S$ is primitive: if $\alpha = m\beta$ with $m\geq 2$, then $\alpha^2\in\theset{-2,-4}$ forces $m = 2$ and $\beta^2 = -1$, which is impossible in an even lattice.
Write
$$
K \definedas \theset{\, x\in\overline{C_S^+} \mid x\cdot\alpha_s\geq 0 \text{ for all } s\in I \,}
$$
for the closed cone over $P_r$, and $\Sym(P_r)\leq\Orth^+(S)$ for the stabilizer of $P_r$.
:::

::: {.proposition #prop:reflective-semidirect title="The symmetry group of the fundamental polytope"}

In @not:reflective-fundamental-polytope:

1. $P_r$ has finite volume, and $\Orth^+(S) = \Sym(P_r)\ltimes W_r(S)$.

2. Each $h\in\Sym(P_r)$ satisfies $h\alpha_s = \alpha_{\bar h(s)}$ for a permutation $\bar h$ of $I$, and $h\mapsto\bar h$ is an injective homomorphism $\Sym(P_r)\to\Aut\Gamma_r$.
:::

::: {.proof}

(1) The group $\Orth^+(S)$ is a discrete group of motions of $\HH^{r-1}_S$ with a fundamental domain of finite volume [@Vin85, Introduction, 4].
The set of reflections in roots of $S$ is stable under conjugation by $\Orth^+(S)$, since $g s_\alpha g^{-1} = s_{g\alpha}$ and $g\alpha$ is a root.
So $\Orth^+(S) = W_r(S)\rtimes\Sym(P_r)$, and $P_r$ has finite volume because $W_r(S)$ has finite index in $\Orth^+(S)$ [@Vin83, §1.1].

(2) An element $h\in\Sym(P_r)$ permutes the walls of $P_r$, say $h(H_s) = H_{\bar h(s)}$.
Then $h\alpha_s$ is a root orthogonal to $H_{\bar h(s)}$ and nonnegative on $P_r$, and roots are primitive, so $h\alpha_s = \alpha_{\bar h(s)}$.
Hence $\bar h$ preserves the norms $\alpha_s^2$ and the products $\alpha_s\cdot\alpha_t$, which determine the colours and edges of $\Gamma_r$ [@Ale22, §2], so $\bar h\in\Aut\Gamma_r$.
Since $P_r$ has finite volume, it is nondegenerate, so no nonzero vector of $S_\RR$ is orthogonal to every $\alpha_s$ [@Vin85, Ch. I, §1.1]; that is, the $\alpha_s$ span $S_\RR$.
An $h$ with $\bar h = \id$ fixes every $\alpha_s$, so $h = \id$.
:::

::: {.lemma #lem:reflective-cone-fundamental-domain title="The cone over $P_r$ is a strict fundamental domain"}

In @not:reflective-fundamental-polytope, let $x\in K$ and $w\in W_r(S)$.
If $wx\in K$, then $wx = x$.
:::

::: {.proof}

The interior $K^\circ$ of $K$ is the cone over the interior of $P_r$, it is connected, and $K$ is its closure.
For $w\neq 1$ the polytopes $P_r$ and $wP_r$ have no common interior point [@Vin85, Introduction, 1].
Consequently, a reflection $r\neq 1$ in $W_r(S)$ fixes no interior point of $P_r$, since such a point would be a common interior point of $P_r$ and $rP_r$.

Choose $v_0\in K^\circ\intersect S$, which exists because $K^\circ$ is a nonempty open cone.
For $w\in W_r(S)$ put $m(w)\definedas v_0\cdot wv_0$.
This is an integer, and $m(w)\geq v_0^2 > 0$ by the reverse Cauchy--Schwarz inequality on $C_S^+$ (@def:hyperbolic-model).
We prove the claim by induction on $m(w)$.

If $w = 1$ there is nothing to prove.
Let $w\neq 1$.
Then $w(K^\circ)\not\subseteq K$, since otherwise $w$ maps the interior of $P_r$ into $P_r$, hence into its interior.
So there are $s\in I$ and $y\in w(K^\circ)$ with $y\cdot\alpha_s < 0$.
The function $y\mapsto y\cdot\alpha_s$ has no zero on the connected set $w(K^\circ)$: a zero would be an interior point of $P_r$ fixed by the reflection $w^{-1}s_{\alpha_s}w\neq 1$.
Hence $y\cdot\alpha_s < 0$ on $w(K^\circ)$, and $y\cdot\alpha_s\leq 0$ on its closure $wK$.
As $wx\in K\intersect wK$, this gives $wx\cdot\alpha_s = 0$, so $s_{\alpha_s}wx = wx$.

For $y = wv_0$ the reflection formula gives
$$
m(s_{\alpha_s}w) = v_0\cdot wv_0 - \frac{2\,(wv_0\cdot\alpha_s)(v_0\cdot\alpha_s)}{\alpha_s^2} < m(w)
,
$$
because $wv_0\cdot\alpha_s < 0$, $v_0\cdot\alpha_s > 0$ and $\alpha_s^2 < 0$.
By induction applied to $s_{\alpha_s}w$, the equality $(s_{\alpha_s}w)x = wx\in K$ gives $(s_{\alpha_s}w)x = x$, so $wx = x$.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:parabolic-isotropic-correspondence title="Maximal parabolic subdiagrams classify isotropic vectors"}

In @not:reflective-fundamental-polytope, let $\mathcal I$ be the set of primitive isotropic vectors of $S$ in $\overline{C_S^+}$.

1. Every $W_r(S)$-orbit in $\mathcal I$ meets $K$ in exactly one vector $\eta$, and $[\eta]$ is an ideal vertex of $P_r$.
   Sending the orbit to the set of walls of $P_r$ through $[\eta]$ is a bijection from $\mathcal I/W_r(S)$ onto the set of maximal parabolic subdiagrams of $\Gamma_r$.

2. This bijection is $\Sym(P_r)$-equivariant, so it induces a bijection from the $\Orth^+(S)$-orbits in $\mathcal I$ onto the maximal parabolic subdiagrams of $\Gamma_r$ modulo the image of $\Sym(P_r)$ in $\Aut\Gamma_r$.
   The $\Orth(S)$-orbits of primitive isotropic vectors of $S$ are in bijection with the $\Orth^+(S)$-orbits in $\mathcal I$.

3. If $\Aut\Gamma_r = 1$, the $\Orth^+(S)$-orbits in $\mathcal I$ correspond bijectively to the maximal parabolic subdiagrams of $\Gamma_r$.
   For the lattices of @thm:coxeter-built-on-complete-graphs they correspond bijectively to the maximal parabolic subdiagrams modulo $\Aut\Gamma_r$.

4. Let $\Gamma\iscontainedin\Gamma_r$ be a maximal parabolic subdiagram with vector $v\in\mathcal I$, and let $\Gamma'$ be a connected component of $\Gamma$ of type $\wA_n$, $\wD_n$ or $\wE_n$.
   Then all vertices of $\Gamma'$ are short roots or all are long roots, and the images in $v^{\perp}/v$ of the roots of $\Gamma'$ span a root system of type $A_n$, $D_n$, $E_n$ in the first case and of type $A_n(2)$, $D_n(2)$, $E_n(2)$ in the second [@Ale22, §2].
:::

::: {.proof}

(1) Let $\eta_0\in\mathcal I$, and let $v_0\in K^\circ\intersect S$ be as in the proof of @lem:reflective-cone-fundamental-domain.
For $w\in W_r(S)$ the number $(w\eta_0)\cdot v_0$ is a positive integer, since $w\eta_0$ is a nonzero vector of $\overline{C_S^+}$ and $v_0\in C_S^+$.
Choose $w$ with $(w\eta_0)\cdot v_0$ minimal, and put $\eta = w\eta_0$.
If $\eta\cdot\alpha_s < 0$ for some $s\in I$, then
$$
(s_{\alpha_s}\eta)\cdot v_0 = \eta\cdot v_0 - \frac{2\,(\eta\cdot\alpha_s)(v_0\cdot\alpha_s)}{\alpha_s^2} < \eta\cdot v_0
,
$$
against the minimality.
So $\eta\in K$, and it is the only vector of the orbit in $K$ by @lem:reflective-cone-fundamental-domain.

The point $[\eta]$ lies in $\overline{P_r}\intersect\del\HH^{r-1}_S$.
By @prop:reflective-semidirect(1) and @thm:coxeter-polytope-volume(1), $\overline{P_r}$ is the convex hull of its finitely many ordinary and ideal vertices.
In an affine chart $\overline{\HH^{r-1}_S}$ is a closed ellipsoid, which is strictly convex, so a point of $\del\HH^{r-1}_S$ in this convex hull is one of the vertices; hence $[\eta]$ is an ideal vertex.
By @prop:maximal-parabolic-ideal-vertices, sending an ideal vertex to its set of walls is a bijection onto the maximal parabolic subdiagrams, and each ideal vertex is $[\eta]$ for a unique $\eta\in\mathcal I$, which lies in $K$.
Composing with $\mathcal I/W_r(S)\to K\intersect\mathcal I$ gives the bijection of (1).

(2) An element $h\in\Sym(P_r)$ maps $K$ to $K$ and $\mathcal I$ to $\mathcal I$, so it maps the representative $\eta\in K$ of an orbit to the representative of the image orbit.
By @prop:reflective-semidirect(2), the walls through $[h\eta]$ are $H_{\bar h(s)}$ for the walls $H_s$ through $[\eta]$, so the bijection is $\Sym(P_r)$-equivariant.
By @prop:reflective-semidirect(1), $\Orth^+(S) = \Sym(P_r)\ltimes W_r(S)$, so the $\Orth^+(S)$-orbits in $\mathcal I$ are the $\Sym(P_r)$-orbits on $\mathcal I/W_r(S)$.
Finally $-1\in\Orth(S)$ exchanges the two components of the positive cone, so $\Orth(S) = \Orth^+(S)\times\theset{\pm 1}$; of each primitive isotropic pair $\pm v$ exactly one vector lies in $\mathcal I$, and two vectors of $\mathcal I$ are in one $\Orth(S)$-orbit if and only if they are in one $\Orth^+(S)$-orbit.

(3) If $\Aut\Gamma_r = 1$, then $\Sym(P_r) = 1$ by @prop:reflective-semidirect(2).
For the lattices of @thm:coxeter-built-on-complete-graphs, $\Orth^+(S) = \Aut G\ltimes W_r(S)$ and $\Aut\Gamma_r\cong\Aut G$ with $G$ finite, so by @prop:reflective-semidirect(1) $\Sym(P_r)\cong\Orth^+(S)/W_r(S)\cong\Aut G$ has the order of $\Aut\Gamma_r$; the injection of @prop:reflective-semidirect(2) is therefore a bijection.
Both assertions follow from (2).

(4) This is [@Ale22, §2].
:::

## Reflection groups and automorphisms of the K3 cover

::: {.theorem #thm:reflection-group-quotient title="The quotient $W_r/W_2$"}

For an even hyperbolic $2$-elementary lattice $S$ the quotient group $W_r(S)/W_2(S)$ is isomorphic to the Coxeter group $W(\Gamma_4)$ generated by the reflections in the long roots.
:::

::: {.proof}

This is [@Ale22 §4].
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:k3-automorphism-sequence title="The automorphism group of a two-elementary K3 surface"}

Let $S\neq\EnriquesInvariants$ be an even $2$-elementary hyperbolic lattice admitting a primitive embedding into $\lkt$, let $X$ be a K3 surface with $S_X = S$, let $\iota$ be the canonical nonsymplectic involution of $X$, let $Y = X/\iota$ with branch divisor $B$, and let $\Aut'(Y, B)$ be the subgroup of automorphisms of the pair $(Y, B)$ acting trivially on $\Pic Y$.
Then there is an exact sequence
$$
0 \to \generators{\iota}\times\Aut'(Y, B) \to \Aut(X) \to
\Sym(P_r) \ltimes W(\Gamma_4) \to 0
,
$$
where $\Gamma_4\iscontainedin\Gamma_r$ is the subdiagram of long roots and $\Sym(P_r)\leq\Orth^+(S)$ is the stabilizer of a fundamental polytope $P_r$ of $W_r(S)$.
In particular $\Aut(X)$ is finite if and only if $S_X$ is two-reflective.
:::

::: {.proof}

This is [@Ale22, Lem. 4.1], and the last assertion is [@Ale22, §1].
The proof of [@Ale22, Lem. 4.1] uses the Torelli theorem, by which $\Aut(X)\to\Orth^+(S)/W_2(S)$ has finite kernel and cokernel and the kernel consists of the automorphisms descending to $(Y, B)$; the surjectivity of $\Orth(H)\to\Orth(A_H, q_H)$ for indefinite $2$-elementary $H$ (@thm:two-elementary-surjectivity), which lifts an isometry of $S$ to one of $\lkt$; and @thm:reflection-group-quotient together with $\Orth^+(S) = \Sym(P_r)\ltimes W_r(S)$ [@Vin83, §1.1], which give $\Orth^+(S)/W_2(S)\cong\Sym(P_r)\ltimes W(\Gamma_4)$.
:::

::: {.corollary #cor:coble-picard-automorphisms title="Automorphisms of a K3 surface with Picard lattice $S_\Co$"}

The lattice $S_\Co = (11,11,1)$ is reflective and not two-reflective.
For a K3 surface $X$ with $S_X = S_\Co$, in the notation of @thm:k3-automorphism-sequence, there is an exact sequence
$$
0 \to \generators{\iota}\times\Aut'(Y, B) \to \Aut(X) \to W(T_{2,3,7}) \to 0
,
$$
and $\Aut(X)$ is infinite.
:::

::: {.proof}

$S_\Co$ is reflective by @thm:nonreflective-r-plus-a-22.
The lattice $S_\Co$ is not $\EnriquesInvariants$, and it embeds primitively in $\lkt$ with the orthogonal complement $N$ of @tbl-coble-lattices (@prop:coble-table-invariants), so @thm:k3-automorphism-sequence applies.
By @thm:long-root-subdiagrams, $\Aut\Gamma_r = 1$ and $\Gamma_4 = T_{2,3,7}$, so $\Sym(P_r) = 1$ by @prop:reflective-semidirect(2), which gives the exact sequence.
The connected diagram $T_{2,3,7}$ has a vertex of valence $3$ and is none of $D_n$, $E_6$, $E_7$, $E_8$, the diagrams of @thm:spherical-classification with such a vertex; so $W(T_{2,3,7})$ is infinite by @thm:coxeter-type-and-geometry (1), and so is $\Aut(X)$.
By @thm:reflection-group-quotient, $W_r(S_\Co)/W_2(S_\Co)\cong W(T_{2,3,7})$ is infinite, so $W_2(S_\Co)$ has infinite index in $\Orth(S_\Co)$.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:elliptic-fibrations-isotropic title="Elliptic fibrations and isotropic vectors"}

Let $X$ be a complex projective K3 surface with Picard lattice $S_X$.

1. Sending an elliptic fibration $\pi\colon X\to\PP^1$ to the class of its fibre is a bijection from the elliptic fibrations of $X$, taken up to automorphisms of $\PP^1$, onto the nonzero primitive isotropic classes $v\in S_X$ that are nef.

2. For every nonzero isotropic $v\in S_X$, one of $\pm v$ is mapped to a nef class by a product of reflections in classes of $(-2)$-curves.
:::

::: {.proof}

(1) Let $F$ be the class of a fibre of $\pi$.
The fibres are the members of the base-point-free pencil $\pi^*\abs{\OO_{\PP^1}(1)}$, so $F^2 = 0$ and $F\cdot C\geq 0$ for every curve $C$, that is, $F$ is nef.
A smooth fibre is an integral curve of arithmetic genus one [@Huy16, Def. 11.1.1], so $F$ is primitive and $h^0(X,\OO(F)) = 2$ [@Huy16, Rem. 2.3.13(i)]; hence $\abs{F}$ is the pencil of fibres, and $\pi$ is the morphism defined by $\abs{F}$, up to an automorphism of $\PP^1$, so the map is injective.
Conversely let $v\neq 0$ be primitive, isotropic and nef, and let $L$ be the line bundle of class $v$.
By [@Huy16, Prop. 2.3.10], $L$ is base point free and $L\cong\OO(mE)$ for a smooth fibre $E$ of an elliptic fibration $X\to\PP^1$ and some $m > 0$.
Since $v$ is primitive, $m = 1$, so $v$ is the fibre class of that fibration and the map is surjective.

(2) Replacing $v$ by $-v$ we may take $v$ in the closure of the positive cone; then a finite product of reflections $s_{[C]}$ in $(-2)$-curves $C$ maps $v$ to a nef class [@Huy16, Rem. 8.2.13].
:::

## The ample cone

::: {.definition #def:ample-cone title="Ample cone"}

Let $X$ be a projective K3 surface with Picard lattice $S_X$.
The **ample cone** $A(X)\iscontainedin S_X\tensor_\ZZ\RR$ is the set of ample classes.
It is one connected component of
$$
\theset{ x\in S_X\tensor_\ZZ\RR \;\middle|\; x^2 > 0,\ x\cdot d \neq 0 \text{ for every short root } d \text{ of } S_X }
,
$$
that is, one chamber of the complement of the mirrors of the roots inside the positive cone, in the sense of @def:weyl-chamber.
:::
