# Reflective two-elementary lattices {#sec-reflective-two-elementary}

::: {.notation title="Sign convention"}

Hyperbolic means signature $(1, r-1)$, root lattices are negative definite, and the roots of an even $2$-elementary lattice are the short roots $v^2 = -2$ and the long roots $v^2 = -4$ with $\div_L(v) = 2$ (@def:2elementary-roots).
:::

## Reflective and two-reflective lattices

::: {.definition #def:reflective-lattice title="Reflective and two-reflective"}

Let $S$ be an even hyperbolic lattice, and let $W_2(S)\leq W_r(S)\leq\Orth(S)$ be the subgroups generated by the reflections in the short roots, respectively in all roots, in the notation of @def:k-root.
Both are normal in $\Orth(S)$ and in its index-two subgroup $\Orth^+(S)$ preserving the positive cone [@Ale22, §2].
The lattice $S$ is **two-reflective** if $W_2(S)$ has finite index in $\Orth(S)$, and **reflective** if $W_r(S)$ has finite index in $\Orth(S)$.
Write $\Gamma_2$ and $\Gamma_r$ for the Coxeter--Vinberg diagrams (@def:coxeter-vinberg-diagram) of the two reflection groups, and $\Gamma_4\iscontainedin\Gamma_r$ for the subdiagram spanned by the long roots.
:::

::: {.corollary #cor:vinberg-terminates-reflective title="Termination of Vinberg's algorithm"}

Let $S$ be an even hyperbolic lattice.
Vinberg's algorithm (@thm:vinberg-algorithm) terminates on $S$ for the group $W_r(S)$ if and only if $S$ is reflective, and for the group $W_2(S)$ if and only if $S$ is two-reflective.
:::

::: {.proof}

By @thm:vinberg-algorithm, the algorithm for a reflection subgroup $\Gamma\iscontainedin\Orth(S)$ terminates if and only if $\Gamma$ has finite covolume in $\Orth(S)$, that is finite index.
For $\Gamma = W_r(S)$ and $\Gamma = W_2(S)$ these are the conditions of @def:reflective-lattice.
:::

::: {.proposition #prop:reflective-two-elementary-finite title="Finiteness of reflective two-elementary lattices"}

Let $S$ be an even hyperbolic $2$-elementary lattice with invariants $(r,a,\delta)$.
Then $a\leq r$, and if $S$ is reflective then $r\leq 20$ or $r = 22$.
In particular there are only finitely many reflective even hyperbolic $2$-elementary lattices.
:::

::: {.proof}

This is [@Ale22, §1], where the bound $r\leq 20$ or $r = 22$ is attributed to Esselmann.
Finiteness follows because an indefinite even $2$-elementary lattice is determined by its signature and $(r,a,\delta)$ (@thm:two-elementary-classification), and $\delta\in\theset{0,1}$.
:::

::: {.definition #def:coeven-coodd title="Coeven and coodd lattices"}

For a $2$-elementary lattice $S$, the **doubled dual** is $S^{\dagger}\definedas \dualof{S}(2)$, the metric dual of @def:metric-dual with its form scaled by $2$.
The lattice $S$ is **coeven** if $S^{\dagger}$ is even and **coodd** if $S^{\dagger}$ is odd; these are the cases $\delta = 0$ and $\delta = 1$ of the coparity $\delta$ of @def:p-elementary [@Ale22, §2].
:::

::: {.proposition #prop:coeven-direct-sum title="Coparity of a direct sum"}

An orthogonal direct sum $S_1\oplus S_2$ of $2$-elementary lattices is coeven if and only if $S_1$ and $S_2$ are coeven.
:::

::: {.proof}

The metric dual of an orthogonal direct sum is the orthogonal direct sum of the metric duals (@def:metric-dual), and scaling the form by $2$ commutes with the sum, so $(S_1\oplus S_2)^{\dagger} = S_1^{\dagger}\oplus S_2^{\dagger}$.
For $x = x_1 + x_2$ in an orthogonal direct sum, $x^2 = x_1^2 + x_2^2$, so the sum is even if and only if both summands are even.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:coeven-duality title="Duality for coeven lattices"}

If $S$ is even hyperbolic $2$-elementary and coeven with invariants $(r, a, 0)$, then $S^{\dagger} = \dualof{S}(2)$ is again even hyperbolic and coeven, with invariants $(r, r - a, 0)$.
The Coxeter diagrams $\Gamma_r(S)$ and $\Gamma_r(S^{\dagger})$ are dual, in that the short and long roots are interchanged, and this duality induces a bijection between the maximal parabolic subdiagrams of the two, interchanging the odd and even ordinary types of @thm:isotropic-trichotomy.
:::

::: {.proof}

This is [@Ale22 §3].
:::

## The decomposition of even hyperbolic two-elementary lattices

::: {.theorem #thm:two-elementary-hyperbolic-decomposition title="Elementary summands"}

Every even hyperbolic $2$-elementary lattice is an orthogonal direct sum of exactly one of the **elementary hyperbolic summands**
$$
\generators{2} = (1,1,1),
\qquad
U = (2,0,0),
\qquad
U(2) = (2,2,0)
,
$$
together with a direct sum of negative definite root lattices drawn from the list
$$
B_n(2) = (n, n, 1),
\quad
C_{4n} = (4n, 2, 0)\ (4n\geq 8),
\quad
C_{4n+2} = (4n+2, 2, 1)\ (4n+2\geq 6),
$$
$$
F_4 = (4, 2, 0),
\qquad
E_7 = (7,1,1),
\qquad
E_8 = (8,0,0),
\qquad
E_8(2) = (8,8,0)
,
$$
each listed with its invariants $(r, a, \delta)$.
Here the non-simply-laced names refer to the full reflection group of the summand; as $(-2)$-root lattices in the sense of @def:root-lattice one has
$$
B_n(2) = A_1^{\oplus n},
\qquad
C_n = D_n,
\qquad
F_4 = D_4
.
$$
:::

::: {.proof}

This is [@Ale22 §2].
:::

::: {.example #ex:coble-picard-decomposition title="The decomposition of $S_\Co$"}

The lattice of the row $n = 1$ of @tbl-coble-lattices is $S_\Co = E_{10}(2)\oplus A_1$.
As $E_{10} = U\oplus E_8$ (@def:enriques-lattice), its decomposition in @thm:two-elementary-hyperbolic-decomposition is
$$
S_\Co = U(2)\oplus\bigl(A_1\oplus E_8(2)\bigr)
,
$$
with hyperbolic summand $U(2)$ and negative definite part $A_1\oplus E_8(2) = B_1(2)\oplus E_8(2)$.
:::

## Main roots and diagrams built on a graph

::: {.definition #def:main-roots title="Main roots"}

Let $L$ be an even $2$-elementary lattice.
The **main roots** of $L$ are the short roots $\alpha$, $\alpha^2 = -2$, of divisibility $\div_L(\alpha) = 1$, that is those satisfying $\beta_L(\alpha, L) = \ZZ$.
:::

::: {.definition #def:edge-n-fold-graph title="The edge $n$-fold graph"}

Let $G$ be a graph and $n\geq 2$ an integer.
The **edge $n$-fold graph** $G^{(n)}$ is obtained from $G$ by subdividing each edge into $n$ edges, inserting $n-1$ new vertices on each.
Thus $G^{(n)}$ has $n\abs{E_G}$ edges and $\abs{V_G} + (n-1)\abs{E_G}$ vertices, where $E_G$ and $V_G$ are the edge and vertex sets of $G$.
The vertices of $G^{(2)}$ are in natural bijection with the vertices and edges of $G$ together.
:::

::: {.definition #def:built-on-top-of title="Built on top of a graph"}

A Coxeter--Vinberg diagram $\Gamma$ is **built on top of** a simple graph $H$ if $\Gamma$ contains a subdiagram of main roots isomorphic to $H$ and the remaining roots of $\Gamma$ are determined by the main roots by explicit rules.
For the lattices of @thm:coxeter-built-on-complete-graphs the rule is that a main root $\alpha$ and an additional root $\beta$ are joined exactly when $\alpha\cdot\beta = 2$.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:coxeter-built-on-complete-graphs title="Coxeter diagrams on the line $r + a = 22$"}

For $n = 3, 4, 5, 6$ the Coxeter diagram $\Gamma_r$ of the even $2$-elementary lattice $(10 + n,\, 12 - n,\, 1)$ is built on top of $K_n^{(2)}$, where $K_n$ is the complete graph on $n$ vertices.
The diagram of $(18, 4, 0)$ is built on top of $K_{4,4}^{(2)}$, the complete bipartite graph on $4 + 4$ vertices, and that of $(14, 8, 0)$ on top of $D_4^{(2)}$.
In every case
$$
\Aut\Gamma_r = \Aut G,
\qquad
\Orth^+(S) = \Aut G \ltimes W_r(S)
,
$$
where $G$ is the graph in question.
:::

::: {.proof}

This is [@Ale22 Thm. 1.4].
:::

::: {.corollary #cor:coble-table-complete-graphs title="Coble lattices built on complete graphs"}

For $n = 3, 4, 5, 6$, let $M$ be the lattice of the row $n$ of @tbl-coble-lattices.
Then $\Gamma_r(M)$ is built on top of $K_n^{(2)}$, and $\Orth^+(M) = S_n\ltimes W_r(M)$.
:::

::: {.proof}

By @prop:coble-table-invariants and @tbl-coble-lattices, $M$ has invariants $(10+n, 12-n, 1)$.
@thm:coxeter-built-on-complete-graphs then gives the diagram and $\Orth^+(M) = \Aut K_n\ltimes W_r(M)$, and $\Aut K_n = S_n$.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:nonreflective-r-plus-a-22 title="Non-reflective lattices on the line $r + a = 22$"}

The even $2$-elementary lattices $(17, 5, 1)$, $(18, 4, 1)$ and $(19, 3, 1)$ are not reflective.
Every other even $2$-elementary lattice on the line $r + a = 22$ is reflective.
:::

::: {.proof}

This is [@Ale22 Thm. 1.1].
:::

::: {.corollary #cor:coble-table-reflectivity title="Reflectivity of the Coble lattices"}

The lattices $(17,5,1)$, $(18,4,1)$ and $(19,3,1)$ of the rows $n = 7, 8, 9$ of @tbl-coble-lattices are not reflective, and Vinberg's algorithm for their groups $W_r$ does not terminate.
The lattices of the other eight rows are reflective.
:::

::: {.proof}

Every row of @tbl-coble-lattices has $r + a = (10+n) + (12-n) = 22$ by @prop:coble-table-invariants.
The first assertion is @thm:nonreflective-r-plus-a-22 together with @cor:vinberg-terminates-reflective, and the second is @thm:nonreflective-r-plus-a-22.
:::

## Maximal parabolic subdiagrams and cusps

::: {.theorem #thm:parabolic-isotropic-correspondence title="Maximal parabolic subdiagrams classify isotropic vectors"}

Let $S$ be a reflective even hyperbolic lattice, so that Vinberg's algorithm terminates and produces a finite diagram $\Gamma_r$.
Then the $\Orth(S)$-orbits of primitive isotropic vectors of $S$ correspond bijectively to the maximal parabolic subdiagrams of $\Gamma_r$, and the orbits modulo $\Aut\Gamma_r$ to the subdiagrams modulo $\Aut\Gamma_r$.
If a parabolic subdiagram $\Gamma\iscontainedin\Gamma_r$ corresponds to $v\in S$ with $v^2 = 0$, then the image of $\Gamma$ in $v^{\perp}/v$ spans an elliptic root system: of type $A_n, D_n, E_n$ when every vertex of $\Gamma$ is a short root, and of type $A_n(2), D_n(2), E_n(2)$ when every vertex is a long root.
:::

::: {.proof}

This is [@Ale22 §1, §2].
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:coble-picard-parabolics title="The Coxeter diagram of $S_\Co$"}

The Coxeter diagram $\Gamma_r$ of $S_\Co = (11,11,1)_1$ has $12$ roots and trivial automorphism group $\Aut\Gamma_r = 1$.
It has exactly two maximal parabolic subdiagrams,
$$
\wE_8(2)\,\wA_1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\wB_9(2)
,
$$
both of even ordinary type, so the primitive isotropic vectors of $S_\Co$ in the closure of the positive cone form two $\Orth^+(S_\Co)$-orbits.
:::

::: {.proof}

This is [@Ale22 §3], the case $(11,11,1)$ of @thm:maximal-parabolic-counts.
:::

::: {.corollary #cor:coble-picard-cusp-quotients title="The two quotient lattices of $S_\Co$"}

Let $v_1,v_2\in S_\Co$ represent the two orbits of @thm:coble-picard-parabolics.
Then $\qty{v_1^{\perp}}^{\mathrm{nd}}$ and $\qty{v_2^{\perp}}^{\mathrm{nd}}$ are negative definite even $2$-elementary lattices with invariants $(9,9,1)$, and they are not isometric.
:::

::: {.proof}

Both vectors are even ordinary, so by @thm:isotropic-trichotomy (2) each reduction is negative definite with invariants $(11-2,11-2,1)=(9,9,1)$.
The isometry $-1$ exchanges the two components of the positive cone, so $\Orth(S_\Co)=\Orth^+(S_\Co)\times\{\pm1\}$, and an element of $\Orth(S_\Co)$ that maps $v_1$ to a vector in the closure of the positive cone lies in $\Orth^+(S_\Co)$.
So $v_1$ and $v_2$ lie in distinct $\Orth(S_\Co)$-orbits, and by @thm:isotropic-trichotomy their reductions are not isometric.
:::

## Reflection groups and automorphisms of the K3 cover

::: {.theorem #thm:reflection-group-quotient title="The quotient $W_r/W_2$"}

For an even hyperbolic $2$-elementary lattice $S$ the quotient group $W_r(S)/W_2(S)$ is isomorphic to the Coxeter group $W(\Gamma_4)$ generated by the reflections in the long roots.
:::

::: {.proof}

This is [@Ale22 §4].
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:k3-automorphism-sequence title="The automorphism group of a two-elementary K3 surface"}

Let $S\neq\EnriquesInvariants$ be an even $2$-elementary hyperbolic lattice admitting a primitive embedding into $\lkt$, let $X$ be a K3 surface with $S_X = S$, let $\iota$ be the canonical nonsymplectic involution of $X$, let $Y = X/\iota$ with branch divisor $B$, and let $\Aut'(Y, B)$ be the subgroup of automorphisms of the pair $(Y, B)$ acting trivially on $\Pic Y$.
Then there is an exact sequence
$$
0 \to \generators{\iota}\times\Aut'(Y, B) \to \Aut(X) \to
\Sym\Gamma_r \ltimes W(\Gamma_4) \to 0
,
$$
where $\Gamma_4\iscontainedin\Gamma_r$ is the subdiagram of long roots.
In particular $\Aut(X)$ is finite if and only if $S_X$ is two-reflective.
:::

::: {.proof}

This is [@Ale22, Lem. 4.1], and the last assertion is [@Ale22, §1].
The proof of [@Ale22, Lem. 4.1] uses the Torelli theorem, by which $\Aut(X)\to\Orth^+(S)/W_2(S)$ has finite kernel and cokernel and the kernel consists of the automorphisms descending to $(Y, B)$; the surjectivity of $\Orth(H)\to\Orth(A_H, q_H)$ for indefinite $2$-elementary $H$ (@thm:two-elementary-surjectivity), which lifts an isometry of $S$ to one of $\lkt$; and @thm:reflection-group-quotient, which identifies $\Orth^+(S)/W_2(S)$ with $\Sym\Gamma_r\ltimes W(\Gamma_4)$.
:::

::: {.corollary #cor:coble-picard-automorphisms title="Automorphisms of a K3 surface with Picard lattice $S_\Co$"}

The lattice $S_\Co = (11,11,1)$ is reflective and not two-reflective.
For a K3 surface $X$ with $S_X = S_\Co$, in the notation of @thm:k3-automorphism-sequence, there is an exact sequence
$$
0 \to \generators{\iota}\times\Aut'(Y, B) \to \Aut(X) \to W(T_{2,3,7}) \to 0
,
$$
and $\Aut(X)$ is infinite.
:::

::: {.proof}

$S_\Co$ is reflective by @thm:nonreflective-r-plus-a-22.
The lattice $S_\Co$ is not $\EnriquesInvariants$, and it embeds primitively in $\lkt$ with the orthogonal complement $N$ of @tbl-coble-lattices (@prop:coble-table-invariants), so @thm:k3-automorphism-sequence applies.
By @thm:long-root-subdiagrams, the automorphism group $\Sym\Gamma_r = \Aut\Gamma_r$ of the diagram is trivial and $\Gamma_4 = T_{2,3,7}$, which gives the exact sequence.
The connected diagram $T_{2,3,7}$ has a vertex of valence $3$ and is none of $D_n$, $E_6$, $E_7$, $E_8$, the diagrams of @thm:spherical-classification with such a vertex; so $W(T_{2,3,7})$ is infinite by @thm:coxeter-type-and-geometry (1), and so is $\Aut(X)$.
By @thm:reflection-group-quotient, $W_r(S_\Co)/W_2(S_\Co)\cong W(T_{2,3,7})$ is infinite, so $W_2(S_\Co)$ has infinite index in $\Orth(S_\Co)$.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:elliptic-fibrations-isotropic title="Elliptic fibrations and isotropic vectors"}

Let $X$ be a K3 surface with Picard lattice $S_X$.
The primitive isotropic vectors $v\in S_X$ correspond bijectively to the elliptic fibrations $X\to\PP^1$, a fibration having $v$ as the class of its fiber.
:::

::: {.proof}

This is [@Huy16 §11.1]; the correspondence in the form used here is [@Ale22 §1].
:::

## The ample cone

::: {.definition #def:ample-cone title="Ample cone"}

Let $X$ be a projective K3 surface with Picard lattice $S_X$.
The **ample cone** $A(X)\iscontainedin S_X\tensor_\ZZ\RR$ is the set of ample classes.
It is one connected component of
$$
\theset{ x\in S_X\tensor_\ZZ\RR \;\middle|\; x^2 > 0,\ x\cdot d \neq 0 \text{ for every short root } d \text{ of } S_X }
,
$$
that is, one chamber of the complement of the mirrors of the roots inside the positive cone, in the sense of @def:weyl-chamber.
:::
