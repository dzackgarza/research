# Reflection data and their replacements {#sec-reflection-data-and-replacements}

This page answers the question of the owning ledger for the milestone *When do reflection data determine the compactification, and what replaces them?*: it states the mechanism by which the reflection data at a $0$-cusp determine the boundary of the compactification, the hypotheses under which the mechanism holds, every failure instance among the sibling work units with its evidence, and the replacement in use for the rows where the data fail.
Each step cites a published statement with its printed page, or a theorem of this volume that already proves it; the page adds no claim without a proof.

## The mechanism at a $0$-cusp

**The cusps.** The $0$-cusps of the Baily--Borel compactification are the orbits of primitive isotropic lines, and the $1$-cusps the orbits of primitive totally isotropic planes, under the arithmetic group $\Gamma$ (@thm:baily-borel-isotropic-boundary, from [@Sca87, §2.1, (2.1.7)--(2.1.8)]).

**The boundary lattice.** At a $0$-cusp $[I]$ with representative $\eta$, the boundary lattice is the quotient $K_I = \eta^{\perp}/\ZZ\eta$.
For the one $0$-cusp of $F^{\mathrm{un}}_\Co$ it is $(9,9,1)_1 = \generators{2}\oplus E_8(2)$ (@thm:coble-cusp-chamber, from [@AN06, Table 1, entry $N = 10$, printed p. 52]); for the five Enriques cusps it is $U(2)\oplus E_8(2)$ at cusp $1$ and $U\oplus E_8(2)$ at cusps $2$--$5$ ([@Ste91, Lem. 4.3.4, printed p. 17]).

**The reflective Weyl group and its chamber.** The group acting on the boundary lattice at the cusp is the image of the cusp stabilizer $\Stab_\Gamma(\eta)$ in $\Orth K_I$, where $\Gamma$ is the arithmetic group of the space under compactification: $\Orth T_\Co$ for $F^{\mathrm{un}}_\Co$, $\Gamma_\Co$ for $\fco$, and at the five Enriques cusps the level-$2$ group $\gent = \Gamma_{\En,2}$ (the stable group $\Gamma_\En = \widetilde\Orth^+ T_\En$ and the full group $\Gamma^K_\En = \Orth T_\En$ are the groups of the other two Enriques quotients, whose cusp counts differ).
Which reflection group the image contains, at each cusp:

- **At the one $0$-cusp of $F^{\mathrm{un}}_\Co$** ($\Gamma = \Orth T_\Co$): the image of $\Stab_{\Orth T_\Co}(\eta)$ in $\Orth K_I$ is **all of $\Orth K_I$**, so it contains $W^{(2,4)}(K_I)$, the reflective Weyl group generated by the reflections in the $(-2)$- and $(-4)$-roots of $K_I$ ([@AN06, List of Notations, and Theorem 3.1, printed p. 51]). A representative $\eta$ is the isotropic basis vector of the $U(2)$ summand of the decomposition $T_\Co = \generators{h}\oplus U(2)\oplus E_8(2)$ of [@DK13, (3.6), printed p. 513], its perpendicular splits as $\ZZ\eta\oplus K_I$, and every $g\in\Orth(K_I)$ extends to the isometry $\id_{U(2)}\oplus g$ fixing $\eta$ (@prop:cusp-stabilizer-orthogonal-coble).

- **At the cusps of $\fco$** ($\Gamma = \Gamma_\Co$): the same extension is an element of $\Gamma_\Co$ only on a subgroup of $\Orth(K_I)$, subject to one discriminant and one sign condition, both named in @prop:cusp-stabilizer-section. Under @conj:gamma-co-stable, $\Gamma_\Co = \widetilde\Orth^+(T_\Co)$, and the image contains $\widetilde\Orth^+(K_I) = \Orth^+(K_I)\cap\ker\rho_{K_I}$, hence every reflection in a $(-2)$-root of $K_I$. It does not thereby contain the reflections in the $(-4)$-roots: those lie outside $\ker\rho_{K_I}$ (@prop:cusp-stabilizer-section(4)). Whether the image contains $W^{(2,4)}(K_I)$ is therefore not decided by this construction, and is the open question @qst:cusp-stabilizer-containment below.

- **At the five Enriques cusps** ($\Gamma = \gent$): the image of $\Stab_{\gent}(\eta_j)$ in $\Orth K_j$ is $\Gamma_{\En,2,\eta_j}$, and it contains the reflection subgroup $W(\Gamma_{\En,2,\eta_j})$, generated by the reflections in the roots of $T_\En$ orthogonal to $\eta_j$; its Coxeter diagram at cusp $j$ is Sterk's diagram $j$ ([@AEGS25, Definition 3.6, printed p. 593; @AEGS25, Corollary 3.12, printed p. 595]; [@Ste91, Props. 4.3.5--4.3.9, printed pp. 19--24]). For $\Gamma^K_\En \supseteq \gent$ the image contains the same reflection subgroup a fortiori.

- **At the five Enriques cusps for $\Gamma_\En = \widetilde\Orth^+ T_\En$**: the extension argument of @prop:cusp-stabilizer-section applies with no conjecture as hypothesis, with $T_\En = U_j\perp K_j$ for the hyperbolic summand $U_j\cong U$ or $U(2)$ that contains $\eta_j$ --- such a summand exists because the primitive isotropic vectors of $T_\En$ form exactly two $\Orth(T_\En)$-orbits, separated by divisibility and represented by the basis vector $e$ of a $U$ summand and the basis vector $e'$ of a $U(2)$ summand ([@Ste91, Prop. 4.5]). The image of $\Stab_{\Gamma_\En}(\eta_j)$ in $\Orth K_j$ contains $\widetilde\Orth^+(K_j)$, hence every reflection in a $(-2)$-root of $K_j$. The generating sets of [@AEGS25, Definition 3.6] contain $(-4)$-roots as well (their Lemma 3.4, printed p. 593, with the classification recorded at @sec-sterk-root-counts-and-computed-chambers), and a reflection in a $(-4)$-root is not in $\ker\rho_{T_\En}$ (@prop:cusp-stabilizer-section(4)). So the containment of $W(\Gamma_{\En,2,\eta_j})$ is open exactly at those roots, and is the open question @qst:cusp-stabilizer-containment below.

The chamber of the reflection group so identified is the fundamental Coxeter polytope $P_r$ of @not:reflective-fundamental-polytope, constructed by Vinberg's algorithm (@thm:vinberg-algorithm, from [@Vin75]).

**The Coxeter diagram.** The simple roots of the chamber are the inward normals of its walls, and the Gram matrix of the simple roots determines the Coxeter--Vinberg diagram $\Gamma_r$ of the chamber (@def:coxeter-vinberg-diagram).
For the Coble cusp the diagram is the nine-node chain with one double bond of @thm:coble-cusp-chamber, from [@AN06, Table 1, entry $N = 10$, printed p. 52]; for the Enriques cusps the diagrams are Sterk's five printed diagrams, with $12$, $10$, $12$, $11$ and $14$ walls ([@Ste91, Props. 4.3.5--4.3.9, printed pp. 19--24]).

**The parabolic subdiagrams and their orbits.** The maximal parabolic subdiagrams of $\Gamma_r$ classify the orbits of primitive isotropic vectors: every $W_r(K_I)$-orbit of them meets the fundamental polytope in exactly one ideal vertex, and sending the orbit to the walls through that vertex is a bijection onto the maximal parabolic subdiagrams of $\Gamma_r$ (@thm:parabolic-isotropic-correspondence(1)--(2)).
The bijection is $\Sym P_r$-equivariant, so the $\Orth(K_I)$-orbits correspond to the maximal parabolic subdiagrams modulo the automorphisms of the diagram (@thm:parabolic-isotropic-correspondence(2)--(3)); for the Coble diagram, which has no nontrivial automorphism, the orbits are counted by the subdiagrams themselves (@thm:coble-cusp-chamber).

**How the data determine the boundary.** The Coxeter semifan $\Fcox$ of the cusp is built from the chamber: its support is the rational closure $C_\QQ$ of the positive cone, its maximal cones the chambers of $W^{(2,4)}(K_I)$, its Type II rays the maximal parabolic subdiagrams, and its Type III cones the elliptic subdiagrams of $\Gamma_r$ ([@AEGS25, Definition 5.1, printed p. 609]; for the Coble cusp this is @thm:coble-coxeter-semifan, whose Type II rays are the nine one-node deletions and whose Type III cones number $510$).
The semifan at the cusp is the datum the semitoroidal compactification needs: a $\Gamma$-admissible semifan for each cusp determines a semitoroidal compactification whose boundary strata at the cusp are the orbits of the cones ([@AE23, Thm. 5.13; @Loo02, Lem. 6.6]), and the strata of the Baily--Borel compactification that it refines are the cusp orbits themselves (@thm:baily-borel-isotropic-boundary).


::: {.question #qst:cusp-stabilizer-containment title="Which reflection group does the image of the cusp stabilizer contain?"}

For $\Gamma$ one of $\Gamma_\Co$, $\Gamma_\En = \widetilde\Orth^+ T_\En$ and $\gent = \Gamma_{\En,2}$: which reflection group does the image of $\Stab_\Gamma(\eta)$ in $\Orth K_I$ contain?

The case $\Gamma = \Orth T_\Co$ is settled and is no longer part of this question: the image is all of $\Orth K_I$, and it contains $W^{(2,4)}(K_I)$ (@prop:cusp-stabilizer-orthogonal-coble).

At the five Enriques cusps of $\fentwo = \bD T_\En/\gent$ the answer is settled for $\gent$: the image of $\Stab_{\gent}(\eta_j)$ in $\Orth K_j$ is $\Gamma_{\En,2,\eta_j}$, and it contains the reflection subgroup $W(\Gamma_{\En,2,\eta_j})$, whose Coxeter diagram at cusp $j$ is Sterk's diagram $j$ ([@AEGS25, Definition 3.6, printed p. 593; @AEGS25, Corollary 3.12, printed p. 595]; [@Ste91, Props. 4.3.5--4.3.9, printed pp. 19--24]).

What is not settled by a source, and what decides it:

1. For $\Gamma_\Co$ at the cusps of $\fco$: whether the image contains $W^{(2,4)}(K_I)$, the reflective Weyl group of the boundary lattice ([@AN06, List of Notations, and Theorem 3.1, printed p. 51]). Under @conj:gamma-co-stable the extension of @prop:cusp-stabilizer-section puts $\widetilde\Orth^+(K_I)$ in the image, and the reflections in the $(-4)$-roots of $K_I$ lie outside $\ker\rho_{K_I}$, so that extension does not reach them; [@AN06] describes the reflection subgroup of $\Orth K_I$ and does not address the cusp stabilizer. What decides it: a computation of the image of the cusp stabilizer --- the two library operations of @qst:sterk-reflection-subgroup (sage-extensions#312 and #297).
2. For $\Gamma_\En \subseteq \gent$: whether the image of $\Stab_{\Gamma_\En}(\eta_j)$ in $\Orth K_j$ contains $W(\Gamma_{\En,2,\eta_j})$. The reflections in the $(-2)$-roots are in the image (@prop:cusp-stabilizer-section); the open part is the reflections in the $(-4)$-roots of Sterk's generating sets, which lie outside $\ker\rho_{T_\En}$. What decides it: a computation of the image of $\Stab_{\Gamma_\En}(\eta_j)$ in $\Orth K_j$, or a determination of which of Sterk's printed roots are $(-4)$-roots.
:::


## The image of the cusp stabilizer at the Coble $0$-cusp {#sec-cusp-stabilizer-image}

**The splitting.** The one $0$-cusp of $F^{\mathrm{un}}_\Co$ has a representative $\eta$ that is an isotropic basis vector of the $U(2)$ summand of $T_\Co$, so that $\eta^{\perp}$ is the line $\ZZ\eta$ together with the orthogonal complement of that summand. Since $\Orth^+(T_\Co)$ is transitive on the primitive isotropic lines (@thm:unpolarized-cusps), every representative is carried to one of this shape, and the image of its stabilizer in the orthogonal group of the quotient is carried with it; so the two propositions below do not depend on the choice.

::: {.proposition #prop:cusp-stabilizer-orthogonal-coble title="The Coble $0$-cusp stabilizer surjects onto $\Orth(K_I)$"}

Let
$$
T_\Co = \generators{h}\oplus U(2)\oplus E_8(2)
$$
be the decomposition of [@DK13, (3.6), printed p. 513], with $h^2 = 2$ and $e,f$ the isotropic basis of $U(2)$, $(e,f) = 2$. Put $\eta \definedas e$ and $K_I \definedas \generators{h}\oplus E_8(2)$. Then:

1. $\eta$ is a primitive isotropic vector, $\eta^{\perp T_\Co} = \ZZ\eta\oplus K_I$, and the quotient $\eta^{\perp}/\ZZ\eta$ is $K_I$, of rank $9$, determinant $2^9$ and signature $(1,8)$ --- the lattice $(9,9,1)_1$ of the boundary of this cusp;
2. $g\mapsto\id_{U(2)}\oplus g$ carries $\Orth(K_I)$ into $\Stab_{\Orth(T_\Co)}(\eta)$ and inverts the restriction map, so the image of $\Stab_{\Orth(T_\Co)}(\eta)$ in $\Orth(K_I)$ is all of $\Orth(K_I)$;
3. every root of $K_I$ has divisibility $2$ and its reflection is an integral isometry of $K_I$, so $W^{(2,4)}(K_I)\leq\Orth(K_I)$; consequently the image contains $W^{(2,4)}(K_I)$.

:::

::: {.proof}

*Item 1.* In the basis $(h;e,f;\beta_1,\dots,\beta_8)$, with $\beta_i$ a basis of the $E_8(2)$ summand, the Gram matrix of $T_\Co$ is block diagonal with entries $h^2 = 2$, $(e,f) = 2$, and the $E_8(2)$ block; its determinant is the product $2\cdot(-4)\cdot2^8 = -2048$ of the determinants of the three blocks, and its signature is $(2,9)$.
The only nonzero entry of the column $(x,\eta)$ is $(f,\eta) = 2$, so
$$
\eta^{\perp T_\Co} = \theset{x\in T_\Co \st x_f = 0} = \ZZ e\oplus\generators{h}\oplus E_8(2) = \ZZ\eta\oplus K_I,
$$
the sum being direct because $e$ is orthogonal to $\generators{h}\oplus E_8(2)$.
$e$ is primitive, and it is isotropic. Quotienting by $\ZZ\eta$ removes one rank and leaves $K_I$, of rank $9$, of determinant $2\cdot2^8 = 2^9$, and of signature $(1,8)$ because $h$ is a positive vector and $E_8(2)$ is negative definite --- $E_{10} = U\oplus E_8$ is of signature $(1,9)$ (@def:enriques-lattice), so $E_8$ and $E_8(2)$ are. Those are the invariants of the boundary lattice of @thm:coble-cusp-chamber.

*Item 2.* The decomposition of item 1 is an orthogonal direct sum of lattices spanned by basis vectors, so the Gram matrix is block diagonal in that basis, and an endomorphism acting as an isometry on each summand is an isometry of $T_\Co$. Hence $\hat g \definedas \id_{U(2)}\oplus g$ lies in $\Orth(T_\Co)$, and $\hat g(e) = e$ because $e$ lies in the $U(2)$ summand.
Its restriction to $\ZZ\eta\oplus K_I$ is $\id_{\ZZ\eta}\oplus g$, so the map it induces on the quotient $\eta^{\perp}/\ZZ\eta = K_I$ is $g$.
Conversely an isometry fixing $\eta$ preserves $\eta^\perp$ and the line $\ZZ\eta$, hence induces an isometry of that quotient, so restriction is a well-defined homomorphism $\Stab_{\Orth(T_\Co)}(\eta)\to\Orth(K_I)$; the assignment $g\mapsto\hat g$ is a section of it, and a surjection with a section is onto. So the image is $\Orth(K_I)$.

*Item 3.* A root $r$ of $K_I$ has $r^2\in\theset{-2,-4}$ and is primitive: from $r = 2s$ we would get $s^2 = -1$ or $-1/2$, and $K_I$ is even, so $s^2$ is even. Every pairing of $r$ with $K_I$ is even --- with $h$ it is $2r_h$, and with a vector of $E_8(2)$ it is twice an integer because $E_8(2)$ is the lattice $E_8$ with its form doubled --- and $r$ is primitive, so $\div_{K_I}(r) = 2$.
The reflection $s_r(x) = x - 2\frac{(x,r)}{(r,r)}r$ is integral on $K_I$: for $r^2 = -2$ its coefficient is $-(x,r)$, an integer, and for $r^2 = -4$ it is $(x,r)/2$, an integer by the divisibility. So each $s_r$ is an isometry of $K_I$, and
$$
W^{(2,4)}(K_I) = \generators{s_r \st r \text{ a root of } K_I} \leq \Orth(K_I)
$$
in the sense of [@AN06, Theorem 3.1, printed p. 51]. By item 2 the image is $\Orth(K_I)$, so it contains $W^{(2,4)}(K_I)$.
:::

::: {.proposition #prop:cusp-stabilizer-section title="The extension for a subgroup: one discriminant and one sign condition"}

In the notation of @prop:cusp-stabilizer-orthogonal-coble let $\rho$ be the discriminant representation of $T_\Co$, and for a lattice $L$ write $\widetilde\Orth^+(L) \definedas \Orth^+(L)\cap\ker\rho_L$. Then for $g\in\Orth(K_I)$:

1. $A_{T_\Co} = A_{U(2)}\oplus A_{K_I}$ and $\rho(\hat g) = \id_{A_{U(2)}}\oplus\rho_{K_I}(g)$; hence $\hat g\in\ker\rho$ if and only if $g\in\ker\rho_{K_I}$;
2. $\hat g\in\Orth^+(T_\Co)$ if and only if $g\in\Orth^+(K_I)$;
3. the extension therefore carries $\widetilde\Orth^+(K_I)$ into $\widetilde\Orth^+(T_\Co)$, so the image of $\Stab_{\widetilde\Orth^+(T_\Co)}(\eta)$ in $\Orth(K_I)$ contains $\widetilde\Orth^+(K_I)$; under @conj:gamma-co-stable this is the statement for $\Gamma_\Co$;
4. for a lattice $L$, the reflection in a $(-2)$-root of $L$ lies in $\ker\rho_L$, and the reflection in a primitive $(-4)$-root of divisibility $2$ does not.
   In particular every reflection in a $(-2)$-root of $K_I$ lies in $\widetilde\Orth^+(K_I)$, and none in a $(-4)$-root does.

:::

::: {.proof}

*Item 1.* For an orthogonal direct sum $L = L_1\perp L_2$ of lattices the dual lattices split, $L^* = L_1^*\oplus L_2^*$, so $A_L = A_{L_1}\oplus A_{L_2}$; here the orders are $4\cdot512 = 2048 = \abs{\det T_\Co}$, and $A_{T_\Co}$ is $2$-elementary of order $2^{11}$.
$\hat g$ is the identity on the $U(2)$ summand, so it acts trivially on $A_{U(2)}$, and its action on $A_{K_I}$ is $\rho_{K_I}(g)$.

*Item 2.* Each of $U(2)$ and $K_I$ has signature $(1,\ast)$, so a maximal positive definite subspace of each is the line its positive cone spans, and the positive $2$-plane of $T_\Co$ is the orthogonal sum of those two lines. $\hat g$ acts on the line of $U(2)$ as the identity and on the line of $K_I$ as $g$ does, so it preserves the two halves of the positive cone of $T_\Co$ exactly when $g$ preserves those of $K_I$. That sign is the same datum as membership in $\Orth^+$ (@def:spinor-norm), hence the equivalence.

*Item 3.* Combine items 1 and 2.

*Item 4.* Let $r$ be a root of $L$ and $x\in L^*$. The coefficient of $s_r(x)-x = -2\frac{(x,r)}{(r,r)}r$ is $-(x,r)$ when $r^2 = -2$ and $(x,r)/2$ when $r^2 = -4$. The pairing $(x,r)$ is an integer because $r\in L$ and $x\in L^*$, so in the first case $s_r(x)-x\in\ZZ r\subseteq L$ and $s_r$ acts trivially on $A_L$.
In the second case suppose first that $(x,r)$ is odd for some $x$; then $r = 2\rho$ with $\rho \definedas r/2\in L^*$ by the divisibility, and $s_r(x)-x = \frac{(x,r)}{2}r = (x,r)\rho$ is not in $L$, because $\rho\notin L$ --- the root $r$ is primitive. If instead $(x,r)$ is even for every $x\in L^*$, then $(L^*,r)\subseteq2\ZZ$, so $r\in2(L^*)^* = 2L$, contradicting primitivity. So some class of $A_L$ is moved, and $s_r\notin\ker\rho_L$.
Finally $r^2<0$, so $s_r$ lies in $\Orth^+(L)$ (@prop:period-spinor-character), which gives both clauses for $K_I$.
:::


## The hypotheses

**Reflectivity.** The mechanism needs the boundary lattice to be **reflective**: the subgroup $W_r(S)\leq\Orth(S)$ generated by the reflections in all roots of $S$ must have finite index in $\Orth(S)$ (@def:reflective-lattice, from [@Ale22, §2]).
The hypothesis is what makes the diagram finite: Vinberg's algorithm terminates on $S$ for the group $W_r(S)$ if and only if $S$ is reflective (@cor:vinberg-terminates-reflective, from [@Vin75]), so a non-reflective lattice has no finite Coxeter diagram for its full reflection group, and the chamber step of the mechanism does not exist.

**Two-reflectivity.** The stronger hypothesis is that $S$ be **two-reflective**: the subgroup $W_2(S)\leq\Orth(S)$ generated by the reflections in the short roots alone has finite index in $\Orth(S)$ (@def:reflective-lattice).
Its source is the classification of the lattices for which it holds: a hyperbolic lattice of rank at least $5$ that is two-reflective belongs to the class $\mathcal F_{\langle 0\rangle}$, and membership in that class forces $\rank S + l(A_S)\leq 18$, with two named exceptions ([@Nik79b, Prop. 0.2.1, printed p. 1402, and Thm. 0.2.3, printed pp. 1403--1404]).
The hypothesis enters the mechanism through the same termination criterion: the algorithm terminates for $W_2(S)$ if and only if $S$ is two-reflective (@cor:vinberg-terminates-reflective).

## The failure instances and the replacement in use

**The non-reflective rows.** The even $2$-elementary lattices $(17,5,1)$, $(18,4,1)$ and $(19,3,1)$ are not reflective, and every other even $2$-elementary lattice on the line $r + a = 22$ is (@thm:nonreflective-r-plus-a-22, from [@Ale22, Thm. 1.1, printed p. 3]).
For these three rows $W_r$ has infinite index in $\Orth$, Vinberg's algorithm does not terminate (@cor:vinberg-terminates-reflective), and the reflection semifan of $W_r$ is not a fan ([@AE22, Def. 3.5, printed p. 10]).

**The two-reflectivity failure.** No lattice of the Coble table is two-reflective: for every one of them $W_2$ has infinite index in $\Orth$ (@thm:coble-table-not-two-reflective, from [@Nik79b, Prop. 0.2.1, printed p. 1402, and Thm. 0.2.3, printed pp. 1403--1404]).

**The replacement in use.** For the three non-reflective rows the $0$-cusps and their boundary lattices are classified without any diagram, by the mirror moves ([@AE22, Def. 5.6 and Thm. 5.8, printed pp. 22--23]): the boundary lattices are $(3,3,1)$ for $n = 7$, $(2,2,1)$ and $(2,2,0)$ for $n = 8$, and $(1,1,1) = \generators{2}$ for $n = 9$ (@prop:coble-table-cusp-boundary-lattices).
The collection of semifans that the semitoroidal compactification of these three families uses at their $0$-cusps is the open question @qst:coble-nonreflective-semifans, whose candidate replacements are semifans from recognizable divisors, where no chamber exists, and the induced reflection subgroup, where the diagram would belong to a subgroup.

## The settled and open work units

**Settled.**

- The chamber at the one $0$-cusp of $F^{\mathrm{un}}_\Co$, with the Coble roots marked and the orbits of the maximal parabolic subdiagrams under the diagram symmetries: @thm:coble-cusp-chamber, from [@AN06, Table 1, entry $N = 10$, printed p. 52]; the ten-node enumeration that the question originally asked for is superseded by it, the chamber having nine nodes, and the library enumeration of the subdiagram orbits is sage-extensions#254.
- The reflectivity verdict for each row of the Coble table, the two-reflectivity failure, and the boundary lattices of the non-reflective rows: @cor:coble-table-reflectivity, @thm:coble-table-not-two-reflective, @prop:coble-table-cusp-boundary-lattices, with the open replacement @qst:coble-nonreflective-semifans.
- The Coble arrangement is nonempty, so the GIT compactification of the ten-nodal sextics is a proper Looijenga model and not the Baily--Borel compactification: @thm:coble-arrangement-nonempty, from [@YZ23, Prop. 3.7, p. 10] with the Winger parametrization of [@Dol18, §7, pp. 13--14] (stated on the open-problems page); the $\Gamma_{10A_1}$-orbit count of the arrangement is stated as open there.
- The group of the one $0$-cusp of $F^{\mathrm{un}}_\Co$: a representative lies in the $U(2)$ summand of $T_\Co$, its perpendicular splits as $\ZZ\eta\oplus K_I$, and every isometry of $K_I$ extends to one of $T_\Co$ fixing $\eta$, so the image of $\Stab_{\Orth T_\Co}(\eta)$ in $\Orth K_I$ is all of $\Orth K_I$ and contains $W^{(2,4)}(K_I)$ (@prop:cusp-stabilizer-orthogonal-coble). What the same extension gives for $\Gamma_\Co$, and where it stops, is @prop:cusp-stabilizer-section; the part of @qst:cusp-stabilizer-containment that named $\Orth T_\Co$ is discharged and the question now names only $\Gamma_\Co$, $\Gamma_\En$ and $\gent$.

**Open, with the exact missing hypothesis.**

- *The reflection subgroup bounding each published Sterk diagram.* The quotients $K_j$ for $j = 2,\dots,5$ are mutually isometric, so their full Weyl groups are conjugate and cannot alone explain four inequivalent published diagrams with $10$, $12$, $11$, $14$ walls. The subgroup that bounds each diagram is the reflection subgroup $W(\Gamma_{\En,2,\eta_j})$ of the image $\Gamma_{\En,2,\eta_j}$ of the stabilizer of $\eta_j$ in $\gent$ ([@AEGS25, Definition 3.6, printed p. 593]); its Coxeter diagram at cusp $j$ is Sterk's diagram $j$ ([@AEGS25, Corollary 3.12, printed p. 595]). For the stable group the remainder has a name: the reflections in the $(-2)$-roots of $K_j$ already lie in the image of $\Stab_{\Gamma_\En}(\eta_j)$, and what the extension does not supply is the reflections in the $(-4)$-roots (@prop:cusp-stabilizer-section(4)). What remains open is the index of $\Gamma_{\En,2,\eta_j}$ in $\Orth K_j$ --- a covolume ratio that no Euler characteristic supplies, the Euler characteristics vanishing in odd dimension --- and the two library operations it waits on are the group $G_v$ together with the image of $U_v$ (sage-extensions#312) and a chamber for a prescribed subgroup (sage-extensions#297).
- *The global questions on hyperbolic Coxeter diagrams*, genuinely open in the literature: the cost of finding maximal parabolic subdiagrams, Galois invariance of their count, its growth with rank, arithmeticity from the diagram, exact covolumes in odd dimension, and a regularized theta series for an indefinite lattice.
- *The $n\geq 2$ programme.* Three conjectures for the families $F_{\Co,n}$: that the Baily--Borel boundaries of all ten are computable by the $n = 1$ techniques; that the ramification locus of the K3 cover is a recognizable divisor in the Alexeev--Engel sense, which would give a semitoroidal compactification; and that the semifans refine or coarsen the Coxeter fans. The organizing observation is that the ten lattices $(10+n,\, 12-n,\, \delta)$ are exactly the $g = 0$ row of Nikulin's triangle.
