# Reflection data and their replacements {#sec-reflection-data-and-replacements}

This page answers the question of the owning ledger for the milestone *When do reflection data determine the compactification, and what replaces them?*: it states the mechanism by which the reflection data at a $0$-cusp determine the boundary of the compactification, the hypotheses under which the mechanism holds, every failure instance among the sibling work units with its evidence, and the replacement in use for the rows where the data fail.
Each step cites a published statement with its printed page, or a theorem of this volume that already proves it; the page adds no claim without a proof.

## The mechanism at a $0$-cusp

**The cusps.** The $0$-cusps of the Baily--Borel compactification are the orbits of primitive isotropic lines, and the $1$-cusps the orbits of primitive totally isotropic planes, under the arithmetic group $\Gamma$ (@thm:baily-borel-isotropic-boundary, from [@Sca87, §2.1, (2.1.7)--(2.1.8)]).

**The boundary lattice.** At a $0$-cusp $[I]$ with representative $\eta$, the boundary lattice is the quotient $K_I = \eta^{\perp}/\ZZ\eta$.
For the one $0$-cusp of $F^{\mathrm{un}}_\Co$ it is $(9,9,1)_1 = \generators{2}\oplus E_8(2)$ (@thm:coble-cusp-chamber, from [@AN06, Table 1, entry $N = 10$, printed p. 52]); for the five Enriques cusps it is $U(2)\oplus E_8(2)$ at cusp $1$ and $U\oplus E_8(2)$ at cusps $2$--$5$ ([@Ste91, Lem. 4.3.4, printed p. 17]).

**The reflective Weyl group and its chamber.** The group acting on the boundary lattice at the cusp is the image of the cusp stabilizer $\Stab_\Gamma(\eta)$ in $\Orth K_I$, where $\Gamma$ is the arithmetic group of the space under compactification: $\Orth T_\Co$ for $F^{\mathrm{un}}_\Co$, $\Gamma_\Co$ for $\fco$, and at the five Enriques cusps the level-$2$ group $\gent = \Gamma_{\En,2}$ (the stable group $\Gamma_\En = \widetilde\Orth^+ T_\En$ and the full group $\Gamma^K_\En = \Orth T_\En$ are the groups of the other two Enriques quotients, whose cusp counts differ).
Which reflection group the image contains, at each cusp:

- **At the five Enriques cusps** ($\Gamma = \gent$): the image of $\Stab_{\gent}(\eta_j)$ in $\Orth K_j$ is $\Gamma_{\En,2,\eta_j}$, and it contains the reflection subgroup $W(\Gamma_{\En,2,\eta_j})$, generated by the reflections in the roots of $T_\En$ orthogonal to $\eta_j$; its Coxeter diagram at cusp $j$ is Sterk's diagram $j$ ([@AEGS25, Definition 3.6, printed p. 593; @AEGS25, Corollary 3.12, printed p. 596]; [@Ste91, Props. 4.3.5--4.3.9, printed pp. 19--24]). For $\Gamma^K_\En \supseteq \gent$ the image contains the same reflection subgroup a fortiori; for $\Gamma_\En \subseteq \gent$ the containment is not settled by a source and is the open question @qst:cusp-stabilizer-containment below.

- **At the one $0$-cusp of $F^{\mathrm{un}}_\Co$** ($\Gamma = \Orth T_\Co$) **and at the cusps of $\fco$** ($\Gamma = \Gamma_\Co$): whether the image contains $W^{(2,4)}(K_I)$ --- the reflective Weyl group of the boundary lattice, generated by the reflections in all $(-2)$- and $(-4)$-roots of $K_I$ ([@AN06, List of Notations, and Theorem 3.1, printed p. 51]) --- is not settled by a source: [@AN06] describes the reflection subgroup of $\Orth K_I$ and does not address the cusp stabilizer. It is the open question @qst:cusp-stabilizer-containment below.

The chamber of the reflection group so identified is the fundamental Coxeter polytope $P_r$ of @not:reflective-fundamental-polytope, constructed by Vinberg's algorithm (@thm:vinberg-algorithm, from [@Vin75]).

**The Coxeter diagram.** The simple roots of the chamber are the inward normals of its walls, and the Gram matrix of the simple roots determines the Coxeter--Vinberg diagram $\Gamma_r$ of the chamber (@def:coxeter-vinberg-diagram).
For the Coble cusp the diagram is the nine-node chain with one double bond of @thm:coble-cusp-chamber, from [@AN06, Table 1, entry $N = 10$, printed p. 52]; for the Enriques cusps the diagrams are Sterk's five printed diagrams, with $12$, $10$, $12$, $11$ and $14$ walls ([@Ste91, Props. 4.3.5--4.3.9, printed pp. 19--24]).

**The parabolic subdiagrams and their orbits.** The maximal parabolic subdiagrams of $\Gamma_r$ classify the orbits of primitive isotropic vectors: every $W_r(K_I)$-orbit of them meets the fundamental polytope in exactly one ideal vertex, and sending the orbit to the walls through that vertex is a bijection onto the maximal parabolic subdiagrams of $\Gamma_r$ (@thm:parabolic-isotropic-correspondence(1)--(2)).
The bijection is $\Sym P_r$-equivariant, so the $\Orth(K_I)$-orbits correspond to the maximal parabolic subdiagrams modulo the automorphisms of the diagram (@thm:parabolic-isotropic-correspondence(2)--(3)); for the Coble diagram, which has no nontrivial automorphism, the orbits are counted by the subdiagrams themselves (@thm:coble-cusp-chamber).

**How the data determine the boundary.** The Coxeter semifan $\Fcox$ of the cusp is built from the chamber: its support is the rational closure $C_\QQ$ of the positive cone, its maximal cones the chambers of $W^{(2,4)}(K_I)$, its Type II rays the maximal parabolic subdiagrams, and its Type III cones the elliptic subdiagrams of $\Gamma_r$ ([@AEGS25, Definition 5.1, printed p. 609]; for the Coble cusp this is @thm:coble-coxeter-semifan, whose Type II rays are the nine one-node deletions and whose Type III cones number $510$).
The semifan at the cusp is the datum the semitoroidal compactification needs: a $\Gamma$-admissible semifan for each cusp determines a semitoroidal compactification whose boundary strata at the cusp are the orbits of the cones ([@AE23, Thm. 5.13; @Loo02, Lem. 6.6]), and the strata of the Baily--Borel compactification that it refines are the cusp orbits themselves (@thm:baily-borel-isotropic-boundary).

For $\Gamma$ one of $\Orth T_\Co$, $\Gamma_\Co$, $\Gamma_\En = \widetilde\Orth^+ T_\En$ and $\gent = \Gamma_{\En,2}$: which reflection group does the image of $\Stab_\Gamma(\eta)$ in $\Orth K_I$ contain?

At the five Enriques cusps of $\fentwo = \bD T_\En/\gent$ the answer is settled for $\gent$: the image of $\Stab_{\gent}(\eta_j)$ in $\Orth K_j$ is $\Gamma_{\En,2,\eta_j}$, and it contains the reflection subgroup $W(\Gamma_{\En,2,\eta_j})$, whose Coxeter diagram at cusp $j$ is Sterk's diagram $j$ ([@AEGS25, Definition 3.6, printed p. 593; @AEGS25, Corollary 3.12, printed p. 596]; [@Ste91, Props. 4.3.5--4.3.9, printed pp. 19--24]).

What is not settled by a source:

1. For $\Orth T_\Co$ at the one $0$-cusp of $F^{\mathrm{un}}_\Co$ and for $\Gamma_\Co$ at the cusps of $\fco$: whether the image contains $W^{(2,4)}(K_I)$, the reflective Weyl group of the boundary lattice ([@AN06, List of Notations, and Theorem 3.1, printed p. 51]). [@AN06] describes the reflection subgroup of $\Orth K_I$ and does not address the cusp stabilizer. What decides it: a computation of the image of the cusp stabilizer --- the two library operations of @qst:sterk-reflection-subgroup (sage-extensions#312 and #297).
2. For $\Gamma_\En \subseteq \gent$: whether the image of $\Stab_{\Gamma_\En}(\eta_j)$ in $\Orth K_j$ contains $W(\Gamma_{\En,2,\eta_j})$. What decides it: a computation of the image of $\Stab_{\Gamma_\En}(\eta_j)$ in $\Orth K_j$.

## The hypotheses

**Reflectivity.** The mechanism needs the boundary lattice to be **reflective**: the subgroup $W_r(S)\leq\Orth(S)$ generated by the reflections in all roots of $S$ must have finite index in $\Orth(S)$ (@def:reflective-lattice, from [@Ale22, §2]).
The hypothesis is what makes the diagram finite: Vinberg's algorithm terminates on $S$ for the group $W_r(S)$ if and only if $S$ is reflective (@cor:vinberg-terminates-reflective, from [@Vin75]), so a non-reflective lattice has no finite Coxeter diagram for its full reflection group, and the chamber step of the mechanism does not exist.

**Two-reflectivity.** The stronger hypothesis is that $S$ be **two-reflective**: the subgroup $W_2(S)\leq\Orth(S)$ generated by the reflections in the short roots alone has finite index in $\Orth(S)$ (@def:reflective-lattice).
Its source is the classification of the lattices for which it holds: a hyperbolic lattice of rank at least $5$ that is two-reflective belongs to the class $\mathcal F_{\langle 0\rangle}$, and membership in that class forces $\rank S + l(A_S)\leq 18$, with two named exceptions ([@Nik79b, Prop. 0.2.1, printed p. 1402, and Thm. 0.2.3, printed pp. 1403--1404]).
The hypothesis enters the mechanism through the same termination criterion: the algorithm terminates for $W_2(S)$ if and only if $S$ is two-reflective (@cor:vinberg-terminates-reflective).

## The failure instances and the replacement in use

**The non-reflective rows.** The even $2$-elementary lattices $(17,5,1)$, $(18,4,1)$ and $(19,3,1)$ are not reflective, and every other even $2$-elementary lattice on the line $r + a = 22$ is (@thm:nonreflective-r-plus-a-22, from [@Ale22, Thm. 1.1, printed p. 3]).
For these three rows $W_r$ has infinite index in $\Orth$, Vinberg's algorithm does not terminate (@cor:vinberg-terminates-reflective), and the reflection semifan of $W_r$ is not a fan ([@AE22, Def. 3.5, printed p. 10]).

**The two-reflectivity failure.** No lattice of the Coble table is two-reflective: for every one of them $W_2$ has infinite index in $\Orth$ (@thm:coble-table-not-two-reflective, from [@Nik79b, Prop. 0.2.1, printed p. 1402, and Thm. 0.2.3, printed pp. 1403--1404]).

**The replacement in use.** For the three non-reflective rows the $0$-cusps and their boundary lattices are classified without any diagram, by the mirror moves ([@AE22, Def. 5.6 and Thm. 5.8, printed pp. 22--23]): the boundary lattices are $(3,3,1)$ for $n = 7$, $(2,2,1)$ and $(2,2,0)$ for $n = 8$, and $(1,1,1) = \generators{2}$ for $n = 9$ (@prop:coble-table-cusp-boundary-lattices).
The collection of semifans that the semitoroidal compactification of these three families uses at their $0$-cusps is the open question @qst:coble-nonreflective-semifans, whose candidate replacements are semifans from recognizable divisors, where no chamber exists, and the induced reflection subgroup, where the diagram would belong to a subgroup.

## The settled and open work units

**Settled.**

- The chamber at the one $0$-cusp of $F^{\mathrm{un}}_\Co$, with the Coble roots marked and the orbits of the maximal parabolic subdiagrams under the diagram symmetries: @thm:coble-cusp-chamber, from [@AN06, Table 1, entry $N = 10$, printed p. 52]; the ten-node enumeration that the question originally asked for is superseded by it, the chamber having nine nodes, and the library enumeration of the subdiagram orbits is sage-extensions#254.
- The reflectivity verdict for each row of the Coble table, the two-reflectivity failure, and the boundary lattices of the non-reflective rows: @cor:coble-table-reflectivity, @thm:coble-table-not-two-reflective, @prop:coble-table-cusp-boundary-lattices, with the open replacement @qst:coble-nonreflective-semifans.
- The Coble arrangement is nonempty, so the GIT compactification of the ten-nodal sextics is a proper Looijenga model and not the Baily--Borel compactification: @thm:coble-arrangement-nonempty, from [@YZ23, Prop. 3.7, p. 10] with the Winger parametrization of [@Dol18, §7, pp. 13--14] (stated on the open-problems page); the $\Gamma_{10A_1}$-orbit count of the arrangement is stated as open there.

**Open, with the exact missing hypothesis.**

- *The reflection subgroup bounding each published Sterk diagram.* The quotients $K_j$ for $j = 2,\dots,5$ are mutually isometric, so their full Weyl groups are conjugate and cannot alone explain four inequivalent published diagrams with $10$, $12$, $11$, $14$ walls. The subgroup that bounds each diagram is the reflection subgroup $W(\Gamma_{\En,2,\eta_j})$ of the image $\Gamma_{\En,2,\eta_j}$ of the stabilizer of $\eta_j$ in $\gent$ ([@AEGS25, Definition 3.6, printed p. 593]); its Coxeter diagram at cusp $j$ is Sterk's diagram $j$ ([@AEGS25, Corollary 3.12, printed p. 596]). What remains open is the index of $\Gamma_{\En,2,\eta_j}$ in $\Orth K_j$ --- a covolume ratio that no Euler characteristic supplies, the Euler characteristics vanishing in odd dimension --- and the two library operations it waits on are the group $G_v$ together with the image of $U_v$ (sage-extensions#312) and a chamber for a prescribed subgroup (sage-extensions#297).
- *The global questions on hyperbolic Coxeter diagrams*, genuinely open in the literature: the cost of finding maximal parabolic subdiagrams, Galois invariance of their count, its growth with rank, arithmeticity from the diagram, exact covolumes in odd dimension, and a regularized theta series for an indefinite lattice.
- *The $n\geq 2$ programme.* Three conjectures for the families $F_{\Co,n}$: that the Baily--Borel boundaries of all ten are computable by the $n = 1$ techniques; that the ramification locus of the K3 cover is a recognizable divisor in the Alexeev--Engel sense, which would give a semitoroidal compactification; and that the semifans refine or coarsen the Coxeter fans. The organizing observation is that the ten lattices $(10+n,\, 12-n,\, \delta)$ are exactly the $g = 0$ row of Nikulin's triangle.
