# Scattone's method for cusp enumeration {#sec-scattone-cusp-method}

## The cusp--isotropic-subspace correspondence

::: {.theorem #thm:baily-borel-isotropic-boundary title="Boundary components of the Baily–Borel compactification"}

Let $d\geq 1$, let $\lkttd\cong\langle -2d\rangle\oplus U^2\oplus E_8^2$ be the lattice of signature (@def:signature) $(2,19)$ of @def:degree-2d-k3-period-data, let $D$ be a connected component of the type IV domain $\Omega_{\lkttd}$, and let $\Gamma\leq\Orth(\lkttd)$ be an arithmetic subgroup (@def:hsd-arithmetic-subgroup) that preserves $D$.
Then the boundary of the Baily--Borel compactification $\bbcpt{D/\Gamma}$ is the disjoint union of

1. one point $p_v$, a **$0$-cusp** (Type III boundary component (@def:coble-surface)), for each $\Gamma$-orbit of primitive (@def:primitive-embedding) isotropic (@def:orthogonal-complement) lines $\ZZ v\iscontainedin\lkttd$, and

2. one curve $C_E$, a **$1$-cusp** (Type II boundary component), for each $\Gamma$-orbit of primitive totally isotropic planes $E\iscontainedin\lkttd$

[@Sca87, §2.1, (2.1.7)–(2.1.8)].
:::

## Divisibility and the discriminant group

::: {.proposition #prop:coble-divisibility-discriminant}

Let $L$ be an integral nondegenerate (@def:polarization) lattice and $v\in L$ a nonzero primitive (@def:primitive-embedding) vector, not necessarily isotropic (@def:orthogonal-complement), with divisibility $\div_L(v)$ of @def:lattice-divisibility and dual vector $v^*$ of @def:lattice-discriminant-class.
Then $v^*$ is primitive in $L^\#$, and its image $[v^*]$ in the discriminant group (@def:group) $A_L = L^\#/L$ has order $\div_L(v)$.

In particular $\div_L(v)$ divides $\abs{A_L} = \abs{\disc{L}}$.
:::

::: {.proof}

Write $d = \div_L(v)$.
By @def:lattice-discriminant-class, $v^*\in L^\#$.
Suppose $v^* = m y$ with $m\geq 1$ and $y\in L^\#$.
Then $\beta_L(v,x) = dm\,\beta_L(y,x)\in dm\ZZ$ for every $x\in L$, so $dm$ divides $d$ by @def:lattice-divisibility, and $m = 1$.

For $n\in\ZZ$, $nv^* = (n/d)v$.
Write $n/d = a/b$ with $\gcd(a,b) = 1$ and $b\geq 1$.
If $av\in bL$, choose $s,t\in\ZZ$ with $sa + tb = 1$; then $v = s(av) + tbv\in bL$, so $b = 1$ by primitivity of $v$.
Hence $nv^*\in L$ if and only if $d$ divides $n$, and $[v^*]$ has order $d$.
The order of an element of the finite group $A_L$, of order $\abs{\disc L}$, divides $\abs{A_L}$, which is the last clause.
:::

## Eichler's criterion

::: {.theorem #thm:eichler-transvection title="Scattone’s condition for Eichler transvection orbits"}

Let $L$ be an even lattice (@def:even-lattice) admitting a splitting $L \isoto U^{2}\oplus M$, and let $E_{\mathrm{iso}}(L)\leq\Orth(L)$ be the subgroup generated by the transvections $E_{e,a}$ of @def:eichler-siegel with $e, a\in L$, $e^2 = 0$ and $ea = 0$.
It contains the Eichler transvection group (@def:group) $E(L)$ of @def:eichler-group, whose generating transvections $E_{e,a}$ require in addition that $e$ be primitive (@def:primitive-embedding) with $\div_L(e) = 1$.
Then $E_{\mathrm{iso}}(L)\leq\discriminantkernel{L}$.
Fix $k\in\ZZ$ and write $L[k] \definedas \theset{ x\in L \mid x^2 = k }$.
For primitive $v, w\in L[k]$,
$$
v \sim_{E_{\mathrm{iso}}(L)} w
\iff
[v^*] = [w^*]\in A_L
,
$$
where $\sim_{E_{\mathrm{iso}}(L)}$ denotes $E_{\mathrm{iso}}(L)$-equivalence.
:::

::: {.proof}

This is [@Sca87, Prop. 3.7.3], and $E_{\mathrm{iso}}(L)\leq\discriminantkernel{L}$ is [@Sca87, §3.7].
The elementary transformation $E_{f,x}$ of [@Sca87, §3.7] is the transvection $E_{f,-x}$ of @def:eichler-siegel, so $E_{\mathrm{iso}}(L)$ is the group $E(L)$ of [@Sca87, §3.7], generated by the $E_{f,x}$ for all $f, x\in L$ with $f^2 = fx = 0$.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:scattone-eichler-criterion title="Eichler’s criterion"}

Let $L \isoto U^{2}\oplus M$ be an even lattice (@def:even-lattice) containing two orthogonal copies of the hyperbolic plane (@def:hyperbolic-plane) $U$, and let $v, w\in L$ be primitive (@def:primitive-embedding) vectors.
Then $v$ and $w$ lie in the same $\discriminantkernel{L}$-orbit (where $\discriminantkernel{L}$ acts trivially on $A_L$) if and only if
$$
v^2 = w^2
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
[v^*] = [w^*]\in A_L
.
$$
Equivalently, the $\discriminantkernel{L}$-orbit of a primitive vector is determined by its [length]{.other-sense} together with the pair $\bigl(\div_L(v),\ v/\div_L(v)\bmod L\bigr)$ in the discriminant group (@def:group).
:::

::: {.proof}

If $v^2 = w^2$ and $[v^*] = [w^*]$, then @thm:eichler-transvection gives $\phi\in E_{\mathrm{iso}}(L)$ with $\phi(v) = w$, and $E_{\mathrm{iso}}(L)\leq\discriminantkernel{L}$.
Conversely, let $\phi\in\discriminantkernel{L}$ with $\phi(v) = w$.
By @prop:orbit-necessary-conditions, $v^2 = w^2$ and the isometry induced by $\phi$ on $A_L$ sends $[v^*]$ to $[w^*]$; this isometry is the identity, so $[v^*] = [w^*]$.
By @prop:coble-divisibility-discriminant, $\div_L(v)$ is the order of $[v^*]$, and $v/\div_L(v)\bmod L$ is $[v^*]$, so the pair $\bigl(\div_L(v),\ v/\div_L(v)\bmod L\bigr)$ and the class $[v^*]$ determine each other.
:::

## Sterk's orbit criterion

::: {.theorem #thm:sterk-orbit title="Sterk's orbit criterion"}

Let $P$ be an even (@def:form-axioms) nondegenerate (@def:polarization) lattice and let $T = U\oplus U(2)\oplus P$.
Let $\eta_1, \eta_2\in T$ be primitive (@def:primitive-embedding) vectors satisfying

1. $\eta_1^2 = \eta_2^2$,

2. $\div_T(\eta_1) = \div_T(\eta_2) = p > 0$,

3. $\eta_1 \equiv \eta_2 \pmod{pT}$.

Then there exists an isometry $\phi\in\discriminantkernel{T}$ with $\phi(\eta_1) = \eta_2$ [@Ste91, Cor. 3.3].
:::

::: {.proof}

This is [@Ste91, Cor. 3.3], where $\Lambda = U\oplus U(2)$, $N = \Lambda\oplus P$, and $O^*(N)$ is the subgroup of $\Orth(N)$ acting as the identity on $N^*/N$, that is $\discriminantkernel{N}$.
:::

::: {.corollary #cor:enriques-divisibility-one-orbit title="Divisibility-one vectors of $\ten$"}

Let $v, w\in\ten$ be primitive (@def:primitive-embedding) with $v^2 = w^2$ and $\div_{\ten}(v) = \div_{\ten}(w) = 1$.
Then $w = \phi(v)$ for some $\phi\in\discriminantkernel{\ten}$.
In particular the primitive isotropic (@def:orthogonal-complement) vectors of $\ten$ of divisibility (@def:lattice-divisibility) $1$ form a single $\discriminantkernel{\ten}$-orbit.
:::

::: {.proof}

By @prop:enriques-cover-eigenlattices, $\ten\cong U\oplus U(2)\oplus E_8(2)$, and $E_8(2)$ is even (@def:form-axioms) and nondegenerate (@def:polarization).
For $p = 1$ the congruence $v\equiv w\pmod{\ten}$ holds for all $v, w\in\ten$, so @thm:sterk-orbit with $P = E_8(2)$ applies.
:::

## Enumeration via Niemeier lattices

::: {.proposition #prop:scattone-niemeier-complements title="Boundary lattices as complements in Niemeier lattices"}

Let $d\geq 1$ and let $\mathcal G(d)$ be the genus of $\langle -2d\rangle\oplus E_8^{\oplus 2}$, a genus of even (@def:form-axioms) negative definite (@def:definiteness) lattices of rank $17$.

1. For every primitive (@def:primitive-embedding) totally isotropic (@def:orthogonal-complement) plane $E\iscontainedin\lkttd$ that is primitive in $\lkttd^\#$, the lattice $E^{\perp}/E$ lies in $\mathcal G(d)$, and every lattice in $\mathcal G(d)$ is isometric to $E^{\perp}/E$ for such a plane $E$ [@Sca87, §5, (5.0.1)].

2. There are exactly $24$ even unimodular negative definite lattices of rank $24$ up to isometry, and each is determined up to isometry by its sublattice spanned by the vectors of norm (@def:lattice-basic-invariants) $-2$ [@Sca87, Thm. 3.5.1].

3. Let $v\in E_8$ be primitive with $v^2 = -2d$ and let $N\definedas v^{\perp E_8}$.
   Every lattice in $\mathcal G(d)$ is isometric to the orthogonal complement $N^{\perp M}$ of some primitive embedding (@def:primitive-embedding) $N\injects M$ into a lattice $M$ of (2) [@Sca87, Prop. 6.1.2].
:::

## Example: the cusp diagram of $F_2$

::: {.example #ex:scattone-f2-diagram title="Scattone cusp diagram"}

Let $d = 1$ and $\Gamma = \discriminantkernel{L_{\mathrm{K3},2}}\cap\Orth^+(L_{\mathrm{K3},2})$, so that $F_2 = D/\Gamma$ for a connected component $D$ of $\Omega_{L_{\mathrm{K3},2}}$ [@Sca87, §1.1], with boundary described by @thm:baily-borel-isotropic-boundary.
There is exactly one $\Gamma$-orbit of primitive isotropic lines in $L_{\mathrm{K3},2}$, so $\bbcpt{F_2}$ has a unique $0$-cusp [@Sca87, Thm. 4.0.1].

The genus $\mathcal G(1)$ of @prop:scattone-niemeier-complements consists of four isometry classes, and $\bbcpt{F_2}$ has four $1$-cusps, one for each class [@Sca87, §6.2].
Here $N\cong E_7$, its primitive embeddings into the lattices of @prop:scattone-niemeier-complements (2) are
$$
E_7\iscontainedin E_8^{3},
\qquad
E_7\iscontainedin E_8\oplus D_{16},
\qquad
E_7\iscontainedin E_7^{2}\oplus D_{10},
\qquad
E_7\iscontainedin E_7\oplus A_{17},
$$
each unique up to equivalence, and the four complements are distinguished by their root sublattices
$$
A_1\oplus E_8^{\oplus 2},
\qquad
A_1\oplus D_{16},
\qquad
E_7\oplus D_{10},
\qquad
A_{17}
$$
[@Sca87, §6.2, (6.2.1)].
:::

## Modular curves on the boundary

For $L_k=\langle -2k\rangle\oplus U^2\oplus E_8^2$, let $E\subset L_k$ be a primitive totally isotropic plane of divisibility $e$ and suppose $\gcd(e,2k/e^2)=1$. Scattone identifies the image of its stabilizer in $\SL_2(\ZZ)$ for the stable orthogonal group with $\Gamma^1(e)$, so the open one-cusp $C_E$ is $Y_1(e)=\mathbb H/\Gamma^1(e)$ [@Sca87, Prop. 5.5.3]. The compact modular curve $X_1(e)$ has the following invariants in the cases recorded there:

| $e$ | $1$ | $3$ | $5$ | $7$ | $9$ | $11$ | $13$ | $15$ | prime $q>3$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Genus | $0$ | $0$ | $0$ | $0$ | $0$ | $1$ | $2$ | $1$ | $(q-5)(q-7)/24$ |
| Cusps | $1$ | $2$ | $4$ | $6$ | $8$ | $10$ | $12$ | $16$ | $q-1$ |

The closure $\overline{C_E}$ in the Baily–Borel compactification need not be $X_1(e)$: the extension $X_1(e)\to\overline{C_E}$ can identify several of the modular cusps at one boundary point [@Sca87, §5.5]. Thus the table counts cusps on the normalization; it does not by itself give the number of distinct zero-cusps incident to $C_E$.

Scattone's oriented incidence graph has one vertex for each $\Gamma$-orbit of primitive isotropic lines or planes. For a line $\ell$ and a plane $E$, an edge $[\ell]\to[E]$ means that a representative of $[\ell]$ lies in a representative of $[E]$. Its multiplicity is
$$
\mu([\ell],[E])
=\#\left(N_\Gamma(E)\backslash
\left\{\ell'\subset E:\ell'\text{ primitive},\ [\ell']=[\ell]\text{ under }\Gamma\right\}\right),
\qquad
N_\Gamma(E)=\operatorname{im}\left(\operatorname{Stab}_\Gamma(E)\to\SL(E)\right).
$$
If $\mu>1$, the normalization of $\overline{C_E}$ has distinct cusps identified at the same zero-cusp, producing a self-intersection there [@Sca87, (2.1.9)–(2.1.10)].

## The degree-four K3 example

For $k=2$, the complement $N$ of a primitive vector of square $-4$ in $E_8$ has type $D_7$. Scattone obtains nine classes in $\mathcal G(2)$ from primitive embeddings of $D_7$ into eight Niemeier lattices. The row with $E_8\oplus D_{16}$ admits two inequivalent embeddings; all other rows admit one [@Sca87, §6.3]. The resulting generalized types of the complements are:

| Niemeier root system containing $D_7$ | Generalized type of $D_7^\perp$ |
| --- | --- |
| $E_8^3$ | $\langle-4\rangle\oplus E_8^2$ |
| $E_8\oplus D_{16}$ | $\langle-4\rangle\oplus D_{16}$; $E_8\oplus D_9$ |
| $E_7^2\oplus D_{10}$ | $E_7^2\oplus A_3$ |
| $D_{24}$ | $D_{17}$ |
| $D_{12}^2$ | $D_{12}\oplus D_5$ |
| $D_8^3$ | $\langle-4\rangle\oplus D_8^2$ |
| $D_9\oplus A_{15}$ | $A_1^2\oplus A_{15}$ |
| $E_6\oplus D_7\oplus A_{11}$ | $E_6\oplus A_{11}$ |

The direct Vinberg calculation starts from $\langle-4\rangle\oplus U\oplus E_8^2$. Its first $24$ wall diagram, redrawn in the native notes from [@Sca87, Fig. 6.3.1], is the diagram below. This *preliminary* reflection group has infinite index; Scattone adds six further roots to obtain a finite-volume chamber. The degree-four diagram is therefore not the degree-two $(19,1,1)$ diagram of @fig-k3-coxeter-19-1-1 [@Sca87, §6.3].
