# Hyperbolic reflection groups and Vinberg's algorithm {#sec-reflection-groups-and-vinberg}

::: {.notation title="Sign convention"}

Roots are *negative-definite* directions, a **short root** having norm $v^2 = -2$ and a **long root** norm $v^2 = -4$.
An indefinite lattice $L$ of signature $(1, n)$ is called **hyperbolic**; its **positive cone**
$$
C_L \definedas \theset{ v\in L_\RR \mid v^2 > 0 }
$$
has two connected components, and mirrors of roots are the hyperplanes orthogonal to negative-norm vectors.
Fixing one component $C_L^+$ identifies $L_\RR$ with a model of hyperbolic $n$-space, on which the reflection group acts.
:::

## Reflections and the Weyl group

::: {.definition #def:reflection}

Let $(L,\beta_L)$ be a nondegenerate integral lattice and $\alpha\in L$ an anisotropic vector (that is, $\beta_L(\alpha,\alpha)\neq 0$) satisfying the integrality condition
$$
2\,\beta_L(\alpha, L) \iscontainedin \beta_L(\alpha,\alpha)\,\ZZ
.
$$
The **reflection in $\alpha$** is the isometry
$$
\begin{aligned}
s_\alpha : L &\to L \\
x &\mapsto x - 2\,\frac{\beta_L(\alpha,x)}{\beta_L(\alpha,\alpha)}\,\alpha
.
\end{aligned}
$$
Each reflection has determinant $-1$, fixes the orthogonal complement $\generators{\alpha}^{\perp L}$ pointwise, and sends $\alpha\mapsto -\alpha$.
The **full reflection group** of $L$ is the subgroup
$$
W_r(L) \definedas \generators{\, s_\alpha \mid \alpha\in L,\ \beta_L(\alpha,\alpha) < 0,\ s_\alpha\in\Orth(L) \,}
\leq \Orth(L)
$$
generated by the integral reflections in the vectors of negative norm [@Ale22, §2].
When no distinction from the group $W^{(2)}(L)$ of @def:k-root is needed, $W_r(L)$ is written $W(L)$.
:::

## Roots and $k$-roots

::: {.definition #def:k-root}

Let $L$ be a nondegenerate integral lattice and $v\in L$ a primitive anisotropic vector.
The reflection $s_v$ is always an isometry of $L_\QQ$, but is integral, $s_v\in\Orth(L)$, precisely when
$$
2\,\beta_L(v, L) \iscontainedin v^2\,\ZZ
.
$$
A vector $v\in L$ is a **$(-k)$-root**, for $k>0$, if $v^2 = -k$ and $2\,\beta_L(v, L) \iscontainedin k\,\ZZ$.
The **$k$-reflection group** $W^{(k)}(L)$ is the subgroup generated by the reflections in the $(-k)$-roots:
$$
W^{(k)}(L) \definedas \generators{\, s_v \mid v \text{ a } (-k)\text{-root} \,}
.
$$
The full reflection group $W_r(L)$ of @def:reflection is generated by the integral reflections in vectors of any negative norm, so that $W_r(L) = \generators{\, W^{(k)}(L) \mid k>0 \,}$; its subgroup $W^{(2)}(L)$ is the Weyl group in the root-lattice sense of @def:root-lattice.
:::

::: {.definition #def:2elementary-roots}

For a $2$-elementary lattice $L$, the roots are precisely the vectors $v\in L$ of one of the two following types:

- a **short root**, with $v^2 = -2$; or

- a **long root**, with $v^2 = -4$ and $\div_L(v) = 2$.
:::

::: {.proposition #prop:root-integrality title="Integrality of the two root types"}

Let $L$ be an even $2$-elementary lattice and $v\in L$ a primitive vector with $v^2\in\theset{-2,-4}$.
Then $v$ satisfies the integrality condition of @def:k-root if and only if $v$ is a root of @def:2elementary-roots.
For a short root $s_v(x) = x + \beta_L(v,x)\,v$, and for a long root $s_v(x) = x + \tfrac12\beta_L(v,x)\,v$.
:::

::: {.proof}

The formulas for $s_v$ are those of @def:reflection with $\beta_L(v,v) = -2$, respectively $-4$.
If $v^2 = -2$, the integrality condition $2\beta_L(v,L)\iscontainedin 2\ZZ$ holds because $L$ is integral.
If $v^2 = -4$, the integrality condition is $\beta_L(v,L)\iscontainedin 2\ZZ$, that is $2\mid\div_L(v)$.
The vector $v/\div_L(v)$ lies in $\dualof{L}$, and since $v$ is primitive its class in $A_L$ has order $\div_L(v)$.
As $A_L$ is $2$-elementary, $\div_L(v)\in\theset{1,2}$, so $2\mid\div_L(v)$ holds exactly when $\div_L(v) = 2$.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:2elementary-roots-characterization title="The two root types exhaust the roots"}

Let $L$ be an even $2$-elementary lattice and let $v\in L$ be primitive with $v^2 < 0$.
Then $s_v\in\Orth(L)$ if and only if either $v^2 = -2$, or $v^2 = -4$ and $\div_L(v) = 2$.
:::

::: {.proof}

This is [@Ale22, §2], where a root is a vector $\alpha$ with $\alpha^2<0$ and $s_\alpha(L) = L$.
Sufficiency is @prop:root-integrality.
For necessity, $\div_L(v)\in\theset{1, 2}$ by the proof of @prop:root-integrality, so the integrality condition $2\div_L(v)/v^2\in\ZZ$ of @def:k-root restricts $v^2$ to $\theset{-2, -4}$, with $\div_L(v) = 2$ forced in the second case.
:::

## Coroots and the weight space

::: {.definition #def:coroot-weight}

Let $R \definedas \generators{\Phi}_\ZZ$ be a root lattice generated by a root system $\Phi = \theset{\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n}$, with coroot lattice
$$
R^\vee \definedas \generators{\, \alpha_i^\vee = 2\alpha_i/(\alpha_i,\alpha_i) \,}_\ZZ
.
$$
The **weight lattice** is the dual lattice $P(R) \definedas \dualof{(R^\vee)}$, and the **fundamental weights** are the dual-basis elements $\omega_i$ determined by $(\omega_i, \alpha_j^\vee) = \delta_{ij}$, the pairing being against the coroots.
When all roots have the same length, in particular for the simply-laced systems, $R^\vee = R$ and $P(R) = \dualof{R}$.
:::

::: {.example #ex:e8-fundamental-weights title="Coroots and fundamental weights of $E_8$"}

Let $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_8$ be the simple roots of $E_8$ and $G = (\alpha_i\cdot\alpha_j)$ their Gram matrix, so that $\alpha_i^2 = -2$ (@def:root-lattice).
The coroots of @def:coroot-weight are $\alpha_i^\vee = 2\alpha_i/(\alpha_i,\alpha_i) = -\alpha_i$, so $R^\vee = E_8$.
As $E_8$ is unimodular (@thm:E8-characterization), $P(E_8) = \dualof{E_8} = E_8$.
The fundamental weights are
$$
\omega_i = -\Sum_{j=1}^{8} (G^{-1})_{ij}\,\alpha_j
,
$$
since then $(\omega_i, \alpha_k^\vee) = \Sum_{j} (G^{-1})_{ij}\,G_{jk} = \delta_{ik}$.
:::

## Hyperbolic lattices and their polytopes

::: {.definition #def:hyperbolic-model}

Let $L$ be a hyperbolic lattice and $C_L^+$ one connected component of its positive cone $C_L$.
The intersection pairing induces a hyperbolic metric on $L_\RR$, and the associated hyperbolic $n$-space $\HH^n_L$ admits two standard models:

- the **unit hyperboloid model**
  $$
  \HH^n_L \definedas \theset{ v\in L_\RR \mid v^2 = 1,\ v\in C_L^+ }
  ;
  $$

- the **projective (Klein) model**
  $$
  \BB^n_L \definedas \PP(C_L) \subset \PP(L_\RR)
  ,
  $$
  the image of the positive cone in projective space.

In the projective model the boundary $\partial\HH^n_L$ is the image $\PP(\theset{v\in L_\RR \mid v^2 = 0})$ of the isotropic cone, and geodesics are straight line segments.

The hyperbolic distance between points is recovered from the form: for $v, w\in C_L^+$,
$$
\cosh d\!\left([v],[w]\right) = \frac{\beta_L(v,w)}{\sqrt{v^2\,w^2}}
,
$$
the right-hand side being at least $1$ by the reverse Cauchy--Schwarz inequality on the positive cone, with equality exactly when $[v] = [w]$.
On the unit hyperboloid, where $v^2 = w^2 = 1$, this reads $\cosh d(v,w) = \beta_L(v,w)$.

To a vector $v$ with $v^2 < 0$ we associate the hyperplane $H_v \definedas v^{\perp}\intersect \HH^n_L$.
Taking $v,w$ to be unit normals of $H_v, H_w$, the relative position of two hyperplanes is governed by the pairing $v\cdot w \definedas \beta_L(v,w)$:

- $|v\cdot w| < 1$: the hyperplanes meet in the interior of $\HH^n_L$, at dihedral angle $\theta$ with $-v\cdot w = \cos\theta$;

- $|v\cdot w| = 1$: the hyperplanes are asymptotically parallel, meeting at an ideal point of the boundary $\partial\HH^n_L$;

- $|v\cdot w| > 1$: the hyperplanes are ultra-parallel (divergent), at distance $d$ with $-v\cdot w = \cosh d$.
:::

## Coxeter groups and Coxeter polytopes

::: {.definition #def:coxeter-group}

A **Coxeter group** is a group given by a presentation
$$
W = \generators{\, s_1,\dots,s_n \mid s_i^2 = e,\ (s_i s_j)^{m_{ij}} = e \,}
,
$$
where $m_{ii} = 1$ and $m_{ij} = m_{ji}\in\theset{2,3,\dots}\union\theset{\infty}$ for $i\neq j$, the relation $(s_i s_j)^{m_{ij}} = e$ being imposed only when $m_{ij} < \infty$.
Writing $S = \theset{s_1,\dots,s_n}$ for the generating set, the pair $(W,S)$ is the associated **Coxeter system**, and $(m_{ij})$ is its Coxeter matrix (@def:coxeter-matrix).
:::

::: {.definition #def:coxeter-polytope}

A **Coxeter polytope** $P$ in $X = \EE^n$, $S^n$, or $\HH^n$ is a polytope such that:

1. each face lies in a hyperplane $H_i \definedas \alpha_i^{\perp}$;

2. the dihedral angles between adjacent faces are $\pi/m_{ij}$ for integers $m_{ij}\geq 2$; and

3. the reflection group $W = \generators{ s_{\alpha_i} }$ acts properly discontinuously on $X$ with fundamental domain $P$.
:::

## The discriminant locus and Weyl chambers

::: {.definition #def:discriminant-locus}

The **discriminant locus** $\Delta(L)$ is the union of all mirrors, i.e. the hyperplanes orthogonal to the short roots $\Phi_2(L) = \theset{ v\in L \mid v^2 = -2 }$:
$$
\Delta(L) \definedas \Union_{v\in\Phi_2(L)} v^{\perp L}
.
$$
:::

::: {.definition #def:weyl-chamber}

The **Weyl chambers** of $L$ are the connected components of
$$
C_L^{\circ} \definedas C_L \sm \Delta(L)
,
$$
where $C_L$ is the cone of positive-norm vectors.
A **simple system** is a set of roots whose mirrors form the bounding hyperplanes of a single Weyl chamber, i.e. a fundamental domain for the action of the $2$-Weyl group $W^2(L)$ generated by the $(-2)$-reflections.
:::

## Coxeter--Vinberg diagrams

::: {.definition #def:coxeter-vinberg-diagram}

The **Coxeter--Vinberg diagram** of a hyperbolic Coxeter polytope is a graph whose vertices correspond to the simple roots $r_i$ (equivalently, to the walls $H_i$ of the fundamental chamber), and whose edges encode the relative position of the mirrors $H_i, H_j$ through the normalized pairing
$$
g_{ij} \definedas \frac{r_i\cdot r_j}{\sqrt{r_i^2\, r_j^2}} = \cos\!\left(\frac{\pi}{m_{ij}}\right)
.
$$
Edges are drawn according to the following conventions:

- **omitted** if $m_{ij}\leq 2$ (orthogonal mirrors);

- **multiple (or labeled) lines** if $3\leq m_{ij} < \infty$, when $H_i\intersect H_j$ lies in the interior of $\HH^n$;

- **thick / bold** if $g_{ij} = 1$, when $H_i\intersect H_j$ lies on the boundary $\partial\HH^n$ (asymptotically parallel mirrors);

- **dotted** if $g_{ij} > 1$ (ultra-parallel mirrors).

For $2$-elementary lattices, vertices are drawn **white** for short roots ($v^2 = -2$) and **black** for long roots ($v^2 = -4$).
:::

## Elliptic and parabolic subdiagrams

::: {.definition #def:elliptic-subdiagram}

Let $G$ be a Coxeter--Vinberg diagram with vertices the simple roots $v_i$.
A subdiagram $D\iscontainedin G$ is:

- **elliptic** if its Gram matrix $(v_i\cdot v_j)$ is negative definite;

- **parabolic** if the Gram matrix of each of its connected components is negative semidefinite with a one-dimensional kernel;

- **hyperbolic** if its Gram matrix is nondegenerate of signature $(1, k-1)$, where $k$ is the number of vertices of $D$.
:::

::: {.notation title="The opposite normalization for subdiagrams"}

For roots of norm $+2$, the three conditions of @def:elliptic-subdiagram read positive definite, positive semidefinite with a one-dimensional kernel on each connected component, and signature $(k-1,1)$.
:::

::: {.definition #def:parabolic-subdiagram}

A parabolic subdiagram is **maximal** if it is not properly contained in a larger parabolic subdiagram.
:::

## Vinberg's algorithm

::: {.theorem #thm:vinberg-algorithm title="Vinberg’s algorithm for a fundamental chamber"}

Let $\Gamma\iscontainedin\Orth(L)$ be an arithmetic reflection subgroup of a hyperbolic lattice $L$.
Vinberg's algorithm constructs a fundamental chamber $P\iscontainedin\HH^n$ for $\Gamma$ as follows [@Vin75].

1. **Control vector.** Choose a generic positive-norm vector $v_0\in C_L^+$.
   Its stabilizer in $\Gamma$ is the reflection group of the finite subdiagram of roots orthogonal to $v_0$, which are used as the first walls.

2. **Enumeration by height.** Seek primitive vectors $v\in L$ with $v^2 < 0$, $s_v\in\Gamma$, and mirror $H_v$ meeting the current cone $\tilde P$.
   Order the candidate roots by increasing **height**
   $$
   h(v) \definedas \frac{-2\,(v_0\cdot v)}{v^2}
   ,
   $$
   and process them in successive batches of increasing height.

3. **Acceptance / pruning.** A candidate root $v$ at the current height is kept only if its mirror bounds the chamber on the side containing $v_0$; equivalently, the inward normals of accepted walls pair non-negatively, so $v$ is accepted iff $v\cdot r_i \geq 0$ for every previously accepted root $r_i$ (a mirror with some $v\cdot r_i < 0$ would cut into the interior of $\tilde P$). Roots whose hyperplanes do not bound $\tilde P$ are discarded.

4. **Diagram.** The algorithm proceeds until $\tilde P$ becomes a convex polytope of finite volume.
   The Gram matrix of the accepted simple roots then determines the Coxeter--Vinberg diagram of $P$ (@def:coxeter-vinberg-diagram).

The algorithm terminates in finitely many steps if and only if $\Gamma$ has finite covolume in $\Orth(L)$.
:::

::: {.proof}

This is [@Vin75].
:::

::: {.definition #def:lanner-subgraph title="Lannér subgraph"}

Let $D$ be a hyperbolic subdiagram of a Coxeter--Vinberg diagram (@def:elliptic-subdiagram).
Then $D$ is a **Lannér subgraph** if all of its proper subdiagrams are elliptic, and a **quasi-Lannér subgraph** if each of its proper subdiagrams is elliptic or connected parabolic.
:::

::: {.proposition #prop:lanner-simplices title="Lannér subgraphs and hyperbolic Coxeter simplices"}

Let $D$ be a subdiagram without dotted edges on $d+1$ vertices.

1. If $D$ is a Lannér subgraph, respectively a quasi-Lannér subgraph, then there is a compact, respectively finite-volume, Coxeter simplex in $\HH^d$ with Coxeter diagram $D$.

2. Conversely, the Coxeter diagram of a compact, respectively finite-volume, Coxeter simplex in $\HH^d$ is a Lannér subgraph, respectively a quasi-Lannér subgraph.
:::

::: {.proof}

This is [@LM22, §3.4], stated there for the cosine matrix $C_W$ with diagonal entries $2$, which is $-G$ for the Gram form $G$ of @def:coxeter-gram-form.
There a Coxeter group is Lannér, respectively quasi-Lannér, if $\det C_W < 0$ and every proper subset of $S$ generates a spherical, respectively spherical or irreducible affine, Coxeter group; these are the elliptic and connected parabolic subdiagrams of @def:coxeter-system-type.
These conditions agree with those of @def:lanner-subgraph.
A Lannér or quasi-Lannér Coxeter group of rank $d+1$ has $C_W$ of signature $(d,1)$ [@LM22, §3.4], so $D$ is hyperbolic.
Conversely, if $G$ has signature $(1,d)$, then $C_W$ has exactly one negative eigenvalue and $\det C_W < 0$.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:vinberg-completeness-criterion title="A sufficient criterion for completeness"}

Let $\Gamma$ be a Coxeter--Vinberg diagram without dotted edges, obtained from a run of @thm:vinberg-algorithm.
If $\Gamma$ contains no Lannér subgraph, and every connected parabolic subdiagram of $\Gamma$ is contained in a maximal parabolic subdiagram of maximal rank, then $\Gamma$ is complete: the accepted roots bound a finite-volume polytope and the algorithm may be stopped.
:::

::: {.proof}

This is the sufficient condition of [@Vin75], in the form used in [@Ale22, §3].
:::

## Root-system conventions

::: {.notation title="Bourbaki labelling and twists"}

The classical root systems are taken with the Bourbaki labelling, in the negative-definite twists
$$
A_n(-1),\quad B_n(-2),\quad C_n(-1),\quad D_n(-1),\quad
E_n(-1)\ (n\in\theset{6,7,8}),\quad F_4(-2),\quad G_2(-1)
.
$$
:::
