## Mirror moves {#sec-mirror-moves}

The mirror move algorithm of [@AE22, Thm. 5.10], applied to the $2$-elementary lattice $S_\Co = (11, 11, 1)_1$, gives the 0-cusps and 1-cusps of $F^{\mathrm{un}}_\Co$ in @fig-mirror-moves-coble.

\end{tikzpicture}

Blue (resp. red) indicate lattices which are valid (resp. invalid) targets of mirror moves.

By @thm:unpolarized-cusps there is one $\Orth^+(T_\Co)$-orbit of $0$-cusps, represented by an isotropic vector $v_0$ with

$$
v_0^{\perp T_{\Co}}/\generators{v_0} \cong (9,9,1)_1 \cong \generators{2} \oplus E_{8}(2)
$$

The group $\widetilde\Orth^+(T_\Co)$ is not transitive on the primitive isotropic vectors (@lem:stable-orthogonal-not-transitive); it has $527$ orbits of them, one for each nonzero isotropic class of $A_{T_\Co}$ (@thm:coble-zero-cusp-count(2)), the fibre over one class being a single orbit (@thm:coble-integral-lifting-fibre(2)), and the discriminant kernel has $527$ as well.
This $0$-cusp $v_0$ is incident to one $\Orth^+(T_\Co)$-orbit of $1$-cusps, represented by an isotropic plane $J = \generators{v_0, v_1}$ with

$$
J^{\perp T_{\Co}}/\generators{J} \cong (7,7,1)_0 \cong A_1^{\oplus 7} ,
$$

where $v_1 \in v_0^{\perp T_{\Co}}/\generators{v_0}$.
In the diagrammatic language of [@AE22 Fig. 1, Thm. 5.10], this corresponds to a composite of two even ordinary mirror moves, with $H = U(2)$, summarized in the following mirror move diagram:

::: {.theorem #thm:coble-cusp-chamber title="The reflection chamber at the one $0$-cusp of $F^{\mathrm{un}}_\Co$"}
Let $v_0$ be a primitive isotropic vector (@def:isotropic-subspace) of $T_\Co$, $\bar T = v_0^{\perp}/\langle v_0\rangle\cong(9,9,1)_1 = \generators{2}\oplus E_8(2)$ its boundary lattice, and $W^{(2,4)}(\bar T)$ the full reflection group (@def:reflection) of $\bar T$, generated by the reflections in all $(-2)$- and $(-4)$-roots ([@AN06, List of Notations and Thm. 3.1]). Then $F^{\mathrm{un}}_\Co = \bD(T_\Co)/\Orth(T_\Co)$ (@def:gamma-co-groups) has one $0$-cusp (@thm:unpolarized-cusps, computed by sage-extensions#286 in its $\Orth(T_\Co)$ row), and at it the chamber of $W^{(2,4)}(\bar T)$ is the simplex with nine walls whose adjacencies are

| wall | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| adjacent | 3 | 4 | 1, 4 | 3, 2, 5 | 4, 6 | 5, 7 | 6, 8 | 7, 9 | 8 |

with every bond of label $3$ except $8-9$, of label $4$. It is the chamber $\mathcal M^{(2,4)}$ of [@AN06, Table 1], entry $N = 10$, that is $(r,a,\delta) = (9,9,1)$ with $k = 0$, $g = 2$ and $l(H) = 0$; the source's convention is that a hyperbolic lattice of rank $r$ has signature $(1, r-1)$, here $(1,8)$, with roots of square $-2$ and $-4$, which is this volume's convention. Its Gram matrix lies over $\QQ[\sqrt2]$, it has $8$ finite vertices and one ideal vertex, and its $f$-vector is $(9,36,84,126,126,84,36,9,1)$.

The source's diagram and this wall list agree node by node, under
$$
1\mapsto v_1,\quad 3\mapsto v_2,\quad 4\mapsto v_3,\quad 2\mapsto v_9,\quad 5\mapsto v_4,\quad 6\mapsto v_5,\quad 7\mapsto v_6,\quad 8\mapsto v_7,\quad 9\mapsto v_8,
$$
where $v_1-\cdots-v_8$ is the source's chain, $v_9$ its node below $v_3$, $v_7-v_8$ its double bond, and $v_8$ its open vertex. The maximal parabolic subdiagrams (@def:parabolic-subdiagram) are the nine one-node deletions; the diagram has no nontrivial automorphism, so distinct deletions are distinct subdiagrams (@def:coxeter-subdiagram); the deletion whose remaining diagram is affine of type $\wt B_7$ is the facet (@def:facet-maximal-face) carrying the adjacent $1$-cusp.
:::


::: {.theorem #thm:coble-coxeter-semifan title="The Coxeter semifan at the one $0$-cusp of $F^{\mathrm{un}}_\Co$, and what the generalized semifan still needs"}
Let $G$ be the nine-node chamber diagram of @thm:coble-cusp-chamber at the one $0$-cusp of $F^{\mathrm{un}}_\Co$ and $W = W^{(2,4)}(\bar T)$ its reflection group (@def:reflection-group). Then:

1. the Coxeter semifan (@def:coxeter-semifan) $\F^{\cox}$ of [@AEGS25, Def. 5.1] --- support $C_\QQ$, maximal cones (@def:maximal-cone-of-fan) the chambers of $W$ --- is a **fan**, the chamber having finite volume;
2. its **Type II rays** are in bijection with the maximal parabolic subdiagrams of $G$, which are the nine one-node deletions of @thm:coble-cusp-chamber; exactly one of them is affine, of type $\wt B_7$. The ray it gives is the ideal vertex of the simplex --- the intersection of the eight walls other than the deleted one --- and not a facet (@def:facet-maximal-face): it is the unique vertex at infinity that the recorded run reports, and it is the $1$-cusp adjacent to $v_0$, whose boundary lattice is $(7,7,1)_0 = A_1^{\oplus 7}$ (`computations/reports/one_cusp_plane_quotient.py` and its transcript);
3. its **Type III cones (@def:closed-cone)** are the faces $\bigcap_{j\in S}\alpha_j^{\perp}\cap C$ for the elliptic subdiagrams (@def:elliptic-subdiagram) $S\subseteq\{1,\dots,9\}$ of $G$ --- all of them, the empty one included, which gives $C$ itself --- of every dimension, the face of an $S$ with $\abs{S} = k$ having dimension $8-k$. Since $G$ is a simplex diagram the faces of $C$ are counted by the $f$-vector $(9,36,84,126,126,84,36,9,1)$; a subset gives a face of the interior exactly when its diagram is elliptic, and the recorded run reports that the nine $8$-subsets give eight finite vertices and one vertex at infinity, so eight of them are elliptic and the ninth is the affine one of (2), while the whole of $G$ is neither. The Type III cones therefore number
$$
1+9+36+84+126+126+84+36+8 = 510,
$$
one for each elliptic subdiagram.

The generalized Coxeter semifan (@def:generalized-coxeter-semifan) of [@AEGS25, §5.2], p. 610 (after [@AET23, §4D]) is formed from a split $I\sqcup J$ of the nine nodes into relevant and irrelevant roots, by $C_{\mathrm{gen}} = \bigcup_{h\in W_{\mathrm{irr}}} hC$, the coarsening of $\F^{\cox}$ obtained by deleting the faces $\bigcap_j\alpha_j^\perp\cap C$ spanned by irrelevant roots. **It is not formed here.** [@AEGS25, §5.2], p. 610, derives the marking of $\fram$ from the ramification divisor: the relevant roots are those that lie on the boundary of the square, respectively the triangle. The corresponding datum on the Coble side is the divisor $R_{\bar S}$ of a Coble stable pair $(\bar S,\eps R_{\bar S})$, and it is not defined on these pages; see the warning below.
:::

::: {.proof}
(1) is [@AEGS25, Def. 5.1], read with @thm:coble-cusp-chamber. The criterion stated there for the semifan (@def:semitoroidal-compactification) to be a fan is that the Coxeter polyhedron $P$ have finite volume, equivalently that $W$ have finite index in $\Orth(\Lambda)$; the chamber here is a simplex of finite volume with one ideal vertex, its $f$-vector being $(9,36,84,126,126,84,36,9,1)$ and its transcript reporting one vertex at infinity.

(2) By the same definition the Type II rays are the rays $\RR_{\ge0}v$ with $v^2 = 0$ on $\partial C_\QQ$, in bijection with the maximal parabolic subdiagrams of $G$, which by @thm:coble-cusp-chamber are the nine one-node deletions. A facet of a finite-volume chamber lies at infinity exactly when its own reflection group does not have finite covolume (@def:covolume), that is when the deletion is affine rather than elliptic. The recorded run `computations/coxiter/logs/9-9-1_1.txt` reports $8$ finite vertices and $1$ vertex at infinity, and a simplex with nine facets has nine vertices; so exactly one of the nine deletions is affine. That facet is the one @thm:coble-cusp-chamber identifies, carrying the adjacent $1$-cusp with boundary lattice $(7,7,1)_0$.

(3) By the definition the Type III cones correspond to the elliptic subdiagrams of $G$, and the face attached to $S$ is $\bigcap_{j\in S}\alpha_j^{\perp}\cap C$, of dimension $8-\abs{S}$ for a simplex chamber; the empty $S$ gives $C$, the singletons the nine facets, and in general the $k$-subsets the faces of dimension $8-k$. A subset gives a face of $C$ lying in the interior of the cone exactly when its diagram is elliptic, and the enumeration is read off the record: the transcript reports the $f$-vector $(9,36,84,126,126,84,36,9,1)$ of a simplex and exactly one vertex at infinity, so of the nine $8$-subsets eight are elliptic and one is the affine one of (2), which contributes the Type II ray instead; the whole of $G$ is hyperbolic and contributes nothing. Summing over $k = 0,\dots,8$ with the $8$-level reduced by that one gives $510$.

::: {.proposition #prop:coble-pair-boundary title="The boundary of the Coble pair, derived from the degree-$2$ construction"}
Let $S$ be an unnodal (@def:nodal-enriques-surface) Coble surface (@def:coble-surface) with a degree-$2$ polarization (@def:numerical-polarization) $h = [F_1+F_2]$, $\varphi_h\colon S\to D\subset\PP^4$ the map given by $\abs{2h}$ onto a $4$-nodal quartic del Pezzo surface (@def:degree-of-del-pezzo-surface) $D$, $\sigma$ its deck transformation, $W$ the fixed curve of $\sigma$, and $\mathfrak c\in\abs{-2K_S}$ the anti-canonical curve. Then:

1. the fixed locus of $\sigma$ is the smooth curve $W$ of genus $4$ together with three isolated points, so $W$ is the ramification divisor of $\varphi_h$ and the isolated points contribute no component; $\mathfrak c$ maps to a singular point $q$ of $D$, $W$ meets $\mathfrak c$ in two points, and the image $\bar W\subset D$ has arithmetic genus $5$ with a double point at $q$ and is a complete intersection (@def:complete-intersection-variety) of a net of quadrics ([@DK13], §8);
2. $K_S = -\tfrac12\mathfrak c$, since $\mathfrak c\in\abs{-2K_S}$, so $K_S+\tfrac12\mathfrak c\equiv0$; and $\mathfrak c^2 = -4$ gives $K_S^2 = -1$, so $K_S$ is not numerically trivial. On the standard model $S = \Bl_{p_1,\dots,p_{10}}\PP^2$, where $\mathfrak c = 6H-2\sum_iE_i$ and $K_S = -3H+\sum_iE_i$, every exceptional curve has $K_S\cdot E_i = -1$ and $\mathfrak c\cdot E_i = 2$;
3. consequently $(S,\eps W)$ is **not** a KSBA pair for any small $\eps>0$: $(K_S+\eps W)\cdot E_i = -1+\eps(W\cdot E_i) < 0$ for $\eps\ll1$, whatever $W$ is;
4. the boundary must carry $\mathfrak c$ with the coefficient that makes the pair numerically Calabi--Yau: for $0<\eps\le\tfrac12$,
$$
\Delta \definedas \tfrac12\mathfrak c + \eps W, \qquad K_S+\Delta \equiv \eps W,
$$
and $(S,\Delta)$ is lc (hence slc): the two smooth curves form a simple normal crossings divisor, and their coefficients are at most one;
5. hence $(S,\tfrac12\mathfrak c+\eps W)$ is a KSBA pair with $0<\eps\le\tfrac12$ exactly when $W$ is ample, which is the step the citations do not give; see the warning.
:::


::: {.proof}
(1) is [@DK13], §8: the deck transformation fixes a smooth curve $W$ of genus $4$ and three isolated points, and the anti-canonical curve is mapped to a singular point of $D$; the ramification divisor of a double cover is its fixed curve, so the ramification of $\varphi_h$ is $W$.

(2) reads the sign off the same passage: $\mathfrak c\in\abs{-2K_S}$ there, so $K_S = -\tfrac12\mathfrak c$ and $K_S+\tfrac12\mathfrak c\equiv0$; squaring gives $4K_S^2 = \mathfrak c^2 = -4$, hence $K_S^2 = -1$. On the blow-up of $\PP^2$ at the ten nodes of a rational sextic, $\mathfrak c$ is the proper transform (@def:strict-transform) $6H-2\sum_iE_i$ and $K_S = -3H+\sum_iE_i$, so $K_S\cdot E_i = -1$ and $\mathfrak c\cdot E_i = 2$.

(3) is immediate from (2): $(K_S+\eps W)\cdot E_i = -1+\eps(W\cdot E_i)$, and $W\cdot E_i\ge0$ because $E_i$ is a curve and $W$ is effective; the value is negative for $\eps$ small enough, so $K_S+\eps W$ is not nef and in particular not ample.

(4) adds the two classes: $K_S+\tfrac12\mathfrak c+\eps W\equiv\eps W$ by (2). For log canonicity, the unique curve $\mathfrak c\in|-2K_S|$ is preserved by the involution and is not fixed pointwise, since its fixed curve is $W$. Uniqueness follows from $\mathfrak c^2=-4$: any effective member must contain $\mathfrak c$ and its residual effective divisor has trivial class, hence is zero. At each of the two intersection points the involution acts by $-1$ on the tangent of $\mathfrak c$ (a nontrivial involution of a smooth rational curve) and by $+1$ on the tangent of $W$. The two eigendirections are distinct. Thus the two smooth curves meet transversely and form a simple normal crossings divisor. The pair with coefficients in $[0,1]$ is log canonical by the simple normal crossings criterion, hence slc.

(5) is the definition of a KSBA pair: slc with $K+\Delta$ ample, and $K_S+\Delta\equiv\eps W$ is ample exactly when $W$ is.
:::

::: {.proposition #prop:coble-pair-class title="The class of the fixed curve, and what Nakai--Moishezon gives for it"}
With $S$, $h$, $\varphi_h\colon S\to D\subset\PP^4$, $W$ and $\mathfrak c$ as in @prop:coble-pair-boundary, and $\varphi_h^*\mathcal O_D(1) = 2h$:

1. $W \equiv K_S + 2h$. Consequently $W^2 = 7$, $K_S\cdot W = -1$, $W\cdot\mathfrak c = 2$, and $2g(W)-2 = W^2 + K_S\cdot W = 6$, that is $g(W) = 4$, which is the genus [@DK13], §8 assigns to $W$;
2. $W^2 = 7 > 0$ and $W\cdot\mathfrak c = 2 > 0$;
3. the branch curve $B=\bar W\subset D$ is a Cartier divisor in $|\mathcal O_D(2)|$, and $\varphi_h^*B=2W+\mathfrak c$. For every irreducible curve $C\neq\mathfrak c$, the projection formula therefore gives
$$
W\cdot C=2(h\cdot C)-\tfrac12(\mathfrak c\cdot C)=\tfrac12\bigl(\deg(\varphi_h|_C)(B\cdot\varphi_h(C))-\mathfrak c\cdot C\bigr).
$$
The ampleness of $B$ does not by itself give the strict positivity of $W\cdot C$ on the resolution; that positivity is proved in @thm:coble-pair-ksba.
:::

::: {.proof}
(1) Hurwitz's formula for the double cover $\varphi_h$, away from its contracted curve, reads $K_S = \varphi_h^*K_D + R$ with $R$ the ramification divisor, and $R = W$ by @prop:coble-pair-boundary(1). Since $D$ is anticanonically embedded --- a del Pezzo surface (@def:degree-of-del-pezzo-surface) of degree (@def:graded-object) $4$ in $\PP^4$ --- one has $K_D = -\mathcal O_D(1)$, so
$$
W = R = K_S - \varphi_h^*K_D = K_S + \varphi_h^*\mathcal O_D(1) = K_S + 2h .
$$
The formula is applied on $S\smallsetminus\mathfrak c$, where $\varphi_h$ is finite; on $S$ itself the difference $W - K_S - 2h$ is a line bundle trivial off $\mathfrak c$, hence of the form $m\mathfrak c$ for an integer $m$. The cited intersection $W\cdot\mathfrak c = 2$ ([@DK13], §8, p. 522: $W$ meets $\mathfrak c$ in two points) and $h\cdot\mathfrak c = 0$, $K_S\cdot\mathfrak c = 2$ give $2 = 2 + m\,(\mathfrak c^2) = 2 - 4m$, so $m = 0$.

The numbers follow in $K_S^{\perp}$: $h\cdot K_S = 0$ because $h$ is a polarization (@def:numerical-polarization) of $S$ lying in $K_S^{\perp}\cong E$ ([@DK13], §8, p. 522), so $W^2 = K_S^2 + 4h^2 = -1 + 8 = 7$ and $K_S\cdot W = K_S^2 = -1$; and $\mathfrak c = -2K_S$ gives $W\cdot\mathfrak c = -2K_S\cdot W = 2$. Then $2g(W)-2 = W\cdot(W+K_S) = 7 - 1 = 6$, which agrees with the genus $4$ of [@DK13], §8, p. 522.

(2) is the first two displays.

(3) In [@DK13, §8, p. 522], $D$ is a complete intersection of two quadrics, and $B$ is cut out on $D$ by the additional quadric of the net. Thus $B\sim 2\mathcal O_D(1)$ is Cartier and ample. Away from $\mathfrak c$ the finite map is ramified to order two along $W$, so its Cartier pullback is $2W+m\mathfrak c$ for an integer $m$. Intersect with $\mathfrak c$: the projection formula gives zero because $\mathfrak c$ is contracted, while $(2W+m\mathfrak c)\cdot\mathfrak c=4-4m$ by (1). Hence $m=1$. Intersecting the pullback with any noncontracted $C$ gives the stated equality; the other expression follows from (1).
:::

::: {.proposition #prop:coble-negative-curves title="The curves of negative square on an unnodal Coble surface, and $W$ on each"}
Let $S$ be an unnodal (@def:nodal-enriques-surface) Coble surface (@def:coble-surface), so that $\Pic(S) = E\perp\ZZ K_S$ with $E = K_S^{\perp}\cong\II_{1,9}$ even (@def:form-axioms) unimodular, and let $C$ be an irreducible curve with $C^2<0$. Write $C = x + mK_S$ with $x\in E$ and $m = -K_S\cdot C$, so that $\mathfrak c\cdot C = 2m$ and $W\cdot C = 2\,(h\cdot x) - m$. Then:

1. adjunction gives $p_a(C) = 1 + \tfrac12(C^2 - m)$, so $C^2 \ge m-2$; with $C^2<0$ this forces $m\le1$;
2. $m\le-1$: then $W\cdot C = 2(h\cdot x) + \abs{m} \ge 1$;
3. $m = 0$: then $C^2 = -2$ and $p_a(C) = 0$, that is $C$ is a $(-2)$-curve, and there is none;
4. $m = 1$: then $C^2 = -1$ and $p_a(C) = 0$, that is $C$ is a $(-1)$-curve, $x^2 = 0$, and $h\cdot C = h\cdot x \ge 1$; so $W\cdot C = 2(h\cdot C)-1 \ge 1$.

Consequently $W\cdot C>0$ for every irreducible curve of negative square.
:::

::: {.proof}
(1) Adjunction on the smooth surface $S$ gives $2p_a(C)-2 = C^2 + K_S\cdot C = C^2 - m$, so $p_a(C) = 1 + \tfrac12(C^2-m)$, which is a non-negative integer; hence $C^2 \ge m-2$, and $C^2<0$ gives $m < 2$.

(2) is the formula $W\cdot C = 2(h\cdot x) - m$ with $m\le-1$ and $h\cdot x = h\cdot C \ge 0$, the latter because $h$ is a polarization (@def:numerical-polarization) and $C$ is effective.

(3) With $m = 0$ the integrality in (1) forces $C^2$ even, and $C^2<0$ with $C^2\ge-2$ leaves $C^2 = -2$, so $p_a(C) = 0$ and $C$ is a smooth rational curve of self-intersection $-2$. The surface is unnodal, so there is none ([@DK13], §8).

(4) With $m = 1$ the integrality forces $C^2$ odd, and $C^2<0$ with $C^2\ge-1$ leaves $C^2 = -1$, $p_a(C) = 0$. Then $x^2 = C^2 - K_S^2m^2 = -1+1 = 0$, $x\perp K_S$, and $h\cdot C = h\cdot x$. If $h\cdot x = 0$ then $x$ lies in $h^{\perp}\cap E$, which is negative definite (@def:definiteness) because $E$ has signature (@def:signature) $(1,9)$ and $h^2 = 2>0$; a vector of square $0$ in a negative definite lattice is $0$, so $x = 0$ and $C = K_S$, which is not effective since $\abs{K_S} = \varnothing$. Hence $h\cdot x\ge1$.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:coble-pair-ksba title="The fixed curve is ample, and the Coble pair is a KSBA stable pair"}
Let $S$ be an unnodal (@def:nodal-enriques-surface) Coble surface (@def:coble-surface), $h$ a degree-$2$ polarization (@def:numerical-polarization), $W$ the fixed curve of the deck transformation of $\varphi_h$ and $\mathfrak c\in\abs{-2K_S}$. Then $W$ is ample, and for $0<\eps\le\tfrac12$ the pair $\bigl(S,\;\tfrac12\mathfrak c+\eps W\bigr)$ is a KSBA stable pair.
:::

::: {.proof}
Ampleness by the Nakai--Moishezon criterion, in the two cases of the sign of $C^2$ for an irreducible curve $C$.

$W^2 = 7>0$ by @prop:coble-pair-class(1).

*$C^2\ge0$.* $W$ is the fixed curve of an involution, hence effective, and $W^2 = 7>0$, so $W$ lies in the interior of the positive cone (@not:reflection-groups-sign-convention) of $S$; $C$, being effective, lies in its closure. Write $C = aW + C'$ with $C'\perp W$. Since the intersection form is negative definite (@def:definiteness) on $W^{\perp}$ (the Hodge index theorem, $W^2>0$) one has $C'^2\le0$, so $C^2 = 7a^2 + C'^2 \ge 0$ forces $a\neq0$ whenever $C\neq0$; then $W\cdot C = 7a$, and $a>0$ because $W$ and $C$ lie in the same cone (@def:closed-cone). Hence $W\cdot C>0$.

*$C^2<0$.* @prop:coble-negative-curves gives $W\cdot C>0$ in every case of its list.

So $W$ is ample. For the pair: $K_S+\tfrac12\mathfrak c+\eps W\equiv\eps W$ by @prop:coble-pair-boundary(4), which is ample since $W$ is; and $(S,\tfrac12\mathfrak c+\eps W)$ is slc by the transverse intersection and simple normal crossings argument of @prop:coble-pair-boundary(4). A pair that is slc with $K+\Delta$ ample is a KSBA stable pair.
:::

::: {.remark #rmk:coble-relevance-state title="What the sources say about relevance at a Coble cusp"}
The marking of relevance is **not** read off a boundary divisor by [@AEGS25]. Their generalized semifans of $F_{(2,2,0)}$ are taken from [2], §9A --- in their notation $\mathcal F^{ram}$, where the irrelevant roots are those that do not lie on the boundary of the square, respectively the triangle, in Figure 5 --- and [@AEGS25, Def. 5.3], obtains the semifans of $\fentwo$ by intersecting them with $T_\En$. [@AEGS25, Lem. 5.5], proves, from [@AEGS25, Lem. 3.8], that the intersection is the generalized Coxeter semifan of the folded diagram with the folded marking of [@AEGS25, Def. 5.4]. So the reading of relevance off a divisor is that of [2], §9A, quoted by [@AEGS25], and not a criterion stated in [@AEGS25] in the form "the node is relevant when the divisor meets the wall's curve"; an earlier version of this page asserted the latter and it is withdrawn.

What does survive is a lattice obstruction, and it is a citation. [@AEGS25, Lem. 3.9; @AEGS25, Lem. 3.10; @AEGS25, Cor. 3.12], give the fixed lattices of the five involutions: $U(2)\oplus E_8(2)$ for cusp $1$ and $U\oplus E_8(2)$ for cusps $2, 3, 4, 5$. Those four quotient lattices are therefore isometric, while by Lemma 5.6, $W_{\mathrm{irr}}$ is $S_2$ at cusp $2$, $S_2^2$ at cusp $4$ and infinite at cusps $1$, $3$, $5$. A datum that varies across isometric lattices is not a function of the lattice and its chamber: the marking is extra data, and no computation inside $T_\Co$ produces it.

The effective marking under restriction to a $(-2)$-Heegner sublattice is given by the dihedral criterion of @thm:semifan-restriction. At a $0$-cusp of $F_{\En,2}$ a $(-2)$-root $\delta\perp e$ has its reflection among the generators of $W(\Gamma_{\En,2,e})$ ([@AEGS25, Def. 3.6]), so $\bar\delta$ is a mirror of $W$. Case B, including the possibility of an irrelevant cut mirror, applies at all four arithmetic Coble cusps: its hypotheses are verified in @thm:coble-pair-semifan. The effective marks are not in general the literal ambient marks. This calculation does not give the intrinsic dictionary from roots to curves at the unpolarized cusp.
:::

::: {.warning #warn:coble-marking title="The step that fails: the dictionary from the roots of the chamber to curves on $S$"}
The split cannot be computed, and no node's assignment is proved, because the sources read give no dictionary from the nine roots of $\bar T$ to curves of the Coble surface meeting $\tfrac12\mathfrak c+\eps W$. [@AEGS25, §5.2], p. 610, reads the marking of $\fram$ from the explicit geometry of their diagrams, where the relevant roots are visibly the ones on the boundary of the square, respectively the triangle; [@DK13], §8, p. 522, describes $S$ through $\varphi_h$ but says nothing about the curves attached to the roots of the cusp lattice. The statement this step needs is a **cusp marking**: for the one $0$-cusp of $\fco$, the identification of the nine simple roots of the chamber with the curves of $S$ (equivalently, with the $(-2)$-curves of the K3 cover) which the period map realises --- the Coble analogue of the identification [@AEGS25] obtain from Sterk's and Alexeev--Nikulin's explicit diagrams. ## The generalized Coxeter semifan at the four $0$-cusps of $\bD(T_\Co)/R_{\gent}$ {#sec-coble-pair-semifan}

The degree-$2$ numerical polarization in @thm:coble-pair-ksba determines the actual classical open locus of the degree-$2$ Coble arithmetic quotient (@lem:d2-degree-two-base). Its period group is
$$
\Gamma_{\Co,2}=R_{\gent}\cap\Orth^+(T_\Co),\qquad
R_{\gent}=\text{the image of }\Stab_{\gent}(\delta)\text{ in }\Orth(T_\Co),
$$
by @thm:gamma-co-is-the-stack-monodromy. The quotient by $R_{\gent}$, which allows both components, is the arithmetic normalization of the Heegner divisor $\Delta_2$ of $\fentwo$ (@thm:normalization); restricting to one component gives the quotient by $\Gamma_{\Co,2}$ (@prop:coble-polarized-actual-period-loci). This is not an identification of the whole arithmetic quotient with the actual smooth classical locus. The vector is $\delta=e-f$ of square $-2$ and $T_\Co=\delta^{\perp T_\En}$ (@prop:cusp-white-root-complements). The semifans at the four arithmetic cusps are inherited by restriction from the Enriques semifans through @thm:semifan-restriction. Their identification with the normalization of the actual degree-$2$ stable-pair closure, formed using the contracted pairs, is @cor:coble-polarized-semitoroidal-closures, not a conclusion from the ampleness of the resolution pair alone. The unmarked half --- the Coxeter semifan at the one $0$-cusp of $F^{\mathrm{un}}_\Co$ --- is @thm:coble-coxeter-semifan.

The definitions used are those of [@AEGS25]: the Coxeter semifan (Definition 5.1); the generalized Coxeter semifan $\mathcal F^{\mathrm{gen}}(I\sqcup J)$ of a split of the diagram into relevant and irrelevant roots, with $C_{\mathrm{gen}} = \bigcup_{h\in W_{\mathrm{irr}}} hC$ (after [@AET23], §4D and [2], §9A); and the marking of the Enriques diagrams (Definition 5.3, with Definition 5.4 and Lemma 5.5). The fan and rank-two conclusions use the actual restricted chambers and the local criterion of @thm:semifan-restriction, rather than inferring them from the ambient Enriques fan verdict.

Then:

**(0) The cusps.** The lattice group $R = \bar\rho^{-1}(P)$ has exactly four orbits (@def:group-orbit) on the primitive isotropic lines of $T_\Co$, one over each of Sterk's $0$-cusps $k = 2,3,4,5$ and none over cusp $1$, and the $0$-cusps of $D(T_\Co)/R$ are those four (@thm:cusp-fibres-four, with @thm:four-r-orbits and @thm:orbit-per-cusp); these four are also precisely the cusps of the arithmetic quotient for the actual group $\Gamma_{\Co,2}=R\cap\Orth^+(T_\Co)$ by @thm:gamma-co-is-the-stack-monodromy and @thm:o2-positive-parabolic-families, which proves equality of the integral line orbit sets, not equality of the two groups. of rank $9$, determinant $-2^9$ and signature $(1,8)$.

::: {.proof}
*(0)* The four cusps are @thm:four-r-orbits, and the sharper form is @thm:orbit-per-cusp: $R$ has four orbits on the primitive isotropic lines of $T_\Co$, and the orbit of Sterk's representative $\eta_k$ lies over Sterk's cusp $k$ for $k = 2,3,4,5$, in the $P$-orbits of sizes $2$, $120$, $270$, $135$. So it is enough to exhibit four primitive isotropic vectors of $T_\Co$ in four different $R$-orbits, each lying over the Enriques cusp claimed for it, which the assignment $k\leftrightarrow\mathcal O$ above does. The printed representatives of @ex:sterk-isotropic-divisibilities are isotropic with divisibility $2$ in $T_\En$; every one of them is orthogonal to $\delta$ (the transcript prints $\eta_k\cdot\delta = 0$ for $k = 2,3,4,5$), hence lies in $T_\Co = \delta^{\perp T_\En}$; and the Enriques cusp $[\eta_k]$ is $k$ by construction. They are pairwise inequivalent under $R = \rho^{-1}(P)$ because the $P$-orbit of $\bar\eta = \tfrac12\eta \bmod T_\En$ is an invariant of the $R$-orbit: $R$ acts on $A_{T_\Co}$ through $P$ (@prop:gamma-co-images(3), @def:gamma-co-groups(4)), and $\bar\eta$ is carried with it. The group $\Gamma_{\Co,2}$ equals $R\cap\Orth^+(T_\Co)$ by @thm:gamma-co-is-the-stack-monodromy, and is distinct from $R$. Every integral line stabilizer in $R$ has a component-reversing element in the discriminant kernel by @thm:o2-positive-parabolic-families; multiplying an orientation-reversing transporter by that element leaves its image of the line unchanged. Thus the orbits of $R$ and $\Gamma_{\Co,2}$ on lines agree exactly, and none of these four arithmetic cusps splits. The transcripts print the canonical representatives of the four classes in $\{0,\tfrac12\}^{12}$; their $U$ coordinates vanish, and their $U(2)$ and $E_8(2)$ coordinates are
$$
2:\ (1,0) \text{ with } E_8(2)\text{ trivial}, \quad
3:\ (1,1) \text{ with } E_8(2)\text{ nontrivial}, \quad
4:\ (0,1) \text{ with } E_8(2)\text{ nontrivial}, \quad
5:\ (0,0) \text{ with } E_8(2)\text{ nontrivial},
$$
which are in four different orbits of $P$: these are the orbits $\mathcal O_2 = \{u_1,u_2\}$, $\mathcal O_{120}$, $\mathcal O_{270}$ and $\mathcal O_{135}$ of @prop:coble-cong-point-stabilizer(2), whose description there is by the vanishing of the $E_8(2)$ component and by the three classes $u_1,u_2,u_3$ of $A_{U(2)}$ with $q(u_1) = q(u_2) = 0 \ne q(u_3)$, and isotropy forces the $E_8(2)$ component of a class with $u$-part $u_3$ to have value $1$. So the four orbits are the four, and the exhibit is complete. Cusp $1$ is not among them: its representative has divisibility $1$ in $T_\En$, and every primitive vector of $T_\Co$ has divisibility $2$ (@lem:divisibilityAlwaysTwoTco), which is @prop:cusp-map-not-onto. For the boundary lattice: the four cusp vectors are primitive isotropic vectors of one lattice and $\Orth(T_\Co)$ is transitive on those (@thm:unpolarized-cusps), so the four boundary lattices are isometric; the one over cusp $2$ is computed as $\generators{2}\oplus E_8(2)$ in the restricted semifan theorem, which is $(9,9,1)_1$ of rank $9$, determinant $-2^9$ and signature $(1,8)$.

*(1)* The exact input is the four wall Gram matrices in figure order, with their cut roots, in `computations/reports/coble_enriques-white-root-facets-run.txt`. To identify the marking in that order, compare the graphs in [@AEGS25, Figs. 7, 8] with those matrices. At cusp $2$ node $4$ is the off-boundary spur. At cusp $3$ the two chains $1,2,3,4,5$ and $1,6,7,8,9$ lie on the folded outer boundary, the interior chain between the two white corners is $9,10,11,5$, and node $12$ is the spur attached to $1$. The interior folded nodes are exactly $10,11,12$. At cusp $4$ the outer chain is $1,2,3,4,5$, with boundary branches $1,6,7$ and $5,8,9$; the two interior spurs are $10,11$. At cusp $5$ the outer cycle is $1,\ldots,8$ and the interior nodes are $9,\ldots,14$. This reads the marking from the fold of the interior vertices of Figure 5, as required by Definition 5.4; it also recovers the ambient finite-versus-infinite distinction of Lemma 5.6. In particular the earlier lists at cusps $3$ and $4$ mixed printed Sterk labels with figure-order labels.

For the cut node $d$ write the projected normal as $\nu_i=r_i+\tfrac12(r_i,r_d)r_d$; its Gram is
$$
P_{ij}=G_{ij}+\tfrac12G_{id}G_{jd}.
$$
The exact computation `computations/reports/coble_796_actual_restriction.py`, with output `coble_796_actual_restriction-run.txt`, checks rank nine, negative squares, and nonproportionality of every pair of projected normals using the rank of their two full Gram rows. A pair with $P_{ij}^2=P_{ii}P_{jj}$ need not be proportional; it may be a pair of independent parallel mirrors. The computation also checks that every inequality defines an actual facet, by the spanning rank of the extreme rays in its zero set. Thus (B5) holds for the corrected @thm:semifan-restriction. The ambient pairings with the cut are zero or two, the latter only for roots of square $-4$, so the ambient angles are $\pi/2$ and $\pi/4$. The projected normals are integral and the corresponding reflections are restrictions of ambient isometries preserving the perpendicular, by the dihedral half-turn in the proof of that theorem. Their intersecting dihedral angles are $\pi/2,\pi/3,\pi/4$, by the full projected Gram and the angle tables in @prop:facet-angles-aegs. These verify the other mirror hypotheses. The actual ambient coarsening is the generalized Coxeter semifan by [@AEGS25, Lem. 5.5]; hence the effective marking follows from @thm:semifan-restriction(3). At cusp $3$ the nonorthogonal cut meets nodes $4,11$, of which only $11$ is ambient irrelevant; at cusp $4$ the nonorthogonal incidence is node $8$, which is relevant. At cusp $5$ both the cut and the nonorthogonally incident node $13$ are irrelevant. These are the actual deletion tests, not a comparison of the number of projected walls.

*(2)* Taking the submatrices of $P$ on the effective irrelevant sets gives the four displayed Gram matrices. Negative definiteness of the first three and orthogonality of their generators yield the stated finite groups. At cusp $5$ the full five-by-five matrix has nonzero determinant of positive sign and contains a positive direction in the subspace on walls $13,14$ (their two-by-two principal determinant is $-8$). As it is a Gram matrix in a Lorentzian space, its signature is therefore $(1,4)$. The pair $10,13$ is independent and has Gram $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-2&2\\2&-2\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, hence the mirrors are parallel with distinct normals and their two reflections generate an infinite affine dihedral group. In particular no semidefinite-radical description of the entire five-wall group is valid.

*(3)* The rational polyhedral chamber assertion also follows directly from the ambient Coxeter fans of [@AEGS25, §5.1, Lem. 5.2]: intersection of a rational polyhedral cone in the rational closure of the ambient positive cone with the rational hyperplane is rational polyhedral. To compute its rays, choose a basis of nine independent projected normals using the rank-nine Gram matrix. Write its invertible Gram as $G_B$ and all wall linear forms in these coordinates as the rows of $A$. The halfspace cone $\{x:Ax\ge0\}$ is pointed, since the normals span. Every extreme ray is the common kernel of eight independent active rows; conversely a feasible ray with eight independent active rows is extreme. The computation enumerates every eight-subset of the rows, retains precisely the one-dimensional feasible kernels in either sign, and normalizes the ray to primitive integral coordinates before removing duplicates. It checks that all ray generators are in the same closed positive cone by their pairings with the sum of the rays, that this sum is timelike and lies strictly inside every halfspace, and that their span has rank nine. It classifies their squares exactly using $G_B$ and asserts the displayed counts, with no outside rays. The finite-volume criterion is @thm:coxeter-polytope-volume.

A finite effective irrelevant group merges finitely many of these rational polyhedral chambers, and their convex union is again rational polyhedral. The trace semifan is then a fan. At cusp $5$ its maximal merged cone contains the initial full-dimensional chamber and is preserved by the infinite subgroup generated by reflections in the effective irrelevant walls. If it were a rational polyhedral cone, it would be pointed (it lies in a closed positive cone) and that subgroup would permute its finite set of primitive lattice extreme-ray generators. An element fixing them all is the identity since the cone is full dimensional. This would make the subgroup finite, a contradiction. Thus this is a nonfan assertion for the **actual ambient intersection**, not for an unproved auxiliary marking.

At each ideal ray take its entire active set of walls, given by the zero entries of $Ax$. In the Euclidean parabolic arrangement on a horosphere, only the walls through that ray contribute to the stabilizer. The effective irrelevant subset of the active set generates the subgroup whose union of fundamental tiles defines the coarsened tile. By @thm:semifan-restriction(4) and [@AE23, Def. 5.11, Ex. 5.12] the corresponding Type II contraction is nonzero exactly when that subgroup is infinite. The active-root Gram is negative semidefinite because the roots are perpendicular to an isotropic vector; the subgroup is finite exactly when its effective irrelevant principal submatrix is negative definite. The exact computation tests its rank against its size at every ideal ray (a zero-size set is finite). At cusp $5$ the two singular submatrices contain the displayed affine triple and pair; all other submatrices are nonsingular. At cusps $2,3,4$ the entire effective irrelevant group is already finite, so all adjacent rank-two cusps are toroidal, as must hold for a fan. This proves the table and resolves the previous contradictory use of isolated finite components as contraction directions.

::: {.remark #rmk:coble-pair-semifan-marking title="The effective marking at a cut mirror"}

Literal inheritance of relevance is not the rule for a nonorthogonal mirror restriction. At an ambient even dihedral ridge, deleting one wall type alone does not join the two opposite rays of the restricted line. The correction is @thm:semifan-restriction(3). In particular cusp $3$ has an effective finite irrelevant group even though its ambient Enriques group is infinite. At cusp $5$ the cut is irrelevant, so the same rule does give the other five marks, whose full Gram is indefinite. The nonfan result there is established for the actual intersection.

:::

::: {.remark #rmk:coble-pair-semifan-scope title="What is inherited and what is not"}
The theorem gives the semifans at the four arithmetic $0$-cusps of the actual degree-$2$ monodromy quotient: the equality $\Gamma_{\Co,2}=R_{\gent}\cap\Orth^+(T_\Co)$ is @thm:gamma-co-is-the-stack-monodromy, and @thm:o2-positive-parabolic-families proves that the four line orbits do not split. The normalization of the KSBA closure of the actual polarized locus is identified with this restricted semifan by @cor:coble-polarized-semitoroidal-closures and @thm:coble-semitoroidal-normalized-closure. Neither the arithmetic comparison nor the normalization produces a canonical polarization or a marking for the unpolarized stack. In fact @thm:coble-symmetrized-compactification supplies a common-refinement compactification of its arithmetic quotient without requiring the degree-$2$ semifan to descend unchanged. The intrinsic marking at the unpolarized cusp is still missing (@warn:coble-marking).
:::
