## Mirror moves {#sec-mirror-moves}

The mirror move algorithm of [@AE22, Thm. 5.10], applied to the $2$-elementary lattice $S_\Co = (11, 11, 1)_1$, gives the 0-cusps and 1-cusps of $F^{\mathrm{un}}_\Co$ in @fig-mirror-moves-coble.

By @thm:unpolarized-cusps there is one $\Orth^+(T_\Co)$-orbit of $0$-cusps, represented by an isotropic vector $v_0$ with

$$
v_0^{\perp T_{\Co}}/\generators{v_0} \cong (9,9,1)_1 \cong \generators{2} \oplus E_{8}(2)
$$

The group $\widetilde\Orth^+(T_\Co)$ is not transitive on the primitive isotropic vectors (@lem:stable-orthogonal-not-transitive); it has $527$ orbits of them, one for each nonzero isotropic class of $A_{T_\Co}$ (@thm:coble-zero-cusp-count(2)), the fibre over one class being a single orbit (@thm:coble-integral-lifting-fibre(2)), and the discriminant kernel has $527$ as well.
This $0$-cusp $v_0$ is incident to one $\Orth^+(T_\Co)$-orbit of $1$-cusps, represented by an isotropic plane $J = \generators{v_0, v_1}$ with

$$
J^{\perp T_{\Co}}/\generators{J} \cong (7,7,1)_0 \cong A_1^{\oplus 7} ,
$$

where $v_1 \in v_0^{\perp T_{\Co}}/\generators{v_0}$.
In the diagrammatic language of [@AE22 Fig. 1, Thm. 5.10], this corresponds to a composite of two even ordinary mirror moves, with $H = U(2)$, summarized in the following mirror move diagram:

::: {.theorem #thm:coble-cusp-chamber title="The reflection chamber at the one $0$-cusp of $F^{\mathrm{un}}_\Co$"}
Let $v_0$ be a primitive isotropic vector of $T_\Co$, $\bar T = v_0^{\perp}/\langle v_0\rangle\cong(9,9,1)_1 = \generators{2}\oplus E_8(2)$ its boundary lattice, and $W^{(2,4)}(\bar T)$ the full reflection group (@def:reflection) of $\bar T$, generated by the reflections in all $(-2)$- and $(-4)$-roots ([@AN06, List of Notations and Thm. 3.1]). Then $F^{\mathrm{un}}_\Co = \bD(T_\Co)/\Orth(T_\Co)$ (@def:gamma-co-groups) has one $0$-cusp (@thm:unpolarized-cusps, computed by sage-extensions#286 in its $\Orth(T_\Co)$ row), and at it the chamber of $W^{(2,4)}(\bar T)$ is the simplex with nine walls whose adjacencies are

| wall | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| adjacent | 3 | 4 | 1, 4 | 3, 2, 5 | 4, 6 | 5, 7 | 6, 8 | 7, 9 | 8 |

with every bond of label $3$ except $8-9$, of label $4$. It is the chamber $\mathcal M^{(2,4)}$ of [@AN06, Table 1], entry $N = 10$, that is $(r,a,\delta) = (9,9,1)$ with $k = 0$, $g = 2$ and $l(H) = 0$; the source's convention is that a hyperbolic lattice of rank $r$ has signature $(1, r-1)$, here $(1,8)$, with roots of square $-2$ and $-4$, which is this volume's convention. Its Gram matrix lies over $\QQ[\sqrt2]$, it has $8$ finite vertices and one ideal vertex, and its $f$-vector is $(9,36,84,126,126,84,36,9,1)$.

The source's diagram and this wall list agree node by node, under
$$
1\mapsto v_1,\quad 3\mapsto v_2,\quad 4\mapsto v_3,\quad 2\mapsto v_9,\quad 5\mapsto v_4,\quad 6\mapsto v_5,\quad 7\mapsto v_6,\quad 8\mapsto v_7,\quad 9\mapsto v_8,
$$
where $v_1-\cdots-v_8$ is the source's chain, $v_9$ its node below $v_3$, $v_7-v_8$ its double bond, and $v_8$ its open vertex. The maximal parabolic subdiagrams (@def:parabolic-subdiagram) are the nine one-node deletions; the diagram has no nontrivial automorphism, so distinct deletions are distinct subdiagrams (@def:coxeter-subdiagram); the deletion whose remaining diagram is affine of type $\wt B_7$ is the facet carrying the adjacent $1$-cusp.
:::


::: {.theorem #thm:coble-coxeter-semifan title="The Coxeter semifan at the one $0$-cusp of $F^{\mathrm{un}}_\Co$, and what the generalized semifan still needs"}
Let $G$ be the nine-node chamber diagram of @thm:coble-cusp-chamber at the one $0$-cusp of $F^{\mathrm{un}}_\Co$ and $W = W^{(2,4)}(\bar T)$ its reflection group. Then:

1. the Coxeter semifan (@def:coxeter-semifan) $\F^{\cox}$ of [@AEGS25, Def. 5.1] --- support $C_\QQ$, maximal cones the chambers of $W$ --- is a **fan**, the chamber having finite volume;
2. its **Type II rays** are in bijection with the maximal parabolic subdiagrams of $G$, which are the nine one-node deletions of @thm:coble-cusp-chamber; exactly one of them is affine, of type $\wt B_7$. The ray it gives is the ideal vertex of the simplex --- the intersection of the eight walls other than the deleted one --- and not a facet: it is the unique vertex at infinity that the recorded run reports, and it is the $1$-cusp adjacent to $v_0$, whose boundary lattice is $(7,7,1)_0 = A_1^{\oplus 7}$ (`computations/reports/one_cusp_plane_quotient.py` and its transcript);
3. its **Type III cones** are the faces $\bigcap_{j\in S}\alpha_j^{\perp}\cap C$ for the elliptic subdiagrams (@def:elliptic-subdiagram) $S\subseteq\{1,\dots,9\}$ of $G$ --- all of them, the empty one included, which gives $C$ itself --- of every dimension, the face of an $S$ with $\abs{S} = k$ having dimension $8-k$. Since $G$ is a simplex diagram the faces of $C$ are counted by the $f$-vector $(9,36,84,126,126,84,36,9,1)$; a subset gives a face of the interior exactly when its diagram is elliptic, and the recorded run reports that the nine $8$-subsets give eight finite vertices and one vertex at infinity, so eight of them are elliptic and the ninth is the affine one of (2), while the whole of $G$ is neither. The Type III cones therefore number
$$
1+9+36+84+126+126+84+36+8 = 510,
$$
one for each elliptic subdiagram.

The generalized Coxeter semifan (@def:generalized-coxeter-semifan) of [@AEGS25, §5.2], p. 610 (after [@AET23, §4D]) is formed from a split $I\sqcup J$ of the nine nodes into relevant and irrelevant roots, by $C_{\mathrm{gen}} = \bigcup_{h\in W_{\mathrm{irr}}} hC$, the coarsening of $\F^{\cox}$ obtained by deleting the faces $\bigcap_j\alpha_j^\perp\cap C$ spanned by irrelevant roots. **It is not formed here.** [@AEGS25, §5.2], p. 610, derives the marking of $\fram$ from the ramification divisor: the relevant roots are those that lie on the boundary of the square, respectively the triangle. The corresponding datum on the Coble side is the divisor $R_{\bar S}$ of a Coble stable pair $(\bar S,\eps R_{\bar S})$, and it is not defined on these pages; see the warning below.
:::

::: {.proof}
(1) is [@AEGS25, Def. 5.1], read with @thm:coble-cusp-chamber. The criterion stated there for the semifan (@def:semitoroidal-compactification) to be a fan is that the Coxeter polyhedron $P$ have finite volume, equivalently that $W$ have finite index in $\Orth(\Lambda)$; the chamber here is a simplex of finite volume with one ideal vertex, its $f$-vector being $(9,36,84,126,126,84,36,9,1)$ and its transcript reporting one vertex at infinity.

(2) By the same definition the Type II rays are the rays $\RR_{\ge0}v$ with $v^2 = 0$ on $\partial C_\QQ$, in bijection with the maximal parabolic subdiagrams of $G$, which by @thm:coble-cusp-chamber are the nine one-node deletions. A facet of a finite-volume chamber lies at infinity exactly when its own reflection group does not have finite covolume (@def:covolume), that is when the deletion is affine rather than elliptic. The recorded run `computations/coxiter/logs/9-9-1_1.txt` reports $8$ finite vertices and $1$ vertex at infinity, and a simplex with nine facets has nine vertices; so exactly one of the nine deletions is affine. That facet is the one @thm:coble-cusp-chamber identifies, carrying the adjacent $1$-cusp with boundary lattice $(7,7,1)_0$.

(3) By the definition the Type III cones correspond to the elliptic subdiagrams of $G$, and the face attached to $S$ is $\bigcap_{j\in S}\alpha_j^{\perp}\cap C$, of dimension $8-\abs{S}$ for a simplex chamber; the empty $S$ gives $C$, the singletons the nine facets, and in general the $k$-subsets the faces of dimension $8-k$. A subset gives a face of $C$ lying in the interior of the cone exactly when its diagram is elliptic, and the enumeration is read off the record: the transcript reports the $f$-vector $(9,36,84,126,126,84,36,9,1)$ of a simplex and exactly one vertex at infinity, so of the nine $8$-subsets eight are elliptic and one is the affine one of (2), which contributes the Type II ray instead; the whole of $G$ is hyperbolic and contributes nothing. Summing over $k = 0,\dots,8$ with the $8$-level reduced by that one gives $510$.

::: {.proposition #prop:coble-pair-boundary title="The boundary of the Coble pair, derived from the degree-$2$ construction"}
Let $S$ be an unnodal (@def:nodal-enriques-surface) Coble surface (@def:coble-surface) with a degree-$2$ polarization (@def:numerical-polarization) $h = [F_1+F_2]$, $\varphi_h\colon S\to D\subset\PP^4$ the map given by $\abs{2h}$ onto a $4$-nodal quartic del Pezzo surface $D$, $\sigma$ its deck transformation, $W$ the fixed curve of $\sigma$, and $\mathfrak c\in\abs{-2K_S}$ the anti-canonical curve. Then:

1. the fixed locus of $\sigma$ is the smooth curve $W$ of genus $4$ together with three isolated points, so $W$ is the ramification divisor of $\varphi_h$ and the isolated points contribute no component; $\mathfrak c$ maps to a singular point $q$ of $D$, $W$ meets $\mathfrak c$ in two points, and the image $\bar W\subset D$ has arithmetic genus $5$ with a double point at $q$ and is a complete intersection of a net of quadrics ([@DK13], §8);
2. $K_S = -\tfrac12\mathfrak c$, since $\mathfrak c\in\abs{-2K_S}$, so $K_S+\tfrac12\mathfrak c\equiv0$; and $\mathfrak c^2 = -4$ gives $K_S^2 = -1$, so $K_S$ is not numerically trivial. On the standard model $S = \Bl_{p_1,\dots,p_{10}}\PP^2$, where $\mathfrak c = 6H-2\sum_iE_i$ and $K_S = -3H+\sum_iE_i$, every exceptional curve has $K_S\cdot E_i = -1$ and $\mathfrak c\cdot E_i = 2$;
3. consequently $(S,\eps W)$ is **not** a KSBA pair for any small $\eps>0$: $(K_S+\eps W)\cdot E_i = -1+\eps(W\cdot E_i) < 0$ for $\eps\ll1$, whatever $W$ is;
4. the boundary must carry $\mathfrak c$ with the coefficient that makes the pair numerically Calabi--Yau: for $0<\eps\le\tfrac12$,
$$
\Delta \definedas \tfrac12\mathfrak c + \eps W, \qquad K_S+\Delta \equiv \eps W,
$$
and $(S,\Delta)$ is slc, $S$ being smooth and the coefficients $\tfrac12$ and $\eps$ lying in $[0,1]$ with sum $\eps+\tfrac12\le1$ at the two points $W\cap\mathfrak c$;
5. hence $(S,\tfrac12\mathfrak c+\eps W)$ is a KSBA pair with $0<\eps\le\tfrac12$ exactly when $W$ is ample, which is the step the citations do not give; see the warning.
:::


::: {.proof}
(1) is [@DK13], §8: the deck transformation fixes a smooth curve $W$ of genus $4$ and three isolated points, and the anti-canonical curve is mapped to a singular point of $D$; the ramification divisor of a double cover is its fixed curve, so the ramification of $\varphi_h$ is $W$.

(2) reads the sign off the same passage: $\mathfrak c\in\abs{-2K_S}$ there, so $K_S = -\tfrac12\mathfrak c$ and $K_S+\tfrac12\mathfrak c\equiv0$; squaring gives $4K_S^2 = \mathfrak c^2 = -4$, hence $K_S^2 = -1$. On the blow-up of $\PP^2$ at the ten nodes of a rational sextic, $\mathfrak c$ is the proper transform $6H-2\sum_iE_i$ and $K_S = -3H+\sum_iE_i$, so $K_S\cdot E_i = -1$ and $\mathfrak c\cdot E_i = 2$.

(3) is immediate from (2): $(K_S+\eps W)\cdot E_i = -1+\eps(W\cdot E_i)$, and $W\cdot E_i\ge0$ because $E_i$ is a curve and $W$ is effective; the value is negative for $\eps$ small enough, so $K_S+\eps W$ is not nef and in particular not ample.

(4) adds the two classes: $K_S+\tfrac12\mathfrak c+\eps W\equiv\eps W$ by (2). For log canonicity: $S$ is smooth, so a boundary with coefficients in $[0,1]$ is lc, and the only points where two components of $\Delta$ meet are the two points of $W\cap\mathfrak c$, where the coefficient sum is $\tfrac12+\eps\le1$ for $\eps\le\tfrac12$. The pair is therefore slc.

(5) is the definition of a KSBA pair: slc with $K+\Delta$ ample, and $K_S+\Delta\equiv\eps W$ is ample exactly when $W$ is.
:::

::: {.proposition #prop:coble-pair-class title="The class of the fixed curve, and what Nakai--Moishezon gives for it"}
With $S$, $h$, $\varphi_h\colon S\to D\subset\PP^4$, $W$ and $\mathfrak c$ as in @prop:coble-pair-boundary, and $\varphi_h^*\mathcal O_D(1) = 2h$:

1. $W \equiv K_S + 2h$. Consequently $W^2 = 7$, $K_S\cdot W = -1$, $W\cdot\mathfrak c = 2$, and $2g(W)-2 = W^2 + K_S\cdot W = 6$, that is $g(W) = 4$, which is the genus [@DK13], §8 assigns to $W$;
2. $W^2 = 7 > 0$ and $W\cdot\mathfrak c = 2 > 0$;
3. for every irreducible curve $C\neq\mathfrak c$ one has $W\cdot C = 2\,(h\cdot C) - \tfrac12\,(\mathfrak c\cdot C)$, and $W\cdot C > 0$ for every such $C$ if and only if the branch curve $B\subset D$ of $\varphi_h$ is ample.

The last equivalence (@def:equivalence-of-objects) is the step not completed here; see the warning.
:::

::: {.proof}
(1) Hurwitz's formula for the double cover $\varphi_h$, away from its contracted curve, reads $K_S = \varphi_h^*K_D + R$ with $R$ the ramification divisor, and $R = W$ by @prop:coble-pair-boundary(1). Since $D$ is anticanonically embedded --- a del Pezzo surface of degree $4$ in $\PP^4$ --- one has $K_D = -\mathcal O_D(1)$, so
$$
W = R = K_S - \varphi_h^*K_D = K_S + \varphi_h^*\mathcal O_D(1) = K_S + 2h .
$$
The formula is applied on $S\smallsetminus\mathfrak c$, where $\varphi_h$ is finite; on $S$ itself the difference $W - K_S - 2h$ is a line bundle trivial off $\mathfrak c$, hence of the form $m\mathfrak c$ for an integer $m$. The cited intersection $W\cdot\mathfrak c = 2$ ([@DK13], §8, p. 522: $W$ meets $\mathfrak c$ in two points) and $h\cdot\mathfrak c = 0$, $K_S\cdot\mathfrak c = 2$ give $2 = 2 + m\,(\mathfrak c^2) = 2 - 4m$, so $m = 0$.

The numbers follow in $K_S^{\perp}$: $h\cdot K_S = 0$ because $h$ is a polarization (@def:numerical-polarization) of $S$ lying in $K_S^{\perp}\cong E$ ([@DK13], §8, p. 522), so $W^2 = K_S^2 + 4h^2 = -1 + 8 = 7$ and $K_S\cdot W = K_S^2 = -1$; and $\mathfrak c = -2K_S$ gives $W\cdot\mathfrak c = -2K_S\cdot W = 2$. Then $2g(W)-2 = W\cdot(W+K_S) = 7 - 1 = 6$, which agrees with the genus $4$ of [@DK13], §8, p. 522.

(2) is the first two displays.

(3) Every irreducible $C\neq\mathfrak c$ is not contracted by $\varphi_h$, so its image is a curve and $W\cdot C = \deg(\varphi_h|_C)\,(B\cdot\varphi_h(C))$, while the class formula of (1) gives $W\cdot C = 2(h\cdot C) - \tfrac12(\mathfrak c\cdot C)$. Both expressions have the sign of the intersection of $B$ with a curve of $D$, so $W\cdot C > 0$ for all $C\neq\mathfrak c$ exactly when $B$ meets every curve of $D$ positively, that is when $B$ is ample.
:::

::: {.proposition #prop:coble-negative-curves title="The curves of negative square on an unnodal Coble surface, and $W$ on each"}
Let $S$ be an unnodal (@def:nodal-enriques-surface) Coble surface (@def:coble-surface), so that $\Pic(S) = E\perp\ZZ K_S$ with $E = K_S^{\perp}\cong\II_{1,9}$ even (@def:form-axioms) unimodular, and let $C$ be an irreducible curve with $C^2<0$. Write $C = x + mK_S$ with $x\in E$ and $m = -K_S\cdot C$, so that $\mathfrak c\cdot C = 2m$ and $W\cdot C = 2\,(h\cdot x) - m$. Then:

1. adjunction gives $p_a(C) = 1 + \tfrac12(C^2 - m)$, so $C^2 \ge m-2$; with $C^2<0$ this forces $m\le1$;
2. $m\le-1$: then $W\cdot C = 2(h\cdot x) + \abs{m} \ge 1$;
3. $m = 0$: then $C^2 = -2$ and $p_a(C) = 0$, that is $C$ is a $(-2)$-curve, and there is none;
4. $m = 1$: then $C^2 = -1$ and $p_a(C) = 0$, that is $C$ is a $(-1)$-curve, $x^2 = 0$, and $h\cdot C = h\cdot x \ge 1$; so $W\cdot C = 2(h\cdot C)-1 \ge 1$.

Consequently $W\cdot C>0$ for every irreducible curve of negative square.
:::

::: {.proof}
(1) Adjunction on the smooth surface $S$ gives $2p_a(C)-2 = C^2 + K_S\cdot C = C^2 - m$, so $p_a(C) = 1 + \tfrac12(C^2-m)$, which is a non-negative integer; hence $C^2 \ge m-2$, and $C^2<0$ gives $m < 2$.

(2) is the formula $W\cdot C = 2(h\cdot x) - m$ with $m\le-1$ and $h\cdot x = h\cdot C \ge 0$, the latter because $h$ is a polarization (@def:numerical-polarization) and $C$ is effective.

(3) With $m = 0$ the integrality in (1) forces $C^2$ even, and $C^2<0$ with $C^2\ge-2$ leaves $C^2 = -2$, so $p_a(C) = 0$ and $C$ is a smooth rational curve of self-intersection $-2$. The surface is unnodal, so there is none ([@DK13], §8).

(4) With $m = 1$ the integrality forces $C^2$ odd, and $C^2<0$ with $C^2\ge-1$ leaves $C^2 = -1$, $p_a(C) = 0$. Then $x^2 = C^2 - K_S^2m^2 = -1+1 = 0$, $x\perp K_S$, and $h\cdot C = h\cdot x$. If $h\cdot x = 0$ then $x$ lies in $h^{\perp}\cap E$, which is negative definite (@def:definiteness) because $E$ has signature (@def:signature) $(1,9)$ and $h^2 = 2>0$; a vector of square $0$ in a negative definite lattice is $0$, so $x = 0$ and $C = K_S$, which is not effective since $\abs{K_S} = \varnothing$. Hence $h\cdot x\ge1$.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:coble-pair-ksba title="The fixed curve is ample, and the Coble pair is a KSBA stable pair"}
Let $S$ be an unnodal (@def:nodal-enriques-surface) Coble surface (@def:coble-surface), $h$ a degree-$2$ polarization (@def:numerical-polarization), $W$ the fixed curve of the deck transformation of $\varphi_h$ and $\mathfrak c\in\abs{-2K_S}$. Then $W$ is ample, and for $0<\eps\le\tfrac12$ the pair $\bigl(S,\;\tfrac12\mathfrak c+\eps W\bigr)$ is a KSBA stable pair.
:::

::: {.proof}
Ampleness by the Nakai--Moishezon criterion, in the two cases of the sign of $C^2$ for an irreducible curve $C$.

$W^2 = 7>0$ by @prop:coble-pair-class(1).

*$C^2\ge0$.* $W$ is the fixed curve of an involution, hence effective, and $W^2 = 7>0$, so $W$ lies in the interior of the positive cone (@not:reflection-groups-sign-convention) of $S$; $C$, being effective, lies in its closure. Write $C = aW + C'$ with $C'\perp W$. Since the intersection form is negative definite (@def:definiteness) on $W^{\perp}$ (the Hodge index theorem, $W^2>0$) one has $C'^2\le0$, so $C^2 = 7a^2 + C'^2 \ge 0$ forces $a\neq0$ whenever $C\neq0$; then $W\cdot C = 7a$, and $a>0$ because $W$ and $C$ lie in the same cone. Hence $W\cdot C>0$.

*$C^2<0$.* @prop:coble-negative-curves gives $W\cdot C>0$ in every case of its list.

So $W$ is ample. For the pair: $K_S+\tfrac12\mathfrak c+\eps W\equiv\eps W$ by @prop:coble-pair-boundary(4), which is ample since $W$ is; and $(S,\tfrac12\mathfrak c+\eps W)$ is slc by @prop:coble-pair-boundary(4), the coefficients $\tfrac12$ and $\eps$ lying in $[0,1]$ with $\eps+\tfrac12\le1$ at the two points of $W\cap\mathfrak c$. A pair that is slc with $K+\Delta$ ample is a KSBA stable pair.
:::

::: {.remark #rmk:coble-relevance-state title="What the sources say about relevance at a Coble cusp"}
The marking of relevance is **not** read off a boundary divisor by [@AEGS25]. Their generalized semifans of $F_{(2,2,0)}$ are taken from [2], §9A --- in their notation $\mathcal F^{ram}$, where the irrelevant roots are those that do not lie on the boundary of the square, respectively the triangle, in Figure 5 --- and [@AEGS25, Def. 5.3], obtains the semifans of $\fentwo$ by intersecting them with $T_\En$. [@AEGS25, Lem. 5.5], proves, from [@AEGS25, Lem. 3.8], that the intersection is the generalized Coxeter semifan of the folded diagram with the folded marking of [@AEGS25, Def. 5.4]. So the reading of relevance off a divisor is that of [2], §9A, quoted by [@AEGS25], and not a criterion stated in [@AEGS25] in the form "the node is relevant when the divisor meets the wall's curve"; an earlier version of this page asserted the latter and it is withdrawn.

What does survive is a lattice obstruction, and it is a citation. [@AEGS25, Lem. 3.9; @AEGS25, Lem. 3.10; @AEGS25, Cor. 3.12], give the fixed lattices of the five involutions: $U(2)\oplus E_8(2)$ for cusp $1$ and $U\oplus E_8(2)$ for cusps $2, 3, 4, 5$. Those four quotient lattices are therefore isometric, while by Lemma 5.6, $W_{\mathrm{irr}}$ is $S_2$ at cusp $2$, $S_2^2$ at cusp $4$ and infinite at cusps $1$, $3$, $5$. A datum that varies across isometric lattices is not a function of the lattice and its chamber: the marking is extra data, and no computation inside $T_\Co$ produces it.

The general statement of how a marking behaves under restriction to a $(-2)$-Heegner sublattice is @thm:semifan-restriction. At a $0$-cusp of $F_{\En,2}$ a $(-2)$-root $\delta\perp e$ has its reflection among the generators of $W(\Gamma_{\En,2,e})$ ([@AEGS25, Def. 3.6]), so $\bar\delta$ is a **mirror** of $W$, and Case A of that theorem does not apply: the facets of the chambers on the mirror tile it, but the facet's own angles are not the ambient ones, being given by the formula recorded in the research report, and the tiling is the chamber tiling of a reflection group of $\bar S$ only when those angles are submultiples of $\pi$. That condition is not verified here; @thm:coble-cusp-chamber computes a nine-node chamber whose bonds are those of a Coxeter simplex, which is a check on it and not the proof. The mirror case is Case B of @thm:semifan-restriction, and at the four $0$-cusps of $F_{\Co,2}$ its hypotheses are verified one by one in @thm:coble-pair-semifan, where the restricted chambers are the facets of @prop:facet-angles-aegs.
:::

::: {.warning #warn:coble-marking title="The step that fails: the dictionary from the roots of the chamber to curves on $S$"}
The split cannot be computed, and no node's assignment is proved, because the sources read give no dictionary from the nine roots of $\bar T$ to curves of the Coble surface meeting $\tfrac12\mathfrak c+\eps W$. [@AEGS25, §5.2], p. 610, reads the marking of $\fram$ from the explicit geometry of their diagrams, where the relevant roots are visibly the ones on the boundary of the square, respectively the triangle; [@DK13], §8, p. 522, describes $S$ through $\varphi_h$ but says nothing about the curves attached to the roots of the cusp lattice. The statement this step needs is a **cusp marking**: for the one $0$-cusp of $\fco$, the identification of the nine simple roots of the chamber with the curves of $S$ (equivalently, with the $(-2)$-curves of the K3 cover) which the period map realises --- the Coble analogue of the identification [@AEGS25] obtain from Sterk's and Alexeev--Nikulin's explicit diagrams. With it, each node's relevance is a single intersection number against $\tfrac12\mathfrak c+\eps W$, by @thm:semifan-restriction(3) and the marking it inherits. Without it the three questions of this unit have no answers here, and no marking is chosen.
:::

::: {.proof}
The boundary lattice is $v_0^{\perp}/\generators{v_0}\cong(9,9,1)_1\cong\generators{2}\oplus E_8(2)$, of signature $(1,8)$. ## The generalized Coxeter semifan at the $0$-cusps of $F_{\Co,2}$ {#sec-coble-pair-semifan}

The pair $(S,\tfrac12\mathfrak c+\eps W)$ of @thm:coble-pair-ksba carries a degree-$2$ polarization, so its moduli space is the $(-2)$-Heegner divisor
$$
F_{\Co,2} = \bD(T_\Co)/\Gamma_{\Co,2}, \qquad
R_{\gent} = \text{the image of } \Stab_{\gent}(\delta) \text{ in } \Orth(T_\Co),
$$
of $\fentwo$ (@def:gamma-co-groups)--(5), @thm:normalization). The group $\Gamma_{\Co,2}$ is the monodromy group of the family of @def:coble-family-degree-two and is not defined as $R_{\gent}$; it is a subgroup of it (@lem:d2-degree-two-base(3)), and the diagrams computed on this page are those of $\bD(T_\Co)/R_{\gent}$, which is the normalization of $\Delta_2$ (@lem:d2-degree-two-base(1)). The vector is $\delta = e-f$ of square $-2$ and $T_\Co = \delta^{\perp T_\En}$ (@prop:cusp-white-root-complements). The semifan at a $0$-cusp of $F_{\Co,2}$ is not formed from a divisor of the Coble surface: it is inherited, by restriction of the Enriques semifan at the cusp above it, through the theorem of @thm:semifan-restriction. The unmarked half --- the Coxeter semifan at the one $0$-cusp of $F^{\mathrm{un}}_\Co$, where no such restriction is available --- is @thm:coble-coxeter-semifan.

The three definitions used are those of [@AEGS25]: the Coxeter semifan (Definition 5.1); the generalized Coxeter semifan $\mathcal F^{\mathrm{gen}}(I\sqcup J)$ of a split of the diagram into relevant and irrelevant roots, with $C_{\mathrm{gen}} = \bigcup_{h\in W_{\mathrm{irr}}} hC$ (after [@AET23], §4D and [2], §9A); and the marking of the Enriques diagrams (Definition 5.3, with Definition 5.4 and Lemma 5.5). The two lemmas the third item uses are [@AEGS25], Lemma 5.6 and Lemma 5.7.

::: {.proof}
*(0)* The four cusps are @thm:four-r-orbits, and the sharper form is @thm:orbit-per-cusp: $R$ has four orbits on the primitive isotropic lines of $T_\Co$, and the orbit of Sterk's representative $\eta_k$ lies over Sterk's cusp $k$ for $k = 2,3,4,5$, in the $P$-orbits of sizes $2$, $120$, $270$, $135$. So it is enough to exhibit four primitive isotropic vectors of $T_\Co$ in four different $R$-orbits, each lying over the Enriques cusp claimed for it, which the assignment $k\leftrightarrow\mathcal O$ above does. The printed representatives of @ex:sterk-isotropic-divisibilities are isotropic with divisibility $2$ in $T_\En$; every one of them is orthogonal to $\delta$ (the transcript prints $\eta_k\cdot\delta = 0$ for $k = 2,3,4,5$), hence lies in $T_\Co = \delta^{\perp T_\En}$; and the Enriques cusp $[\eta_k]$ is $k$ by construction. They are pairwise inequivalent under $\Gamma_{\Co,2}$ because $\bar\eta = \tfrac12\eta \bmod T_\En$ is an invariant of the $\Gamma_{\Co,2}$-orbit: $\Gamma_{\Co,2} = \rho^{-1}(P)$ acts on $A_{T_\Co}$ through $P$ (@prop:coble-cong-point-stabilizer(1), @def:gamma-co-groups(5)), and $\bar\eta$ is carried with it. The transcripts print the canonical representatives of the four classes in $\{0,\tfrac12\}^{12}$; their $U$ coordinates vanish, and their $U(2)$ and $E_8(2)$ coordinates are
$$
2:\ (1,0) \text{ with } E_8(2)\text{ trivial}, \quad
3:\ (1,1) \text{ with } E_8(2)\text{ nontrivial}, \quad
4:\ (0,1) \text{ with } E_8(2)\text{ nontrivial}, \quad
5:\ (0,0) \text{ with } E_8(2)\text{ nontrivial},
$$
which are in four different orbits of $P$: these are the orbits $\mathcal O_2 = \{u_1,u_2\}$, $\mathcal O_{120}$, $\mathcal O_{270}$ and $\mathcal O_{135}$ of @prop:coble-cong-point-stabilizer(2), whose description there is by the vanishing of the $E_8(2)$ component and by the three classes $u_1,u_2,u_3$ of $A_{U(2)}$ with $q(u_1) = q(u_2) = 0 \ne q(u_3)$, and isotropy forces the $E_8(2)$ component of a class with $u$-part $u_3$ to have value $1$. So the four orbits are the four, and the exhibit is complete. Cusp $1$ is not among them: its representative has divisibility $1$ in $T_\En$, and every primitive vector of $T_\Co$ has divisibility $2$ (@lem:divisibilityAlwaysTwoTco), which is @prop:cusp-map-not-onto. For the boundary lattice: the four cusp vectors are primitive isotropic vectors of one lattice and $\Orth(T_\Co)$ is transitive on those (@thm:unpolarized-cusps), so the four boundary lattices are isometric; the one over cusp $2$ is computed as $\generators{2}\oplus E_8(2)$ in the restricted semifan theorem, which is $(9,9,1)_1$ of rank $9$, determinant $-2^9$ and signature $(1,8)$.

*(1)* The chamber is $C\cap\delta^\perp$ and the inherited marking is the statement of @thm:semifan-restriction(1) and (3); the hypotheses (B1)--(B5) of its Case B are read off the recorded wall Gram matrices and induced angle tables. The pairings $(\alpha,\delta)$ are $0$ and $+2$ only (no negative value occurs), so $\alpha^\perp\cap\delta^\perp$ meets $\delta^\perp$ at $\pi/2$ or at $\pi/4 = \pi/(2\cdot2)$, which is (B2). The induced angles are computed from $\cos\phi_{ij} = (g_{ij}+g_{\delta i}g_{\delta j})/\sqrt{(1-g_{\delta i}^2)(1-g_{\delta j}^2)}$ as in @prop:facet-angles-aegs and are $\pi/2$, $\pi/3$, $\pi/4$, parallel, or — in the single pair $10,11$ at cusp $3$ and $13,14$ at cusp $5$ — no intersection, which is (B4). The coincident walls are the parallel pairs, listed in the table, and at cusps $2$, $3$, $4$ each of them carries one relevance only, which is (B5). The relevance itself is Definition 5.4 of [@AEGS25] read through Lemma 5.5: at cusp $2$ the irrelevant root is the single interior spur of @thm:coble-restricted-semifan, and at cusps $3,4,5$ the irrelevant printed labels are those computed in `computations/reports/coble_irrelevant-marking-face-run.txt`. At cusp $5$ the cut mirror is on that list and one wall carries roots of both relevances, so Case B does not apply and the marking is not inherited there as a marking; that is the obstruction recorded in @rmk:coble-pair-semifan-marking.

*(2)* The squares are $\alpha^2+\tfrac12(\alpha,\delta)^2$ and the pairings $\alpha\cdot\beta+\tfrac12(\alpha,\delta)(\beta,\delta)$, in the recorded table. of rank two and determinant zero, so their radical is one-dimensional and the group they generate is affine, hence infinite; the reflection in the wall of $3$ is orthogonal to all three and contributes a direct factor.

*(3)* For the volume row: the closure of the chamber in the projective model is the projectivization of the cone $C = \{x : (\nu_i,x)\ge 0\}$, whose faces are the rays, and its vertices are the extreme rays of $C$. The last row is [@AEGS25], Lemma 5.7, whose proof is the criterion stated.

::: {.remark #rmk:coble-pair-semifan-marking title="What the semifan at the cusp over Sterk $5$ is not"}
At three of the four cusps the marking descends literally: each wall of $C_\delta$ comes from one relevance class of roots of $C$, so the intersection $\mathcal F^{k}\cap\delta^\perp$ is a generalized Coxeter semifan of a marked diagram, and its irrelevant group is the one tabulated. At the cusp over Sterk $5$ both (B1) and (B5) of @thm:semifan-restriction fail: the cut mirror $\delta$ is irrelevant, so the face $\delta^\perp$ is one the coarsening deletes, and one restricted wall carries the relevant root $3$ together with the irrelevant roots $10,13$. What is defined there is the intersection $\mathcal F^{5}\cap\delta^\perp$, which is what the restriction of a semifan to a subspace always gives; it is not the generalized Coxeter semifan of the restricted diagram with an inherited marking, and this theorem asserts no such identification at that cusp. Its irrelevant group is infinite either way, by (2), so the fan verdict at that cusp is the same under either reading.
:::

::: {.remark #rmk:coble-pair-semifan-scope title="What is inherited and what is not"}
The theorem gives the semifan at the four $0$-cusps of $F_{\Co,2}$, which is the datum a semitoroidal compactification of $F_{\Co,2}$ consumes at its $0$-cusps. It does not assert that the normalization of the KSBA closure of $F_{\Co,2}$ is the semitoroidal compactification of that datum: that is @prob:restricted-semifan, which needs in addition the saturated intersection of the toroidal charts with the Coble cusp subspaces (@prob:A6). Nor does it supply a marking at the one $0$-cusp of $F^{\mathrm{un}}_\Co$, where no Enriques cusp sits above it to restrict from; that marking is what @warn:coble-marking records as missing, and @thm:coble-coxeter-semifan is the whole of what is available there.
:::
