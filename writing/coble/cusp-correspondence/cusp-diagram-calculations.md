Thus there is one $\Orth^+(T_\Co)$-orbit of $0$-cusps, represented by an isotropic vector $v_0$ with

$$
v_0^{\perp T_{\Co}}/\generators{v_0} \cong (9,9,1)_1 \cong \generators{2} \oplus E_{8}(2)

::: {.theorem #thm:coble-cusp-chamber title="The reflection chamber at the one $0$-cusp of $\fco$"}
Let $v_0$ be a primitive isotropic vector of $T_\Co$, $\bar T = v_0^{\perp}/\langle v_0\rangle\cong(9,9,1)_1 = \generators{2}\oplus E_8(2)$ its boundary lattice, and $W^{(2,4)}(\bar T)$ the full reflection group of $\bar T$, generated by the reflections in all $(-2)$- and $(-4)$-roots ([@AN06], List of Notations, and Theorem 3.1, printed p. 51). Then $\fco$ has one $0$-cusp (@prop:gamma-co-images(3), @thm:unpolarized-cusps, computed by sage-extensions#286 in its $\Orth(T_\Co)$ row), and at it the chamber of $W^{(2,4)}(\bar T)$ is the simplex with nine walls whose adjacencies are

| wall | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| adjacent | 3 | 4 | 1, 4 | 3, 2, 5 | 4, 6 | 5, 7 | 6, 8 | 7, 9 | 8 |

with every bond of label $3$ except $8-9$, of label $4$. It is the chamber $\mathcal M^{(2,4)}$ of [@AN06], Table 1, entry $N = 10$, that is $(r,a,\delta) = (9,9,1)$ with $k = 0$, $g = 2$ and $l(H) = 0$, printed p. 52; the source's convention is that a hyperbolic lattice of rank $r$ has signature $(1, r-1)$, here $(1,8)$, with roots of square $-2$ and $-4$, which is this volume's convention. Its Gram matrix lies over $\QQ[\sqrt2]$, it has $8$ finite vertices and one ideal vertex, and its $f$-vector is $(9,36,84,126,126,84,36,9,1)$.

The source's diagram and this wall list agree node by node, under
$$
1\mapsto v_1,\quad 3\mapsto v_2,\quad 4\mapsto v_3,\quad 2\mapsto v_9,\quad 5\mapsto v_4,\quad 6\mapsto v_5,\quad 7\mapsto v_6,\quad 8\mapsto v_7,\quad 9\mapsto v_8,
$$
where $v_1-\cdots-v_8$ is the source's chain, $v_9$ its node below $v_3$, $v_7-v_8$ its double bond, and $v_8$ its open vertex. The maximal parabolic subdiagrams are the nine one-node deletions; the diagram has no nontrivial automorphism, so distinct deletions are distinct subdiagrams; the deletion whose remaining diagram is affine of type $\wt B_7$ is the facet carrying the adjacent $1$-cusp.
:::


::: {.theorem #thm:coble-coxeter-semifan title="The Coxeter semifan at the one $0$-cusp of $\fco$, and what the generalized semifan still needs"}
Let $G$ be the nine-node chamber diagram of @thm:coble-cusp-chamber at the one $0$-cusp of $\fco$ and $W = W^{(2,4)}(\bar T)$ its reflection group. Then:

1. the Coxeter semifan $\Fcox$ of [@AEGS25], Definition 5.1, printed p. 609 --- support $C_\QQ$, maximal cones the chambers of $W$ --- is a **fan**, the chamber having finite volume;
2. its **Type II rays** are in bijection with the maximal parabolic subdiagrams of $G$, which are the nine one-node deletions of @thm:coble-cusp-chamber; exactly one of them is affine, of type $\wt B_7$. The ray it gives is the ideal vertex of the simplex --- the intersection of the eight walls other than the deleted one --- and not a facet: it is the unique vertex at infinity that the recorded run reports, and it is the $1$-cusp adjacent to $v_0$, whose boundary lattice is $(7,7,1)_0 = A_1^{\oplus 7}$ (`computations/reports/one_cusp_plane_quotient.py` and its transcript);
3. its **Type III cones** are the faces $\bigcap_{j\in S}\alpha_j^{\perp}\cap C$ for the elliptic subdiagrams $S\subseteq\{1,\dots,9\}$ of $G$ --- all of them, the empty one included, which gives $C$ itself --- of every dimension, the face of an $S$ with $\abs{S} = k$ having dimension $8-k$. Since $G$ is a simplex diagram the faces of $C$ are counted by the $f$-vector $(9,36,84,126,126,84,36,9,1)$; a subset gives a face of the interior exactly when its diagram is elliptic, and the recorded run reports that the nine $8$-subsets give eight finite vertices and one vertex at infinity, so eight of them are elliptic and the ninth is the affine one of (2), while the whole of $G$ is neither. The Type III cones therefore number
$$
1+9+36+84+126+126+84+36+8 = 510,
$$
one for each elliptic subdiagram.

The generalized Coxeter semifan of [@AEGS25], p. 610 (after [@AET23], §4D) is formed from a split $I\sqcup J$ of the nine nodes into relevant and irrelevant roots, by $C_{\mathrm{gen}} = \bigcup_{h\in W_{\mathrm{irr}}} hC$, the coarsening of $\Fcox$ obtained by deleting the faces $\bigcap_j\alpha_j^\perp\cap C$ spanned by irrelevant roots. **It is not formed here.** [@AEGS25], p. 610 derives the marking of $\Fram$ from the ramification divisor: the relevant roots are those that lie on the boundary of the square, respectively the triangle. The corresponding datum on the Coble side is the divisor $R_{\bar S}$ of a Coble stable pair $(\bar S,\eps R_{\bar S})$, and it is not defined on these pages; see the warning below.
:::

::: {.proof}
(1) is [@AEGS25], Definition 5.1, printed p. 609, read with @thm:coble-cusp-chamber. The criterion stated there for the semifan to be a fan is that the Coxeter polyhedron $P$ have finite volume, equivalently that $W$ have finite index in $\Orth(\Lambda)$; the chamber here is a simplex of finite volume with one ideal vertex, its $f$-vector being $(9,36,84,126,126,84,36,9,1)$ and its transcript reporting one vertex at infinity.

(2) By the same definition the Type II rays are the rays $\RR_{\ge0}v$ with $v^2 = 0$ on $\partial C_\QQ$, in bijection with the maximal parabolic subdiagrams of $G$, which by @thm:coble-cusp-chamber are the nine one-node deletions. A facet of a finite-volume chamber lies at infinity exactly when its own reflection group does not have finite covolume, that is when the deletion is affine rather than elliptic. The recorded run `computations/coxiter/logs/9-9-1_1.txt` reports $8$ finite vertices and $1$ vertex at infinity, and a simplex with nine facets has nine vertices; so exactly one of the nine deletions is affine. That facet is the one @thm:coble-cusp-chamber identifies, carrying the adjacent $1$-cusp with boundary lattice $(7,7,1)_0$.

(3) By the definition the Type III cones correspond to the elliptic subdiagrams of $G$, and the face attached to $S$ is $\bigcap_{j\in S}\alpha_j^{\perp}\cap C$, of dimension $8-\abs{S}$ for a simplex chamber; the empty $S$ gives $C$, the singletons the nine facets, and in general the $k$-subsets the faces of dimension $8-k$. A subset gives a face of $C$ lying in the interior of the cone exactly when its diagram is elliptic, and the enumeration is read off the record: the transcript reports the $f$-vector $(9,36,84,126,126,84,36,9,1)$ of a simplex and exactly one vertex at infinity, so of the nine $8$-subsets eight are elliptic and one is the affine one of (2), which contributes the Type II ray instead; the whole of $G$ is hyperbolic and contributes nothing. Summing over $k = 0,\dots,8$ with the $8$-level reduced by that one gives $510$.
:::

::: {.proposition #prop:coble-pair-boundary title="The boundary of the Coble pair, derived from the degree-$2$ construction"}
Let $S$ be an unnodal Coble surface with a degree-$2$ polarization $h = [F_1+F_2]$, $\varphi_h\colon S\to D\subset\PP^4$ the map given by $\abs{2h}$ onto a $4$-nodal quartic del Pezzo surface $D$, $\sigma$ its deck transformation, $W$ the fixed curve of $\sigma$, and $\mathfrak c\in\abs{-2K_S}$ the anti-canonical curve. Then:

1. the fixed locus of $\sigma$ is the smooth curve $W$ of genus $4$ together with three isolated points, so $W$ is the ramification divisor of $\varphi_h$ and the isolated points contribute no component; $\mathfrak c$ maps to a singular point $q$ of $D$, $W$ meets $\mathfrak c$ in two points, and the image $\bar W\subset D$ has arithmetic genus $5$ with a double point at $q$ and is a complete intersection of a net of quadrics ([@DK13], §8, printed p. 522);
2. $K_S = -\tfrac12\mathfrak c$, since $\mathfrak c\in\abs{-2K_S}$, so $K_S+\tfrac12\mathfrak c\equiv0$; and $\mathfrak c^2 = -4$ gives $K_S^2 = -1$, so $K_S$ is not numerically trivial. On the standard model $S = \Bl_{p_1,\dots,p_{10}}\PP^2$, where $\mathfrak c = 6H-2\sum_iE_i$ and $K_S = -3H+\sum_iE_i$, every exceptional curve has $K_S\cdot E_i = -1$ and $\mathfrak c\cdot E_i = 2$;
3. consequently $(S,\eps W)$ is **not** a KSBA pair for any small $\eps>0$: $(K_S+\eps W)\cdot E_i = -1+\eps(W\cdot E_i) < 0$ for $\eps\ll1$, whatever $W$ is;
4. the boundary must carry $\mathfrak c$ with the coefficient that makes the pair numerically Calabi--Yau: for $0<\eps\le\tfrac12$,
$$
\Delta \definedas \tfrac12\mathfrak c + \eps W, \qquad K_S+\Delta \equiv \eps W,
$$
and $(S,\Delta)$ is slc, $S$ being smooth and the coefficients $\tfrac12$ and $\eps$ lying in $[0,1]$ with sum $\eps+\tfrac12\le1$ at the two points $W\cap\mathfrak c$;
5. hence $(S,\tfrac12\mathfrak c+\eps W)$ is a KSBA pair with $0<\eps\le\tfrac12$ exactly when $W$ is ample, which is the step the citations do not give; see the warning.
:::


::: {.proof}
(1) is [@DK13], §8, printed p. 522: the deck transformation fixes a smooth curve $W$ of genus $4$ and three isolated points, and the anti-canonical curve is mapped to a singular point of $D$; the ramification divisor of a double cover is its fixed curve, so the ramification of $\varphi_h$ is $W$.

(2) reads the sign off the same passage: $\mathfrak c\in\abs{-2K_S}$ there, so $K_S = -\tfrac12\mathfrak c$ and $K_S+\tfrac12\mathfrak c\equiv0$; squaring gives $4K_S^2 = \mathfrak c^2 = -4$, hence $K_S^2 = -1$. On the blow-up of $\PP^2$ at the ten nodes of a rational sextic, $\mathfrak c$ is the proper transform $6H-2\sum_iE_i$ and $K_S = -3H+\sum_iE_i$, so $K_S\cdot E_i = -1$ and $\mathfrak c\cdot E_i = 2$.

(3) is immediate from (2): $(K_S+\eps W)\cdot E_i = -1+\eps(W\cdot E_i)$, and $W\cdot E_i\ge0$ because $E_i$ is a curve and $W$ is effective; the value is negative for $\eps$ small enough, so $K_S+\eps W$ is not nef and in particular not ample.

(4) adds the two classes: $K_S+\tfrac12\mathfrak c+\eps W\equiv\eps W$ by (2). For log canonicity: $S$ is smooth, so a boundary with coefficients in $[0,1]$ is lc, and the only points where two components of $\Delta$ meet are the two points of $W\cap\mathfrak c$, where the coefficient sum is $\tfrac12+\eps\le1$ for $\eps\le\tfrac12$. The pair is therefore slc.

(5) is the definition of a KSBA pair: slc with $K+\Delta$ ample, and $K_S+\Delta\equiv\eps W$ is ample exactly when $W$ is.
:::

::: {.proposition #prop:coble-pair-class title="The class of the fixed curve, and what Nakai--Moishezon gives for it"}
With $S$, $h$, $\varphi_h\colon S\to D\subset\PP^4$, $W$ and $\mathfrak c$ as in @prop:coble-pair-boundary, and $\varphi_h^*\mathcal O_D(1) = 2h$:

1. $W \equiv K_S + 2h$. Consequently $W^2 = 7$, $K_S\cdot W = -1$, $W\cdot\mathfrak c = 2$, and $2g(W)-2 = W^2 + K_S\cdot W = 6$, that is $g(W) = 4$, which is the genus [@DK13], §8, printed p. 522 assigns to $W$;
2. $W^2 = 7 > 0$ and $W\cdot\mathfrak c = 2 > 0$;
3. for every irreducible curve $C\neq\mathfrak c$ one has $W\cdot C = 2\,(h\cdot C) - \tfrac12\,(\mathfrak c\cdot C)$, and $W\cdot C > 0$ for every such $C$ if and only if the branch curve $B\subset D$ of $\varphi_h$ is ample.

The last equivalence is the step not completed here; see the warning.
:::

::: {.proof}
(1) Hurwitz's formula for the double cover $\varphi_h$, away from its contracted curve, reads $K_S = \varphi_h^*K_D + R$ with $R$ the ramification divisor, and $R = W$ by @prop:coble-pair-boundary(1). Since $D$ is anticanonically embedded --- a del Pezzo surface of degree $4$ in $\PP^4$ --- one has $K_D = -\mathcal O_D(1)$, so
$$
W = R = K_S - \varphi_h^*K_D = K_S + \varphi_h^*\mathcal O_D(1) = K_S + 2h .
$$
The formula is applied on $S\smallsetminus\mathfrak c$, where $\varphi_h$ is finite; on $S$ itself the difference $W - K_S - 2h$ is a line bundle trivial off $\mathfrak c$, hence of the form $m\mathfrak c$ for an integer $m$. The cited intersection $W\cdot\mathfrak c = 2$ ([@DK13], §8, p. 522: $W$ meets $\mathfrak c$ in two points) and $h\cdot\mathfrak c = 0$, $K_S\cdot\mathfrak c = 2$ give $2 = 2 + m\,(\mathfrak c^2) = 2 - 4m$, so $m = 0$.

The numbers follow in $K_S^{\perp}$: $h\cdot K_S = 0$ because $h$ is a polarization of $S$ lying in $K_S^{\perp}\cong E$ ([@DK13], §8, p. 522), so $W^2 = K_S^2 + 4h^2 = -1 + 8 = 7$ and $K_S\cdot W = K_S^2 = -1$; and $\mathfrak c = -2K_S$ gives $W\cdot\mathfrak c = -2K_S\cdot W = 2$. Then $2g(W)-2 = W\cdot(W+K_S) = 7 - 1 = 6$, which agrees with the genus $4$ of [@DK13], §8, p. 522.

(2) is the first two displays.

(3) Every irreducible $C\neq\mathfrak c$ is not contracted by $\varphi_h$, so its image is a curve and $W\cdot C = \deg(\varphi_h|_C)\,(B\cdot\varphi_h(C))$, while the class formula of (1) gives $W\cdot C = 2(h\cdot C) - \tfrac12(\mathfrak c\cdot C)$. Both expressions have the sign of the intersection of $B$ with a curve of $D$, so $W\cdot C > 0$ for all $C\neq\mathfrak c$ exactly when $B$ meets every curve of $D$ positively, that is when $B$ is ample.
:::

::: {.warning #warn:coble-stable-pair-missing title="The step that fails: ampleness of the branch curve on $D$"}
What is not proved is the ampleness of $B\subset D$. The surface $D$ is a quartic del Pezzo with four nodes, and the covering is branched over $B$ and over the four nodes, one of which is the image of the contracted curve $\mathfrak c$; [@DK13], §8, p. 522, gives no intersection numbers on $D$ and no description of the curves of $D$ that a branch curve must meet. The statement this page needs is one of: an intersection computation of $B$ (equivalently of $W$) against the curves of a model of $S$, or a proof that no irreducible curve of $S$ other than $\mathfrak c$ is contracted by $\varphi_h$ together with $h\cdot C\ge1$ for all remaining $C$, from which $W\cdot C = 2(h\cdot C)-\tfrac12(\mathfrak c\cdot C)$ would have to be shown positive by a bound on $\mathfrak c\cdot C$.

One candidate obstruction is worth recording as a candidate only: an elliptic curve $C$ with $C^2 = 0$, $h\cdot C = 1$ and $\mathfrak c\cdot C = 4$ satisfies $p_a(C) = 1$ and would give $W\cdot C = 2 - 2 = 0$, so $W$ would fail ampleness and the stable model would be the contraction of $C$. No such curve is exhibited here, and none is excluded by the sources read, so neither alternative is asserted.

Without the step the marking is not derived: no split $I\sqcup J$, hence no $W_{\mathrm{irr}}$, and the three questions of this unit have no answers here. No divisor and no marking are chosen beyond the boundary $\Delta$ of @prop:coble-pair-boundary(4). The Enriques-side results do not transfer: [@AEGS25], Lemma 5.6, printed p. 611, computes $W_{\mathrm{irr}}$ finite at cusps $2$ and $4$ and infinite at $1,3,5$, and Lemma 5.7, p. 611, gives the survival criterion for a Type II ray.
:::

::: {.proof}
The boundary lattice is computed on this page above: $v_0^{\perp}/\generators{v_0}\cong(9,9,1)_1\cong\generators{2}\oplus E_8(2)$, of signature $(1,8)$. By [@AN06], Theorem 3.1 with Table 1, the fundamental chamber $\mathcal M^{(2,4)}$ of $W^{(2,4)}(S)$ for the $2$-elementary even hyperbolic lattice of invariants $(9,9,1)$ is the diagram printed in row $N = 10$ of that table, p. 52: a chain $v_1-\cdots-v_8$ with the single double bond $v_7-v_8$, one further node $v_9$ attached to $v_3$, and $v_8$ drawn open, with $k = 0$ and $l(H) = 0$. Reading its bonds off gives the wall list displayed in the statement, which is the bond list of the recorded input `computations/coxiter/9-9-1_1.coxiter`; the two agree, so the table is the source and the recorded run is the check. Its transcript `computations/coxiter/logs/9-9-1_1.txt` reports nine vertices, the Gram field $\QQ[\sqrt2]$, the $f$-vector $(9,36,84,126,126,84,36,9,1)$ and one vertex at infinity, all consistent with that entry.

The last clause records which deletion carries the adjacent $1$-cusp: that $1$-cusp has boundary lattice $(7,7,1)_0 = A_1^{\oplus 7}$, computed above, and its diagram is the facet of the chamber at $v_0$, hence affine; the recorded run lists the affine eight-node subdiagrams it finds, and $\wt B_7$ among them.

We note that by [@AE22 §5], such isotropic vectors are unique up to $\Orth(T_\Co)$, and so we can choose representatives:

- $v_0 = e'$,

- $v_1 = 2h + \alpha_1 + \alpha_2$.

Calculations verify that $v_0^2 = v_1^2 = 0$, that $v_1 \in v_0^{\perp T_{\Co}}/\generators{v_0}$, and that $v_0v_1 = 0$, and thus $J \definedas \generators{v_0, v_1}$ is an admissible choice of an isotropic plane.
We further note that $\div_{T_{\Co}}(v_0) = \div_{T_{\Co}}(v_1) = 2$, which will be an important invariant for establishing a correspondence to cusps of other moduli spaces.
For an isotropic plane $J$, we denote the divisibilities of the constituent generating vectors as a tuple $(d_1, d_2)$, and in this convention we have $\div_{T_{\Co}}(v_0, v_1) = (2, 2)$.

By [@CDL25 Prop. 5.4.6], there is a locally closed immersion $\fco \injects \mch_{-2}/\Gamma_\En \subset \fen$ whose image is open in the Heegner divisor $H_{-2}$, realizing $\fco$ as the coarse space of marked Coble surfaces with $n=1$, where $n$ is the number of boundary components in $C = C_1 + \cdots + C_n$.
The image is an open subset of a closed irreducible subset of $\mch_{-2}/\Gamma_\En$.
By [@CDL25 Thm. 5.8.2], the coarse space of $\fco$ is a rational variety, and since $\fen$ is quasiprojective, so too is $\fco$.
Moreover, $\partial \overline{\fen}^{\mathrm{BB}}$ consists of $\fco$ and two modular curves $X$ and $X_0(2)$ by [@CDL25 Thm. 5.9.8], and the closure of $\mch_{-2}$ contains the modular curve $X$.

::: {.remark}

As further proof that the cusp diagram of $\fco$ is correct, we can use the theory of Coxeter diagrams.
Given an isotropic vector $e\in L$ a lattice of signature $(2, n)$, the lattice $e^{\perp L}/\generators{e}$ is a hyperbolic lattice equipped with a root system $R_e$ with a Coxeter diagram $G_e$.
Generally, when $e$ corresponds to a 0-cusp in a Baily-Borel compactification, the adjacent 1-cusps correspond precisely to maximal parabolic subdiagrams of $G_e$.
The cusp diagram of $\fco$ suggests that the 0-cusp $v_0$ should have a Coxeter diagram $G_{v_0}$ with precisely one maximal parabolic subdiagram.
One can run Vinberg's algorithm to determine the Coxeter diagram for $v_0$, and it is a straightforward check to determine that there is indeed a unique maximal parabolic subdiagram of the form $\tilde B_7(2)$; see @fig-coble-cusp-9-9-1-parabolics.
:::
