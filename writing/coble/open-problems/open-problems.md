# Open problems and technical gaps {#sec-open-problems}

::: {.question #qst:inverse-period-realization title="Inverse period realization"}

Given an orthogonal period domain $\bD(L)$ and an arithmetic subgroup
$\Gamma\leq\Orth(L)$, when is an open subset of $\bD(L)/\Gamma$ the
period image of a geometric moduli problem for algebraic surfaces with
specified polarization and involution? A realization must supply an
integral embedding into the relevant cohomology lattice, a geometric
ample cone, an arrangement of excluded periods, and the arithmetic
group induced by the geometric markings. The Enriques space
$\fentwo$ and its normalization inside the degree-four hyperelliptic
K3 period space (@lem:fen2_normalization) give a concrete instance of
this inverse question from the archived Enriques meeting notes.
:::

## Lattice-theoretic gaps

::: {.remark title="The embedding matrices of the chain"}

The matrices of the chain $T_\Co\injects\ten\injects\tdp\injects\lkt$, in the standard basis of the marking of @thm:theta-co-coordinates, are @thm:chain-matrices: the four bases, the three block inclusion matrices, their primitivity, and the identification of the first step with $\eta$ and of the composite with the display of @lem:sequence_of_embeddings. What that theorem does not give is the equations of the sextic $C$ and of its double plane, which is @thm:coble-explicit-sextic; a basis of $H^2$ determines no equation, so the two questions were never one.
:::

::: {.remark title="The folding conjecture for $\theta_\Co$, resolved"}

The entry that stood here, whose wording this note quotes, conjectured two things about $\theta_\Co$: that it induces the "horizontal folding" of the $(18,0,0)_1$ Coxeter diagram in the sense of @def:folded-root, and that it exchanges the polarization classes $h_\En$ and $h_\Co$. The first phrase is no standard term -- a folding of a Coxeter diagram is folding by a diagram automorphism, whose folded roots are the orbit sums of @def:folded-root [@Bou08, Ch. 6 §4] -- and the second has no content, the two classes being one. Both are decided by @thm:diagram-roots-coordinates, and this note stays here because the second cannot be stated that way.

*The folding.* @def:folded-root asks for an involution preserving a root system inside its own lattice, the folded roots being the orbit sums. $\theta_\Co$ preserves no copy of $(18,0,0)_1$ in $\lkt$: it carries the copy $W = U_2\oplus E_{8,1}\oplus E_{8,2}$ onto the second one $W' = U_3\oplus E_{8,1}\oplus E_{8,2}$. What the involution induces is an automorphism of the diagram: $I_\Co = -\theta_\Co$ carries the nineteen roots of the diagram in $W$ to the nineteen roots of the diagram in $W'$, node $k$ to node $18-k$, which is the reflection $J\colon\alpha_k\mapsto\alpha_{20-k}$ of [@AEGS25, Lemma 3.9, printed p. 593], and the folded roots are the ten of the cusp-$2$ diagram [@AEGS25, Fig. 7, printed p. 594]. That is the precise content of "the folding is realized by the Coble involution"; the folded root system inside a single copy is the one of $J$, not one of $\theta_\Co$.

*The polarization classes.* $\tilde h_\Co$ and $h_\En$ are one class: $\tilde h_\Co = H$ (@prop:coble-cover-marking) and $H = h$ (@prop:coble-enriques-ambient-isometry(3)). So there are not two classes for an involution to exchange. Read literally as $\theta_\Co(h_\En) = h_\Co$ the clause asserts $\theta_\Co(H) = H$, and that is false, $H\in S_\Co$ being an eigenvector of $\theta_\Co$ with eigenvalue $-1$. With the covering involution in place of $\theta_\Co$ the clause is true and empty: $I_\Co(H) = H = h_\Co$.
:::

## Isotropic orbits, cusps, and reflection groups

::: {.question title="Open problem: the 0-cusps that carry the Coble stable limits"}

Which of the $527$ $0$-cusps of the stable quotient $\bD(T_\Co)/\widetilde\Orth^+(T_\Co)$ (@thm:coble-zero-cusp-count(2), the cusps being the classes themselves by @thm:coble-integral-lifting-fibre(1)) carry the Coble stable limits?
:::

::: {.question #qst:gamma-co-generators title="Open problem: the stable group $\widetilde\Orth^+(T_\Co)$"}

1. Give a finite set of matrices that generates $\widetilde\Orth^+(T_\Co)$.
2. Let $W(T_\Co)$ be the group generated by the reflections in the vectors of square $-2$ of $T_\Co$; these are the reflections in $\widetilde\Orth^+(T_\Co)$ (@prop:gamma-co-reflective-set). What is the index $[\widetilde\Orth^+(T_\Co) : W(T_\Co)]$? The unrestricted form, $\ker\rho_{T_\Co} = W(T_\Co)$, is false, by the reflection in the $\langle 2\rangle$ summand (@prop:kernel-exceeds-short-roots), which lies in the kernel and outside $\Orth^+$; so what remains is the index inside $\widetilde\Orth^+(T_\Co)$, and @qst:gamma-co-reflection-index names what would settle it: a finite presentation of $W(T_\Co)$ with its chamber.
3. Does $\rho_{T_\Co}$ admit a section $\sigma\colon P\too\Gamma^{\mathrm{cong}}_{\Co,2}$ whose image normalizes $W(T_\Co)$ and is a homomorphism modulo $W(T_\Co)$, that is, with $\sigma(p)w\sigma(p)^{-1}\in W(T_\Co)$ and $\sigma(p)\sigma(q)\sigma(pq)^{-1}\in W(T_\Co)$ for all $p,q\in P$ and $w\in W(T_\Co)$? It would give $\langle W(T_\Co),\sigma(P)\rangle = W(T_\Co)\rtimes P$, and with item 2 it would make the congruence group that semidirect product. It needs a membership test in $W(T_\Co)$ — the reflections generating it are the $s_r$ for $r^2 = -2$ (@prop:gamma-co-reflective-set), while item 1 asks for generators of the whole group and does not supply them — and item 2, which is what would make $W(T_\Co)$ the whole of $\widetilde\Orth^+(T_\Co)$, hence the kernel of $\rho_{T_\Co}$ up to the reflection $s_\zeta$ of @prop:gamma-cong-eq-heegner(3). The normalization clause costs nothing: $W(T_\Co)$ is generated by the reflections in all $(-2)$-vectors of $T_\Co$ and every isometry permutes those vectors, $gs_rg^{-1} = s_{gr}$, so $W(T_\Co)$ is normal in $\Orth(T_\Co)$ and the condition is the cocycle condition alone — the splitting of $1\to\ker\rho_{T_\Co}/W(T_\Co)\to\Gamma^{\mathrm{cong}}_{\Co,2}/W(T_\Co)\to P\to1$. The section has a concrete candidate, and it leaves one query: $\langle\tau\rangle\times W(E_8)\subseteq\Orth(T_\Co)$, with $\tau$ the swap of the two isotropic generators of the $U(2)$ summand and $W(E_8)$ acting on $E_8(2)$, maps onto $P$ — the mod-2 image of $W(E_8)$ on $E_8/2E_8$ has order $348\,364\,800 = \abs{\Orth(q_{E_8(2)})}$ with kernel of order $2$, so $\abs{\rho(H_0)} = 2\cdot 348\,364\,800 = \abs P$ — and $H_0\intersect\ker\rho_{T_\Co} = \langle-1\rangle$, the block-diagonal $-1$ on $E_8(2)$; hence the item holds if that $-1$ lies in $W(T_\Co)$, and that is a single membership query against the missing operation (`computations/reports/coble_rel_block_diagonal_section-run.txt`). It is a sufficient condition and not an equivalence, a section outside $H_0$ not being covered by the argument. The query is also not monotone in the two block signs: @lem:block-signs-not-both-in-w shows that the block-diagonal $-1$ on $E_8(2)$ and the complementary block sign $(-\id_{\langle2\rangle\perp U(2)})\oplus\id_{E_8(2)}$ are not both in $W(T_\Co)$, their product being $-\id_{T_\Co}$, which no word in the reflections gives (@cor:no-word-for-the-sign); so an affirmative answer for one of them is an answer against the other, and the query has to be run on this one, not inferred from the sign element's. The cheapest shadow of the query is computed and is exhausted, and the shadow is consistent with either answer, which is what @prop:level-two-shadow-consistent-with-both-answers proves: no linear functional of it separates the block sign from $W(T_\Co)$, while the same span contains the images of two elements that are not in $W(T_\Co)$. In detail: for $g\equiv\id\pmod 2$ the assignment $g = \id + 2h\mapsto h\in\End_{\F_2}(B/2B)$, with $T_\Co = B(2)$, is a homomorphism into an abelian group, so $h(W(T_\Co))$ is the span of the images of the reflections, and over the $1024$ classes whose $\beta$-square is $-2$ modulo $4$ that span has dimension $56$ and contains two elements which are *not* in $W(T_\Co)$ — $\iota = -\id_{T_\Co}$ (@cor:no-word-for-the-sign) and $\epsilon$ — so it cannot decide membership (`computations/reports/coble_663-rel-level2-shadow-run.txt`). What the query needs is a non-abelian computation at level $\ge3$: the same operation, at a level where the shadow is no longer abelian. One step of that is done: the block sign is a product $w\in W(T_\Co)$ of $14$ reflections times an explicit element $k$ of the level-four congruence subgroup (@lem:block-sign-reduces-to-gamma-four, `computations/reports/coble_663-rel-gamma4-reduction-run.txt`), so the query is the membership of that one element $k$, and it stays open. The membership query is the operation the item is missing, and it is missing at the provider (`sage_extensions` at `cf6ba0b2`): `Lattices.reflective_root_locus()` (`src/sage_extensions/categories/lattices.py:9169`) returns a condition set, possibly infinite on an indefinite lattice, and the group objects `T.stable_orthogonal_group()` and `T.O_plus()` offer `subgroup(generators, *, cardinality_decision=None)` and `predicate_subgroup(predicate, description, ...)` — a finite generator list, respectively a decidable element predicate — so neither takes a locus; every finite enumeration refuses, `T.roots()` and `T.roots_of_square(-2)` with `ValueError: cannot run a definite-lattice algorithm on Integral lattice of rank 11 and signature (2, 9)`, and `T.reflective_root_system_components()` with `ValueError: finite reflective-root enumeration requires a definite lattice` (`computations/reports/coble_rel-reflection-group-capability-run.txt`, `-capability2-run.txt`).

What this question asked about the two gluings — whether $\gamma$ or $\gamma_\Co$ is block-compatible — is decided affirmatively for both, on the marked embeddings: @prop:coble-block-compatibility, and with it @cor:gamma-hdg-is-congruence.
:::

::: {.question #qst:distinguished-elliptic-fibration title="Open problem: the elliptic fibration distinguished by the Coble structure"}

The Coxeter diagram of $S_\Co$ has exactly two maximal parabolic subdiagrams, $\wE_8(2)\wA_1$ and $\wB_9(2)$ (@thm:coble-picard-parabolics). They correspond to the two $\Orth^+(S_\Co)$-orbits of primitive isotropic vectors of $S_\Co$ in the closure of the positive cone (@thm:parabolic-isotropic-correspondence (3)), and so to two classes of elliptic fibrations of the K3 cover (@thm:elliptic-fibrations-isotropic).
Which of the two classes does the Coble structure distinguish, and how does the pair of subdiagrams match the boundary data of the degree-$2$ polarized moduli space?
:::

::: {.remark title="The reflection group of each Sterk fundamental domain"}

Sterk's published fundamental domains for the five cusps of $\fentwo$ have $12, 10, 12, 11, 14$ walls [@Ste91], while the full reflection group of each quotient $\eta_j^{\perp\ten}/\eta_j$ has a ten-wall chamber (@sec-sterk-root-counts-and-computed-chambers).
The quotients for $j = 2,3,4,5$ are mutually isometric, so their full Weyl groups are conjugate and cannot account for four inequivalent published diagrams: the datum that does is the reflection subgroup $W_j\leq W(\eta_j^{\perp\ten}/\eta_j)$ induced by the stabilizer of $\eta_j$, and it is identified by @thm:sterk-reflection-subgroups.
What the diagram does not supply is the index: @thm:sterk-reflection-subgroup-index exhibits each published domain as a union of $[W(\eta_j^{\perp\ten}/\eta_j) : W_j]$ chambers of the full group and counts the ideal vertices of the constituent chambers against those of the domain.
:::

### General questions on hyperbolic reflection groups

::: {.question #qst:maximal-parabolic-complexity title="Open problem: the cost of enumerating maximal parabolic subdiagrams"}

Given a Coxeter--Vinberg diagram $\Sigma$ on $n$ vertices, what is the cost of determining its maximal parabolic subdiagrams, equivalently the ideal vertices of the chamber (@cor:ideal-vertices-are-parabolic)?

Enumerating the $2^{n}$ subsets is an upper bound, and @cor:subdiagram-inheritance prunes it: a subset that fails to be elliptic can be discarded together with every subset containing it, so the search runs over the order ideal of elliptic subsets.
No lower bound is known, and no polynomial-time algorithm is known even for the decision problem asked of the whole diagram:

- decide whether a given hyperbolic Coxeter diagram has finite covolume by @thm:coxeter-polytope-volume without enumerating its maximal parabolic subdiagrams;

- exhibit a family of diagrams on which the elliptic order ideal is of exponential size, or show that it is polynomial for the diagrams of finite-covolume groups.

An instance is the uniqueness of $\wB_7(2)$ as a maximal parabolic subdiagram of the Coxeter diagram of $(9,9,1)_1$ (@fig-coble-cusp-9-9-1-parabolics): does such a uniqueness statement admit an argument short of the subdiagram search?
:::

::: {.conjecture #conj:galois-invariance-parabolics title="Open problem: Galois invariance of the maximal parabolic count"}

Let $\Sigma$ be a Coxeter--Vinberg diagram whose Gram form $G$ is defined over a totally real number field $K$, and let $\sigma\in\gal(K/\QQ)$ be such that $\sigma(G)$ is again hyperbolic; the signature of $\sigma(G)$ is not determined by that of $G$ (@ex:galois-conjugate-gram-form).
Then $\Sigma$ and $\sigma(\Sigma)$ have the same number of maximal parabolic subdiagrams.
:::

::: {.question #qst:parabolic-count-growth title="Open problem: growth of the maximal parabolic count with the rank"}

How does the number of maximal parabolic subdiagrams of a hyperbolic Coxeter diagram grow with its rank $n$?
Is there a bound, exponential or otherwise, valid for all diagrams of rank $n$, and does the finite-covolume condition of @thm:coxeter-polytope-volume improve it?
:::

::: {.question #qst:exact-covolume title="Open problem: exact covolumes of hyperbolic Coxeter polytopes"}

Compute $\vol(P)$ exactly for a hyperbolic Coxeter polytope $P$ given by its diagram, with $\vol(P)$ as in @def:covolume.

In even dimension the volume is a rational multiple of the Euler characteristic and is computable from the diagram; in odd dimension the Euler characteristic vanishes and supplies no information.
Find:

- closed-form volume formulas for specific families, through Schläfli's volume differential or through polylogarithmic values;

- the relation between $\vol(P)$ and the maximal parabolic structure of $\Sigma$;

- a covolume computation sharp enough to decide the index $[W : W_j]$ of a reflection subgroup $W_j$ of the group $W$ generated by the reflections in the walls of $P$, in general: the covolume of $W_j$ itself, and not only the index, which is a ratio of two covolumes and is all that counting the chambers of $W$ inside one chamber of $W_j$ can supply.
:::

::: {.question #qst:regularized-theta title="Open problem: theta series of indefinite lattices"}

For a positive-definite lattice the theta series $\theta_L(q) = \Sum_{v\in L} q^{v^2/2}$ converges and is a modular form.
For an indefinite lattice the set of vectors of square $k$ can be infinite, the series does not converge, and a regularization is required before any modular statement can be made.

Define a regularized theta series for the hyperbolic lattices of the Coble and Enriques programs, determine its transformation behaviour, and relate it to the discriminant form, the genus and the reflection group of the lattice.
:::

## The GIT and Looijenga models of the Coble moduli space

::: {.theorem #thm:coble-arrangement-nonempty title="The Coble arrangement is nonempty, and the GIT compactification is not Baily--Borel"}
For a singular type $T$, write $\overline{\Sigma}_T$ for the Zariski closure of the nodal stratum in $\PP(\operatorname{Sym}^6 V^\vee)$. The arrangement $\mch_T^*$ is empty if and only if $\overline{\Sigma}_T$ contains no cube $Q^3$ of a **smooth** conic $Q$. This is Yu--Zheng's Proposition 3.7 [@YZ23, p. 10], read together with Remark 2.2 [@YZ23, p. 5] and diagram (8) [@YZ23, p. 9].

For $T=10A_1$, the closure contains such a cube, and hence
$$
\mch^{*}_{10A_1} = \mch_\infty \intersect D(T_\Co) \neq \varnothing
.
$$
Consequently the GIT compactification is the proper Looijenga model for this nonempty arrangement, not the Baily--Borel compactification of $\Gamma_{10A_1}\backslash D(T_\Co)$. Its arrangement-indexed boundary divisors are indexed by the $\Gamma_{10A_1}$-orbits on $\mch^{*}_{10A_1}$, whose decomposition is computed in @thm:coble-looijenga-coxeter.
:::

::: {.proof}
**Step 1: the type, its lattices, and its group agree with the period setting in this section.** A singular type $T$ in Yu--Zheng is an irreducible component of $\Sigma_n$, whose members are plane sextics with $n$ distinct nodes ([@YZ23, beginning of §3, p. 5]), and the GIT construction uses its Zariski closure and normalization ([@YZ23, §3.1, p. 6]). For $T=10A_1$, @prop:coble-is-the-ten-nodal-sextic-type gives $P=S_\Co$ and $Q=T_\Co$, with $Q=P^{\perp\lkt}$. Yu--Zheng's period lattice $\Lambda_T$ is the same orthogonal complement, so the arrangement in Proposition 3.7 is the arrangement denoted $\mch^*_{10A_1}$ here. The normalizer description of the arithmetic group agrees with $\Gamma_{10A_1}$: [@YZZ25, Lemma 4.5, p. 13] identifies $\Gamma_W|_Q$ with $\Gamma_T$, and the Weyl group is $\{\pm1\}^{10}$, as recorded after @not:sextic-singular-type. Moreover, [@YZZ25, Lemma 4.8, p. 14] proves that every root of $L$ has divisibility $1$, whereas the roots in $\mch^*_{10A_1}$ have divisibility $2$, so no sextic-node root lies in this arrangement.

**Step 2: the good parametrizations are dense in the space of degree-six nets.** Let $\mathbb G=\operatorname{Gr}(3,H^0(\PP^1,\OO(6)))$, let $B\subset\mathbb G$ be the open subset of base-point-free nets, and let $c\colon B\to\PP(\operatorname{Sym}^6 V^\vee)$ take a net to its image cycle. Let $U\subset B$ consist of the nets for which $\phi_N\colon\PP^1\to\PP^2$ is birational onto its image and that image has only ordinary nodes. This condition is open: at a node with preimage points $p\neq q$, transversality of the two branches makes the two equations $\phi_N(p)=\phi_N(q)$ regular at $(p,q)$, so the node and its two preimage points persist under a small perturbation; properness and compactness exclude new identifications away from these pairs and the diagonal.

Dolgachev places the Winger curve at $(\lambda:\mu)=(27,5)$ in the pencil
$$
\mu(xy+z^2)^3+\lambda z(x^5+y^5+z^5+5x^2y^2z-5xyz^3)
$$
(the quintic's $z^5$ appears once; the transcription that stood here carried it twice, and the member with the duplicate does not contain the parametrized curve at all, while the member above has it as its implicit equation — @thm:coble-explicit-sextic and its record, where both are printed)
and, in his coordinates $(x,y,z)=(A_1,A_2,A_0)$, parametrizes it by
$$
[u:v]\longmapsto[-v^6+3u^5v:\;u^6+3uv^5:\;-5u^3v^3]
$$
[@Dol18, §7, pp. 13--14]. The three forms have no common zero. The ten nodes already account for the total defect $\delta=10$ in the genus formula used to prove @lem:rational_sextic_ten_nodes, so they are the curve's only singularities. The Winger curve therefore belongs to $U$ and to $\Sigma_{10A_1}$. Every other member of $U$ likewise has exactly ten nodes by the same genus count. Since $\mathbb G$ is irreducible, the nonempty open set $U$ is dense in $B$.

**Step 3: a triple smooth conic is in the closure.** The base-point-free net
$$
N_0=\generators{u^6,\,-v^6,\,u^3v^3}\in B
$$
gives the map $[u:v]\mapsto[u^6:-v^6:u^3v^3]$, the composite of the degree-three map $[u:v]\mapsto[u^3:v^3]$ with the conic parametrization $[a:b]\mapsto[a^2:-b^2:ab]$. Its image cycle is therefore $Q^3$, where
$$
Q=(xy+z^2=0)
$$
is smooth because its defining quadratic form is nondegenerate. Density of $U$ and continuity of $c$ give $Q^3=c(N_0)\in\overline{c(U)}$. Also $c(U)$ is irreducible and contains the Winger curve, so $c(U)\subseteq\Sigma_{10A_1}$. Thus $Q^3\in\overline{\Sigma}_{10A_1}$, and the criterion in the statement gives $\mch^*_{10A_1}\neq\varnothing$.

**Step 4: the resulting compactification.** @thm:git-equals-looijenga identifies the GIT compactification with the Looijenga model of $\Gamma_{10A_1}\backslash D(T_\Co)$ ([@YZZ25, Theorem 4.11, p. 15]). The nonempty-arrangement case is not the Baily--Borel case described immediately before Proposition 3.7 [@YZ23, p. 10]. The construction above decides nonemptiness only; it does not determine the $\Gamma_{10A_1}$-orbits.
:::

::: {.question #qst:semifan-poset-position title="The position of the three semifans: degeneration cusps and the Looijenga comparison settled, KSBA open"}

The Coxeter, KSBA, and Looijenga semifans of the Coble period domain arise from distinct inputs; @def:looijenga-compactification and the following remark record the Looijenga input. By @thm:coble-arrangement-nonempty the arrangement is nonempty and the compactification is a proper Looijenga model. The orbit decomposition and the Coxeter comparison are settled by @thm:coble-looijenga-coxeter.

**(1) Closure and cusps of Shah's semistable degenerations.** Shah's Theorem 2.4 [@Sha80, pp. 491--492] lists the minimal-orbit curves, and his Theorem 3.2 [@Sha80, p. 493] supplies the nilpotence exponents; the first theorem does not itself assign a cusp. The double smooth cubic in Group II(4) is **not** in $\overline{\Sigma}_{10A_1}$, so it receives no Coble cusp. The remaining rows have the following proved assignments. Only the orbit information shown is claimed.

| Shah row | membership in $\overline{\Sigma}_{10A_1}$ | cusp level |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Group I, irreducible ten-nodal curves | the stratum itself | interior |
| Group II(1), three distinct nonsingular conics $\prod_{i=1}^3(x_0x_2+a_ix_1^2)$ | included | a $1$-cusp |
| Group II(2), a double line and four distinct lines | included | a $1$-cusp |
| Group II(3), the double conic $(x_0x_2+x_1^2)^2f_2$ with a quadric $f_2$ meeting it in four distinct points | included | a $1$-cusp |
| Group III(1), $(x_0x_2+x_1^2)^2(x_0x_2+ax_1^2)$, $a\neq1$ | included | the unique $0$-cusp $(9,9,1)_1$ |
| Group III(2), three distinct double lines $x_0^2x_1^2x_2^2$ | included | the unique $0$-cusp $(9,9,1)_1$ |
| Group IV, the cube $Q^3$ of a nonsingular conic | included | the special Looijenga point, not a Baily--Borel cusp |

In particular the **double smooth cubic is excluded**, rather than assigned to either cusp. Group II gives *a* $1$-cusp, without selecting an orbit of planes. For Group III, the quotient is $F^{\mathrm{un}}_\Co = \bD(T_\Co)/\Orth(T_\Co)$ (@def:gamma-co-groups), and @thm:unpolarized-cusps gives the unique $\Orth(T_\Co)$-orbit of primitive isotropic vectors, computed in its $\Orth(T_\Co)$ row at commit `963843f735`; that justifies "the" $0$-cusp of $F^{\mathrm{un}}_\Co$, whose boundary type is $(9,9,1)_1$ by @thm:cusp_correspondence. Group IV has no family-independent Baily--Borel assignment because Shah's Remark 3.3 [@Sha80, p. 497] permits every exponent $1$, $2$, and $3$; it is the contraction point of [@YZ23, Remark 2.2, p. 5].

**(2) Is the Looijenga semifan the Coxeter semifan of a reflection group? Yes.** @thm:coble-looijenga-coxeter identifies every hyperplane as a pure root. At each $0$-cusp, only roots orthogonal to the cusp line contribute; their boundary traces are primitive $(-2)$-roots and are exactly the mirrors of the reflection group they generate. The induced semifan is therefore Coxeter. This reflection group has only $(-2)$-mirrors; the full $W^{(2,4)}$-Coxeter semifan of @thm:coble-coxeter-semifan additionally has $(-4)$-mirrors.

**(3) Does the Looijenga semifan refine or coarsen the KSBA semifan, or are they incomparable? Open.** The Coble KSBA semifan to which this comparison applies has not been formed: it is a theorem at the four $0$-cusps of $F_{\Co,2}$, with the marking inherited by restriction (@thm:coble-pair-semifan), and it is not formed at the one $0$-cusp of $F^{\mathrm{un}}_\Co$, where @thm:coble-coxeter-semifan leaves the generalized semifan unformed and @warn:coble-marking records the missing dictionary from the nine simple roots to curves on the Coble surface; the quotient $\Gamma_{10A_1}\backslash\bD(T_\Co)$ of this question is neither of the two. A comparison requires the universal stable pair, the relevance marking of the roots, and the resulting cones of combinatorial constancy; the nonemptiness of $\mch^{*}_{10A_1}$ supplies none of these data. The arithmetic layer of the relevance marking is supplied by @thm:coble-cusp-admissibility: it decides, at every cusp of $\fentwo$, which roots of the folded diagrams are traces of the Coble root, and which cusps and incidences carry that marking. No refinement, coarsening, or incomparability is asserted.
:::

::: {.proof}
**The image-cycle criterion.** Put $M=\overline M_{0,0}(\PP^2,6)$ and write $\Phi(\mu)=\mu_*[D]$ for the image cycle of a stable map $\mu:D\to\PP^2$, with multiplicities retained. Chen--Coskun's Theorem 1.4 [@CC11, p. 4; proof pp. 9--10] makes this a morphism to $\operatorname{Chow}_{6,0,2}$. Fulton--Pandharipande's §0.4 and Theorems 1--3 [@FulPan97, pp. 6--7, 11--12] show that $M$ is irreducible and that its smooth-domain locus is dense.

Let $U\subset M$ consist of birational maps from $\PP^1$ whose images have only ordinary nodes. Transversality makes this condition open: a node has two regular identification equations at its preimage pair, compactness excludes new identifications away from those pairs and the diagonal, and the immersion condition is open. Dolgachev's Winger parametrization ([@Dol18, §7, pp. 13--14]) supplies a point of $U$, so $U$ is dense in $M$. Normalization gives $\Phi(U)=\Sigma_{10A_1}$: the image is an irreducible rational sextic, whose total $\delta$-invariant is $10$, and therefore has exactly ten $A_1$ singularities. Hence continuity and properness of $M$ give
$$
\Phi(M)=\overline{\Sigma}_{10A_1}.
$$
Every central stable map below therefore certifies closure; curve selection turns it into an algebraic arc whose general members are irreducible ten-nodal sextics.

**Included rows.** Let $h_m(u:v)=(u^m:v^m)$, of degree $m$. For a line $L$ or smooth conic $Q$ choose a birational parametrization $\rho_L$ or $\rho_Q$. Then $(\rho_L\circ h_m)_*[\PP^1]=m[L]$ and $(\rho_Q\circ h_m)_*[\PP^1]=m[Q]$. A nodal tree of $\PP^1$'s has arithmetic genus zero, and the following stable maps have the exact displayed central cycles:

- **II(1).** Three maps $\rho_{Q_i}$ form a chain, glued at the two common points of the conics, with image cycle $Q_1+Q_2+Q_3$. Explicitly, $\rho_{Q_a}(u:v)=[au^2:uv:-v^2]$ parametrizes $Q_a=(x_0x_2+ax_1^2=0)$ birationally.
- **II(2).** A main map $\rho_L\circ h_2$ and four maps $\rho_{L_i}$, each leaf attached at $L\cap L_i$, give image cycle $2L+\sum_iL_i$.
- **II(3).** A double cover $\rho_Q\circ h_2$ and a map $\rho_R$ onto the quadric $R$, joined at one of the four points of $Q\cap R$, give image cycle $2Q+R$. If $R$ splits as two lines, use birational parametrizations of those two lines instead of $\rho_R$.
- **III(1).** The same two-component construction with a double cover of $Q$ and a birational parametrization of the other conic gives image cycle $2Q+R$.
- **III(2).** Three double covers $\rho_{L_i}\circ h_2$, joined in a chain at $L_1\cap L_2$ and $L_2\cap L_3$, give image cycle $2L_1+2L_2+2L_3$.
- **IV.** A single $\rho_Q\circ h_3$ gives image cycle $3Q$, the degree-three conic cover already used in @thm:coble-arrangement-nonempty.

**Exclusion of II(4).** Suppose the double smooth cubic $2E$ belonged to $\overline{\Sigma}_{10A_1}$. The image-cycle criterion would give a stable map $\mu:D\to\PP^2$ with $\mu_*[D]=2[E]$. Every domain component has rational normalization, since the connected nodal domain has arithmetic genus zero. At least one nonconstant component must therefore map onto $E$, producing a nonconstant morphism $\PP^1\to E$. No such morphism exists when $E$ is a smooth elliptic cubic. Hence $2E\notin\overline{\Sigma}_{10A_1}$.

**The cusp directions.** Shah's Theorem 3.2 [@Sha80, p. 493] gives exponents $1$, $2$, and $3$ to Groups I, II, and III. Those exponents match Types I, II, and III in Alexeev--Engel--Thompson's Definition 2.2 [@AET23, p. 6]. Their §3B [@AET23, pp. 15--16] then gives the rank-one/Type III/$0$-cusp and rank-two/Type II/$1$-cusp convention. It does not by itself identify a particular orbit of planes, which is why only a $1$-cusp is asserted for Group II. The unique $0$-cusp asserted for Group III uses the full-group record at `963843f735` stated above, not a cusp correspondence between stable orbit sets.

::: {.theorem #thm:coble-looijenga-coxeter title="The ten-nodal Looijenga semifan is a Coxeter semifan"}
With $P=\generators H\oplus L$ and $Q=P^{\perp\lkt}=T_\Co$ as in the sextic construction, put $c=\gamma([H/2])\in A_Q$. Every hyperplane of $\mch^{*}_{10A_1}$ is cut out by a pure root $r\in Q$ of square $-2$ satisfying the divided-class condition $[r/2]=c$. These hyperplanes form one $\Gamma_{10A_1}$-orbit, and already one orbit of the discriminant kernel.

At a $0$-cusp represented by a primitive isotropic vector $\eta$, the contributing traces form the complete mirror arrangement of the group generated by their integral reflections. Consequently the Looijenga semifan of $\Gamma_{10A_1}\backslash D(T_\Co)$ at every $0$-cusp is the Coxeter semifan of that reflection group. Its mirrors all have normal square $-2$; the full $W^{(2,4)}$-Coxeter semifan of @thm:coble-coxeter-semifan also has $(-4)$-mirrors.
:::

::: {.proof}
**Pure roots.** Let $\Lambda_1=H^{\perp\lkt}$. A root $r\in\Lambda_1$ has divisibility two there exactly when $u=(H+r)/2$ is integral in the K3 lattice [@YZZ25, Lemma 4.8, p. 14, and its proof]. Decompose $r=t+s$ with $t\in L_\QQ$ and $s\in Q_\QQ$, and write $t=\tfrac12\sum_i n_i\ell_i$ for the node roots $\ell_i$ of $L$. Integrality of $r$ makes $t\in L^*$, so $n_i\in\ZZ$. Since $r^2=s^2-\tfrac12\sum_i n_i^2=-2$, a nonempty hyperplane section of $D(Q)$ has $s^2<0$; indeed $s^\perp$ has signature $(2,8)$ exactly when $s^2<0$. Thus $\sum_i n_i^2<4$, so each nonzero $n_i$ is $\pm1$. But $u\cdot\ell_i=-n_i/2$ is an integer, forcing every $n_i$ to be even. Consequently $t=0$. The root therefore lies in $Q$ itself, and the graph of the discriminant gluing shows that $u$ is integral exactly when $[r/2]=\gamma([H/2])$ in $A_Q$ (with the quadratic-form twist suppressed in this notation).

**The orbit calculation.** Put $B=U_1\perp U_2\perp I_{0,7}$ and let $\rho$ be reduction modulo $2$ on $B$. Wall's Theorem 1.2 and §1.3 [@Wal62] classify the primitive vectors of an indefinite unimodular lattice by norm and ordinary/characteristic type. A norm-$(-1)$ vector of $B$ is ordinary: a characteristic vector has odd square congruent to $\sigma(B)=2-9\equiv1\pmod8$, whereas $-1\equiv7\pmod8$. Hence these vectors form one $O(B)$-orbit. Let $F=\Stab_{O(A_Q)}(\gamma[H/2])$ and let $R$ be the image in $F$ of the stabilizer of a root $r$ in this orbit. Since $B=\ZZ r\perp r^\perp$, this stabilizer is $O(r^\perp)$; surjectivity for the even $2$-elementary twist $r^\perp(2)$ identifies $R$ with $O(r^\perp/2r^\perp)$ (@thm:two-elementary-surjectivity). The discriminant kernel's orbits over the fixed class are the right cosets $F/R$.

The finite image of $\Gamma_{10A_1}$ is the group $H_{10}$ obtained from the ten node permutations through the same gluing, and the $\Gamma_{10A_1}$-orbits are the double cosets $H_{10}\backslash F/R$. Indeed, the normalizer description in [@YZZ25, Lemma 4.5, p. 13] reduces the action on $A_P$ to node permutations: the sign changes in $W(A_1^{10})$ are trivial modulo $2$. Every permutation lifts by the two discriminant-surjectivity theorems, and the discriminant kernel on $Q$ is contained in $\Gamma_{10A_1}$. Passing to the component-preserving subgroup causes no further split: $r^\perp$ is odd unimodular of signature $(2,8)$, so it contains a positive norm-one vector, whose reflection is discriminant-trivial, component-reversing, and fixes the root. The sign map $-\id$ also lies in the kernel, so root vectors and their hyperplanes have the same orbits. The transcript `computations/reports/coble_10a1-looijenga-cusp-run.txt` computes these finite groups and double cosets, rather than enumerating the infinite root lattice. It returns $|F|=|R|=94\,755\,225\,600$, hence $[F:R]=1$, and exactly one double coset $H_{10}\backslash F/R$. Thus both orbit counts in the theorem are $1$.

**The boundary traces.** At an isotropic line $I=\ZZ\eta$, Looijenga's Example 6.4 uses exactly the hyperplanes containing $I$ to decompose the cone $C_{I,+}$ [@Loo02, printed p. 30]. Thus a contributing root satisfies $(r,\eta)=0$, and its normal in $K=\eta^\perp/\ZZ\eta$ is $\bar r=r\bmod\ZZ\eta$, with $\bar r^2=-2$. It is primitive: if $\bar r=kv$ in the even lattice $K$, then $-2=k^2v^2$ forces $k=\pm1$. Its reflection is therefore an integral $(-2)$-reflection of $K$.

Write $\mathcal R_{c,\eta}$ for this set of trace roots, and let $W_{c,\eta}\leq O(K)$ be the group generated by the reflections $s_{\bar r}$, $\bar r\in\mathcal R_{c,\eta}$. Each trace root furnishes a mirror $m_{\bar r}=\bar r^\perp\intersect\HH(K)$ in the hyperbolic space $\HH(K)$ of $K\tensor\RR$: the lattice $K=\eta^\perp/\ZZ\eta$ has signature $(1,8)$, one positive direction, since $Q$ has signature $(2,9)$ and $\eta$ is isotropic, and $\HH(K)$ is a fixed component of $\{x:x^2>0\}$, or equivalently the sheet of $\{x:x^2=1\}$ lying over it; for $x,y$ on the sheet, $(x,y)\geq1$ and $d(x,y)=\operatorname{arccosh}(x,y)$. The mirror is nonempty, because $\bar r^2=-2$ leaves $\bar r^\perp$ of signature $(1,7)$, which meets every component of $\{x:x^2>0\}$. Let $\mathscr H=\{m_{\bar r}:\bar r\in\mathcal R_{c,\eta}\}$ be the trace arrangement. I claim that every reflection of $W_{c,\eta}$ has its mirror in $\mathscr H$.

**Stability.** For a contributing root $r$, the formula $s_r(x)=x+(x,r)r$ shows that $s_r$ fixes $\eta$, because $(r,\eta)=0$, and preserves $\eta^\perp$, so it descends to an isometry of $K$; that isometry is $s_{\bar r}$, since for $x\in\eta^\perp$ the pairing $(x,r)$ depends only on $\bar x$. Because pairings in $Q=B(2)$ are even, $s_r(x)-x\in2Q$ for every $x\in Q$; hence $s_r$ acts trivially on $A_Q$. If $r'$ is another contributing root, it follows that $s_r(r')$ has square $-2$, that $[s_r(r')/2]=[r'/2]=c$, and that $(s_r(r'),\eta)=(r',s_r\eta)=0$: it is again a contributing root, and its trace is
$$
\overline{s_r(r')}=\overline{r'+(r',r)r}=\bar r'+(\bar r',\bar r)\bar r=s_{\bar r}(\bar r').
$$
Each $s_{\bar r}$ is an involution, so it carries $\mathcal R_{c,\eta}$ onto itself; therefore $W_{c,\eta}$ permutes $\mathcal R_{c,\eta}$ and permutes the mirrors, $w(m_{\bar r})=m_{w\bar r}$. Each generator fixes its mirror, a nonempty subset of the chosen component, pointwise, hence maps that component to itself, and $W_{c,\eta}$ acts on $\HH(K)$. This verifies condition (D1) of [@Bou08, Ch. V §3 no. 1, printed p. 76] for $\HH(K)$, $\mathscr H$ and $W_{c,\eta}$: the $W_{c,\eta}$-translates of the members of $\mathscr H$ are again members of $\mathscr H$.

**Local finiteness.** Lemma 1 of [@Bou08, Ch. V §3 no. 1, printed p. 76] states that such an arrangement is locally finite once condition (D'2) of the same number holds: for compact subsets $C,C'$ of the space, the set of $w\in W_{c,\eta}$ with $w(C)\intersect C'\neq\varnothing$ is finite. The proof uses no Euclidean structure: a mirror $m$ meeting a compact $C$ furnishes an element $s_m$ of the group with $s_m(C)\intersect C\neq\varnothing$, because $s_m$ fixes $C\intersect m$ pointwise, and (D'2) leaves finitely many such elements; distinct mirrors give distinct reflections: two trace roots with the same mirror are proportional, since at a point $x$ of that mirror both lie in $\{v:(v,x)=0,\ v\perp T_xm\}$, a line, the orthogonal complement of $T_xm$ being two-dimensional and containing $x$; and proportional roots of the same square $-2$ differ by a sign. I verify (D'2). The elements of $W_{c,\eta}$ are integral matrices in a basis of $K$, so they form a discrete set of isometries of $\HH(K)$. The isometries $g$ with $d(gx_0,x_0)\leq R$, for fixed $x_0\in\HH(K)$ and $R>0$, form a compact set: writing a point $y$ of the sheet as $y=\lambda x_0+y'$ with $y'\perp x_0$ gives $\lambda=(y,x_0)$ and $y'^2=1-\lambda^2$, so the bound $(gx_0,x_0)\leq\cosh R$ bounds the column $gx_0$, and it bounds $g^{-1}x_0$ as well; for $v\in x_0^\perp$ the component of $gv$ along $x_0$ is $(gv,x_0)=(v,g^{-1}x_0)$, which is bounded, and the component in $x_0^\perp$ has square $v^2-(gv,x_0)^2$, which is bounded; in the negative definite space $x_0^\perp$ a bounded square bounds the vector. A closed set of matrices with bounded entries is compact. Finally, if $w(C)\intersect C'\neq\varnothing$, say $wc\in C'$ with $c\in C$, then
$$
d(wx_0,x_0)\leq d(wx_0,wc)+d(wc,x_0)=d(x_0,c)+d(wc,x_0),
$$
bounded in terms of $C$ and $C'$ alone; so $\{w\in W_{c,\eta}: w(C)\intersect C'\neq\varnothing\}$ is a discrete subset of a compact set, and is finite. Since $\HH(K)$ is locally compact, this is (D2) in the equivalent form (D'2) recorded on the same page, and since the closed balls of $\HH(K)$ are compact, $\mathscr H$ is locally finite — Lemma 1, with its proof unchanged.

**Simple transitivity.** Let the chambers of $\mathscr H$ be the connected components of $\HH(K)\setminus\bigcup\mathscr H$; by the stability just proved, $W_{c,\eta}$ permutes them. The chamber calculus of [@Bou08, Ch. V §1 nos. 2--4, printed pp. 62--66] holds for the locally finite family $\mathscr H$ of hyperbolic hyperplanes with the same proofs, because it rests only on the local finiteness of the family, the convexity of its half-spaces, geodesic segments, and the restriction of the family to one of its hyperplanes, all of which hyperbolic space shares. I use four of its statements: a chamber is open and convex and is a component of the complement of the union (Ch. V §1 no. 3, Def. 2 and the discussion following it, printed p. 64); the closure of a chamber is the intersection of the closed half-spaces bounded by its walls (Ch. V §1 no. 4, Prop. 9, printed p. 66); every member of the arrangement is the wall of some chamber (Ch. V §1 no. 4, Prop. 8, printed p. 66); and a hyperplane that is not a member of the arrangement meets some chamber (Ch. V §1 no. 3, Prop. 7 (ii), printed p. 65). I claim that $W_{c,\eta}$ permutes the chambers simply transitively: for a fixed chamber $C$ and every chamber $C'$ there is a unique $w\in W_{c,\eta}$ with $w(C)=C'$. That is Theorem 1 (iii) of [@Bou08, Ch. V §3 no. 3, printed pp. 78--79], whose hypotheses (D1) and (D2) hold by the two preceding paragraphs. Bourbaki proves the theorem over a Euclidean affine space; within §3 the Euclidean structure enters exactly once, in the inequality of the proof of Lemma 2 (i), [@Bou08, Ch. V §3 no. 2, printed p. 77], and the rest is the chamber calculus of §1 and the Coxeter relations. Here is that step for hyperbolic reflections. Let $a\in C$ and let $J$ be the orbit of a point $x\in\HH(K)$ under the group generated by the reflections in the walls of $C$. A closed ball about $a$ meeting $J$ meets it in finitely many points, by (D'2), so some $y\in J$ minimizes $d(a,y')$ over $y'\in J$; I show that $y\in\bar C$. Let $m_{\bar r}$ be a wall of $C$, its root labeled so that $(a,\bar r)>0$; the label exists because $(x,\bar r)$ has constant sign on the connected chamber $C$ and vanishes only on the mirror. Since $s_{\bar r}$ belongs to the group of which $J$ is an orbit, the point $s_{\bar r}(y)$ lies in $J$, so $d(a,s_{\bar r}y)\geq d(a,y)$, that is, $\operatorname{arccosh}$ being increasing, $(a,s_{\bar r}y)\geq(a,y)$. For a hyperbolic reflection,
$$
(a,s_{\bar r}y)=\bigl(a,\;y+(y,\bar r)\bar r\bigr)=(a,y)+(a,\bar r)(y,\bar r),
$$
so $(a,\bar r)(y,\bar r)\geq0$, and $(a,\bar r)>0$ gives $(y,\bar r)\geq0$: the point $y$ lies in the closed half-space of the wall $m_{\bar r}$ that contains $C$. Bourbaki's Euclidean proof of the same step reduces the inequality to $(t|u)\geq0$ for the components $t$ and $u$ of $a$ and $y$ orthogonal to the wall; the display above is its hyperbolic form, with $(a,\bar r)$ and $(y,\bar r)$ the signed normal components. The closed chamber being the intersection of the closed half-spaces of its walls, $y\in\bar C$. This proves Lemma 2 (i) of [@Bou08, Ch. V §3 no. 2, printed pp. 77--78] for $\HH(K)$; parts (ii) and (iii) of that lemma, every chamber being a $W_{c,\eta}$-translate of $C$ and $W_{c,\eta}$ being generated by the reflections in the walls of $C$, follow from (i) by the combinatorial arguments of the same number, and Theorem 1 (iii) then gives the simple transitivity, its uniqueness argument being the relation (3) of that proof, a consequence of the Coxeter relations among the wall reflections in which no geometry enters.

**The conclusion.** Let $t\in W_{c,\eta}$ be a reflection: $t\ne1$, $t^2=1$, and the fixed set of $t$ in $\HH(K)$ is a hyperbolic hyperplane $M$, which $t$ fixes pointwise. Suppose $M\notin\mathscr H$. By Prop. 7 (ii) of §1, cited above, some chamber $C'$ of $\mathscr H$ meets $M$. Every point of $M\intersect C'$ is fixed by $t$, so the chambers $C'$ and $t(C')$, the latter a chamber because $t$ permutes the chambers, meet; two components of one complement are equal or disjoint, so $t(C')=C'$, and simple transitivity gives $t=1$, contradicting $t\ne1$. Every reflection of $W_{c,\eta}$ therefore has its mirror in $\mathscr H$: the trace arrangement is exactly the complete mirror arrangement of $W_{c,\eta}$, which is Theorem 1 (iv) of [@Bou08, Ch. V §3 no. 3, printed p. 79] read for $\HH(K)$. By [@Vin85, Introduction, no. 1, printed pp. 31--32], a discrete group of motions of a space of constant curvature that is generated by reflections in hyperplanes is the group generated by the reflections in the walls of any of the convex polytopes into which the space is partitioned by the mirrors of all its reflections, those polytopes being fundamental domains; the hypotheses hold here, $W_{c,\eta}$ being discrete by the preceding paragraphs and the mirrors of all its reflections being the members of $\mathscr H$ by what was just proved. The chambers of $\mathscr H$ therefore give the stated Coxeter semifan in the sense of [@AEGS25, Definition 5.1, printed p. 609]. Theorem 6.4.3 of [@Dav08, printed p. 83] gives the properness and the fundamental domain from the hypothesis that the chamber be a simple convex polytope with finitely many faces and dihedral angles of the form $\pi/m$; that hypothesis is not available here, the chambers of $\mathscr H$ being known only to be locally finite polyhedra, so the Bourbaki route above is taken instead. All these mirrors have normal square $-2$; the full $W^{(2,4)}$-Coxeter semifan additionally has $(-4)$-mirrors.

## Foundational equations and computational verification

::: {.theorem #thm:coble-explicit-sextic title="The ten-nodal sextic and its double plane, explicitly"}

The image of
$$
\varphi\colon\PP^1\too\PP^2,\qquad [u:v]\mapsto[-v^6+3u^5v:\;u^6+3uv^5:\;-5u^3v^3]
$$
is the irreducible plane sextic
$$
F = 27x^5z+5x^3y^3+150x^2y^2z^2-120xyz^4+27y^5z+32z^6,
$$
which is the member at $(\lambda:\mu)=(27:5)$ of the pencil $\mu(xy+z^2)^3+\lambda z(x^5+y^5+z^5+5x^2y^2z-5xyz^3)$ of [@Dol18, §7, pp. 13--14], read with the correction recorded in the proof of @thm:coble-arrangement-nonempty; $\varphi$ is birational onto it.

The sextic has exactly ten singular points, all ordinary nodes. They lie in the chart $z = 1$; their $y$-coordinates are the roots of
$$
(y^2+6y+4)\,(y^4-8y^3+24y^2+8y+16)\,(y^4+2y^3+24y^2-32y+16),
$$
and for each such $y$ the $x$-coordinate is $x = -(y^9+3948y^4)/1760$. The two points at infinity, $(1:0:0)$ and $(0:1:0)$, are smooth, the line $z = 0$ being a three-fold tangent at each.

The double plane
$$
w^2 = F(x,y,z)\qquad\text{in }\PP(1,1,1,3)
$$
has an ordinary double point over each of the ten nodes. Blowing up the ten nodes separates the two branches of $F$ at each, so the double cover of $\Bl_{10}\PP^2$ branched along the proper transform of $F$ is the minimal resolution of the double plane; that cover is the surface $X$ of @prop:coble-cover-marking, whose proof computes its classes, and it has $K = 0$. Over the exceptional curve $E_i\subset\Bl_{10}\PP^2$ the cover is branched exactly at the two points where $E_i$ meets the proper transform, so $f^{-1}(E_i)\to E_i$ is a double cover of $\PP^1$ branched at two points, hence a $\PP^1$, and the ten $f^*E_i$ together with $f^*H$ are the eleven classes $e_0,e_1,\dots,e_{10}$ that proposition names, with $e_0^2 = 2H^2 = 2$ and $e_i^2 = 2E_i^2 = -2$ by the projection formula (@prop:double-cover-is-k3), all mutually orthogonal. This sextic is therefore a plane model of the marked Coble cover, and its ten nodes are the ten $(-2)$-classes of the marking of @thm:theta-co-coordinates.
:::

::: {.proof}

Every value below is in `computations/reports/coble_explicit-sextic-run.txt`.

**The curve and its equation.** Substituting the three forms into $F$ gives $0$, so the image lies on $F = 0$; and the sextics through the image form a one-dimensional space, since the $37$ coefficient equations in the $28$ coefficients of a degree-$6$ form have rank $27$ — so $F$ is the implicit equation of the image, found without using the pencil at all, and it coincides with the pencil member. $F$ is irreducible of degree $6$, and $\gcd(f_0,f_1) = 1$ gives the three forms no common zero, so the degree-$6$ map is birational onto a degree-$6$ curve. That curve is rational, so the sum of the $\delta$-invariants of its singularities is $(6-1)(6-2)/2 = 10$.

**The ten nodes.** Dehomogenize at $z = 1$ and let $I$ be the ideal of $f, f_x, f_y$ there. Its lexicographic Gröbner basis has leading monomials $x$ and $y^{10}$, so $I$ is zero-dimensional of degree $10$ — the number of standard monomials — and the elimination gives $x = -(y^9+3948y^4)/1760$ as a polynomial in $y$, with the $y$-coordinates the roots of the displayed product of three squarefree factors of degrees $2$, $4$, $4$. Hence there are exactly ten distinct singular points, and no more. That they are ordinary nodes is decided point by point: at a singular point of a plane curve the lowest term of the local expansion has degree at least $2$, and the point is a node exactly when that term is a nondegenerate quadratic form, whose two factors are then distinct linear forms and give the two smooth branches; the quadratic part is nondegenerate exactly when the Hessian determinant $f_{xx}f_{yy}-f_{xy}^2$ is nonzero there. The record evaluates that determinant at each point, in the number field $\Q[y]/(g)$ for each irreducible factor $g$ of the degree-$10$ polynomial, and finds it nonzero on all three factors — so every one of the ten points has a nondegenerate quadratic part, hence is an ordinary node. With $\sum_p\delta_p = 10$ from rationality and ten distinct singular points of $\delta\geq1$ each, no singular point is unaccounted for.
**The two points at infinity.** $F(x,y,0) = 5x^3y^3$, so $z = 0$ meets the curve only at $(1:0:0)$ and $(0:1:0)$, and in the charts $x = 1$ and $y = 1$ the linear terms at those points are $27z\neq0$: both are smooth, and all ten singularities lie in the chart $z = 1$.

**The double plane.** At an ordinary node a linear change of coordinates makes $F = xy + (\text{terms of degree}\geq3)$, so the surface $w^2 = F$ has the quadratic part $w^2-xy$ there, whose matrix $\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&0&-\frac12\\0&-\frac12&0\end{pmatrix}$ has determinant $-\frac14$: a nondegenerate quadric cone, that is an ordinary double point. The double plane has $K = \pi^*(K_{\PP^2}+3H) = 0$, the branch curve being $6H$, and resolving an ordinary double point is crepant, so the minimal resolution has $K = 0$; its exceptional curve over the $i$-th node is $f^{-1}(E_i)$, a $\PP^1$ as computed above, and adjunction $2g-2 = E^2+E\cdot K$ gives it square $-2$, in agreement with $2E_i^2 = -2$. The pullback of a line has square $2\cdot1 = 2$ by the projection formula. These eleven classes are mutually orthogonal, and they are the ones @prop:coble-cover-marking names.
