# Discriminant-form descent {#sec-discriminant-descent}

A counting problem about primitive isotropic vectors of an indefinite lattice is often attacked by passing to the discriminant group, where the set of classes is finite and the isometries act through the representation $\rho_{L,q}$.
This page states the mechanism, the hypotheses under which it holds, and the cases in the tree where it was tried and did not hold, each with the record that shows it.

## The mechanism {#sec-descent-mechanism}

::: {.definition #def:descent-class title="The descent class of a primitive vector"}

Let $L$ be an even nondegenerate lattice with discriminant group $A_L=L^\sharp/L$ and discriminant form $q_L$.
For a nonzero $v\in L$ the divisibility $\div_L(v)$ of @def:lattice-divisibility is a positive integer, and
$$
v^* \definedas \frac{v}{\div_L(v)} \in A_L
$$
is its **descent class**.
Write
$$
\mathcal C(L) \definedas \theset{c \in A_L \st q_L(c) = 0}
$$
for the set of isotropic classes, the zero class included.
:::

::: {.lemma #lem:descent-well-defined title="The descent class is isotropic and equivariant"}

Let $v \in L^{\mathrm{prim}}[0]$ be a primitive isotropic vector.
Then $q_L(v^*) = 0$, so $v^* \in \mathcal C(L)$; and for every $g \in \Orth(L)$ one has $(gv)^* = \rho_{L,q}(g)(v^*)$, where $\rho_{L,q}$ is the quadratic discriminant representation of $\Orth(L)$ on $A_L$.
:::

::: {.proof}

$v$ primitive means $v/\div_L(v) \in L^\sharp$, so $v^*$ is a class, and $q_L(v^*) = (v^*)^2 \bmod 2\bZ = v^2/\div_L(v)^2 = 0$ because $v$ is isotropic.
An isometry preserves $\beta$, hence $\div_L(gv) = \div_L(v)$, and it passes to $A_L$ because it maps $L$ to $L$; so $(gv)^* = \rho_{L,q}(g)(v^*)$.
:::

Define the attained subset
$$ \mathcal C_{\mathrm{prim}}(L) \definedas \theset{[v/\div_L(v)]\in\mathcal C(L)\st v\in L^{\mathrm{prim}}[0]}. $$
Then the class is an invariant of the orbit, and attained $\Gamma\leq\Orth(L)$-orbits map onto the corresponding $\rho_{L,q}(\Gamma)$-orbits inside $\mathcal C_{\mathrm{prim}}(L)$. This is not an assertion that every isotropic class lifts. Surjectivity to $\mathcal C(L)$ requires a separate lift theorem; the general fibre theorem below is stated only for a fixed integral orbit and its attained data.

## The integral orbit-fibre formula {#sec-descent-fibre}

For a primitive isotropic plane $P\subset L$, define its discriminant image intrinsically by
$$ D(P)\definedas\im\bigl((P_{\QQ}\cap L^\sharp)\to A_L\bigr). $$
For a flag $x=(X_1\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq X_r)$ of primitive isotropic sublattices, put $D(x)=(D(X_1),\dots,D(X_r))$. Keep the actual objects and their stabilizers distinct:

- for a primitive isotropic **vector** $v$, $D(v)=[v/\div_L(v)]$ is one class, with the point stabilizer $\Stab_Q(D(v))$;
- for an unoriented **line** $X=\ZZ v$, $D(X)=\langle[v/\div_L(v)]\rangle$ is a cyclic subgroup. Its stabilizer contains the point stabilizer:
  $$ \Stab_Q(D(v))\leq\Stab_Q(D(X)). $$
  Outside the two-torsion case the larger group typically also contains automorphisms sending $D(v)$ to $-D(v)$; for example, when $A_L$ has an element of order three represented by $c$, multiplication by $-1$ stabilizes $\langle c\rangle$ but not $c$;
- for a plane, $D(P)$ is the isotropic subgroup displayed above and need not be cyclic;
- for a flag, $D(x)$ is the chain of those discriminant subgroups, again with the stabilizer of the chain. No plane or flag datum is assumed cyclic.

The integral object must be fixed before any finite quotient is taken, and the exponent-two examples are not used in the general formulas.

::: {.lemma #lem:descent-subobject-data title="The subobject data are well defined and equivariant"}
For every primitive isotropic sublattice $X\leq L$, the subgroup $D(X)$ is isotropic. If $X=\ZZ v$ is primitive, then
$$ X_\QQ\cap L^\sharp=\ZZ\cdot\frac{v}{\div_L(v)},\qquad D(X)=\langle[v/\div_L(v)]\rangle. $$
For every $g\in\Orth(L)$,
$$ D(gX)=\rho_{L,q}(g)(D(X)), $$
and consequently $D(gx)=\rho_{L,q}(g)(D(x))$ for each object type above.
:::

::: {.proof}
If $x\in X_\QQ\cap L^\sharp$, then $x\in X_\QQ$ and $X$ is totally isotropic, so $q_L(x)=0$; hence every class in the image is isotropic. For $X=\ZZ v$, an element $av\in L^\sharp$ is determined by the least positive denominator of $a$, namely $1/\div_L(v)$, so the displayed intersection is the cyclic module generated by $v/\div_L(v)$.

An isometry $g$ preserves $L^\sharp$ and carries $X_\QQ$ to $(gX)_\QQ$, so it carries the intersection to the corresponding intersection for $gX$. Passing to $A_L$ gives the displayed equivariance; for a flag it is applied term by term.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:descent-double-coset title="The orbit fibre over one discriminant datum: the actual double cosets"}
Let $L$ be even and nondegenerate, and let $Q=\rho_{L,q}(\Orth(L))$. Fix an actual integral object $x$ of one of the kinds above—primitive vector, unoriented line, plane, or flag—and let $D(x)$ be its matching finite datum: a class for a vector, a cyclic subgroup for a line, an isotropic subgroup for a plane, or a chain of isotropic subgroups for a flag. Choose the full integral orbit $\mathcal X=\Orth(L)\cdot x$, and put $D=D(x)$. Put
$$ F=\Stab_Q(D),\qquad \mathcal G_D=\rho_{L,q}^{-1}(F). $$
If the orbit-fibre $\mathcal F_{\mathcal X}(D)=\{y\in\mathcal X:D(y)=D\}$ is nonempty, then $\mathcal G_D$ acts transitively on it. Thus $\mathcal F_{\mathcal X}(D)$ is the single orbit $\mathcal G_D\cdot x$, even when several integral objects of this fixed type have the same finite datum.

For $\Gamma\leq\Orth(L)$, write $\mathcal G_D(x)=\Stab_{\mathcal G_D}(x)$. The $\Gamma$-orbits in this fibre are exactly
$$ (\Gamma\cap\mathcal G_D)\bigl\backslash\mathcal G_D\bigr/\mathcal G_D(x). $$
In particular, if $K=\ker\rho_{L,q}\subseteq\Gamma$, set $H=\rho_{L,q}(\Gamma)$ and $S=\rho_{L,q}(\Stab_{\Orth(L)}(x))$. The orbit count is
$$ (H\cap F)\bigl\backslash F\bigl/S. $$
For any translated datum $qD$ with $q\in Q$ and $\widetilde q\in\Orth(L)$ a lift of $q$, the fibre over $qD$ is $\widetilde q\,\mathcal F_{\mathcal X}(D)$ and, under the same kernel hypothesis, its $\Gamma$-orbits are
$$ (q^{-1}Hq\cap F)\bigl\backslash F\bigl/S. $$
:::

::: {.proof}
Let $y\in\mathcal F_{\mathcal X}(D)$. Since $\mathcal X$ is one $\Orth(L)$-orbit, some $g\in\Orth(L)$ satisfies $gx=y$. Equivariance gives $\rho_{L,q}(g)D=D$, hence $g\in\mathcal G_D$. Therefore $\mathcal G_D$ acts transitively on the fibre. The distinction between the whole class $\{y:D(y)=D\}$ and $\mathcal F_{\mathcal X}(D)$ matters when the full integral group has several orbits: every formula here is for a fixed $\mathcal X$, not silently for all primitive objects.

For $\Gamma$, the left action of $\Gamma\cap\mathcal G_D$ on the fibre has stabilizer $\mathcal G_D(x)$ on the right, so its orbits are the displayed double cosets. If $K\subseteq\Gamma$, reduction modulo $K$ is faithful on these double cosets: the group $\mathcal G_D$ is $\rho^{-1}(F)$, $\Gamma\cap\mathcal G_D$ is $\rho^{-1}(H\cap F)$, and $\mathcal G_D(x)$ maps to $S$. Conjugation by $\widetilde q$ identifies the new fibre with the old one and replaces $H$ by $q^{-1}Hq$; the order of a double-coset family is therefore independent of the chosen representative and lift.
:::

::: {.corollary #cor:descent-finite-orbit-sum title="The sum over finite orbits"}
With $\mathcal X$ as above, let $\mathcal R$ be a set of representatives of the $H=\rho_{L,q}(\Gamma)$-orbits on the finite data attained by $\mathcal X$. If $K\subseteq\Gamma$, the total number of $\Gamma$-orbits in $\mathcal X$ is the sum over $D\in\mathcal R$ of the double-coset counts in @thm:descent-double-coset. No surjectivity assumption on $\rho_{L,q}(\Gamma)$ is used. Surjectivity of $\rho_{L,q}$ on $\Orth(L)$ is used only to choose an integral lift of any desired $q\in Q$.
:::

The two input groups are genuinely different: $F$ stabilizes a finite datum, whereas $S$ is the image of the actual integral stabilizer of the chosen object. Replacing $S$ by $F$ silently asserts a lifting theorem. Independent computations of $|F|$ and of a native quotient group do not supply it.

## Spinor restriction {#sec-descent-spinor}

::: {.proposition #prop:descent-spinor-fibre title="Orbit-by-orbit spinor restriction"}
Put $\Gamma^+=\Gamma\cap\Orth^+(L)$, where $\Orth^+(L)$ is the normalized real spinor kernel, and fix one $\Gamma$-orbit $\mathcal O=\Gamma x\subseteq\mathcal F_{\mathcal X}(D)$. If $\Stab_{\mathcal G_D\cap\Gamma}(x)$ contains a spinor-negative element, then $\mathcal O$ is still one $\Gamma^+$-orbit.

Consequently, when this hypothesis holds at a point $x$, the $\Gamma^+$-orbits inside $\mathcal G_D\cdot x$ are computed by the *unreduced* double cosets
$$ (\Gamma^+\cap\mathcal G_D)\bigl\backslash\mathcal G_D\bigl/\Stab_{\mathcal G_D}(x). $$
If $K=\ker\rho_{L,q}\subseteq\Gamma^+$, these reduce to $(H^+\cap F)\backslash F/S$ with $H^+=\rho_{L,q}(\Gamma^+)$. That last simplification is *not* allowed merely because $K\subseteq\Gamma$: $\Gamma^+$ need not contain $K$.
:::

::: {.proof}
For $y=gx$ with $g\in\Gamma$, if $g\notin\Orth^+(L)$ and $\tau\in\Stab_{\mathcal G_D\cap\Gamma}(x)$ is spinor-negative, then $g\tau\in\Gamma^+$ also takes $x$ to $y$ (the product $g\tau$ has positive spinor norm). Conjugation by the same $g$ keeps $\Gamma$ stable, so the hypothesis at $x$ gives the required witness at every point of $\mathcal O$; this still says nothing about another $\Gamma$-orbit within the same $\mathcal G_D$-fibre. If the hypothesis holds at a representative of every $\Gamma$-orbit, every $\Gamma$-orbit in the fibre remains one $\Gamma^+$-orbit.

The displayed formula is @thm:descent-double-coset applied with $\Gamma^+$ substituted for $\Gamma$. Only when $K\subseteq\Gamma^+$ does its final reduction modulo $K$ apply; replacing $H$ by $H^+$ without this containment would discard the $\Gamma^+$-ineffective double cosets.
:::

## Flags: why containment is not enough {#sec-descent-flags}

Apply @thm:descent-double-coset to the full flag $x=(P,L)$ with $L\subset P$. Set $J=D(P)$ and $C=D(L)$, both as isotropic subgroups of $A_L$, and
$$ F_{J,C}=\Stab_Q(J)\cap\Stab_Q(C). $$
Then the flags over $(J,C)$ are governed by
$$ (\Gamma\cap\rho^{-1}(F_{J,C}))\bigl\backslash\rho^{-1}(F_{J,C})\bigr/\Stab_{\rho^{-1}(F_{J,C})}(P,L). $$
If $K\subseteq\Gamma$, this reduces to the corresponding double coset with $H\cap F_{J,C}$ on the left and $\rho(\Stab_{\Orth(L)}(P)\cap\Stab_{\Orth(L)}(L))$ on the right. A table saying only that $C$ is contained in $J$ proves containment. The extra mathematical content is transitivity of the integral plane stabilizer on the primitive lines in a fixed discriminant subgroup; that supplies the flag-fibre alignment while the plane is held fixed.

## Supporting lift theorems {#sec-descent-eichler}

::: {.proposition #prop:descent-eichler title="Eichler filling, with its hypotheses stated"}
Suppose $L$ contains $U\perp U_1\perp L_0$ as an orthogonal summand, with $U_1$ a second hyperbolic plane. For primitive isotropic $v,v'$ with fixed full $\Orth(L)$-orbit and $v^*=v'^*$, @thm:eichler-criterion supplies $g\in\widetilde{\SO}^+(L)$ with $g(v)=v'$, where the hypotheses on squares, divisibilities and congruence class are checked, not inferred from the class alone. Then the orbit-fibre has index one. Any $\Gamma$ containing $\widetilde{\SO}^+(L)$ inherits the corresponding fibre statement, provided the chosen lift belongs to $\Gamma$.
:::

Whether this proposition applies is a property of the integral embedding and its congruence class. It does not apply to a definite quotient such as the Coble plane quotient $I_{0,7}$, and it does not replace the integral plane-stabilizer computation.

## Proved instances {#sec-descent-coble}

**Enriques degree two.** The full integral stabilizer images for the primitive isotropic lines and for the two $O(T_\En)$-plane classes are proved in @lem:line-stabilizer-image and @thm:enriques-integral-plane-stabilizers. Since $A_{T_\En}$ has exponent two, the point stabilizer of a vector class agrees with the subgroup stabilizer of the corresponding line. The plane datum $D(P)$ is an isotropic subgroup: order two in the divisibility-one class and a noncyclic order-four subgroup in the divisibility-two class. In the latter, the actual integral reflection lifts, their dual action through the proved gluing $Q^*/Q\simeq D^\perp/D$, and the generated full kernel establish the equality. An order-$40320$ action on $Q^*/Q$ alone would not have been a substitute. For the source-defined full-$O$ group $\Gamma_{\En,2}=\rho^{-1}(M)$, which contains $\ker\rho$, the finite formula returns one double coset per $M$-orbit. The line type-set produces five arithmetic 0-cusps and the plane type-set nine arithmetic 1-cusps. The positive-spinor statement is used only where the level-two negative witness has been transported within the relevant $\Gamma_{\En,2}$-orbit; the two full-$O$ plane representatives carry such witnesses directly in @thm:enriques-integral-plane-stabilizers.

**Coble vectors.** For $\Gamma_\Co=\ker\rho\cap\Orth^+(B)$, the full integral line-stabilizer image with its Eichler transvections gives a single $\ker\rho$-orbit over each attained nonzero line datum. Here $A_{T_\Co}$ has exponent two, so the vector class and the line subgroup determine the same stabilizer. To check the spinor hypothesis at every relevant orbit, let $g=\id_{U_1}\oplus(-\id_{K_v})$ be the negative witness at $e_1$. For any chosen primitive representative $v_c$ of an attained line class, choose $h\in\Orth(B)$ with $h(e_1)=v_c$ (the full transitivity of @thm:unpolarized-cusps); then $hgh^{-1}$ is still level-two by normality of $\ker\rho$, still spinor-negative, and stabilizes the line $\ZZ v_c$. Thus the witness reaches every relevant line orbit, and the $\ker\rho$-orbits do not split under $\Gamma_\Co$. The numerical count remains a **page proof with the public Coble computation sage-extensions#286**, not a new computed invariant.

**Coble planes.** The integral parabolic image is the proper subgroup $S$ of the full finite stabilizer $F$ described by @thm:coble-plane-stabilizer-image: the definite quotient $I_{0,7}$ induces only $S_7$ in $\Orth^+(6,2)$. Hence $|F/S|=8$. The full finite stabilizer cannot be substituted for $S$, and this is a proved counterexample to any claim that discriminant-form lifting is automatic. Let $\tau=-\id_{U_1}\oplus\id$ be the negative witness at the standard plane $P$. For any plane $Q$, choose $h\in\Orth(B)$ with $h(P)=Q$ (again @thm:unpolarized-cusps); then $h\tau h^{-1}$ is again level-two and spinor-negative, so the witness reaches every plane and in particular a representative of every $\ker\rho$-orbit in every fibre. Therefore the eight $\ker\rho$-orbits do not split under $\Gamma_\Co$. The $189\,720$ total is likewise a **page proof with sage-extensions#286**, while the liftability and finite discriminant counts have their own computation records.

**Coble plane-line flags.** The integral plane Levi gives $GL(P)$. Level two and the real spinor character select $\{A\in SL(P):A\equiv I\pmod2\}$. If primitive vectors $v,w$ have the same parity and are completed to $M_v=[v,z_v],M_w=[w,z_w]$, then $\det(c,z_w-z_v)=0$ in $\F_2$ for their common nonzero first column $c$, hence $z_w-z_v=tc$, because the kernel of $x\mapsto\det(c,x)$ is $\langle c\rangle$. Replacing $z_w$ by $z_w-tw$ aligns the completions. The adjusted $M_wM_v^{-1}$ is level two and sends $v$ to $w$. Thus the plane stabilizer is transitive within each parity class but cannot permute the three classes. Each arithmetic plane orbit and each of the three line classes has exactly one arithmetic flag orbit. There are $24$ flag orbits over one $J$; there is no extra hidden flag splitting. This local flag statement is also a page proof with sage-extensions#286, not a finite containment table repackaged as an orbit computation. Its proof is @prop:coble-arithmetic-flag-incidence.

The discriminant approach remains conditional on the actual integral stabilizer at every stage. The public Coble computation remains sage-extensions#286; those calls and any known-family cards used by other pages retain their own dependencies.
