# Discriminant-form descent {#sec-discriminant-descent}

A counting problem about primitive isotropic vectors of an indefinite lattice is often attacked by passing to the discriminant group, where the set of classes is finite and the isometries act through the representation $\rho_{L,q}$.
This page states the mechanism, the hypotheses under which it holds, and the cases in the tree where it was tried and did not hold, each with the record that shows it.

## The mechanism {#sec-descent-mechanism}

::: {.definition #def:descent-class title="The descent class of a primitive vector"}

Let $L$ be an even nondegenerate lattice with discriminant group $A_L=L^\sharp/L$ and discriminant form $q_L$.
For a nonzero $v\in L$ the divisibility $\div_L(v)$ of @def:lattice-divisibility is a positive integer, and
$$
v^* \definedas \frac{v}{\div_L(v)} \in A_L
$$
is its **descent class**.
Write
$$
\mathcal C(L) \definedas \theset{c \in A_L \st q_L(c) = 0}
$$
for the set of isotropic classes, the zero class included.
:::

::: {.lemma #lem:descent-well-defined title="The descent class is isotropic and equivariant"}

Let $v \in L^{\mathrm{prim}}[0]$ be a primitive isotropic vector.
Then $q_L(v^*) = 0$, so $v^* \in \mathcal C(L)$; and for every $g \in \Orth(L)$ one has $(gv)^* = \rho_{L,q}(g)(v^*)$, where $\rho_{L,q}$ is the quadratic discriminant representation of $\Orth(L)$ on $A_L$.
:::

::: {.proof}

$v$ primitive means $v/\div_L(v) \in L^\sharp$, so $v^*$ is a class, and $q_L(v^*) = (v^*)^2 \bmod 2\bZ = v^2/\div_L(v)^2 = 0$ because $v$ is isotropic.
An isometry preserves $\beta$, hence $\div_L(gv) = \div_L(v)$, and it passes to $A_L$ because it maps $L$ to $L$; so $(gv)^* = \rho_{L,q}(g)(v^*)$.
:::

Define the attained subset
$$ \mathcal C_{\mathrm{prim}}(L) \definedas \theset{[v/\div_L(v)]\in\mathcal C(L)\st v\in L^{\mathrm{prim}}[0]}. $$
Then the class is an invariant of the orbit, and attained $\Gamma\leq\Orth(L)$-orbits map onto the corresponding $\rho_{L,q}(\Gamma)$-orbits inside $\mathcal C_{\mathrm{prim}}(L)$. This is not an assertion that every isotropic class lifts. Surjectivity to $\mathcal C(L)$ requires a separate lift theorem; the general fibre theorem below is stated only for a fixed integral orbit and its attained data.

## The integral orbit-fibre formula {#sec-descent-fibre}

For a primitive isotropic plane $P\subset L$, define its discriminant image intrinsically by
$$ D(P)\definedas\im\bigl((P_{\QQ}\cap L^\sharp)\to A_L\bigr). $$
For a flag $x=(X_1\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq X_r)$ of primitive isotropic sublattices, put $D(x)=(D(X_1),\dots,D(X_r))$. Keep the actual objects and their stabilizers distinct:

- for a primitive isotropic **vector** $v$, $D(v)=[v/\div_L(v)]$ is one class, with the point stabilizer $\Stab_Q(D(v))$;
- for an unoriented **line** $X=\ZZ v$, $D(X)=\langle[v/\div_L(v)]\rangle$ is a cyclic subgroup. Its stabilizer contains the point stabilizer:
  $$ \Stab_Q(D(v))\leq\Stab_Q(D(X)). $$
  Outside the two-torsion case the larger group typically also contains automorphisms sending $D(v)$ to $-D(v)$; for example, when $A_L$ has an element of order three represented by $c$, multiplication by $-1$ stabilizes $\langle c\rangle$ but not $c$;
- for a plane, $D(P)$ is the isotropic subgroup displayed above and need not be cyclic;
- for a flag, $D(x)$ is the chain of those discriminant subgroups, again with the stabilizer of the chain. No plane or flag datum is assumed cyclic.

The integral object must be fixed before any finite quotient is taken, and the exponent-two examples are not used in the general formulas.

::: {.lemma #lem:descent-subobject-data title="The subobject data are well defined and equivariant"}
For every primitive isotropic sublattice $X\leq L$, the subgroup $D(X)$ is isotropic. If $X=\ZZ v$ is primitive, then
$$ X_\QQ\cap L^\sharp=\ZZ\cdot\frac{v}{\div_L(v)},\qquad D(X)=\langle[v/\div_L(v)]\rangle. $$
For every $g\in\Orth(L)$,
$$ D(gX)=\rho_{L,q}(g)(D(X)), $$
and consequently $D(gx)=\rho_{L,q}(g)(D(x))$ for each object type above.
:::

::: {.proof}
If $x\in X_\QQ\cap L^\sharp$, then $x\in X_\QQ$ and $X$ is totally isotropic, so $q_L(x)=0$; hence every class in the image is isotropic. For $X=\ZZ v$, an element $av\in L^\sharp$ is determined by the least positive denominator of $a$, namely $1/\div_L(v)$, so the displayed intersection is the cyclic module generated by $v/\div_L(v)$.

An isometry $g$ preserves $L^\sharp$ and carries $X_\QQ$ to $(gX)_\QQ$, so it carries the intersection to the corresponding intersection for $gX$. Passing to $A_L$ gives the displayed equivariance; for a flag it is applied term by term.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:descent-double-coset title="The orbit fibre over one discriminant datum: the actual double cosets"}
Let $L$ be even and nondegenerate, and let $Q=\rho_{L,q}(\Orth(L))$. Fix an actual integral object $x$ of one of the kinds above—primitive vector, unoriented line, plane, or flag—and let $D(x)$ be its matching finite datum: a class for a vector, a cyclic subgroup for a line, an isotropic subgroup for a plane, or a chain of isotropic subgroups for a flag. Choose the full integral orbit $\mathcal X=\Orth(L)\cdot x$, and put $D=D(x)$. Put
$$ F=\Stab_Q(D),\qquad \mathcal G_D=\rho_{L,q}^{-1}(F). $$
If the orbit-fibre $\mathcal F_{\mathcal X}(D)=\{y\in\mathcal X:D(y)=D\}$ is nonempty, then $\mathcal G_D$ acts transitively on it. Thus $\mathcal F_{\mathcal X}(D)$ is the single orbit $\mathcal G_D\cdot x$, even when several integral objects of this fixed type have the same finite datum.

For $\Gamma\leq\Orth(L)$, write $\mathcal G_D(x)=\Stab_{\mathcal G_D}(x)$. The $\Gamma$-orbits in this fibre are exactly
$$ (\Gamma\cap\mathcal G_D)\bigl\backslash\mathcal G_D\bigr/\mathcal G_D(x). $$
In particular, if $K=\ker\rho_{L,q}\subseteq\Gamma$, set $H=\rho_{L,q}(\Gamma)$ and $S=\rho_{L,q}(\Stab_{\Orth(L)}(x))$. The orbit count is
$$ (H\cap F)\bigl\backslash F\bigl/S. $$
For any translated datum $qD$ with $q\in Q$ and $\widetilde q\in\Orth(L)$ a lift of $q$, the fibre over $qD$ is $\widetilde q\,\mathcal F_{\mathcal X}(D)$ and, under the same kernel hypothesis, its $\Gamma$-orbits are
$$ (q^{-1}Hq\cap F)\bigl\backslash F\bigl/S. $$
:::

::: {.proof}
Let $y\in\mathcal F_{\mathcal X}(D)$. Since $\mathcal X$ is one $\Orth(L)$-orbit, some $g\in\Orth(L)$ satisfies $gx=y$. Equivariance gives $\rho_{L,q}(g)D=D$, hence $g\in\mathcal G_D$. Therefore $\mathcal G_D$ acts transitively on the fibre. The distinction between the whole class $\{y:D(y)=D\}$ and $\mathcal F_{\mathcal X}(D)$ matters when the full integral group has several orbits: every formula here is for a fixed $\mathcal X$, not silently for all primitive objects.

For $\Gamma$, the left action of $\Gamma\cap\mathcal G_D$ on the fibre has stabilizer $\mathcal G_D(x)$ on the right, so its orbits are the displayed double cosets. If $K\subseteq\Gamma$, reduction modulo $K$ is faithful on these double cosets: the group $\mathcal G_D$ is $\rho^{-1}(F)$, $\Gamma\cap\mathcal G_D$ is $\rho^{-1}(H\cap F)$, and $\mathcal G_D(x)$ maps to $S$. Conjugation by $\widetilde q$ identifies the new fibre with the old one and replaces $H$ by $q^{-1}Hq$; the order of a double-coset family is therefore independent of the chosen representative and lift.
:::

::: {.corollary #cor:descent-finite-orbit-sum title="The sum over finite orbits"}
With $\mathcal X$ as above, let $\mathcal R$ be a set of representatives of the $H=\rho_{L,q}(\Gamma)$-orbits on the finite data attained by $\mathcal X$. If $K\subseteq\Gamma$, the total number of $\Gamma$-orbits in $\mathcal X$ is the sum over $D\in\mathcal R$ of the double-coset counts in @thm:descent-double-coset. No surjectivity assumption on $\rho_{L,q}(\Gamma)$ is used. Surjectivity of $\rho_{L,q}$ on $\Orth(L)$ is used only to choose an integral lift of any desired $q\in Q$.
:::

The two input groups are genuinely different: $F$ stabilizes a finite datum, whereas $S$ is the image of the actual integral stabilizer of the chosen object. Replacing $S$ by $F$ silently asserts a lifting theorem. Independent computations of $|F|$ and of a native quotient group do not supply it.

## Spinor restriction {#sec-descent-spinor}

::: {.proposition #prop:descent-spinor-fibre title="Orbit-by-orbit spinor restriction"}
Put $\Gamma^+=\Gamma\cap\Orth^+(L)$, where $\Orth^+(L)$ is the normalized real spinor kernel, and fix one $\Gamma$-orbit $\mathcal O=\Gamma x\subseteq\mathcal F_{\mathcal X}(D)$. If $\Stab_{\mathcal G_D\cap\Gamma}(x)$ contains a spinor-negative element, then $\mathcal O$ is still one $\Gamma^+$-orbit.

Consequently, when this hypothesis holds at a point $x$, the $\Gamma^+$-orbits inside $\mathcal G_D\cdot x$ are computed by the *unreduced* double cosets
$$ (\Gamma^+\cap\mathcal G_D)\bigl\backslash\mathcal G_D\bigl/\Stab_{\mathcal G_D}(x). $$
If $K=\ker\rho_{L,q}\subseteq\Gamma^+$, these reduce to $(H^+\cap F)\backslash F/S$ with $H^+=\rho_{L,q}(\Gamma^+)$. That last simplification is *not* allowed merely because $K\subseteq\Gamma$: $\Gamma^+$ need not contain $K$.
:::

::: {.proof}
For $y=gx$ with $g\in\Gamma$, if $g\notin\Orth^+(L)$ and $\tau\in\Stab_{\mathcal G_D\cap\Gamma}(x)$ is spinor-negative, then $g\tau\in\Gamma^+$ also takes $x$ to $y$ (the product $g\tau$ has positive spinor norm). Conjugation by the same $g$ keeps $\Gamma$ stable, so the hypothesis at $x$ gives the required witness at every point of $\mathcal O$; this still says nothing about another $\Gamma$-orbit within the same $\mathcal G_D$-fibre. If the hypothesis holds at a representative of every $\Gamma$-orbit, every $\Gamma$-orbit in the fibre remains one $\Gamma^+$-orbit.

The displayed formula is @thm:descent-double-coset applied with $\Gamma^+$ substituted for $\Gamma$. Only when $K\subseteq\Gamma^+$ does its final reduction modulo $K$ apply; replacing $H$ by $H^+$ without this containment would discard the $\Gamma^+$-ineffective double cosets.
:::

## Flags: why containment is not enough {#sec-descent-flags}

Apply @thm:descent-double-coset to the full flag $x=(P,L)$ with $L\subset P$. Set $J=D(P)$ and $C=D(L)$, both as isotropic subgroups of $A_L$, and
$$ F_{J,C}=\Stab_Q(J)\cap\Stab_Q(C). $$
Then the flags over $(J,C)$ are governed by
$$ (\Gamma\cap\rho^{-1}(F_{J,C}))\bigl\backslash\rho^{-1}(F_{J,C})\bigr/\Stab_{\rho^{-1}(F_{J,C})}(P,L). $$
If $K\subseteq\Gamma$, this reduces to the corresponding double coset with $H\cap F_{J,C}$ on the left and $\rho(\Stab_{\Orth(L)}(P)\cap\Stab_{\Orth(L)}(L))$ on the right. A table saying only that $C$ is contained in $J$ proves containment. The extra mathematical content is transitivity of the integral plane stabilizer on the primitive lines in a fixed discriminant subgroup; that supplies the flag-fibre alignment while the plane is held fixed.

## Forgetting a flag, and the incidence bijections {#sec-descent-forget}

The object families of @sec-descent-fibre are not independent: a flag has a line and a plane, and both forgetting maps carry discriminant data to discriminant data.
But a group that moves discriminant data does not preserve the set of flags of a *fixed* datum, so the fibres over a datum are acted on only by the subgroup that stabilizes the datum.
This section fixes that group, proves the bijections over it, and then states the incidence criterion in the same form.

::: {.lemma #lem:flag-datum-compatibility title="The datum of a flag is the chain of the data of its components"}

Let $L$ be even and nondegenerate, let $x=(\ell\subset P)$ be a flag with $\ell$ a primitive isotropic line and $P$ a primitive isotropic plane, and put
$$ C\definedas D(\ell)\leq A_L,\qquad J\definedas D(P)\leq A_L, $$
so that $C$ is the *cyclic subgroup* generated by the class of $v/\div_L(v)$, for a generator $v$ of $\ell$, and $J$ is the isotropic subgroup $\im(P_\QQ\cap L^\sharp\to A_L)$ of @sec-descent-fibre; the datum of the flag is the pair $D(x)=(C,J)$.
Then $C\subseteq J$, both are isotropic, and for every $g\in\Orth(L)$
$$ D(g\ell)=\rho_{L,q}(g)(C),\qquad D(gP)=\rho_{L,q}(g)(J). $$
So the forgetting maps $\pi_\ell\colon y\mapsto\ell$ and $\pi_P\colon y\mapsto P$ commute with the datum: the data of $\pi_\ell y$ and of $\pi_P y$ are the two components of $D(y)$, for every flag $y$.

Consequently $\pi_\ell$ and $\pi_P$ carry the full flag orbit $\mathcal X_{\mathrm{fl}}=\Orth(L)\cdot x$ onto the line orbit $\mathcal X_\ell=\Orth(L)\cdot\ell$ and the plane orbit $\mathcal X_P=\Orth(L)\cdot P$ respectively, $\Orth(L)$-equivariantly, and their fibres over full-orbit points are
$$ \pi_\ell^{-1}(\ell')=\Stab_{\Orth(L)}(\ell')\cdot P',\qquad \pi_P^{-1}(P')=\Stab_{\Orth(L)}(P')\cdot\ell', $$
for any plane $P'$ with $(\ell'\subset P')\in\mathcal X_{\mathrm{fl}}$ and any line $\ell'$ with $(\ell'\subset P')\in\mathcal X_{\mathrm{fl}}$; that is, a fibre is one stabilizer orbit of incident objects occurring in the flag orbit, not the set of all planes through $\ell'$ or all lines in $P'$.
So $\pi_\ell$ is injective exactly when those orbits are singletons.

:::

::: {.proof}

For the containment, let $\ell=\ZZ v$ with $v$ primitive.
The class of $v/\div_L(v)$ generates $C$ by @lem:descent-subobject-data, and $v/\div_L(v)\in P_\QQ$ because $v\in\ell\subset P$; so that class lies in the image of $P_\QQ\cap L^\sharp\to A_L$, which is $J$, and therefore so does the subgroup it generates.
Isotropy of $J$ is @lem:descent-subobject-data, and $C\subseteq J$ inherits it.

The equivariance is @lem:descent-subobject-data applied to the line and to the plane, together with $D(x)=(D(\ell),D(P))$: the datum of a flag is the pair of component data, so forgetting carries it to its components.

Surjectivity on the full orbits: a line of $\mathcal X_\ell$ is $g\ell$ and equals $\pi_\ell(gx)$ with $gx\in\mathcal X_{\mathrm{fl}}$, and likewise for planes.
For the fibre over $\ell'$, take $g\in\Orth(L)$ with $g\ell=\ell'$, so that $gx=(\ell'\subset gP)$ lies over $\ell'$; then $P'=gP$ and $\Stab_{\Orth(L)}(\ell')\cdot P'\subseteq\pi_\ell^{-1}(\ell')$.
Conversely let $(\ell'\subset P')\in\mathcal X_{\mathrm{fl}}$, say $P'=hP$ with $h\ell=\ell'$; then $h^{-1}g\ell=\ell$, and $hg^{-1}\in\Stab_{\Orth(L)}(\ell')$, so $P'=hg^{-1}gP$ lies in $\Stab_{\Orth(L)}(\ell')\cdot gP$.
The computation for $\pi_P$ is the same with the roles exchanged, and the injectivity criterion is immediate.

:::

::: {.lemma #lem:chain-stabilizer title="The group of a fixed chain, and the sets it acts on"}

Let $L$ be even and nondegenerate, $\Gamma\leq\Orth(L)$, $Q=\rho_{L,q}(\Orth(L))$, and let $(C,J)$ be a chain of isotropic subgroups of $A_L$ with $C\subseteq J$.
Put
$$ F_{C,J}\definedas\Stab_Q(C)\intersect\Stab_Q(J),\qquad \Gamma_{(C,J)}\definedas\Gamma\intersect\rho_{L,q}^{-1}\qty(F_{C,J}), $$
and write
$$ \Phi(C,J)\definedas\theset{\text{flags }(\ell\subset P)\st D(\ell)=C,\ D(P)=J},\qquad
   \Sigma_2(J)\definedas\theset{P\st D(P)=J},\qquad
   \Sigma_1(C)\definedas\theset{\ell\st D(\ell)=C} $$
for the flags of datum $(C,J)$, the planes of datum $J$ and the lines of datum $C$, inside the full orbits.
Then $\Gamma_{(C,J)}$ acts on each of these three sets, and $\Gamma$ itself acts on the sets of *all* flags, all planes and all lines, and on no one of the three displayed sets unless $\rho_{L,q}(\Gamma)\subseteq F_{C,J}$.
Moreover the forgetting maps of @lem:flag-datum-compatibility restrict to $\Gamma_{(C,J)}$-equivariant maps
$$ \Phi(C,J)\longrightarrow\Sigma_1(C),\qquad \Phi(C,J)\longrightarrow\Sigma_2(J), $$
surjective onto their images; the first has image all of $\Sigma_1(C)$ exactly when every line of datum $C$ occurs as the line component of a flag of datum $(C,J)$, which is an incidence statement.
:::

::: {.proof}

For $g\in\Gamma_{(C,J)}$ and a flag $y=(\ell\subset P)$ of datum $(C,J)$ one has $D(\ell)=C$ and $D(P)=J$, so by @lem:flag-datum-compatibility
$$ D(g\ell)=\rho_{L,q}(g)(C)=C,\qquad D(gP)=\rho_{L,q}(g)(J)=J, $$
because $\rho_{L,q}(g)\in F_{C,J}$; hence $g y\in\Phi(C,J)$.
The same two identities give the action on $\Sigma_2(J)$ and on $\Sigma_1(C)$.
For the last sentence: an element $g\in\Gamma$ carries $\Phi(C,J)$ onto $\Phi\qty(\rho_{L,q}(g)(C),\rho_{L,q}(g)(J))$ by the equivariance proved in @lem:flag-datum-compatibility, so it preserves $\Phi(C,J)$ only if $\rho_{L,q}(g)$ stabilizes $C$ and $J$, and the same holds for $\Sigma_2(J)$ and $\Sigma_1(C)$.

For the restriction statement: by @lem:flag-datum-compatibility the forgetting maps commute with the datum and are $\Orth(L)$-equivariant, hence $\Gamma_{(C,J)}$-equivariant, and they carry $\Phi(C,J)$ into $\Sigma_1(C)$ and into $\Sigma_2(J)$ respectively.
:::

::: {.lemma #lem:zero-datum-divisibility title="The zero line datum is the divisibility-one condition, not a consequence of containment"}

Let $L$ be integral and nondegenerate, let $\ell=\ZZ v$ be a primitive isotropic line with $v$ primitive, and let $C=D(\ell)$.
Then
$$ C=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \div_L(v)=1 . $$
Consequently a primitive isotropic plane $P$ realizes the zero line datum exactly when it contains a primitive isotropic vector of divisibility one, which is not implied by $0\subseteq D(P)$.
:::

::: {.proof}

If $\div_L(v)=1$ then $v/\div_L(v)=v\in L$, so its class in $A_L=L^\sharp/L$ is zero and $C=\langle 0\rangle=0$.
Conversely if $C=0$ then $v/\div_L(v)\in L$; that vector is primitive of divisibility $\div_L(v)/\div_L(v)=1$ in $L$, and it generates $\ell$, so $\ell$ has a generator of divisibility one and $\div_L(v)$ is the least positive value of $\beta(\ell,L)$, namely $1$.

For the last sentence: $0\subseteq D(P)$ holds for every plane because $D(P)$ is a subgroup, whereas $P$ realizes the zero datum exactly when one of its primitive isotropic lines has divisibility one, and a plane all of whose primitive isotropic vectors have divisibility two realizes no such line.
:::

::: {.lemma #lem:stabilizer-image-intersection title="An intersection of stabilizer images is the image of the intersection only under a lifting hypothesis"}

Let $A,B\leq\Orth(L)$ be subgroups with $\ker\rho_{L,q}\subseteq A$.
Then
$$ \rho_{L,q}(A\intersect B)=\rho_{L,q}(A)\intersect\rho_{L,q}(B). $$
In particular, for a flag $x=(\ell\subset P)$ the flag stabilizer image
$$ S_{C,J}\definedas\rho_{L,q}\qty(\Stab_{\Orth(L)}(P)\intersect\Stab_{\Orth(L)}(\ell)) $$
satisfies
$$ S_{C,J}\subseteq\rho_{L,q}\qty(\Stab_{\Orth(L)}(P))\intersect\rho_{L,q}\qty(\Stab_{\Orth(L)}(\ell)), $$
and this inclusion is an equality when $\ker\rho_{L,q}$ is contained in one of the two stabilizers.
:::

::: {.proof}

The inclusion $\subseteq$ is clear.
For the converse let $h=\rho_{L,q}(a)=\rho_{L,q}(b)$ with $a\in A$ and $b\in B$; then $\rho_{L,q}(ab^{-1})=\id$, so $ab^{-1}\in\ker\rho_{L,q}\subseteq A$, hence $b=(ab^{-1})^{-1}a\in A$, and $b\in A\intersect B$ with $\rho_{L,q}(b)=h$.

The last sentence applies this to $A=\Stab_{\Orth(L)}(P)$ and $B=\Stab_{\Orth(L)}(\ell)$, whose intersection is the flag stabilizer, and symmetrically with $A$ and $B$ exchanged.
:::

::: {.proposition #prop:flag-incidence-bijection title="Flags over a plane orbit, and the bijection over a fixed chain"}

Let $L$ be even and nondegenerate, $\Gamma\leq\Orth(L)$, and $P$ a primitive isotropic plane with $J=D(P)$; let $\Pi_1(P)$ be the set of primitive isotropic lines contained in $P$, and for $C\leq J$ let $\Pi_1^C(P)\definedas\theset{\ell\in\Pi_1(P)\st D(\ell)=C}$.

1. **Unrestricted.** The map
$$ \Gamma\backslash\theset{\text{flags }(\ell\subset P')\st P'\in\Gamma P}\ \longrightarrow\ \Stab_\Gamma(P)\backslash\Pi_1(P),\qquad
   \Gamma\cdot(\ell\subset P)\longmapsto\Stab_\Gamma(P)\cdot\ell $$
is a bijection of the $\Gamma$-orbit set of the flags over the plane orbit with the $\Stab_\Gamma(P)$-orbit set of the lines of $P$.
2. **Over a fixed chain.** Let $(C,J)$ be a chain with $C\subseteq J$, and let $\Gamma_{(C,J)}$ be its group of @lem:chain-stabilizer. Then $\Gamma_{(C,J)}$ acts on $\Phi(C,J)$ and on $\Sigma_2(J)$, and for the orbits of planes in $\Sigma_2(J)$,
$$ \Gamma_{(C,J)}\backslash\Phi(C,J)\ \cong\ \bigsqcup_{\mathcal R\in\Gamma_{(C,J)}\backslash\Sigma_2(J)}\ \Gamma_{P_{\mathcal R},C}\backslash\Pi_1^{C}(P_{\mathcal R}), $$
where $P_{\mathcal R}\in\mathcal R$ is any representative and
$$ \Gamma_{P,C}\definedas\Stab_\Gamma(P)\intersect\rho_{L,q}^{-1}\qty(\Stab_Q(C)) $$
is exactly the stabilizer $\Stab_{\Gamma_{(C,J)}}(P)$.
3. **Dual.** Interchanging line and plane, $\Gamma_{(C,J)}$ acts on $\Sigma_1(C)$, and
$$ \Gamma_{(C,J)}\backslash\Phi(C,J)\ \cong\ \bigsqcup_{\mathcal S\in\Gamma_{(C,J)}\backslash\Sigma_1(C)}\ \Gamma_{\ell_{\mathcal S},J}\backslash\Pi_2^{J}(\ell_{\mathcal S}),\qquad
   \Gamma_{\ell,J}\definedas\Stab_\Gamma(\ell)\intersect\rho_{L,q}^{-1}\qty(\Stab_Q(J)). $$
If moreover $\ker\rho_{L,q}\subseteq\Gamma$, then $\rho_{L,q}\qty(\Gamma_{P,C})=\rho_{L,q}\qty(\Stab_\Gamma(P))\intersect\Stab_Q(C)$.
:::

::: {.proof}

*Item 1.*
The flags whose plane lies in $\Gamma P$ are $\Gamma\cdot\theset{(\ell\subset P)\st\ell\in\Pi_1(P)}$, and every $\Gamma$-orbit in that set meets the displayed set, since a flag with plane $gP$ is the image under $g$ of a flag over $P$.
Two members $(\ell\subset P)$ and $(\ell'\subset P)$ lie in one $\Gamma$-orbit exactly when some $g\in\Gamma$ satisfies $gP=P$ and $g\ell=\ell'$, that is $g\in\Stab_\Gamma(P)$; so the orbits correspond to the $\Stab_\Gamma(P)$-orbits on $\Pi_1(P)$.

*Item 2.*
By @lem:chain-stabilizer the group $\Gamma_{(C,J)}$ acts on $\Phi(C,J)$ and on $\Sigma_2(J)$, so both orbit sets are defined, and the planes of $\Phi(C,J)$ lie in $\Sigma_2(J)$.
Fix $\mathcal R\in\Gamma_{(C,J)}\backslash\Sigma_2(J)$ and $P\in\mathcal R$; a flag of $\Phi(C,J)$ whose plane lies in $\mathcal R$ is $g(\ell\subset P)$ for some $g\in\Gamma_{(C,J)}$ and some $\ell\in\Pi_1^C(P)$, and two such flags $(\ell\subset P)$, $(\ell'\subset P)$ lie in one $\Gamma_{(C,J)}$-orbit exactly when some $g\in\Gamma_{(C,J)}$ satisfies $gP=P$ and $g\ell=\ell'$.
The elements of $\Gamma_{(C,J)}$ fixing $P$ are the elements of $\Gamma\cap\rho_{L,q}^{-1}(F_{C,J})$ fixing $P$, and for $g\in\Stab_\Gamma(P)$ one has $\rho_{L,q}(g)(J)=D(gP)=D(P)=J$ by @lem:flag-datum-compatibility, automatically; so that stabilizer is $\Stab_\Gamma(P)\intersect\rho_{L,q}^{-1}(\Stab_Q(C))=\Gamma_{P,C}$, and its orbits on $\Pi_1^C(P)$ are the pieces of the fibre over $\mathcal R$.
Distinct $\mathcal R$ give disjoint pieces because the plane of a flag determines its plane orbit.

*Item 3.*
The same argument with line and plane exchanged, where now for $g\in\Stab_\Gamma(\ell)$ one has $\rho_{L,q}(g)(C)=D(g\ell)=D(\ell)=C$ automatically.

The image statement is two applications of @lem:stabilizer-image-intersection: first to the pair $\rho_{L,q}^{-1}\qty(\Stab_Q(C))$, which contains $\ker\rho_{L,q}$, and $\Stab_{\Orth(L)}(P)$, giving
$$ \rho_{L,q}\qty(\rho_{L,q}^{-1}\qty(\Stab_Q(C))\intersect\Stab_{\Orth(L)}(P))=\Stab_Q(C)\intersect\rho_{L,q}\qty(\Stab_{\Orth(L)}(P)); $$
then to the pair $\Gamma$, which contains $\ker\rho_{L,q}$ by hypothesis, and that intersection.
:::

::: {.proposition #prop:line-plane-incidence title="The incidence criterion in data-orbit form"}

Let $\Gamma\leq\Orth(L)$, let $\mathcal P=\Gamma P_0$ be a $\Gamma$-orbit of primitive isotropic planes and $\mathcal L=\Gamma\ell_0$ a $\Gamma$-orbit of primitive isotropic lines; put $J\definedas D(P_0)$, $C\definedas D(\ell_0)$ and $\bar\rho\definedas\rho_{L,q}(\Gamma)$, and let $\bar C\definedas\bar\rho\cdot C$ be the orbit of $C$ under $\bar\rho$ on the subgroups of $A_L$.
Say that $\mathcal P$ and $\mathcal L$ are **incident** when a plane of $\mathcal P$ contains a line of $\mathcal L$.

1. **(Necessity.)** If $\mathcal P$ and $\mathcal L$ are incident, then some $C'\in\bar C$ satisfies $C'\subseteq J$.
2. **(Sufficiency.)** Suppose that for every $C'\in\bar C$ the primitive isotropic lines with datum $C'$ form a single $\Gamma$-orbit. If some primitive isotropic line $\ell\subset P_0$ has $D(\ell)\in\bar C$, then $\mathcal P$ and $\mathcal L$ are incident.
3. **(The zero datum.)** For $C=0$ the hypothesis of item 2 is the existence of a primitive isotropic vector of divisibility one in $P_0$, by @lem:zero-datum-divisibility; the containment $0\subseteq J$ alone does not supply it.
:::

::: {.proof}

*Item 1.*
By transitivity of $\Gamma$ on $\mathcal P$, incidence is equivalent to the existence of a line of $\mathcal L$ contained in $P_0$.
Such a line is $h\ell_0$ for some $h\in\Gamma$, and
$$ D(h\ell_0)=\rho_{L,q}(h)(C)\in\bar C $$
by @lem:flag-datum-compatibility, with $D(h\ell_0)\subseteq D(P_0)=J$ by the containment of that lemma.
Take $C'=D(h\ell_0)$.

*Item 2.*
Let $\ell\subset P_0$ satisfy $D(\ell)=C'\in\bar C$; by definition of $\bar C$ there is $h\in\Gamma$ with $C'=\rho_{L,q}(h)(C)$, so $D(h^{-1}\ell)=\rho_{L,q}(h)^{-1}(C')=C$.
The lines of datum $C$ form a single $\Gamma$-orbit by hypothesis, so $h^{-1}\ell=g\ell_0$ for some $g\in\Gamma$, hence $\ell=hg\ell_0$ lies in $\mathcal L$ and is contained in $P_0$.

*Item 3.*
That is @lem:zero-datum-divisibility, which identifies the datum $0$ with divisibility one for a primitive isotropic line.
:::

The double-coset formula of @thm:descent-double-coset for a flag needs the flag stabilizer image $S_{C,J}=\rho_{L,q}\qty(\Stab_{\Orth(L)}(P)\intersect\Stab_{\Orth(L)}(\ell))$ itself; by @lem:stabilizer-image-intersection it is in general a proper subgroup of $\rho_{L,q}(\Stab_{\Orth(L)}(P))\intersect\rho_{L,q}(\Stab_{\Orth(L)}(\ell))$, and substituting that intersection is the flag-level form of replacing the integral $S$ by the finite stabilizer $F$, which @sec-descent-fibre already rejects for the plane.
Item 2 of @prop:flag-incidence-bijection is the alternative: the flag orbits over a fixed chain are read off from a stabilizer of a representative plane, at the cost of assembling over the plane orbits of $\Gamma_{(C,J)}$ rather than of $\Gamma$, and @prop:line-plane-incidence reduces the incidence question to two integral statements about a plane, in data-orbit form.

## The application hypotheses, and their owners {#sec-descent-hypotheses}

@thm:descent-double-coset needs the even lattice, the coefficient group $Q=\rho_{L,q}(\Orth(L))$, the kernel containment $\ker\rho_{L,q}\subseteq\Gamma$, and the integral stabilizer image $S=\rho_{L,q}(\Stab_{\Orth(L)}(x))$ of the object at hand.
@prop:descent-spinor-fibre needs a spinor-negative element of $\Stab_{\mathcal G_D\cap\Gamma}(x)$.
@prop:flag-incidence-bijection needs, over a fixed chain, the groups $\Gamma_{(C,J)}$ and $\Gamma_{P,C}$ of @lem:chain-stabilizer and item 2.
@prop:line-plane-incidence needs, for a pair of orbits, the two implications of its items 1 and 2 in data-orbit form.
Every row below is an integral statement; none is a finite model, and the two families supply the inputs in two different shapes.

**The shape of the spinor step.**
@thm:descent-double-coset is applied at a group containing $\ker\rho_{L,q}$.
When the group of interest is $\Gamma^+=\Gamma\intersect\Orth^+(L)$ with $\ker\rho_{L,q}\not\subseteq\Gamma^+$, the kernel containment is *not* a hypothesis of $\Gamma^+$: the theorem is applied at $\Gamma$, which contains the kernel, and @prop:descent-spinor-fibre descends to $\Gamma^+$ afterwards, which is legitimate exactly when each relevant stabilizer contains a spinor-negative element.
At the Coble family this is the case at $\Gamma=\ker\rho$ with $\Gamma^+=\Gamma_\Co$, since $\Gamma_\Co=\ker\rho\intersect\Orth^+(B)$ does not contain $\ker\rho$.

**Enriques degree two**, $L=T_\En$ even of signature $(2,10)$, $\Gamma_{\En,2}=\rho^{-1}(M)$, so $\bar\rho=M$; this group *moves* discriminant data, and the data-orbit form of @prop:line-plane-incidence is the applicable one.
Lines: $S=\Stab_Q(c)$ exactly, by @lem:line-stabilizer-image, whose integral inputs are $K\subseteq\gent$ (@prop:gamma-en-two-index) and the surjectivity of @thm:two-elementary-surjectivity; at divisibility two the lift is Sterk's congruence step, and each fibre of lines over an $M$-orbit of classes is a single $\gent$-orbit.
Planes: $S=F$ for both plane classes, by @thm:enriques-integral-plane-stabilizers, which supplies the integral reflection lifts, the gluing $A_Q\to D^\perp/D$ and the kernel of order $49152$; its positive-spinor restriction carries its own witnesses.
Spinor: $\sigma_U$ and $\tau$ of @thm:enriques-spinor-orbit-agreement are spinor-negative elements of $\ker\rho$ fixing the line and plane representatives, @thm:enriques-flag-witnesses supplies such an element in each flag stabilizer, and the conjugates reach every object by normality of $\ker\rho$; so @prop:descent-spinor-fibre applies at every line, plane and flag, and $\Gamma_{\En,2}$ and $\Gamma_{\En,2}^+$ have the same orbits on the three families.
Incidence: item 1 of @prop:line-plane-incidence is necessary here in its data-orbit form, because $M$ moves the classes; item 2 needs the single-line-orbit hypothesis, which is the fibre statement of @thm:sterk-line-fibres, and its realization statement, which that theorem states with the zero class separated from the nonzero ones:
every *nonzero* class $d$ of the image $D$ is the class of a primitive combination $av+bw$ of divisibility two — the three nonzero classes of the $(\ZZ/2)^2$ realized by $(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)$ — while the zero class is realized only by a divisibility-one vector, which occurs exactly when a combination has odd pairing, that is precisely for the four $4.3.16$ planes.
By @lem:zero-datum-divisibility the zero line datum is exactly the divisibility-one condition, and the containment $0\subseteq D(P)$, true for every plane and in particular for the five $4.3.17$ planes, does not supply it.
With those inputs the incidence of the nine plane orbits with the five line orbits is: the line orbits whose classes lie in the $M$-orbit of the nonzero classes of the plane's image, together with the divisibility-one line orbit exactly for the four planes that contain a divisibility-one vector — the incidence computed in @thm:sterk-line-fibres, with zero and nonzero kept apart.
Flags are not needed for the boundary strata of $\bD(T_\En)$, which are the lines and the planes; where a flag statement is wanted, item 2 of @prop:flag-incidence-bijection applies, and the flag stabilizer image $S_{C,J}$ is not computed on this page and is not claimed.

**Coble**, the lattice $T_\Co$ of @sec-lattices with the odd unimodular model $B=U\perp U\perp\generators{-1}^{7}$ of rank $11$ (so $T_\Co=B(2)$ and $A_{T_\Co}\cong B/2B$), and $\Gamma_\Co=\ker\rho\intersect\Orth^+(B)$, whose discriminant image is trivial.
The theorem is applied at $\Gamma=\ker\rho$, for which the kernel containment is immediate, and the spinor step then gives $\Gamma_\Co$; no kernel containment is attributed to $\Gamma_\Co$.
Because $\rho_{L,q}(\Gamma_\Co)=1$, every datum is fixed by $\Gamma_\Co$: here $\Gamma_{(C,J)}=\Gamma_\Co$ for every chain, so @lem:chain-stabilizer gives that $\Gamma_\Co$ acts on each fixed-chain fibre, and both the unrestricted bijection of item 1 and the refined one of item 2 of @prop:flag-incidence-bijection are available, the second splitting the fibre by line class.
Lines: the line row of @sec-descent-coble proves, from the integral line stabilizer with its Eichler transvections, that each line datum carries a single $\ker\rho$-orbit, and the spinor witness keeps it single under $\Gamma_\Co$.
Planes: $S=\rho(\Stab_{\Orth(B)}(P))$ of order $247\,726\,080$, a proper subgroup of $F=\Stab_Q(J)$ of index $8$ by @thm:coble-plane-stabilizer-image, whose item 4 shows that no integral isometry supplies the missing eight cosets; so $S=F$ fails at this family, and the plane fibre carries eight $\ker\rho$-orbits.
Spinor: the Levi element $\tau=-\id_{U_1}\oplus\id$ is integral, congruent to the identity modulo $2$, stabilizes the standard plane and is spinor-negative; its conjugates reach every plane of every fibre, so the eight $\ker\rho$-orbits descend to $\Gamma_\Co$.
Flags and incidence: the image $\Gamma(P)=\theset{A\in SL(P)\st A\equiv I\pmod2}$ of $\Stab_{\Gamma_\Co}(P)$ on $P$ is transitive on the primitive lines of $P$ within each nonzero class $c\in J$, by @prop:coble-arithmetic-flag-incidence, and this is transitivity of $\Stab_{\Gamma_\Co}(P)$ itself; since $\Gamma_\Co$ fixes all data, that is item 2 of @prop:flag-incidence-bijection with $\Gamma_{(C,J)}=\Gamma_\Co$ and $\Gamma_{P,C}=\Stab_{\Gamma_\Co}(P)$, so there is one flag orbit over each plane orbit for each nonzero class of $J$.
The same transitivity is the realization input of item 2 of @prop:line-plane-incidence and the line row is its single-orbit input, so the three incidences of each plane node are proved, not assumed; the flag stabilizer image $S_{C,J}$ is not needed for either.

Neither column asserts that a finite stabilizer is the integral image: at Enriques planes the equality $S=F$ is proved from the integral lifts, and at Coble planes it is disproved, with $[F:S]=8$.
What remains outside this page is the public comparison of the arithmetic counts with the known-family cards, which for the Coble family is the lattice-orbit computation `sage-extensions#286` cited throughout this page and for the Enriques family the public call of the degree-two evaluation.
The implications proved and tabulated here are the mechanism and its hypotheses; no count is certified by them.

## The stabilizer's discriminant image is a congruence quotient {#sec-descent-congruence}

The finite data of a commensurability class give the integral stabilizer exactly, but they give its discriminant image only at a modulus refined enough to make the discriminant representation descend.
This section proves that refinement, states the finite objects and the map, and proves the alternative route through the discriminant isometries of the orbit lattices as a second complete construction, comparing the two index sets rather than ranking them.

::: {.definition #def:stabilizer-congruence-data title="The congruence data of a lattice stabilizer"}

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over $\bQ$ with a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form $\beta$.
Let $L\subseteq V$ be an integral nondegenerate lattice, so $L\subseteq L^\sharp$, and let $M\subseteq V$ be a finitely generated full $\bZ$-submodule with $L\subseteq M$.
The form need not be integral on $M$, and $M$ need not be contained in $L^\sharp$; @ex:non-integral-invariant-module is an instance of the commensurability contract in which both fail.
Let $G\leq\Orth(V)$ be a subgroup preserving $M$, so $gM=M$ for every $g\in G$.
Since $M$ and $L^\sharp$ are finitely generated full $\bZ$-submodules of $V$, their intersection is full and of finite index in each, so the two quotients
$$ M/L,\qquad L^\sharp/(L^\sharp\intersect M) $$
are finite abelian groups; put
$$ e_1\definedas\exp(M/L),\qquad e_2\definedas\exp\qty(L^\sharp/(L^\sharp\intersect M)). $$
Then $e_1$ is the least positive integer with $e_1M\subseteq L$, and $e_2$ the least positive integer with $e_2L^\sharp\subseteq L^\sharp\intersect M$, equivalently with $e_2L^\sharp\subseteq M$.
Write $\pi_m\colon G\to\Aut_{\bZ}(M/mM)$ for the reduction modulo $mM$, and for $m$ with $mM\subseteq L$ write $\bar L_m\definedas L/mM$ for the image of $L$ in $M/mM$ and put
$$ \bar K_m\definedas\ker\pi_m,\qquad \bar H_m\definedas\Stab_{\pi_m(G)}(\bar L_m). $$
The $\bZ$-module $L^\sharp/L=A_L$ carries the bilinear discriminant form of @def:discriminant, and $\rho_L$ denotes the bilinear discriminant representation of @def:discriminant-rep; if $L$ is even, $A_L$ carries the quadratic refinement $q_L$ as well and $\rho_L=j_q\circ\rho_{L,q}$ is the composite of the quadratic representation with the forgetful monomorphism.
:::

::: {.example #ex:non-integral-invariant-module title="The invariant module need not be integral, nor contained in the dual of $L$"}

Let $L=U=\bZ e\oplus\bZ f$ with $\beta(e,f)=1$, so that $L$ is unimodular and $L^\sharp=L$, and let $g$ be the rational map $g(e)=f/2$, $g(f)=2e$.
It is an isometry: $(f/2,f/2)=(2e,2e)=0$ and $(f/2,2e)=1=\beta(e,f)$, and $g^2=\id$.
Then
$$ M\definedas L+gL=\bZ e\oplus\bZ\qty(f/2) $$
is a finitely generated full $\bZ$-submodule containing $L$ with $gM=M$, and $M/L\cong\bZ/2$, so the triple $(G,M,L)$ with $G=\generators{g}$ satisfies the module contract of @def:stabilizer-congruence-data.
But $\beta(e,f/2)=1/2$, so $M$ is not integral, and $f/2\notin L=L^\sharp$, so $M$ is not contained in $L^\sharp$; a quotient of $L^\sharp$ by $M$ is therefore not defined, which is exactly the case that the exponent $\exp(L^\sharp/(L^\sharp\intersect M))$ of @def:stabilizer-congruence-data covers.
The exponents of the definition are $e_1=\exp(M/L)=2$ and $e_2=\exp\qty(L^\sharp/(L^\sharp\intersect M))=1$, the latter because $L^\sharp\intersect M=L\cap M=L$; the modulus condition $mM\subseteq e_2L=L$ holds for $m=2$.
:::

::: {.lemma #lem:orbit-permutation-not-module-action title="The action on the orbit of submodules is not the module action"}

Let $m\geq1$ with $mM\subseteq L$, let $\Sigma_m\definedas\pi_m(G)\bar L_m$ be the orbit of $\bar L_m$ in $M/mM$ under the finite image $\pi_m(G)$, and let
$$ \sigma_m\colon G\too\Sym(\Sigma_m) $$
be the action on that orbit, with kernel $\kappa_m\definedas\ker\sigma_m$.
Then:

1. $\bar K_m\subseteq\kappa_m$;
2. if $L=M$, then $\bar L_m=M/mM$ is the whole module, $\Sigma_m$ is a single point, and $\kappa_m=G$ for every $m$, whereas $\bar K_m=\ker\pi_m$ is a proper subgroup of $G$ as soon as $\pi_m$ is nontrivial;
3. $\sigma_m$ neither proves $\bar K_m\subseteq\ker\rho_L$ nor defines a map to $\Orth(A_L)$.
:::

::: {.proof}

An element of $\bar K_m$ acts trivially on $M/mM$, hence fixes every element of $\Sigma_m$, which is item 1.

For item 2, if $L=M$ then $\bar L_m=L/mM=M/mM$ is the whole of $M/mM$, whose orbit is itself; so $\Sigma_m$ is a single point, $\sigma_m$ is trivial, $\kappa_m=G$, and $\bar K_m$ is the congruence subgroup modulo $mM$, a proper subgroup of $G$ when $\pi_m$ is nontrivial.

Item 3 is then immediate in general and follows from an explicit element for the Enriques lattice: let $N=T_\En$ of @sec-lattices, $L=M=N$ and $m=2$, so that $\kappa_2=\Orth(N)$ by item 2; the isometry interchanging $e'\leftrightarrow f'$ in the summand $U(2)$ and fixing $U\oplus E_8(2)$ is integral and carries the class of $e'/2$ to the class of $f'/2$, so it acts nontrivially on $A_N$ while lying in $\kappa_2$.
Hence an element of $\kappa_m$ need not act trivially on $A_L$, and passing to the permutation image loses the module action that $\Phi_m$ is defined on.
:::

::: {.lemma #lem:congruence-stabilizer-preimage title="The integral stabilizer is an exact residue preimage"}

Let $m\geq1$ satisfy $mM\subseteq L$.
Then $\bar L_m$ is a submodule of $M/mM$, the finite group $\pi_m(G)$ acts on $M/mM$, and
$$ \Stab_G(L)=G\intersect\pi_m^{-1}\bigl(\bar H_m\bigr). $$
:::

::: {.proof}

Since $mM\subseteq L\subseteq M$, the submodule $\bar L_m$ is defined.
Both inclusions are checked separately.
If $gL=L$, then $\pi_m(g)\bar L_m=\overline{gL}=\bar L_m$, so $\Stab_G(L)\subseteq G\intersect\pi_m^{-1}(\bar H_m)$.

Conversely let $g\in G$ with $\pi_m(g)\bar L_m=\bar L_m$, that is $gL+mM=L$.
Since $mM\subseteq L$ one has $L+mM=L$, so the equality reads $gL\subseteq L$; and $gL\subseteq M$ because $gM=M$ and $L\subseteq M$.
A $\bQ$-linear automorphism of $V$ carries lattices to lattices of covolume scaled by $\abs{\det g}$, and $\det g=\pm1$ for an isometry of a nondegenerate form, so $[L:gL]=\abs{\det g}=1$ and $gL=L$.
:::

::: {.lemma #lem:congruence-kernel-discriminant title="The congruence kernel acts trivially on the dual lattice modulo the lattice"}

Let $m\geq1$ satisfy $mM\subseteq L$, and let $f\geq1$ satisfy $fL^\sharp\subseteq M$ and $\frac{m}{f}M\subseteq L$.
Then $\bar K_m\subseteq\ker\rho_L$.
:::

::: {.proof}

Let $g\in\bar K_m$ and let $\theta\definedas\frac{1}{m}\qty(g-\id)$ be the induced $\bQ$-linear endomorphism of $V$.
The congruence $g\equiv\id\pmod{mM}$ says $(g-\id)(M)\subseteq mM$, hence $\theta(M)\subseteq M$.

Let $y\in L^\sharp$.
Then $fy\in M$, so $\theta(fy)\in M$ and, by $\bQ$-linearity of $\theta$,
$$ \theta(y)=\frac{1}{f}\theta(fy)\in\frac{1}{f}M, $$
hence
$$ (g-\id)(y)=m\theta(y)\in\frac{m}{f}M\subseteq L. $$
So $g$ acts trivially on the classes of $L^\sharp$ modulo $L$.
It remains to see that $g$ acts on $A_L$ at all: by @lem:congruence-stabilizer-preimage (applied to $mM\subseteq L$) one has $gL=L$, and an isometry carrying $L$ to itself carries $L^\sharp=\theset{y\in V\st\beta(y,L)\subseteq\bZ}$ to itself; so $\rho_L(g)$ is defined and, by the display, equals the identity.
:::

::: {.corollary #cor:stabilizer-modulus title="A sufficient modulus, and the least one this argument gives"}

Let $m\geq1$ satisfy $mM\subseteq e_2L$.
Then $\bar K_m\subseteq\Stab_G(L)\intersect\ker\rho_L$.
The integers with $mM\subseteq e_2L$ are exactly the multiples of $\exp(M/e_2L)$, and $e_1e_2$ is one of them.
In particular, if $L=M$, then $e_1=1$ and the modulus $e_2=\exp(A_M)$ satisfies $e_2M\subseteq e_2L$; reduction modulo $\exp(A_M)\cdot M$ therefore determines the action of $\Orth(M)$ on the discriminant module $A_M$.
:::

::: {.proof}

By hypothesis $mM\subseteq e_2L\subseteq L$, so the first hypothesis of @lem:congruence-kernel-discriminant holds; the second is its hypothesis $e_2L^\sharp\subseteq M$, and the third is $\frac{m}{e_2}M\subseteq L$, which is $mM\subseteq e_2L$.
The set $\theset{m\geq1\st mM\subseteq N}$ for a lattice $N$ with $N\subseteq M$ is the set of multiples of $\exp(M/N)$; take $N=e_2L$.
Finally $e_1e_2M=e_2\qty(e_1M)\subseteq e_2L$ because $e_1M\subseteq L$, and for $L=M$ one has $e_1=1$.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:stabilizer-discriminant-image title="The discriminant image of an integral stabilizer, computed from one finite quotient"}

Let $m\geq1$ satisfy $mM\subseteq e_2L$, so that @cor:stabilizer-modulus gives $\bar K_m\subseteq\Stab_G(L)\intersect\ker\rho_L$.
Let $\bar H_m=\Stab_{\pi_m(G)}(\bar L_m)$ be the orbit-point stabilizer of $\bar L_m$ inside the finite image $\pi_m(G)\leq\Aut_{\bZ}(M/mM)$ of the module action: the stabilizer is taken in that image, not in $\Aut_{\bZ}(M/mM)$ and not in the permutation image of $\pi_m(G)$ on the orbit of $\bar L_m$.
Then:

1. $\Stab_G(L)=G\intersect\pi_m^{-1}(\bar H_m)$, so the integral stabilizer is the exact preimage of the finite orbit-point stabilizer;
2. the assignment
$$ \Phi_m\colon\bar H_m\too\Orth(A_L),\qquad \pi_m(g)\longmapsto\rho_L(g) $$
is a well-defined group homomorphism, with kernel $\pi_m(\Stab_G(L)\intersect\ker\rho_L)$;
3. $\Phi_m(\bar H_m)=\rho_L(\Stab_G(L))$; and if $\bar h_1,\dots,\bar h_r$ generate $\bar H_m$ and $g_i\in G$ is any lift of $\bar h_i$, then
$$ \rho_L(\Stab_G(L))=\generators{\rho_L(g_1),\dots,\rho_L(g_r)}\leq\Orth(A_L); $$
4. every lift $g_i$ is an integral self-isometry of $L$, so the endomorphism case of the discriminant morphism applies to $\restrictionof{g_i}{L}\colon L\to L$.

The construction therefore returns the exact subgroup $\rho_L(\Stab_G(L))$ from finitely many objects: the module $M$, the submodule $\bar L_m$ of $M/mM$, the finite group $\pi_m(G)$, and one lift per generator of the finite stabilizer.
No generating family of $\Stab_G(L)$ itself is materialized.

If $L$ is even, the same assignment lands in $\Orth(A_{L,q})$: an isometry stabilizing $L$ preserves $\beta$ and hence the norm of every element of $L^\sharp$, so it preserves the quadratic refinement $q_L$, and $\Phi_m$ is $\Phi_{m,q}$ followed by the forgetful monomorphism of @def:discriminant-rep.
:::

::: {.proof}

Item 1 is @lem:congruence-stabilizer-preimage applied at the modulus $m$.

For item 2, let $g\in G$ with $\pi_m(g)\in\bar H_m$; by item 1 $g\in\Stab_G(L)$, so $\rho_L(g)$ is defined.
If $g'\in G$ is another lift of the same element, then $\pi_m(g^{-1}g')=\id$, that is $g^{-1}g'\in\bar K_m$, and $\bar K_m\subseteq\ker\rho_L$ by @cor:stabilizer-modulus, so $\rho_L(g)=\rho_L(g')$.
Multiplicativity of $\rho_L$ gives that of $\Phi_m$, and $\Phi_m(\bar h)=\id$ holds exactly when the coset $\pi_m^{-1}(\bar h)\intersect\Stab_G(L)$ meets $\ker\rho_L$, which is the displayed kernel.

Item 3 follows from item 1: $\rho_L(\Stab_G(L))=\rho_L(\pi_m^{-1}(\bar H_m)\intersect G)=\Phi_m(\bar H_m)$, and $\bar H_m$ is generated by the $\bar h_i$, so its image is generated by the $\rho_L(g_i)$.

For item 4, use item 1: an element of $G$ preserving $L$ restricts to an isometry $L\to L$ of lattices, and $\rho_L$ of @def:discriminant-rep is that restriction's discriminant morphism.
:::

The congruence route uses only $\pi_m$ and $\rho_L$ as they are defined: no element of $\Stab_G(L)$ beyond the lifts is formed, and the modulus is the one supplied by @cor:stabilizer-modulus rather than the exponent $e_1$ of $M/L$ alone.
In the vocabulary of the finite commensurability quotient, $M$ is the invariant module, $\bar L_m$ is the orbit point, $\bar H_m$ is its stabilizer in the image of the module action $G\to\Aut_{\bZ}(M/mM)$, and the modulus is the parameter of that construction: $e_1$ is the parameter that makes the stabilizer exact, and a multiple of $\exp(M/e_2L)$ is the parameter that also makes its discriminant image exact.
The module action is needed as a matrix action: by @lem:orbit-permutation-not-module-action the permutation representation on the orbit of $\bar L_m$ has kernel $\kappa_m\supseteq\bar K_m$, which for $L=M$ is all of $G$, and a computation inside that permutation image neither proves $\bar K_m\subseteq\ker\rho_L$ nor defines a map to $\Orth(A_L)$.
The reason $e_1$ alone does not suffice is exactly @lem:congruence-kernel-discriminant: the congruence acts on $L^\sharp$, where the relevant bound involves the quotient $L^\sharp/(L^\sharp\intersect M)$, and not only on $M/L$.

::: {.theorem #thm:discriminant-transport-functor title="The discriminant isometries of the orbit lattices form a functor of the action groupoid"}

Let $\mathcal O=G\cdot L$ be the orbit of $L$ under $G$, regarded as the object set of the translation groupoid $G\ltimes\mathcal O$, whose morphisms $L_0\to L_1$ are the elements $g\in G$ with $gL_0=L_1$.
For $g\in G$ and a full lattice $L_0\subseteq V$:

1. $(gL_0)^\sharp=g\qty(L_0^\sharp)$, and
$$ \alpha_g(L_0)\colon A_{L_0}\isoto A_{gL_0},\qquad [y]\longmapsto[gy] $$
is a well-defined isomorphism of discriminant forms;
2. $\alpha_{\id}(L_0)=\id$ and $\alpha_{g_1g_2}(L_0)=\alpha_{g_1}(g_2L_0)\circ\alpha_{g_2}(L_0)$;
3. if $gL_0=L_0$, then $\alpha_g(L_0)=\rho_{L_0}(g)$;
4. $\alpha_g(L_0)$ is the discriminant morphism of the isometry of lattices $g|_{L_0}\colon L_0\to gL_0$.

Hence $L_0\mapsto A_{L_0}$, $g\mapsto\alpha_g$ is a functor from $G\ltimes\mathcal O$ to the groupoid of discriminant forms with isometries, and the discriminant construction is natural in the lattice variable, not only for self-isometries.
:::

::: {.proof}

For $y\in(gL_0)^\sharp$ one has $\beta(y,gL_0)\subseteq\bZ$, that is $\beta(g^{-1}y,L_0)\subseteq\bZ$, that is $g^{-1}y\in L_0^\sharp$; so $(gL_0)^\sharp=gL_0^\sharp$, and $gy$ depends only on the class of $y$ modulo $L_0$.
Form preservation $\beta(gy,gy')=\beta(y,y')$ gives that $\alpha_g(L_0)$ is an isometry of $A_{L_0}$ onto $A_{gL_0}$ for the bilinear forms, and $(gy)^2=y^2$ for the quadratic refinements of @def:discriminant.
Functoriality is immediate from $g_1(g_2y)=(g_1g_2)y$; for a self-isometry, $\alpha_g(L_0)$ is the map of @def:discriminant-rep; and the description $[y]\mapsto[gy]$ is the dual of the inverse of $g|_{L_0}$, which is the discriminant morphism of that isometry.
:::

::: {.corollary #cor:orbit-uniform-modulus title="One modulus serves the whole orbit, and the images are conjugate"}

For $L_0\in\mathcal O$ write $e_2(L_0)\definedas\exp\qty(L_0^\sharp/(L_0^\sharp\intersect M))$, so that $e_2=e_2(L)$. Let $m\geq1$ with $mM\subseteq e_2(L)L$. Then for every $L_0\in\mathcal O$:

1. $mM\subseteq e_2(L_0)L_0$, so $\bar K_m\subseteq\Stab_G(L_0)\intersect\ker\rho_{L_0}$ and @thm:stabilizer-discriminant-image applies at $L_0$;
2. $\rho_{L_0}(\Stab_G(L_0))=\Phi_{L_0,m}\bigl(\Stab_{\pi_m(G)}(\bar L_{0,m})\bigr)$;
3. for $g\in G$, $\rho_{gL_0}(\Stab_G(gL_0))=\alpha_g(L_0)\rho_{L_0}(\Stab_G(L_0))\alpha_g(L_0)^{-1}$, and $\Stab_G(gL_0)=g\Stab_G(L_0)g^{-1}$.

In particular $e_2(L_0)=e_2(L)$ for every $L_0\in\mathcal O$, and the finite quotient $\pi_m(G)$ computes the discriminant image of the stabilizer at every lattice of the orbit at once.
:::

::: {.proof}

Let $L_0=gL$. Then $L_0^\sharp=gL^\sharp$ and $g\qty(L^\sharp\intersect M)=gL^\sharp\intersect gM=L_0^\sharp\intersect M$, so $g$ induces an isomorphism $L^\sharp/(L^\sharp\intersect M)\cong L_0^\sharp/(L_0^\sharp\intersect M)$ and $e_2(L_0)=e_2(L)$.
Next $mM=gmM\subseteq g\qty(e_2(L)L)=e_2(L)L_0$, which is item 1; item 2 is @thm:stabilizer-discriminant-image at $L_0$.
For item 3, $\Stab_G(gL_0)=g\Stab_G(L_0)g^{-1}$ is immediate, and @thm:discriminant-transport-functor gives $\rho_{gL_0}(gkg^{-1})=\alpha_g(L_0)\rho_{L_0}(k)\alpha_g(L_0)^{-1}$ for $k\in\Stab_G(L_0)$, since $gkg^{-1}$ acts on $A_{gL_0}$ through $\alpha_g(L_0)$.
:::

Two statements delimit what this functor supplies, and both follow from the previous results.

*It descends to the finite quotient.*
If $mM\subseteq e_2L$ and $g,g'\in G$ satisfy $\pi_m(g)=\pi_m(g')$, then $g^{-1}g'\in\bar K_m$; by @cor:orbit-uniform-modulus the hypothesis of @lem:congruence-kernel-discriminant holds at every $L_0\in\mathcal O$, so $g^{-1}g'$ acts trivially on $A_{L_0}$ for every such $L_0$, and $\alpha_g(L_0)=\alpha_{g'}(L_0)$.
The functor therefore factors through the finite groupoid $\pi_m(G)\ltimes\{\bar L_{0,m}\}$, and the retained finite data are the finite set of submodules $\bar L_{0,m}=L_0/mM$ together with the isomorphisms induced by the elements of $\pi_m(G)$.

*It descends to the orbit, and the loops are then reached by Schreier generators.*
The loops at $L_0$ in $G\ltimes\mathcal O$ are the elements of $\Stab_G(L_0)$, an infinite group, so the functor does not by itself exhibit them; what it does give is that the answer is an invariant of the orbit rather than of the chosen lattice, and that a modulus valid at one orbit point is valid at every one of them (@cor:orbit-uniform-modulus).
The loops are recovered exactly, without any kernel computation, by passing to the finite orbit of the residue submodule and using Schreier's lemma on it: this is the content of @lem:schreier-stabilizer and @thm:schreier-discriminant-image, and it is a second complete construction of the same image.

::: {.lemma #lem:schreier-stabilizer title="Schreier generators for the stabilizer of a point"}

Let a group $G$ act on a set $\Sigma$, let $S\subseteq G$ satisfy $G=\generators{S}$ and $S^{-1}=S$, let $\alpha\in\Sigma$, and for each $\beta$ in the orbit $G\alpha$ choose $t_\beta\in G$ with $t_\beta\alpha=\beta$ and $t_\alpha=\id$.
Then
$$ \Stab_G(\alpha)=\generators{t_{s\beta}^{-1}\,s\,t_\beta\st s\in S,\ \beta\in G\alpha}. $$
:::

::: {.proof}

Each $t_{s\beta}^{-1}st_\beta$ carries $\alpha$ to $t_{s\beta}^{-1}(s\beta)=\alpha$, so the right-hand group lies in $\Stab_G(\alpha)$.

Conversely let $g\in\Stab_G(\alpha)$.
Since $S^{-1}=S$ and $G=\generators{S}$, write $g$ as a word in the letters of $S$ and write it in decreasing index order,
$$ g=s_r\,s_{r-1}\cdots s_1,\qquad s_i\in S; $$
put $g_0\definedas\id$, $g_i\definedas s_i g_{i-1}=s_i\,s_{i-1}\cdots s_1$ and $\beta_i\definedas g_i\alpha$, so that each $g_i$ is an initial segment of the word for $g$ read from the right.
Then
$$ s_i\beta_{i-1}=s_i\,g_{i-1}\alpha=g_i\alpha=\beta_i=t_{\beta_i}\alpha, $$
so $k_i\definedas t_{\beta_i}^{-1}s_it_{\beta_{i-1}}$ is one of the displayed elements.
The product taken in decreasing index order telescopes,
$$ k_r\,k_{r-1}\cdots k_1=\qty(t_{\beta_r}^{-1}s_rt_{\beta_{r-1}})\qty(t_{\beta_{r-1}}^{-1}s_{r-1}t_{\beta_{r-2}})\cdots\qty(t_{\beta_1}^{-1}s_1t_{\beta_0})=t_{\beta_r}^{-1}\,s_r s_{r-1}\cdots s_1\,t_{\beta_0}=t_{\beta_r}^{-1}\,g\,t_{\beta_0}, $$
and $\beta_r=g\alpha=\alpha$ because $g\in\Stab_G(\alpha)$, so $t_{\beta_r}=t_\alpha=\id$ and $t_{\beta_0}=t_\alpha=\id$; hence $k_r\cdots k_1=g$.
So $g$ lies in the displayed subgroup.
:::

::: {.lemma #lem:orbit-reduction-injective title="Reduction modulo $mM$ is injective on the orbit"}

Let $m\geq1$ with $mM\subseteq L$, and let $\mathcal O=G\cdot L$.
Then $mM\subseteq L_0$ for every $L_0\in\mathcal O$, and the map
$$ \mathcal O\too\qty{\text{submodules of }M/mM},\qquad L_0\longmapsto\bar L_{0,m}\definedas L_0/mM $$
is injective.
:::

::: {.proof}

For $L_0=gL$ one has $mM=gmM\subseteq gL=L_0$, so $\bar L_{0,m}$ is defined.
If $L_1,L_2\in\mathcal O$ have equal images, then $L_1+mM=L_2+mM$, and $L_i+mM=L_i$ because $mM\subseteq L_i$; hence $L_1=L_2$.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:schreier-discriminant-image title="The Schreier data give the exact image, with no kernel computation"}

Take the data of @def:stabilizer-congruence-data with $m\geq1$ and $mM\subseteq L$, and let $S\subseteq G$ be a finite symmetric generating set: $G=\generators{S}$, $S^{-1}=S$ and $\abs{S}<\infty$.
Let $\Sigma_m$ be the orbit of $\bar L_m$ in the finite module $M/mM$ under $\pi_m(G)$.
For each $p\in\Sigma_m$ choose $t_p\in G$ with $\pi_m(t_p)\bar L_m=p$ and $t_{\bar L_m}=\id$, and put $L_p\definedas t_pL$; by @lem:orbit-reduction-injective the lattice $L_p$ depends only on $p$, so it is a representative of the orbit point, not of the transversal.
For $s\in S$ and $p\in\Sigma_m$, with $sp$ denoting the image of $p$ under $\pi_m(s)$, put
$$ k_{s,p}\definedas t_{sp}^{-1}\,s\,t_p. $$
Then:

1. $\pi_m(k_{s,p})$ fixes $\bar L_m$, so $k_{s,p}\in\Stab_G(L)$ by @lem:congruence-stabilizer-preimage, and $k_{s,p}$ is an integral self-isometry of $L$;
2. $\Stab_G(L)=\generators{k_{s,p}\st s\in S,\ p\in\Sigma_m}$;
3. consequently
$$ \rho_L(\Stab_G(L))=\generators{\rho_L(k_{s,p})},\qquad \rho_L(k_{s,p})=\alpha_{t_{sp}^{-1}}(L_{sp})\circ\alpha_s(L_p)\circ\alpha_{t_p}(L) $$
with $L_{sp}\definedas sL_p$, a composite of discriminant isometries between integral lattices of the orbit.
The lattice $L_{sp}$ is integral because $s\in\Orth(V)$ carries the lattice $L_p$ to a lattice, and it satisfies $L_{sp}=t_{sp}L$: both contain $mM$ and both reduce to the submodule $sp$, so @lem:orbit-reduction-injective applies.
Hence $\alpha_{t_{sp}^{-1}}(L_{sp})=\alpha_{t_{sp}}(L)^{-1}$, and every factor of the composite is a map between integral lattices, indexed by lattices and not by residue submodules;
4. the finite objects used are the finite orbit $\Sigma_m$ of submodules, the finitely many transversal elements $t_p$, the lattices $L_p$, and the maps $\alpha_s(L_p)$ for $s$ in the finite set $S$ and $p\in\Sigma_m$; no congruence kernel is proved trivial, and no stabilizer inside a finite matrix group is computed.
:::

::: {.proof}

For item 1, $\pi_m(k_{s,p})$ carries $\bar L_m$ to $\pi_m(t_{sp})^{-1}\pi_m(s)p=\pi_m(t_{sp})^{-1}(sp)=\bar L_m$; so $k_{s,p}\in\Stab_G(\bar L_m)$, which is $\Stab_G(L)$ by @lem:congruence-stabilizer-preimage at the modulus $m$ with $mM\subseteq L$.

Item 2 is @lem:schreier-stabilizer applied to the action of $G$ on the orbit $\Sigma_m$ of $\bar L_m$: the displayed elements there are exactly the $k_{s,p}$, the orbit of $\bar L_m$ is $\Sigma_m$, and its stabilizer is $\Stab_G(L)$ by item 1.

For item 3, $\rho_L(k_{s,p})=\alpha_{k_{s,p}}(L)$ by @thm:discriminant-transport-functor, since $k_{s,p}$ stabilizes $L$; functoriality of $\alpha$ along $L\xrightarrow{t_p}L_p\xrightarrow{s}L_{sp}\xrightarrow{t_{sp}^{-1}}L$ gives
$$ \alpha_{k_{s,p}}(L)=\alpha_{t_{sp}^{-1}}(L_{sp})\circ\alpha_s(L_p)\circ\alpha_{t_p}(L), $$
and $\alpha_{t_{sp}^{-1}}(L_{sp})=\alpha_{t_{sp}}(L)^{-1}$ because $\alpha_{t_{sp}^{-1}t_{sp}}(L)=\alpha_{\id}(L)=\id$; the equality $L_{sp}=t_{sp}L$ was proved in item 3.
Item 4 records the objects and maps appearing in items 1 to 3; the modulus enters only through the definition of $\bar L_m$ and the finiteness of $\Sigma_m$, so $m=e_1$ is enough here.
:::

The two constructions are both complete, and their index sets differ.
The congruence construction needs the finite module action $\pi_m\colon G\to\Aut_{\bZ}(M/mM)$, the submodule $\bar L_m$, the stabilizer of $\bar L_m$ inside $\pi_m(G)$, and the modulus condition $mM\subseteq e_2L$ of @cor:stabilizer-modulus; it then needs one lift per generator of that finite stabilizer, and it evaluates $\abs{\text{generators of }\bar H_m}$ discriminant images.
The Schreier construction needs the generating set $S$, the finite orbit $\Sigma_m$ with its transversal words, one representative lattice $L_p$ per orbit point, and the per-letter maps $\alpha_s(L_p)$ for $s\in S$ and $p\in\Sigma_m$; that is a letter-by-orbit family of $\abs{S}\cdot\abs{\Sigma_m}$ maps, and by @thm:schreier-discriminant-image it returns the exact image with $m=e_1$ alone and with no triviality statement.
Neither index set is a function of the other, so neither construction subsumes the other: the congruence one is smaller exactly when the finite stabilizer has few generators compared with $\abs{S}\cdot\abs{\Sigma_m}$, and it pays for that with the refined modulus and the kernel argument, while the Schreier one needs neither and carries the larger family.
What both need is $\bar L_m$ and the finiteness of $M/mM$; @cor:orbit-uniform-modulus is what lets either be run at one lattice and read at the others.

::: {.proposition #prop:spinor-congruence-obstruction title="The real spinor character does not descend to the congruence quotient in general"}

Let $m\geq1$ with $mM\subseteq e_2L$ and let $\spinornorm_\bR\colon\Orth(V_\bR)\to\bR^\times/(\bR^\times)^2=\theset{\pm1}$ be the normalized real spinor norm of @def:spinor-norm, restricted to $G\leq\Orth(V)$.

1. The composite $\Stab_G(L)\to\theset{\pm1}$, $g\mapsto\spinornorm_\bR(g)$, factors through $\pi_m$ if and only if $\spinornorm_\bR$ is trivial on $\Stab_G(L)\intersect\bar K_m$.
2. The condition of item 1 fails in general, and it fails for the Enriques lattice at modulus $2$: with $N=T_\En$ of @sec-lattices, $L=M=N$ and $m=2$, the isometry
$$ \sigma_U=-\id_U\oplus\id_{U(2)}\oplus\id_{E_8(2)}\in\Orth(N) $$
satisfies $\sigma_U\equiv\id\pmod{2N}$ and $\spinornorm_\bR(\sigma_U)=-1$.
Consequently two lifts of one element of $\bar H_2$ can carry opposite spinor signs, and no function of the congruence class alone returns the spinor norm of an element of $\Stab_G(L)$.
3. For the Enriques family the sign is not needed for the primitive isotropic line, plane and flag counts, and that is a consequence of a theorem rather than of a computation at a finite quotient: by @thm:enriques-spinor-orbit-agreement the groups $\Gamma$ and $\Gamma^+=\Gamma\intersect\Orth^+(N)$ have the same orbits on those three families for every $\Gamma\supseteq\ker\rho$, and the proof exhibits for each object of each family a spinor-negative element of $\ker\rho\subseteq\Gamma$ in its stabilizer.
Those witnesses are elements of $\ker\rho$, so they are invisible to $\rho_N$, and they supply the orbit identity without any spinor character being evaluated.
They do not make $\spinornorm_\bR$ a function of the congruence class: items 1 and 2 stand unchanged, so the character remains a separate obligation for any use beyond these three families, and neither the witnesses nor @thm:stabilizer-discriminant-image give a factorization of it through $\pi_m$.
:::

::: {.proof}

Item 1 is the factorization criterion: $\spinornorm_\bR$ restricted to $\Stab_G(L)$ induces a map on the cosets of $\Stab_G(L)\intersect\bar K_m$ exactly when it is constant on each such coset, that is trivial on that kernel.

For item 2, $\sigma_U$ negates $U$ and fixes the other two summands, so $\sigma_U-\id$ is $-2$ on $U$ and zero elsewhere, and $(\sigma_U-\id)(N)\subseteq 2N$ because $2U\subseteq 2N$; that is $\sigma_U\in\bar K_2$.
Its spinor norm is $\spinornorm_\bR(-\id_U)=[(-1)^1]=-1$ by @lem:minus-identity-spinor-norm, the summand $U$ having positive index one, and the spinor norm of an orthogonal sum is the product of those of the summands.
The choice $L=M=N$, $m=2$ satisfies $mM\subseteq e_2L$ because $e_2=\exp(N^\sharp/N)=2$ and $2N\subseteq 2N$.
At that modulus $\bar K_2$ meets $\Stab_G(L)$ whenever $\sigma_U\in G$, and $\sigma_U\in\Orth(N)$; so with $G=\Orth(N)$ the sign is not a function of $\pi_2(g)$, while $\rho_N$ is one by @cor:stabilizer-modulus.

Item 3 records the consequence of the Enriques subgroup theorem: the three families are compared object by object through witnesses in $\ker\rho$, and a witness in $\ker\rho$ is invisible to $\rho_L$, so the discriminant computation of @thm:stabilizer-discriminant-image and the orbit identity of @thm:enriques-spinor-orbit-agreement are consistent and neither requires the other's input.
:::

## Supporting lift theorems {#sec-descent-eichler}

::: {.proposition #prop:descent-eichler title="Eichler filling, with its hypotheses stated"}
Suppose $L$ contains $U\perp U_1\perp L_0$ as an orthogonal summand, with $U_1$ a second hyperbolic plane. For primitive isotropic $v,v'$ with fixed full $\Orth(L)$-orbit and $v^*=v'^*$, @thm:eichler-criterion supplies $g\in\widetilde{\SO}^+(L)$ with $g(v)=v'$, where the hypotheses on squares, divisibilities and congruence class are checked, not inferred from the class alone. Then the orbit-fibre has index one. Any $\Gamma$ containing $\widetilde{\SO}^+(L)$ inherits the corresponding fibre statement, provided the chosen lift belongs to $\Gamma$.
:::

Whether this proposition applies is a property of the integral embedding and its congruence class. It does not apply to a definite quotient such as the Coble plane quotient $I_{0,7}$, and it does not replace the integral plane-stabilizer computation.

## Proved instances {#sec-descent-coble}

**Enriques degree two.** The full integral stabilizer images for the primitive isotropic lines and for the two $O(T_\En)$-plane classes are proved in @lem:line-stabilizer-image and @thm:enriques-integral-plane-stabilizers. Since $A_{T_\En}$ has exponent two, the point stabilizer of a vector class agrees with the subgroup stabilizer of the corresponding line. The plane datum $D(P)$ is an isotropic subgroup: order two in the divisibility-one class and a noncyclic order-four subgroup in the divisibility-two class. In the latter, the actual integral reflection lifts, their dual action through the proved gluing $Q^*/Q\simeq D^\perp/D$, and the generated full kernel establish the equality. An order-$40320$ action on $Q^*/Q$ alone would not have been a substitute. For the source-defined full-$O$ group $\Gamma_{\En,2}=\rho^{-1}(M)$, which contains $\ker\rho$, the finite formula returns one double coset per $M$-orbit. The line type-set produces five arithmetic 0-cusps and the plane type-set nine arithmetic 1-cusps. The positive-spinor statement is used only where the level-two negative witness has been transported within the relevant $\Gamma_{\En,2}$-orbit; the two full-$O$ plane representatives carry such witnesses directly in @thm:enriques-integral-plane-stabilizers.

**Coble vectors.** For $\Gamma_\Co=\ker\rho\cap\Orth^+(B)$, the full integral line-stabilizer image with its Eichler transvections gives a single $\ker\rho$-orbit over each attained nonzero line datum. Here $A_{T_\Co}$ has exponent two, so the vector class and the line subgroup determine the same stabilizer. To check the spinor hypothesis at every relevant orbit, let $g=\id_{U_1}\oplus(-\id_{K_v})$ be the negative witness at $e_1$. For any chosen primitive representative $v_c$ of an attained line class, choose $h\in\Orth(B)$ with $h(e_1)=v_c$ (the full transitivity of @thm:unpolarized-cusps); then $hgh^{-1}$ is still level-two by normality of $\ker\rho$, still spinor-negative, and stabilizes the line $\ZZ v_c$. Thus the witness reaches every relevant line orbit, and the $\ker\rho$-orbits do not split under $\Gamma_\Co$. The numerical count remains a **page proof with the public Coble computation sage-extensions#286**, not a new computed invariant.

**Coble planes.** The integral parabolic image is the proper subgroup $S$ of the full finite stabilizer $F$ described by @thm:coble-plane-stabilizer-image: the definite quotient $I_{0,7}$ induces only $S_7$ in $\Orth^+(6,2)$. Hence $|F/S|=8$. The full finite stabilizer cannot be substituted for $S$, and this is a proved counterexample to any claim that discriminant-form lifting is automatic. Let $\tau=-\id_{U_1}\oplus\id$ be the negative witness at the standard plane $P$. For any plane $Q$, choose $h\in\Orth(B)$ with $h(P)=Q$ (again @thm:unpolarized-cusps); then $h\tau h^{-1}$ is again level-two and spinor-negative, so the witness reaches every plane and in particular a representative of every $\ker\rho$-orbit in every fibre. Therefore the eight $\ker\rho$-orbits do not split under $\Gamma_\Co$. The $189\,720$ total is likewise a **page proof with sage-extensions#286**, while the liftability and finite discriminant counts have their own computation records.

**Coble plane-line flags.** The integral plane Levi gives $GL(P)$. Level two and the real spinor character select $\{A\in SL(P):A\equiv I\pmod2\}$. If primitive vectors $v,w$ have the same parity and are completed to $M_v=[v,z_v],M_w=[w,z_w]$, then $\det(c,z_w-z_v)=0$ in $\F_2$ for their common nonzero first column $c$, hence $z_w-z_v=tc$, because the kernel of $x\mapsto\det(c,x)$ is $\langle c\rangle$. Replacing $z_w$ by $z_w-tw$ aligns the completions. The adjusted $M_wM_v^{-1}$ is level two and sends $v$ to $w$. Thus the plane stabilizer is transitive within each parity class but cannot permute the three classes. Each arithmetic plane orbit and each of the three line classes has exactly one arithmetic flag orbit. There are $24$ flag orbits over one $J$; there is no extra hidden flag splitting. This local flag statement is also a page proof with sage-extensions#286, not a finite containment table repackaged as an orbit computation. Its proof is @prop:coble-arithmetic-flag-incidence.

The discriminant approach remains conditional on the actual integral stabilizer at every stage. The public Coble computation remains sage-extensions#286; those calls and any known-family cards used by other pages retain their own dependencies.
