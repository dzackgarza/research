# K3 Automorphisms {#sec-k3-automorphisms}

## Gluing and the automorphism group

**The automorphism sequence comes from the gluing of $S=\operatorname{NS}(X)$ and $T=T(X)$ inside the unimodular lattice $H^2(X,\mathbf Z)$.** Miranda–Morrison computes the obstruction to extending an isometry across that gluing.

Assume $X$ is a complex projective K3 surface. Let
$$
\gamma:A_T\xrightarrow{\sim}A_S
$$
be the gluing anti-isometry, where $A_L=L^\vee/L$ carries its discriminant quadratic form, and put
$$
G_X=O_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T),\qquad
P_S=\operatorname{Stab}_{O(S)}(\operatorname{Amp}(X)).
$$
Use pushforward for the cohomological representation. Write $\rho_L:O(L)\to O(A_L)$ for the discriminant action, and $\bar u=\rho_L(u)$.

A pair $(s,t)\in O(S)\times O(T)$ extends to $H^2(X,\mathbf Z)$ precisely when its discriminant actions agree:
$$
\bar s=\gamma\bar t\gamma^{-1}.
$$
Torelli says that this extension comes from an automorphism precisely when $s\in P_S$ and $t\in G_X$. Thus the general construction is the **fibre product**
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Aut}(X)\cong P_S\times_{O(A_S)}G_X.}
$$
Here the map from $G_X$ sends $t$ to $\gamma\bar t\gamma^{-1}$. This is the construction used in equation (3.15) of Meinsma–Shinder [@MS24, equation (3.15)].

Projecting this fibre product onto $G_X$ identifies the kernel:
$$
\boxed{A_1=\ker\!\left(P_S\longrightarrow O(A_S)\right).}
$$
An element of $A_1$ extends by the identity on $T$. Geometrically, this is the symplectic automorphism group. In lattice terms,
$$
A_1\cong\widetilde O^{+}(S)/W(S),
$$
where $O^{+}(S)$ preserves the positive-cone component containing the ample cone, $\widetilde O^{+}(S)$ acts trivially on $A_S$, and $W(S)$ is generated by the $(-2)$-reflections [@PS24, Theorem 20.1.1]. We use $O^{+}(S)$ for positive-cone preservation and $P_S$ for ample-cone preservation; Meinsma–Shinder use $O^{+}(S)$ for the latter group in Proposition 3.10.

## The signed Miranda–Morrison obstruction

**The connection to Miranda–Morrison is obtained by computing the image of $P_S$.** The Weyl group moves chambers and acts trivially on the discriminant group. Therefore
$$
\rho_S(P_S)=\rho_S(O^{+}(S)).
$$
So preserving the particular ample chamber introduces no further discriminant obstruction after preserving the positive-cone component [@PS24, §19.1 and Theorem 20.1.1].

For $\rho(X)\ge3$, signed Miranda–Morrison theory supplies
$$
O^{+}(S)\xrightarrow{\rho_S}O(A_S)
\xrightarrow{e_S^{+}}\operatorname{MM}^{+}(S)
\longrightarrow g(S)\longrightarrow1,
$$
where $g(S)$ is the genus group. The group denoted here by $\operatorname{MM}^{+}(S)$ is $\mathrm E_+(S)$ in Akyol–Degtyarev [@AD15, §3.7, equation (3.18)].

Consequently, define
$$
\operatorname{ob}_X(t)
=e_S^{+}\!\left(\gamma\bar t\gamma^{-1}\right),
\qquad t\in G_X.
$$
Its vanishing means that $\bar t$ lifts, after transport by $\gamma$, to a positive-cone-preserving isometry of $S$. A Weyl-group correction makes that lift ample-cone-preserving; Torelli then produces an automorphism. Hence
$$
\boxed{
1\longrightarrow A_1
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(X)
\longrightarrow G_X
\xrightarrow{\operatorname{ob}_X}A_2
\longrightarrow1,
\qquad
A_2=\operatorname{im}(\operatorname{ob}_X).
}
$$
In particular, the signed Miranda–Morrison sequence identifies
$$
C_S^{+}:=O(A_S)/\rho_S(O^{+}(S))
\cong\ker\!\left(\operatorname{MM}^{+}(S)\to g(S)\right),
\qquad A_2\le C_S^{+}.
$$
This is the precise relationship: **the second term is the part of the signed discriminant-lifting obstruction reached by the Hodge isometries of $T$.**

With the full $O(T)$ in the middle, the corresponding group on its left is
$$
P_S\times_{O(A_S)}O(T),
$$
the integral gluing stabilizer with the ample condition. Restricting along $G_X\hookrightarrow O(T)$ gives $\operatorname{Aut}(X)$. Thus the Hodge restriction is essential to the sequence involving actual automorphisms.

For Picard rank two, the fibre-product construction still applies. For an elliptic K3 surface of Picard rank two, Proposition 3.10 additionally proves $A_1=1$; the rank-at-least-three Miranda–Morrison computation must be replaced by the explicit binary-lattice discriminant image [@MS24, Proposition 3.10].

## Computing the pullback

The pullback formula applies to every complex projective K3 surface, with its actual period and ample cone. Its computation requires more than the abstract isometry types of $S$ and $T$.

| Construction | Hypotheses |
| --- | --- |
| $\operatorname{Aut}(X)=P_S\times_{O(A_S)}G_X$ | $X$ is a complex projective K3 surface; $S$ and $T$ are its primitive orthogonal sublattices in $H^2(X,\mathbf Z)$; $\gamma$ is their gluing anti-isometry; $G_X$ preserves its Hodge structure; $P_S$ preserves its ample cone |
| Signed Miranda–Morrison obstruction $e_S^+$ | In addition, $\rho(X)\ge3$, so $S$ is even, indefinite and of rank at least three |
| Injectivity $\operatorname{Aut}(X)\hookrightarrow G_X$ from Proposition 3.10 | $X$ is elliptic and $\rho(X)=2$ |

The first row follows from integral gluing and Torelli [@MS24, Theorem 2.3 and equation (3.15)]. The second is the domain of the signed sequence [@AD15, §3.7]. The last is the additional conclusion of [@MS24, Proposition 3.10].

Suppose exact generators of $P_S$ and $G_X$, their lattice actions, and $\gamma$ are available. Put
$$
Q=O(A_S),\qquad
a=\rho_S|_{P_S}:P_S\to Q,\qquad
b:G_X\to Q,\quad b(t)=\gamma\bar t\gamma^{-1}.
$$
The following steps construct generators of the pullback.

1. Compute the finite subgroup $H=a(P_S)\le Q$ from the images of the generators of $P_S$. Retain words in those generators that lift generators of $H$.
2. Compute $K=b^{-1}(H)\le G_X$. The group $G_X$ is finite cyclic for a complex projective K3 surface [@MS24, §2.2]. Thus this step is a finite group computation.
3. Compute $N=\ker(a)\le P_S$. Its index is $|H|$. A transversal obtained from the finite quotient $P_S\to H$ gives Schreier generators for $N$.
4. For each generator $t_i$ of $K$, express $b(t_i)$ as a word in the generators of $H$ and lift that word to $s_i\in P_S$.
5. Generate a subgroup of $P_S\times G_X$ by the pairs $(n,1)$ for generators $n$ of $N$, together with the pairs $(s_i,t_i)$. Extend each pair across the gluing to obtain its integral action on $H^2(X,\mathbf Z)$. Torelli identifies these actions with automorphisms of $X$.

These generators give the whole pullback: any compatible pair $(s,t)$ has $t\in K$. A word in the chosen lifts has the same second component $t$; dividing by that word leaves a pair in $N\times\{1\}$. This also constructs the exact sequence
$$
1\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(X)
\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow1.
$$

More explicitly, if $K=\langle t\rangle$ has order $m>1$ and $s\in P_S$ lifts $b(t)$, put $u=(s,t)$. The extension is determined by
$$
u(n,1)u^{-1}=(sns^{-1},1),\qquad
u^m=(s^m,1),\qquad s^m\in N.
$$
A presentation of $N$, together with words for this conjugation action and for $s^m$, therefore gives a presentation of $\operatorname{Aut}(X)$. If $K=1$, the automorphism group is $N$.

Obtaining the exact period stabilizer $G_X$ and the ample-cone stabilizer $P_S$ is a separate mathematical computation. The procedure above is effective once those generators and actions are supplied; a Gram matrix or approximate period alone does not supply them. Signed Miranda–Morrison can determine $K=\ker(\operatorname{ob}_X)$ in its range, but constructing automorphisms still requires the lifts and the kernel generators.

## Markings and the two restrictions

**The full cohomological action restricts to both $O(S)$ and $O(T)$. The pullback records the compatibility between those restrictions.** Neither restriction must be faithful by itself.

Choose a marking
$$
\eta:H^2(X,\mathbf Z)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Lambda_{\mathrm{K3}}.
$$
The marked surface determines a period line $\ell=\eta_{\mathbf C}(H^{2,0}(X))$ and an ample cone $\mathcal A=\eta_{\mathbf R}(\operatorname{Amp}(X))$. Torelli identifies
$$
\operatorname{Aut}(X)\cong
\{g\in O(\Lambda_{\mathrm{K3}}):g_{\mathbf C}(\ell)=\ell,\ g(\mathcal A)=\mathcal A\}.
$$
Thus this is a subgroup of the orthogonal group of the **whole marked lattice**, determined by the additional period and cone data [@MS24, Theorem 2.3].

Preserving the Hodge structure preserves
$$
S=H^{1,1}(X)\cap H^2(X,\mathbf Z),
$$
and therefore also $T=S^\perp$. Consequently there are restriction homomorphisms
$$
\operatorname{Aut}(X)\longrightarrow O(S),
\qquad
\operatorname{Aut}(X)\longrightarrow G_X\subset O(T).
$$
Their **joint** map is injective: $S\oplus T$ has finite index in $H^2(X,\mathbf Z)$, so the two restrictions determine the whole isometry.

But arbitrary restrictions do not combine into an integral isometry. If $\gamma:A_T\to A_S$ is the gluing anti-isometry, the pair $(s,t)$ extends exactly when
$$
\bar s=\gamma\bar t\gamma^{-1}.
$$
Hence, writing $O_{\mathrm{amp}}(S)=P_S$,
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Aut}(X)\cong O_{\mathrm{amp}}(S)\times_{O(A_S)}G_X.}
$$
This is precisely the pullback description [@MS24, equation (3.15)].

The two projections have different kernels:
$$
\ker(\operatorname{Aut}(X)\to G_X)
\cong\ker(O_{\mathrm{amp}}(S)\to O(A_S)),
$$
$$
\ker(\operatorname{Aut}(X)\to O_{\mathrm{amp}}(S))
\cong\ker(G_X\to O(A_T)).
$$

So the full-cohomology description and the pullback description are the same construction in different coordinates. **Miranda–Morrison enters when computing the image of one discriminant-action map, hence the obstruction to lifting through the opposite projection.** It computes that lifting obstruction; it does not replace the pair of compatible restrictions by either restriction alone.
