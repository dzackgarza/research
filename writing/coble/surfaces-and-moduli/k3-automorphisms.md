# K3 Automorphisms {#sec-k3-automorphisms}

## Gluing and the automorphism group

::: {.notation #not:k3-automorphism-data title="Lattice data of a K3 surface"}

Let $X$ be a $K3$ surface (@def:k3-surface).
Put $L\definedas H^2(X;\ZZ)$ with its cup-product pairing; it is even and unimodular by @prop:k3-lattice.
Let $S\definedas\NS(X)$ be the Néron–Severi lattice, of rank $\rho=\rho(X)$, and let $T\definedas S^{\perp L}$ be the transcendental lattice.
Let $C_X\iscontainedin H^{1,1}(X)\intersect H^2(X;\RR)$ be the positive cone and $\Amp(X)\iscontainedin S\tensor\RR$ the ample cone of [@PS24, §19.1] (@def:ample-cone).
Put $\mathcal C_S\definedas C_X\intersect(S\tensor\RR)$.

An isometry $g$ of $L$ or of $T$ is a **Hodge isometry** if $g_\CC(H^{2,0}(X))=H^{2,0}(X)$.
Put
$$
G_X\definedas\theset{t\in\Orth(T)\st t\text{ is a Hodge isometry}},
\qquad
P_S\definedas\Stab_{\Orth(S)}\qty(\Amp(X)),
\qquad
\Orth^+(S)\definedas\Stab_{\Orth(S)}\qty(\mathcal C_S).
$$
Let $W(S)\iscontainedin\Orth(S)$ be the subgroup generated by the reflections $s_r$ in the roots $r\in S$ with $r^2=-2$ (@prop:integrality-root-reflections), and put $\widetilde\Orth^+(S)\definedas\widetilde\Orth(S)\intersect\Orth^+(S)$, with $\widetilde\Orth(S)=\ker\rho_S$ as in @def:discriminant-rep.

An automorphism $f$ of $X$ acts on $L$ by pushforward $f_*$.
For an isometry $u$ of $S$ or $T$ write $\bar u_q$ for its image under the quadratic discriminant representation of @def:discriminant-rep.
Let $\gamma_q\colon A_{T,q}\isoto A_{S,q}(-1)$ be the gluing isometry of @cor:lifting-unimodular for the pair $(M,N)=(T,S)$.
Put $Q\definedas\Orth(A_{S,q})$ and
$$
a\colon P_S\too Q,\quad a(s)=\bar s_q,
\qquad
b\colon\Orth(T)\too Q,\quad b(t)=\gamma_q\,\bar t_q\,\gamma_q^{-1}.
$$
[@MS24, Proposition 3.10] writes $\Orth^+(\NS(X))$ for $P_S$.
:::

::: {.proposition #prop:k3-ns-transcendental title="The Néron–Severi and transcendental lattices"}

In @not:k3-automorphism-data:

1. $S=H^{1,1}(X)\intersect L$, and $S$ and $T$ are primitive sublattices of $L$ with $T=S^{\perp L}$ and $S=T^{\perp L}$.
2. $S$ is even and nondegenerate of signature $(1,\rho-1)$.
3. $H^{2,0}(X)\iscontainedin T\tensor\CC$.
4. An isometry of $L$ is a Hodge isometry if and only if its complex extension preserves each of $H^{2,0}(X)$, $H^{1,1}(X)$ and $H^{0,2}(X)$.
:::

::: {.proof}

(1) $S$ is the group of classes of type $(1,1)$ [@PS24, Proposition B.2.5].
If $nx\in S$ with $x\in L$ and $n\neq0$, then $x$ has type $(1,1)$, so $x\in S$; hence $S$ is primitive.
An orthogonal complement is primitive.
Since $X$ is projective, $S$ and $T$ are orthogonal complements of each other in $L$ [@PS24, Lemma B.2.7].

(2) $S$ is even because $L$ is even.
It is nondegenerate [@PS24, Lemma B.2.7], so $S\perp S^{\perp L}=S\perp T$ has finite index in $L$.
The lattice $T$ has signature $(2,20-\rho)$ [@PS24, proof of Theorem 20.1.1], and $L$ has signature $(3,19)$, so $S$ has signature $(1,\rho-1)$.

(3) Classes of type $(2,0)$ and of type $(1,1)$ are orthogonal, because a $(3,1)$-form on a surface is zero.
So $H^{2,0}(X)$ is orthogonal to $S\tensor\CC$, that is, $H^{2,0}(X)\iscontainedin(S^{\perp L})\tensor\CC=T\tensor\CC$.

(4) Let $\sigma$ span $H^{2,0}(X)$.
Then $\sigma^2=0$ and $\sigma\cdot\bar\sigma>0$ [@MS24, §2.2], so the pairing is nondegenerate on $H^{2,0}(X)\oplus H^{0,2}(X)$.
By type, $H^{1,1}(X)$ is orthogonal to that sum, and the dimensions are $2$ and $20$ [@PS24, Proposition 19.1.1].
Hence $H^{1,1}(X)=(H^{2,0}(X)\oplus H^{0,2}(X))^\perp$.
An isometry $g$ of $L$ commutes with complex conjugation on $L\tensor\CC$.
So if $g_\CC$ preserves $H^{2,0}(X)$, it preserves $H^{0,2}(X)=\overline{H^{2,0}(X)}$ and the orthogonal complement $H^{1,1}(X)$.
:::

::: {.lemma #lem:k3-positive-cone-component title="The positive cone of the Néron–Severi lattice"}

In @not:k3-automorphism-data, $\mathcal C_S$ is the connected component of $\theset{x\in S\tensor\RR\st x^2>0}$ that contains $\Amp(X)$, and the other component is $-\mathcal C_S$.
The group $W(S)$ preserves $\mathcal C_S$, and $P_S\iscontainedin\Orth^+(S)$.
If $\rho\geq2$, then $\Orth^+(S)$ is the group $\Orth^+(S)$ of @prop:spinor-plus-cone-components.
:::

::: {.proof}

Put $P\definedas\theset{x\in S\tensor\RR\st x^2>0}$, and let $P_0$ be the component of $P$ that contains the connected set $\Amp(X)$ (@def:ample-cone).
The set $P$ has exactly the two components $P_0$ and $-P_0$.
For $\rho=1$ this holds because $S\tensor\RR$ is a line.
For $\rho\geq2$, $S$ has signature $(1,\rho-1)$ by @prop:k3-ns-transcendental, so $P$ has two components by @prop:spinor-plus-cone-components, each preserved by $\Orth^+(S)$; and $-\id_S\notin\Orth^+(S)$ by @lem:minus-identity-spinor-norm because the positive index of $S$ is $1$, so $-\id_S$ exchanges them.

The positive cone $C_X$ is a connected component of the open set of classes of positive square in $H^{1,1}(X)\intersect H^2(X;\RR)$ [@PS24, §19.1].
So $\mathcal C_S=C_X\intersect P$ is open and closed in $P$, and it is a union of components of $P$.
It contains $\Amp(X)$ [@PS24, §19.1], so $P_0\iscontainedin\mathcal C_S$.

A reflection $s_r$ with $r^2<0$ preserves each component of $P$ (proof of @prop:spinor-plus-cone-components), so $W(S)$ preserves $P_0$ and $-P_0$.
Suppose $-P_0\iscontainedin\mathcal C_S$, and let $x\in-P_0$.
The closure of $\Amp(X)$ is a fundamental domain for $W(S)$ on $\mathcal C_S$ [@PS24, Proposition 19.1.3(2)], so $wx$ lies in the closure of $\Amp(X)$ for some $w\in W(S)$.
The closure of $\Amp(X)$ meets $P$ only in $P_0$, because $P_0$ is closed in $P$.
But $wx\in-P_0$, which is disjoint from $P_0$.
This contradiction gives $\mathcal C_S=P_0$.

An element of $P_S$ maps the component $P_0\supseteq\Amp(X)$ to the component containing $\Amp(X)$, so $P_S\iscontainedin\Orth^+(S)$.
An isometry preserves $P_0$ if and only if it preserves both components of $P$, which gives the last claim.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:k3-aut-fibre-product title="The automorphism group is a fibre product"}

In @not:k3-automorphism-data, the assignment $f\mapsto(f_*|_S,f_*|_T)$ is an isomorphism of groups
$$
\Aut(X)\isoto P_S\times_Q G_X\definedas\theset{(s,t)\in P_S\times G_X\st a(s)=b(t)}.
$$
Equivalently, the following square of groups is a pullback:
```tikzcd
\Aut(X) \arrow[r,"f\mapsto f_*|_T"] \arrow[d,"f\mapsto f_*|_S"'] \arrow[dr, phantom, "\lrcorner", very near start] & G_X \arrow[d,"b|_{G_X}"] \\
P_S \arrow[r,"a"'] & Q
```
For an elliptic $K3$ surface of Picard rank $2$ this is [@MS24, equation (3.15)].
:::

::: {.proof}

By the Torelli theorem [@MS24, Theorem 2.3] with $Y=X$, every Hodge isometry $g$ of $L$ with $g(\Amp(X))=\Amp(X)$ equals $f_*$ for a unique $f\in\Aut(X)$.
Each $f_*$ is a Hodge isometry that preserves $\Amp(X)$, because $f$ maps ample divisors to ample divisors.
Since $(fg)_*=f_*g_*$, the map $f\mapsto f_*$ is an isomorphism from $\Aut(X)$ onto the group of Hodge isometries of $L$ that preserve $\Amp(X)$.

Let $g$ be such an isometry.
By @prop:k3-ns-transcendental (4) and (1), $g$ preserves $S=H^{1,1}(X)\intersect L$ and hence $T=S^{\perp L}$.
Put $s\definedas g|_S$ and $t\definedas g|_T$.
Then $s\in P_S$ because $\Amp(X)\iscontainedin S\tensor\RR$, and $t\in G_X$ because $H^{2,0}(X)\iscontainedin T\tensor\CC$ by @prop:k3-ns-transcendental (3).
By @cor:lifting-unimodular, $\bar s\circ\gamma=\gamma\circ\bar t$ in the bilinear discriminant groups.
The forgetful map $\Orth(A_{S,q})\to\Orth(A_S)$ is a monomorphism and $\gamma_q$ has the same underlying map as $\gamma$ (@def:discriminant-rep, @cor:lifting-unimodular).
So the condition is equivalent to $a(s)=b(t)$, and $(s,t)$ lies in the fibre product.
The pair determines $g$ by the uniqueness clause of @thm:automorphism-lifting-criterion.

Conversely, let $(s,t)\in P_S\times G_X$ with $a(s)=b(t)$.
By @cor:lifting-unimodular, $s\perp t$ extends to an isometry $g$ of $L$.
Then $g_\CC$ preserves $H^{2,0}(X)\iscontainedin T\tensor\CC$ because $t$ does, and $g$ preserves $\Amp(X)$ because $s$ does.
So $g=f_*$ for a unique $f\in\Aut(X)$, with $(f_*|_S,f_*|_T)=(s,t)$.
:::

::: {.corollary #cor:k3-aut-projection-kernels title="The kernels of the two restrictions"}

In @not:k3-automorphism-data, put $A_1\definedas\ker a=P_S\intersect\widetilde\Orth(S)$.

1. The restriction $\Aut(X)\to G_X$ has kernel isomorphic to $A_1$; it consists of the automorphisms $f$ with $f_*|_T=\id_T$, and $s\in A_1$ corresponds to the extension of $s\perp\id_T$.
2. The restriction $\Aut(X)\to P_S$ has kernel isomorphic to $\ker\qty(G_X\to\Orth(A_T))$; $t$ in that kernel corresponds to the extension of $\id_S\perp t$.
:::

::: {.proof}

In the fibre product of @thm:k3-aut-fibre-product, the pairs with second component $1$ are the pairs $(s,1)$ with $a(s)=b(1)=1$, that is, $s\in A_1$.
The pairs with first component $1$ are the pairs $(1,t)$ with $b(t)=1$.
Since $\gamma_q$ is an isomorphism and $\Orth(A_{T,q})\to\Orth(A_T)$ is a monomorphism, $b(t)=1$ if and only if $\bar t=1$ in $\Orth(A_T)$.
:::

::: {.proposition #prop:k3-symplectic-aut title="The symplectic automorphism group"}

In @not:k3-automorphism-data, $A_1$ is isomorphic to the group of symplectic automorphisms of $X$, and
$$
A_1\cong\widetilde\Orth^+(S)/W(S)
$$
[@PS24, Theorem 20.1.1].
The group $\widetilde\Orth^+(S)$ is $\Orth^{-,\#}(S)$ and $W(S)$ is $\mathsf W^-(S)$ in the notation of [@PS24, §19.1 and §20.1].
:::

::: {.proof}

[@PS24, Theorem 20.1.1] identifies the group of symplectic automorphisms with the group of extensions of $s\perp\id_T$ for $s\in\Orth^{-,\#}(S)$ that preserve the ample cone, and identifies this group with $\Orth^{-,\#}(S)/\mathsf W^-(S)$.
An element of $P_S$ preserves $\mathcal C_S\supseteq\Amp(X)$ (@lem:k3-positive-cone-component), so these $s$ are exactly the elements of $A_1$.
By @cor:k3-aut-projection-kernels (1), the extensions of $s\perp\id_T$ for $s\in A_1$ form the kernel of $\Aut(X)\to G_X$.
:::

## The signed Miranda–Morrison obstruction

::: {.lemma #lem:k3-ample-discriminant-image title="Preserving the ample cone adds no discriminant condition"}

In @not:k3-automorphism-data, $W(S)\iscontainedin\widetilde\Orth^+(S)$, and
$$
a(P_S)=\rho_{S,q}\qty(\Orth^+(S)).
$$
:::

::: {.proof}

Let $r\in S$ with $r^2=-2$ and let $x\in S^\#$.
By @prop:integrality-root-reflections, extended linearly to $S\tensor\QQ$, $s_r(x)=x+(x\cdot r)r$, and $x\cdot r\in\ZZ$.
So $s_r(x)-x\in S$, and $s_r$ acts trivially on $A_{S,q}$.
With @lem:k3-positive-cone-component this gives $W(S)\iscontainedin\widetilde\Orth^+(S)$.

Since $P_S\iscontainedin\Orth^+(S)$ by @lem:k3-positive-cone-component, $a(P_S)\iscontainedin\rho_{S,q}(\Orth^+(S))$.
Conversely, let $g\in\Orth^+(S)$, and let $U\iscontainedin \mathcal C_S$ be the complement of the mirrors $r^\perp$ of the roots $r\in S$ with $r^2=-2$.
The ample cone is a connected component of $U$ (@def:ample-cone).
The isometry $g$ permutes these roots and preserves $\mathcal C_S$, so it maps $U$ onto $U$ and $\Amp(X)$ onto a component of $U$.
Let $x\in\Amp(X)$.
By [@PS24, Proposition 19.1.3(2)] there is $w\in W(S)$ with $wgx$ in the closure of $\Amp(X)$.
The group $W(S)$ permutes the mirrors, so $wgx\in U$.
A connected component of $U$ is closed in $U$, so $wgx\in\Amp(X)$.
Then $wg(\Amp(X))$ is a component of $U$ that meets $\Amp(X)$, so $wg(\Amp(X))=\Amp(X)$ and $wg\in P_S$.
Hence $\rho_{S,q}(g)=\rho_{S,q}(wg)=a(wg)$.
:::

::: {.lemma #lem:k3-sign-structure title="Positive sign structures on the Néron–Severi lattice"}

In @not:k3-automorphism-data, suppose $\rho\geq2$.
Then the subgroup of $\Orth(S)$ that preserves positive sign structures [@AD15, §3.7] is $\Orth^+(S)$.
:::

::: {.proof}

A positive sign structure on $S$ is an orientation of a maximal positive-definite subspace of $S\tensor\RR$, and $\det_+\colon\Orth(S)\to\theset{\pm1}$ records whether an isometry preserves or reverses it [@AD15, §3.7].
The same definition gives a homomorphism $\det_+\colon\Orth(S\tensor\RR)\to\theset{\pm1}$.
Let $w\in S\tensor\RR$ with $w^2\neq0$.
If $w^2<0$, then $w^\perp$ has signature $(1,\rho-2)$, so it contains a maximal positive-definite subspace, which $s_w$ fixes; hence $\det_+(s_w)=1$.
If $w^2>0$, then $\RR w$ is a maximal positive-definite subspace and $s_w(w)=-w$; hence $\det_+(s_w)=-1$.
The character $\chi$ of the proof of @prop:spinor-plus-cone-components takes the same values on these reflections.
The reflections generate $\Orth(S\tensor\RR)$ by the Cartan–Dieudonné theorem, so $\det_+=\chi$ on $\Orth(S)$.
So $\ker\det_+=\ker\chi$, which is $\Orth^+(S)$ by @prop:spinor-plus-cone-components and @lem:k3-positive-cone-component.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:signed-miranda-morrison title="The signed Miranda–Morrison sequence"}

Let $N$ be a nondegenerate, indefinite, even lattice of rank at least $3$.
Let $\Orth^+(N)$ be the subgroup of $\Orth(N)$ that preserves positive sign structures, and let $g(N)$ be the genus group of $N$.
There is a group $\operatorname{MM}^+(N)$ and an exact sequence of groups
$$
\Orth^+(N)\xrightarrow{\rho_{N,q}}\Orth(A_{N,q})\xrightarrow{e_N^+}\operatorname{MM}^+(N)\too g(N)\too1.
$$
The group $\operatorname{MM}^+(N)$ is $\mathrm E_+(N)$ in [@AD15, §3.7, equation (3.18)], and $\rho_{N,q}$ is the homomorphism $d$ of [@AD15, §3.2].
:::

::: {.definition #def:k3-mm-obstruction title="The signed Miranda–Morrison obstruction of a K3 surface"}

In @not:k3-automorphism-data, suppose $\rho\geq3$.
Then $S$ is nondegenerate, indefinite, even and of rank $\rho\geq3$ by @prop:k3-ns-transcendental, and its group $\Orth^+(S)$ in @thm:signed-miranda-morrison is the group $\Orth^+(S)$ of @not:k3-automorphism-data by @lem:k3-sign-structure.
Define
$$
\operatorname{ob}_X\definedas e_S^+\circ b|_{G_X}\colon G_X\too\operatorname{MM}^+(S),
\qquad
A_2\definedas\im(\operatorname{ob}_X).
$$
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:k3-aut-four-term title="The four-term automorphism sequence"}

In @not:k3-automorphism-data, suppose $\rho\geq3$.
Then the sequence
$$
1\too A_1\too\Aut(X)\xrightarrow{f\mapsto f_*|_T}G_X\xrightarrow{\operatorname{ob}_X}A_2\too1
$$
is exact, with $A_1$ as in @cor:k3-aut-projection-kernels and $\operatorname{ob}_X$, $A_2$ as in @def:k3-mm-obstruction.
:::

::: {.proof}

Exactness at $A_1$ and at $\Aut(X)$ is @cor:k3-aut-projection-kernels (1).
By @thm:k3-aut-fibre-product, the image of $\Aut(X)\to G_X$ is $\theset{t\in G_X\st b(t)\in a(P_S)}$.
By @lem:k3-ample-discriminant-image, $a(P_S)=\rho_{S,q}(\Orth^+(S))$, and by exactness of @thm:signed-miranda-morrison at $\Orth(A_{S,q})$ this is $\ker e_S^+$.
So the image is $\ker(e_S^+\circ b|_{G_X})=\ker\operatorname{ob}_X$.
The map $\operatorname{ob}_X$ is onto $A_2$ by definition.
:::

::: {.corollary #cor:k3-signed-mm-cokernel title="The obstruction group is a subgroup of the signed cokernel"}

In @not:k3-automorphism-data, suppose $\rho\geq3$, and put
$$
C_S^{+}\definedas\Orth(A_{S,q})/\rho_{S,q}\qty(\Orth^+(S)).
$$
Then $e_S^+$ induces an isomorphism
$$
C_S^{+}\isoto\ker\qty(\operatorname{MM}^+(S)\to g(S)),
$$
and $A_2$ is the subgroup $e_S^+\qty(b(G_X))$ of $\ker\qty(\operatorname{MM}^+(S)\to g(S))$.
:::

::: {.proof}

By exactness of @thm:signed-miranda-morrison, $\rho_{S,q}(\Orth^+(S))=\ker e_S^+$ is a normal subgroup and $\im e_S^+=\ker(\operatorname{MM}^+(S)\to g(S))$.
The first isomorphism theorem gives the isomorphism.
By @def:k3-mm-obstruction, $A_2=e_S^+(b(G_X))\iscontainedin\im e_S^+$.
:::

::: {.corollary #cor:k3-aut-full-orthogonal title="The fibre product with the full orthogonal group of $T$"}

In @not:k3-automorphism-data, the fibre product $P_S\times_Q\Orth(T)\definedas\theset{(s,t)\in P_S\times\Orth(T)\st a(s)=b(t)}$ is isomorphic, by extension of $s\perp t$, to the group of isometries of $L$ that preserve $S$ and $\Amp(X)$.
The group $\Aut(X)$ is its pullback along the inclusion $G_X\injects\Orth(T)$:
```tikzcd
\Aut(X) \arrow[r] \arrow[d] \arrow[dr, phantom, "\lrcorner", very near start] & G_X \arrow[d,hook] \\
P_S\times_Q\Orth(T) \arrow[r] & \Orth(T)
```
:::

::: {.proof}

An isometry of $L$ that preserves $S$ preserves $T=S^{\perp L}$.
By @cor:lifting-unimodular and the uniqueness clause of @thm:automorphism-lifting-criterion, $g\mapsto(g|_S,g|_T)$ is a bijection from such isometries to pairs $(s,t)\in\Orth(S)\times\Orth(T)$ with $\bar s_q=b(t)$, as in the proof of @thm:k3-aut-fibre-product.
It is a homomorphism, and $g$ preserves $\Amp(X)$ if and only if $g|_S\in P_S$.
The pairs with $t\in G_X$ form $P_S\times_Q G_X$, which is $\Aut(X)$ by @thm:k3-aut-fibre-product.
:::

::: {.proposition #prop:k3-aut-elliptic-rank-two title="Elliptic K3 surfaces of Picard rank two"}

In @not:k3-automorphism-data, suppose $X$ is elliptic and $\rho=2$.
Then $a\colon P_S\to Q$ is injective, so $A_1=1$ and $f\mapsto f_*|_T$ embeds $\Aut(X)$ in $G_X$ with image $b|_{G_X}^{-1}(a(P_S))$ [@MS24, Proposition 3.10 and its proof].
:::

## Computing the pullback

::: {.construction #cons:k3-aut-group-generators title="Group generators of $\Aut(X)$ from the fibre product"}

Take @not:k3-automorphism-data.
Let $Y\iscontainedin P_S$ be a symmetric set of group generators of $P_S$ and let $Z\iscontainedin G_X$ be a set of group generators of $G_X$, given as isometries of $S$ and of $T$; let $\gamma_q$ be given.

1. Put $H\definedas a(P_S)=\generators{a(Y)}\le Q$. It is finite because $A_{S,q}$ is finite.
2. Put $K\definedas b|_{G_X}^{-1}(H)\le G_X$. The group $G_X$ is finite cyclic [@MS24, §2.2], so $K$ is finite cyclic.
3. Put $N\definedas\ker a=A_1$. Let $P_S$ act on $H$ by $g\cdot h=a(g)h$. For each $h\in H$ choose $r_h\in P_S$ with $a(r_h)=h$, as a word in $Y$, with $r_1=\id$. Put
   $$
   Y_N\definedas\theset{r_{a(y)h}^{-1}\,y\,r_h\st y\in Y,\ h\in H}.
   $$
4. Choose a set $\theset{t_1,\dots,t_k}$ of group generators of $K$, and put $s_i\definedas r_{b(t_i)}$, so that $a(s_i)=b(t_i)$.
5. Return the pairs $(n,1)$ for $n\in Y_N$ and the pairs $(s_i,t_i)$, as elements of $P_S\times_Q G_X$. The extension of $s\perp t$ to $L$ is $f_*$ for the automorphism $f$ of @thm:k3-aut-fibre-product.
:::

::: {.proposition #prop:k3-aut-pullback-group-generators title="The construction gives group generators of $\Aut(X)$"}

In @cons:k3-aut-group-generators:

1. $N$ has index $\abs{H}$ in $P_S$, and $N=\generators{Y_N}$.
2. The sequence
   $$
   1\too N\xrightarrow{n\mapsto(n,1)}P_S\times_Q G_X\xrightarrow{(s,t)\mapsto t}K\too1
   $$
   is exact.
3. The pairs returned in step 5 generate $P_S\times_Q G_X\cong\Aut(X)$.
:::

::: {.proof}

(1) The action of $P_S$ on $H$ is transitive, and the stabilizer of $1$ is $\ker a=N$; so $[P_S:N]=\abs{H}$.
@lem:schreier-stabilizer, applied to this action with the point $1$ and the elements $r_h$, gives $N=\generators{Y_N}$.

(2) A pair $(s,t)$ in the fibre product has $b(t)=a(s)\in H$, so $t\in K$.
For $t\in K$ there is $s\in P_S$ with $a(s)=b(t)$, so the projection is onto $K$.
Its kernel is the set of pairs $(s,1)$ with $a(s)=1$, that is, $N\times\theset{1}$.

(3) Let $(s,t)$ be in the fibre product.
By (2), $t\in K$, so $t$ is a word $\omega$ in the $t_i^{\pm1}$.
The same word $\omega$ in the pairs $(s_i,t_i)^{\pm1}$ has second component $t$.
So $(s,t)\,\omega^{-1}$ lies in $N\times\theset{1}$, which the pairs $(n,1)$ with $n\in Y_N$ generate by (1).
:::

::: {.proposition #prop:k3-aut-cyclic-presentation title="A presentation of $\Aut(X)$ when $K$ is cyclic"}

In @cons:k3-aut-group-generators, suppose $K=\generators{t}$ has order $m>1$.
Let $s\in P_S$ with $a(s)=b(t)$ and put $u\definedas(s,t)$.
Then $sns^{-1}\in N$ for $n\in N$, $s^m\in N$, and
$$
u(n,1)u^{-1}=(sns^{-1},1),
\qquad
u^m=(s^m,1).
$$
Let $\generators{Y_N\st R}$ be a presentation of $N$.
For $y\in Y_N$ let $\omega_y$ be a word in $Y_N$ that represents $sys^{-1}$, and let $\omega_0$ be a word in $Y_N$ that represents $s^m$.
Then
$$
\Aut(X)\cong\generators{Y_N,\ u\st R,\ uyu^{-1}=\omega_y\ (y\in Y_N),\ u^m=\omega_0}.
$$
If $K=1$, then $\Aut(X)\cong N$.
:::

::: {.proof}

$N=\ker a$ is normal in $P_S$, and $a(s^m)=b(t^m)=1$, so $s^m\in N$.
The two identities hold in $P_S\times G_X$ because $t\,1\,t^{-1}=1$ and $t^m=1$.

Let $F'$ be the presented group and $F\definedas P_S\times_Q G_X$.
The assignment $y\mapsto(y,1)$, $u\mapsto(s,t)$ respects the relations, so it defines a homomorphism $\pi\colon F'\to F$.
It is onto: by @prop:k3-aut-pullback-group-generators (2), $F/(N\times\theset{1})\cong K$ is generated by the image of $u$, and by (1) of that proposition the pairs $(y,1)$ with $y\in Y_N$ generate $N\times\theset{1}$.
Let $N'\le F'$ be the subgroup generated by $Y_N$.
Conjugation by $u$ maps $N'$ into $N'$, and so does conjugation by $u^{-1}=u^{m-1}\omega_0^{-1}$; so $N'$ is normal, and $F'=\bigcup_{0\le k<m}N'u^k$.
The homomorphism $\pi$ maps $N'u^k$ into $N\times\theset{t^k}$, and these sets are disjoint for $0\le k<m$; so $\ker\pi\iscontainedin N'$.
The composite of $\generators{Y_N\st R}\to N'$ with $\pi|_{N'}$ is the given isomorphism onto $N\times\theset{1}$, so $\pi|_{N'}$ is injective.
Hence $\pi$ is an isomorphism, and $F\cong\Aut(X)$ by @thm:k3-aut-fibre-product.
If $K=1$, then $F=N\times\theset{1}$ by @prop:k3-aut-pullback-group-generators (2).
:::

## Markings and the two restrictions

::: {.proposition #prop:k3-aut-marking title="The automorphism group in a marking"}

In @not:k3-automorphism-data, let $(X,\varphi)$ be a marked $K3$ surface (@def:marked-k3-surface).
Put $\ell\definedas\varphi_\CC(H^{2,0}(X))$ and $\mathcal A\definedas\varphi_\RR(\Amp(X))$.
Then $f\mapsto\varphi\circ f_*\circ\varphi^{-1}$ is an isomorphism
$$
\Aut(X)\isoto\theset{g\in\Orth(\lkt)\st g_\CC(\ell)=\ell,\ g_\RR(\mathcal A)=\mathcal A}.
$$
:::

::: {.proof}

Conjugation by $\varphi$ is an isomorphism $\Orth(L)\isoto\Orth(\lkt)$.
It maps the Hodge isometries of $L$ that preserve $\Amp(X)$ onto the displayed group.
By the Torelli theorem [@MS24, Theorem 2.3], $f\mapsto f_*$ is an isomorphism from $\Aut(X)$ onto the Hodge isometries of $L$ that preserve $\Amp(X)$, as in the proof of @thm:k3-aut-fibre-product.
:::
