# Root systems and root lattices

::: {.remark title="Orientation and sign convention"}

The lattices governing the moduli theory of Coble surfaces are assembled from the classical root lattices, whose combinatorics we collect here for reference.
Recall from the Lattice Theory section that we work with the algebraic-geometry sign convention: a **root lattice** is *negative definite*, generated by vectors of norm $-2$, which we call **roots**.

It is however most transparent to construct the classical root lattices inside a *positive-definite* Euclidean space, where the roots have norm $+2$; the lattice used elsewhere in the monograph is then the negative-definite twist $L(-1)$.
Throughout this section we describe the positive-definite Euclidean model and understand $A_n$, $D_n$, $E_n$ to denote its negative twist.
All of the combinatorial invariants recorded below—root counts, simple systems, Weyl groups, and discriminant groups—are invariant under twisting, so no ambiguity results.
:::

## General root lattices

::: {.definition ref="def:root-lattice"}

Let $L$ be a lattice (in the Euclidean model, positive definite).
The set of **roots** of $L$ is
$$
R(L) \da \ts{ v\in L \mid v^2 = 2 }
.
$$
A **simple root system** $\Phi(L)\subseteq R(L)$ is a subset of roots $\ts{\alpha_i}$ such that every root $v\in R(L)$ is either a nonnegative integer combination or a nonpositive integer combination of the $\alpha_i$ --- the **positive** and **negative** roots respectively; that is,
$$
R(L) = \ZZ_{\geq 0}\,\Phi(L) \;\sqcup\; \ZZ_{\leq 0}\,\Phi(L)
,
$$
so every $v\in R(L)$ can be written as $v = \sum_{\alpha_i\in \Phi(L)} c_i \alpha_i$ with all $c_i\in \ZZ_{\geq 0}$ or all $c_i\in \ZZ_{\leq 0}$.
The elements of $\Phi(L)$ are the **simple roots**. Any simple root system spans a sublattice $\ZZ\Phi(L)\injects L$, and we say $L$ is a **root lattice** if this inclusion has index $1$, i.e. $L = \ZZ\Phi(L)$.
:::

::: {.remark title="The Weyl group"}

For a root $\alpha\in R(L)$, the associated **reflection** is the isometry
$$
\begin{aligned}
s_\alpha: L_\QQ &\to L_\QQ \\
x &\mapsto x - \frac{2\,\beta_L(x,\alpha)}{\beta_L(\alpha,\alpha)}\,\alpha
= x - \beta_L(x,\alpha)\,\alpha
,
\end{aligned}
$$
the second equality holding because $\alpha^2 = 2$.
It fixes the hyperplane $H_\alpha \da \alpha^{\perp L}$ pointwise and sends $\alpha\mapsto -\alpha$.
The **Weyl group** of $L$ is the subgroup
$$
W(L) \da \gens{\, s_{\alpha_i} \mid \alpha_i\in \Phi(L) \,} \leq \Orth(L)
$$
generated by the reflections in the simple roots.

For a root lattice we may identify the Gram matrix $G_L$ with $G_{\ZZ\Phi(L)}$, the matrix of pairings $\beta_L(\alpha_i,\alpha_j)$ between the simple roots; its diagonal entries are $\alpha_i^2 = 2$ and its off-diagonal entries record the intersections of simple roots.
:::

## The $ADE$ lattices

The simply-laced root lattices $A_n$, $D_n$, $E_n$ have a single root length and are the ones that occur in the lattice theory of $K3$ and Coble surfaces.

::: {.definition ref="def:lattice-An"}

Let $\ZZ^{n+1}$ denote the standard diagonal lattice $\gens{1}^{\oplus (n+1)}$ with orthonormal generators $\ts{e_1,\dots,e_{n+1}}$, and set $v_{n+1} \da \sum_{i=1}^{n+1} e_i$.
The root lattice $A_n$ is realized as the primitive sublattice
$$
A_n \cong v_{n+1}^{\perp\, \ZZ^{n+1}}
= \ts{ x\in \ZZ^{n+1} \mid \textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{n+1} x_i = 0 }
.
$$
Its roots are the difference vectors
$$
R(A_n) = \ts{ r_{ij} \da e_i - e_j \mid 1\leq i\neq j\leq n+1 }
,
$$
of which there are $n(n+1)$.
A simple system is given by the $n$ vectors
$$
\alpha_i \da e_i - e_{i+1},
\qquad 1\leq i\leq n
.
$$
:::

::: {.definition ref="def:lattice-Dn"}

The root lattice $D_n$ is the sublattice of $\ZZ^n$ cut out by the even-coordinate-sum condition, i.e. the kernel of the reduction $\varepsilon_2: \ZZ^n \to \ZZ/2\ZZ$, $x\mapsto \sum_i x_i \bmod 2$:
$$
D_n \cong \ZZ^n_{\mathrm{ev}}
\da \ts{ x = (x_1,\dots,x_n)\in \ZZ^n \mid \textstyle\sum_{i=1}^n x_i \equiv 0 \pmod{2} }
\injects \ZZ^n
.
$$
Its roots are
$$
R(D_n) = \ts{ \pm e_i \pm e_j \mid 1\leq i < j\leq n }
,
$$
of which there are $2n(n-1)$.
A simple system is given by
$$
\alpha_i \da e_i - e_{i+1} \ \ (1\leq i\leq n-1),
\qquad
\alpha_n \da e_{n-1} + e_n
.
$$
:::

::: {.definition ref="def:lattice-En"}

The root lattice $E_8$ is realized inside $\QQ^8$ as
$$
E_8 \cong \ts{ x\in \ZZ^8 \cup (\ZZ^8 + \tfrac12 v_8) \mid
\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^8 x_i \equiv 0 \pmod 2 },
\qquad v_8 = (1,\dots,1)
,
$$
so that $E_8$ consists of all integer vectors and all half-integer vectors whose coordinate sum is even.
The lattices $E_7$ and $E_6$ are obtained as orthogonal complements inside $E_8$:
$$
E_7 \da L_1^{\perp\, E_8}
\quad\text{for any } L_1\leq E_8 \text{ isometric to } A_1,
\qquad
E_6 \da L_2^{\perp\, E_8}
\quad\text{for any } L_2\leq E_8 \text{ isometric to } A_2
.
$$
(Here $A_1 = \gens{2}$ is generated by a single root and $A_2$ by two roots meeting at the usual angle; the isometry type of the complement is independent of the chosen sublattice.)
:::

::: {.example ref="ex:E8-roots"}

The lattice $E_8$ contains exactly
$$
2^4\cdot 3\cdot 5 = 240
$$
roots, of two kinds:

1. the $112$ difference vectors $\pm e_i \pm e_j$ for $1\leq i < j\leq 8$ (these are the roots of the $D_8$ sublattice), and

2. the $128$ half-sum vectors $\tfrac12(\pm e_1 \pm \cdots \pm e_8)$ with an even number of minus signs.

Indeed $\binom{8}{2}\cdot 4 = 112$ and $2^{8-1} = 128$, giving $240$ in total.
There are $8$ simple roots comprising $\Phi(E_8)$, and the Weyl group $W(E_8)$ acts transitively on the root set $R(E_8)$.
:::

::: {.theorem ref="thm:E8-characterization"}

$E_8$ is the unique even, unimodular, positive-definite lattice of rank $8$.
In particular it is self-dual, $E_8 \cong E_8\dual$.
:::

::: {.remark}

The characterization in \cref{thm:E8-characterization} is a classical fact of lattice theory [@CS10]. The construction of \cref{def:lattice-En} exhibits $E_8$ concretely as the overlattice $D_8^+$ of $D_8$ (see \cref{def:plus-construction} and \cref{thm:plus-construction}), which makes both the evenness and the unimodularity transparent.
:::

## Non-simply-laced root systems

The following root systems carry two root lengths and so are not root lattices in the sense of \cref{def:root-lattice}; we record them for completeness in the Euclidean model.

::: {.definition ref="def:root-system-Bn-Cn"}

The root system $R(B_n)\subseteq \ZZ^n$ consists of the **short roots** $r_i \da \pm e_i$ for $1\leq i\leq n$ together with the **long roots** $r_{ij} \da \pm e_i \pm e_j$ for $1\leq i < j\leq n$; there are $2n + 2n(n-1) = 2n^2$ roots in total ($2n$ short, $2n(n-1)$ long).
A simple system is
$$
\alpha_i \da e_i - e_{i+1} \ \ (1\leq i\leq n-1),
\qquad \alpha_n \da e_n
.
$$

The root system $R(C_n)$ is dual to $B_n$: it consists of the **long roots** $2r_i = \pm 2e_i$ together with the **short roots** $r_{ij} = \pm e_i \pm e_j$, again $2n^2$ roots in total ($2n$ long, $2n(n-1)$ short).
A simple system is
$$
\alpha_i \da e_i - e_{i+1} \ \ (1\leq i\leq n-1),
\qquad \alpha_n \da 2e_n
.
$$
:::

::: {.definition ref="def:root-system-F4-G2"}

The root system $F_4$ is realized in $\ZZ^4$ with root set $R(F_4)$ comprising the $24$ long roots $\pm e_i \pm e_j$ for $1\leq i < j\leq 4$ together with the $24$ short roots consisting of the $8$ vectors $\pm e_i$ ($1\leq i\leq 4$) and the $16$ half-sums $\tfrac12(\pm e_1 \pm e_2 \pm e_3 \pm e_4)$ ranging over all $2^4$ sign patterns, giving $48$ roots in all.
A system of simple roots is
$$
\alpha_1 = e_2 - e_3,\quad
\alpha_2 = e_3 - e_4,\quad
\alpha_3 = e_4,\quad
\alpha_4 = \tfrac12(e_1 - e_2 - e_3 - e_4)
.
$$

The root system $G_2$ embeds into the trace-zero sublattice $\ZZ^3_\Delta \da \ts{ x\in \ZZ^3 \mid \sum_i x_i = 0 } \subseteq \ZZ^3$.
Its roots are the vectors $\pm(e_i - e_j)$ and $\pm(2e_i - e_j - e_k)$, twelve in total.
A simple system is
$$
\alpha_1 = e_1 - e_2,
\qquad
\alpha_2 = -e_1 + 2e_2 - e_3
.
$$
:::

## The plus construction and $D_8^+ \cong E_8$

The realization of $E_8$ in \cref{def:lattice-En} is an instance of a general overlattice construction for the $D_n$ family.

::: {.definition ref="def:plus-construction"}

Define two vectors in $\QQ^n$ by
$$
v_+ = \tfrac12(1,1,1,\dots,1),
\qquad
v_- = \tfrac12(-1,1,1,\dots,1)
.
$$
The **plus** and **minus** overlattices of $D_n$ are
$$
D_n^+ \da D_n \cup (v_+ + D_n),
\qquad
D_n^- \da D_n \cup (v_- + D_n)
,
$$
each obtained by adjoining one half-integer coset to $D_n$.
:::

::: {.theorem ref="thm:plus-construction"}

Let $D_n^+ = D_n\cup(v_+ + D_n)$ with $v_+ = \tfrac12(1,\dots,1)$ be the overlattice of \cref{def:plus-construction}.

1. $D_n^+$ is a lattice if and only if $n$ is even.
   For $n$ odd, $2v_+ = (1,\dots,1)\notin D_n$ --- equivalently $[v_+]$ has order $4$ in $A_{D_n}\cong\ZZ/4\ZZ$ --- so the coset union is not closed under addition and is not a lattice.

2. For $n$ even, $D_n^+$ contains $D_n$ as a sublattice of index $2$ and has determinant $1$.

3. $D_n^+$ is an integral lattice (hence unimodular) if and only if $4\mid n$, and is even if and only if $8\mid n$.
   For $n\equiv 2\pmod 4$ the lattice $D_n^+$ has determinant $1$ but is non-integral, since $v_+^2 = n/4\notin\ZZ$.

4. In particular $D_8^+ \cong E_8$ is the even unimodular lattice of rank $8$.
:::

::: {.remark}

The properties in \cref{thm:plus-construction} are standard for the $D_n$ family [@CS10]. Because $D_8^+$ is unimodular and contains $D_8$ with index $2$, the even-coordinate-sum sublattice $D_8$ has discriminant group of order $4$; the half-integer coset $v_+ + D_8$ supplies precisely the $128$ half-sum roots of $E_8$ recorded in \cref{ex:E8-roots}, while the $112$ roots of $D_8$ supply the difference vectors.
This recovers the count $112 + 128 = 240$ and identifies $E_8$ with $D_8^+$.
:::
