# Invariant and coinvariant lattices

::: {.remark}

We record the lattice-theoretic constructions attached to a group of isometries of a lattice, and specialize them to the case of an involution.
This apparatus underpins the lattice theory of the K3-cover / Enriques-cover picture used throughout: the $2$-elementary lattices $S_\Co$ and $T_\Co$ arise as the invariant and coinvariant lattices of the nonsymplectic involution on the K3 cover of a Coble surface, and it is these lattices that enter the chain of primitive embeddings of \cref{lem:sequence_of_embeddings}.
:::

::: {.definition ref="def:invariant_coinvariant_lattices"}

Let $G\injects \Orth(L)$ be a faithful representation realizing a group $G$ acting on a lattice $L$ by isometries.
The **invariant sublattice** and the **coinvariant sublattice** of the action are defined as
$$
L^G \da \ts{v\in L \mid g.v = v \text{ for all } g\in G},
\qquad
L_G \da (L^G)^{\perp L}
.
$$
Thus $L^G$ is the sublattice of vectors fixed by every element of $G$, and $L_G$ is its orthogonal complement in $L$.
:::

::: {.proposition ref="prop:involution_eigenspaces"}

Suppose $G = \gens{I}$ is generated by an involution $I\in\Orth(L)$, so that $I^2 = \id$.
Then, for the induced action of $I$ on $L_\QQ$, the rational spans $(L^G)_\QQ$ and $(L_G)_\QQ$ are precisely the eigenspaces of $I$ for the eigenvalues $+1$ and $-1$ respectively, and
$$
L_\QQ = (L^G)_\QQ \operatorname{\perp} (L_G)_\QQ
.
$$
:::

::: {.proof}

Since $I^2 = \id$, the induced isometry of $L_\QQ$ is diagonalizable with all eigenvalues in $\ts{+1, -1}$, so $L_\QQ = V_+ \oplus V_-$ where $V_\pm$ denotes the $\pm 1$ eigenspace.
The fixed space $V_+$ is by definition $(L^G)_\QQ$.
For $v\in V_+$ and $w\in V_-$, using that $I$ is an isometry,
$$
\beta_{L_\QQ}(v, w) = \beta_{L_\QQ}(I v, I w) = \beta_{L_\QQ}(v, -w) = -\beta_{L_\QQ}(v, w)
,
$$
so $\beta_{L_\QQ}(v, w) = 0$.
Hence $V_-\subseteq V_+^{\perp}$, and comparing dimensions gives $V_- = V_+^{\perp} = (L_G)_\QQ$.
:::

::: {.proposition ref="prop:invariant_coinvariant_primitive"}

Let $G\injects\Orth(L)$ be a faithful representation of a finite cyclic group $G$.
Then $L^G$ and $L_G$ are primitive sublattices of $L$, and the orthogonal direct sum $L^G \oplus L_G$ is a finite-index sublattice of $L$ [@Nik80].
:::

::: {.proposition ref="prop:involution_decomposition"}

Let $L$ be an even nondegenerate lattice and let $G = \gens{I}\cong \ZZ/2\ZZ$ be generated by an involution $I\in\Orth(L)$.
The invariant and coinvariant sublattices $L^G$ and $L_G$ are primitive and mutually orthogonal.
Moreover, each $v\in L$ decomposes orthogonally, up to a factor of $2$, as
$$
2\cdot v = v_+ + v_-,
\qquad
v_+ \da v + I(v)\in L^G,
\qquad
v_- \da v - I(v)\in L_G
.
$$
:::

::: {.proof}

Orthogonality and primitivity are the case $G = \gens{I}$ of \cref{prop:invariant_coinvariant_primitive}, and $L_G = (L^G)^{\perp L}$ is orthogonal to $L^G$ by definition.
For the decomposition, fix $v\in L$ and set $v_+ \da v + I(v)$ and $v_- \da v - I(v)$, both of which lie in $L$.
Applying $I$ and using $I^2 = \id$,
$$
I(v_+) = I(v) + I^2(v) = I(v) + v = v_+,
\qquad
I(v_-) = I(v) - v = -v_-
,
$$
so $v_+$ is fixed by $I$ and hence $v_+\in L^G$, while $v_-$ lies in the $-1$ eigenspace of $I$ and hence, by \cref{prop:involution_eigenspaces}, in $(L_G)_\QQ\cap L = L_G$.
Adding the two expressions gives $v_+ + v_- = 2v$, and the summands are orthogonal since $v_+\in L^G$ and $v_-\in L_G = (L^G)^{\perp L}$.
:::

::: {.proposition ref="prop:coinvariant_involution_2elementary"}

Let $L$ be a $2$-elementary lattice and let $I\in\Orth^*(L)$ be an involution lying in the stable orthogonal group $\Orth^*(L) = \tilde\Orth(L)$.
Then both the invariant lattice $L^I$ and the coinvariant lattice $L_I$ are again $2$-elementary.
