# Hyperbolic reflection groups and Vinberg's algorithm

::: {.remark title="Orientation and sign convention"}

The Baily--Borel and semitoroidal compactifications of the period domains
attached to Coble and Enriques surfaces are governed by the action of
arithmetic reflection groups on hyperbolic space.
This section collects the reflection-group and hyperbolic-polytope
combinatorics used elsewhere in the monograph, culminating in Vinberg's
algorithm for a fundamental chamber and the Coxeter--Vinberg diagram that
encodes it.

We work throughout with the algebraic-geometry sign convention (see the
Lattice Theory section): roots are *negative-definite* directions, a
**short root** having norm $v^2 = -2$ and a **long root** norm $v^2 = -4$.
An indefinite lattice $L$ of signature $(1, n)$ is called **hyperbolic**;
its **positive cone**
$$
C_L \da \ts{ v\in L_\RR \mid v^2 > 0 }
$$
has two connected components, and mirrors of roots are the hyperplanes
orthogonal to negative-norm vectors.
Fixing one component $C_L^+$ identifies $L_\RR$ with a model of hyperbolic
$n$-space, on which the reflection group acts.
:::

## Reflections and the Weyl group

::: {.definition ref="def:reflection"}

Let $(L,\beta_L)$ be a nondegenerate integral lattice and $\alpha\in L$ an
anisotropic vector (that is, $\beta_L(\alpha,\alpha)\neq 0$) satisfying the
integrality condition
$$
2\,\beta_L(\alpha, L) \subseteq \beta_L(\alpha,\alpha)\,\ZZ
.
$$
The **reflection in $\alpha$** is the isometry
$$
\begin{aligned}
s_\alpha : L &\to L \\
x &\mapsto x - 2\,\frac{\beta_L(\alpha,x)}{\beta_L(\alpha,\alpha)}\,\alpha
.
\end{aligned}
$$
Each reflection has determinant $-1$, fixes the orthogonal complement
$\gens{\alpha}^{\perp L}$ pointwise, and sends $\alpha\mapsto -\alpha$.
The integrality condition is exactly what guarantees $s_\alpha\in\Orth(L)$
rather than merely $s_\alpha\in\Orth(L_\QQ)$.
The **Weyl group** of $L$ is the subgroup
$$
W(L) \da \gens{\, s_\alpha \mid \alpha\in L \text{ anisotropic},\ s_\alpha\in\Orth(L) \,}
\leq \Orth(L)
$$
generated by all such integral reflections.
:::

## Roots and $k$-roots

::: {.definition ref="def:k-root"}

Let $L$ be a nondegenerate integral lattice and $v\in L$ a primitive vector.
The reflection $s_v$ is always an isometry of $L_\QQ$, but is integral,
$s_v\in\Orth(L)$, precisely when
$$
2\,\beta_L(v, L) \subseteq v^2\,\ZZ
.
$$
For $k>0$ the **$k$-Weyl group** $W^k(L)$ is the subgroup generated by the
integral reflections $s_v$ with $v^2 = -k$:
$$
W^k(L) \da \gens{\, s_v \mid s_v\in\Orth(L),\ v^2 = -k \,}
.
$$
A vector $v$ is a **$k$-root** if $s_v\in W^k(L)$.
The full Weyl group $W(L)$ of \Cref{def:reflection} is generated by integral
reflections in vectors of any norm, so that $W(L) = \gens{\, W^k(L) \mid k>0 \,}$.
:::

::: {.definition ref="def:2elementary-roots"}

For a $2$-elementary lattice $L$, the roots are precisely the vectors $v\in L$
of one of the two following types:

- a **short root**, with $v^2 = -2$; or
- a **long root**, with $v^2 = -4$ and $\div_L(v) = 2$.
:::

::: {.remark ref="rmk:root-integrality"}

The two root types of \Cref{def:2elementary-roots} are exactly the primitive
vectors of norm $-2$ or $-4$ satisfying the integrality condition of
\Cref{def:k-root}.
For a short root, $2\beta_L(v,L)\subseteq 2\ZZ = v^2\ZZ$ holds automatically,
and $s_v(x) = x + \beta_L(v,x)\,v$.
For a norm $-4$ vector, integrality requires $\beta_L(v,L)\subseteq 2\ZZ$,
which is precisely the condition $\div_L(v) = 2$; then
$s_v(x) = x + \tfrac12\beta_L(v,x)\,v$ lies in $\Orth(L)$.
:::

## Coroots and the weight space

::: {.definition ref="def:coroot-weight"}

Let $R \da \gens{\Phi}_\ZZ$ be a root lattice generated by a root system
$\Phi = \ts{\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n}$.
The dual lattice $R\dual$ is called the **weight lattice** of $R$, and the
dual-basis elements $\omega_i$ determined by $(\omega_i, \alpha_j) = \delta_{ij}$
are the **fundamental weights**.
:::

::: {.remark}

For the unimodular root lattice $E_8$ one has $E_8\dual\cong E_8$, and the
fundamental weights $\omega_1,\dots,\omega_8$ --- the dual basis to the simple
roots --- have coordinates in the simple-root basis given by the rows of the
inverse Gram matrix.
The **coroots** are $\alpha_i\dual = 2\alpha_i/(\alpha_i,\alpha_i)$, which for
the simply-laced $E_8$ coincide with the simple roots themselves --- distinct
from the $\omega_i$.
:::

## Hyperbolic lattices and their polytopes

::: {.definition ref="def:hyperbolic-model"}

Let $L$ be a hyperbolic lattice and $C_L^+$ one connected component of its
positive cone $C_L$.
The intersection pairing induces a hyperbolic metric on $L_\RR$, and the
associated hyperbolic $n$-space $\mathbb{H}^n_L$ admits two standard models:

- the **unit hyperboloid model**
  $$
  \mathbb{H}^n_L \da \ts{ v\in L_\RR \mid v^2 = 1,\ v\in C_L^+ }
  ;
  $$
- the **projective (Klein) model**
  $$
  \operatorname{BB}^n_L \da \PP(C_L) \subset \PP(L_\RR)
  ,
  $$
  the image of the positive cone in projective space.

To a vector $v$ with $v^2 < 0$ we associate the hyperplane
$H_v \da v^{\perp}\cap \mathbb{H}^n_L$.
Taking $v,w$ to be unit normals of $H_v, H_w$, the relative position of two
hyperplanes is governed by the pairing $v\cdot w \da \beta_L(v,w)$:

- $|v\cdot w| < 1$: the hyperplanes meet in the interior of $\mathbb{H}^n_L$,
  at dihedral angle $\theta$ with $-v\cdot w = \cos\theta$;
- $|v\cdot w| = 1$: the hyperplanes are asymptotically parallel, meeting at an
  ideal point of the boundary $\partial\mathbb{H}^n_L$;
- $|v\cdot w| > 1$: the hyperplanes are ultra-parallel (divergent), at distance
  $d$ with $-v\cdot w = \cosh d$.
:::

## Coxeter groups and Coxeter polytopes

::: {.definition ref="def:coxeter-group"}

A **Coxeter group** is a group given by a presentation
$$
S = \gens{\, s_1,\dots,s_n \mid s_i^2 = (s_i s_j)^{m_{ij}} = e \,}
,
$$
where $m_{ii} = 1$ and $m_{ij} = m_{ji}\geq 2$ for $i\neq j$.
:::

::: {.definition ref="def:coxeter-polytope"}

A **Coxeter polytope** $P$ in $X = \mathbb{E}^n$, $S^n$, or $\mathbb{H}^n$ is a
polytope such that:

1. each face lies in a hyperplane $H_i \da \alpha_i^{\perp}$;
2. the dihedral angles between adjacent faces are $\pi/m_{ij}$ for integers
   $m_{ij}\geq 2$; and
3. the reflection group $W = \gens{ s_{\alpha_i} }$ acts properly
   discontinuously on $X$ with fundamental domain $P$.
:::

## The discriminant locus and Weyl chambers

::: {.definition ref="def:discriminant-locus"}

The **discriminant locus** $\Delta(L)$ is the union of all mirrors, i.e. the
hyperplanes orthogonal to the short roots $\Phi_2(L) = \ts{ v\in L \mid v^2 = -2 }$:
$$
\Delta(L) \da \bigcup_{v\in\Phi_2(L)} v^{\perp L}
.
$$
:::

::: {.definition ref="def:weyl-chamber"}

The **Weyl chambers** of $L$ are the connected components of
$$
C_L^{\circ} \da C_L \setminus \Delta(L)
,
$$
where $C_L$ is the cone of positive-norm vectors.
A **simple system** is a set of roots whose mirrors form the bounding
hyperplanes of a single Weyl chamber, i.e. a fundamental domain for the action
of the $2$-Weyl group $W^2(L)$ generated by the $(-2)$-reflections.
:::

## Coxeter--Vinberg diagrams

::: {.definition ref="def:coxeter-vinberg-diagram"}

The **Coxeter--Vinberg diagram** of a hyperbolic Coxeter polytope is a graph
whose vertices correspond to the simple roots $r_i$ (equivalently, to the walls
$H_i$ of the fundamental chamber), and whose edges encode the relative position
of the mirrors $H_i, H_j$ through the normalized pairing
$$
g_{ij} \da \frac{r_i\cdot r_j}{\sqrt{r_i^2\, r_j^2}} = \cos\!\left(\frac{\pi}{m_{ij}}\right)
.
$$
Edges are drawn according to the following conventions:

- **omitted** if $m_{ij}\leq 2$ (orthogonal mirrors);
- **multiple (or labeled) lines** if $3\leq m_{ij} < \infty$, when
  $H_i\cap H_j$ lies in the interior of $\mathbb{H}^n$;
- **thick / bold** if $g_{ij} = 1$, when $H_i\cap H_j$ lies on the boundary
  $\partial\mathbb{H}^n$ (asymptotically parallel mirrors);
- **dotted** if $g_{ij} > 1$ (ultra-parallel mirrors).

For $2$-elementary lattices, vertices are drawn **white** for short roots
($v^2 = -2$) and **black** for long roots ($v^2 = -4$).
:::

## Elliptic and parabolic subdiagrams

::: {.definition ref="def:elliptic-subdiagram"}

Let $G$ be a Coxeter--Vinberg diagram with vertices the simple roots $v_i$.
A subdiagram $D\subseteq G$ is:

- **elliptic** if its Gram matrix $(v_i\cdot v_j)$ is positive definite; such a
  subdiagram defines a finite face of the Coxeter polytope;
- **parabolic** if each connected component is parabolic (its Gram matrix
  positive semidefinite of corank one) and it is not contained in any strictly
  larger elliptic subdiagram.
:::

::: {.definition ref="def:parabolic-subdiagram"}

A parabolic subdiagram is **maximal** if it is not properly contained in a
larger parabolic subdiagram.
Maximal parabolic subdiagrams correspond bijectively to the rank-$2$ isotropic
subspaces $J\subset L$ containing a fixed isotropic line $I$ --- equivalently,
to the $1$-cusps adjacent to the $0$-cusp $[I]$ in the Baily--Borel
compactification.
:::

## Vinberg's algorithm

::: {.theorem ref="thm:vinberg-algorithm" title="Vinberg's algorithm for a fundamental chamber"}

Let $\Gamma\subseteq\Orth(L)$ be an arithmetic reflection subgroup of a
hyperbolic lattice $L$.
Vinberg's algorithm constructs a fundamental chamber $P\subset\mathbb{H}^n$ for
$\Gamma$ as follows [@Vin75].

1. **Control vector.** Choose a generic positive-norm vector
   $v_0\in C_L^+$. Its stabilizer in $\Gamma$ is the reflection group of the
   finite subdiagram of roots orthogonal to $v_0$, which are used as the first
   walls.

2. **Enumeration by height.** Seek primitive vectors $v\in L$ with $v^2 < 0$,
   $s_v\in\Gamma$, and mirror $H_v$ meeting the current cone $\tilde P$.
   Order the candidate roots by increasing **height**
   $$
   h(v) \da \frac{-2\,(v_0\cdot v)}{v^2}
   ,
   $$
   a quantity monotone in the hyperbolic distance from $v_0$ to $H_v$ (for roots
   of a given norm), used to order the candidate roots and
   process them in successive batches of increasing height.

3. **Acceptance / pruning.** A candidate root $v$ at the current height is kept
   only if its mirror bounds the chamber on the side containing $v_0$;
   equivalently, the inward normals of accepted walls pair non-negatively, so
   $v$ is accepted iff $v\cdot r_i \geq 0$ for every previously accepted root
   $r_i$ (a mirror with some $v\cdot r_i < 0$ would cut into the interior of
   $\tilde P$). Roots whose hyperplanes do not bound $\tilde P$
   are discarded.

4. **Diagram.** The algorithm proceeds until $\tilde P$ becomes a convex
   polytope of finite volume. The Gram matrix of the accepted simple roots then
   determines the Coxeter--Vinberg diagram of $P$
   (\Cref{def:coxeter-vinberg-diagram}).

The algorithm terminates in finitely many steps if and only if $\Gamma$ has
finite covolume in $\Orth(L)$.
:::

::: {.remark}

The source note states step 3 as "discard those roots whose hyperplanes do not
intersect $\tilde P$ transversely"; the explicit inequality
$v\cdot r_i\geq 0$ recorded above is Vinberg's acceptance criterion for that
condition in the algebraic-geometry sign convention [@Vin75].
:::

## Root-system conventions and tables

::: {.remark title="Bourbaki conventions and twists"}

The classical root systems are taken with the Bourbaki labeling, in the
negative-definite twists appropriate to the algebraic-geometry convention:
$$
A_n(-1),\quad B_n(-2),\quad C_n(-1),\quad D_n(-1),\quad
E_n(-1)\ (n\in\ts{6,7,8}),\quad F_4(-2),\quad G_2(-1)
.
$$
Both the simply-laced systems $A_n, D_n, E_n$ and the non-simply-laced systems
$B_n, C_n, F_4, G_2$ occur.
The Coxeter diagrams and Gram matrices used throughout are derived from these
conventions; the ranks, root counts, Weyl groups, discriminant groups, and
explicit Euclidean basis constructions are collected in the root-lattice tables
of the Root Systems section and the Diagrams appendix.
:::
