# Basic invariants of a lattice {#sec-basic-invariants}

Throughout, $R$ is a Dedekind domain with fraction field $K$, the main case being $R=\bZ$ and $K=\bQ$, and $L\in\mathbf{Lat}_R$ is a lattice as in @def:lattice.

::: {.notation #def:lattice-basic-invariants title="Norm and norm shells" defines="norm; norm shell; $L[a]$"}

Let $L$ be an integral lattice.
The *norm* of $v\in L$ is the value of the form at the diagonal (@def:polarization-functors), $\beta_L(v,v)$; the notation $q_L$ is reserved for the quadratic discriminant form of @def:discriminant.
For $a\in\bZ$ the *norm shell* of $a$ is the set of vectors of that norm,
$$
L[a]\definedas\theset{v\in L\st \beta_L(v,v)=a}\subseteq L.
$$
When $L$ is even (@def:even-lattice) every norm is even, so $L[a]$ is empty for odd $a$.
:::

::: {.definition #def:odd-lattice title="Odd lattices" defines="odd lattice"}

An integral lattice $L$ is *odd* if it is not even (@def:even-lattice), that is, if some $v\in L$ has odd norm (@def:lattice-basic-invariants) $\beta_L(v,v)$ [@Nik80, §1.1; @CS10, Ch. 2, §2].
:::

::: {.definition #def:induced-integral-lattice-structure title="Induced integral lattice structure" defines="induced integral lattice structure; induced integral lattice"}

Let $L$ be a lattice (@def:lattice), with form $\beta_L$ extended bilinearly to $L\otimes\bQ$, and let $M\subseteq L\otimes\bQ$ be a subgroup that is free of finite rank.
If $\beta_L(M,M)\subseteq\bZ$, the restriction $\beta_L|_{M\times M}$ makes $M$ an integral lattice (@def:lattice), the **integral lattice structure induced** on $M$ by $L$.
:::

::: {.definition #def:sign-twist-equivalence title="Sign-twist equivalence of lattices" defines="sign-twist equivalence of lattices; sign-twist equivalence; sign twist"}

Two lattices $L$ and $M$ are **sign-twist equivalent** if $L\cong M$ or $L\cong M(-1)$, where $M(-1)$ is the twist (@def:form-twist) of $M$ by $-1$.
:::

::: {.definition #def:lattice-divisibility title="Divisibility of a vector" defines="divisibility ideal; $I_v$; divisibility; $\div_L(v)$"}

Let $v\in L$.
The *divisibility ideal* of $v$ is the image of the functional $\beta_L(v,-)$,
$$
I_v\definedas\operatorname{im}\qty{\beta_L(v,-)\colon L\longrightarrow R}\triangleleft R.
$$
For $R=\bZ$ it is generated by a positive integer, the *divisibility* of $v$,
$$
\div_L(v)\definedas\gcd\theset{\beta_L(v,x)\st x\in L},
\qquad
I_v=\div_L(v)\bZ,
$$
so $\div_L(v)$ is the largest integer by which every value $\beta_L(v,x)$ is divisible.
:::

::: {.definition #def:lattice-discriminant-class title="Discriminant class of a vector" defines="dual vector; $v^*$; discriminant class; $[v^*]$"}

Let $L\in\mathbf{Lat}_{\bZ}^{\mathrm{nd}}$ be integral and let $0\ne v\in L$.
The *dual vector* of $v$ is
$$
v^*\definedas\tfrac{1}{\div_L(v)}\,v\in L^\#,
$$
which lies in the metric dual by @def:lattice-divisibility: every value $\beta_{L}(v^*,x)=\beta_L(v,x)/\div_L(v)$ is an integer.
The *discriminant class* of $v$ is its image
$$
[v^*]\definedas\pi_L(v^*)\in A_L^\sharp
$$
under the cokernel (@def:kernel-cokernel) morphism $\pi_L\colon L^\#\twoheadrightarrow A_L^\sharp$ of @def:discriminant.
:::

::: {.definition #def:orthonormal-basis title="Orthonormal bases" defines="orthonormal basis"}

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional real inner product space with norm (@def:lattice-basic-invariants) $\|v\|=\sqrt{\langle v,v\rangle}$.
A basis (@def:based-module) $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ of $V$ is *orthonormal* if $\|e_i\|=1$ for all $i$ and $\langle e_i,e_j\rangle=0$ for $i\ne j$ [@Axl15, Ch. 6B].
:::

::: {.definition #def:operator-norm title="Operator norm" defines="operator norm; norm of an operator; $\|T\|$"}

Let $T\colon X\to Y$ be a linear map of normed spaces.
The *operator norm* of $T$ is
$$
\|T\|\definedas\sup_{x\in X,\ x\ne0}\frac{\|Tx\|}{\|x\|},
$$
which is the smallest $c$ with $\|Tx\|\le c\|x\|$ for all $x\in X$; it is finite exactly when $T$ is bounded [@Kre78, §2.7, (2)].
For a positive definite lattice (@def:definiteness) the norm on its real span is $\|x\|=\sqrt{\beta(x,x)}$, the Euclidean norm of @def:lattice-basic-invariants.
:::

::: {.definition #def:isotropic-subspace title="Isotropic subspaces" defines="isotropic subspace; totally isotropic subspace; isotropic vector"}

Let $(V,b)$ be a finite-dimensional symmetric bilinear space over a field of characteristic not $2$ and set $q(v)=b(v,v)$.
A nonzero vector $v$ is *isotropic* if $q(v)=0$.
A subspace $W\subseteq V$ is *isotropic* (also *totally isotropic*) if $q(w)=0$ for all $w\in W$, equivalently $b|_{W\times W}=0$ [@Ger08, Def. 2.3].
The greatest dimension of an isotropic subspace of a regular space is its Witt index $i(V)$ (@def:witt-index) [@Ger08, Ch. 2, after Prop. 2.42].
An isotropic subspace of this greatest dimension is *maximal*.
:::

::: {.definition #def:maximal-positive-definite-subspace title="Maximal positive definite subspaces" defines="maximal positive definite subspace; maximal positive-definite subspace; positive index; $\operatorname{ind}^{+}(V)$"}

Let $(V,b)$ be a finite-dimensional real symmetric bilinear space of signature (@def:signature) $(p,q,r)$.
A subspace $P\subseteq V$ is a *maximal positive definite subspace* if $b|_P$ is positive definite (@def:definiteness) and $P$ is maximal under inclusion among such subspaces.
All maximal positive definite subspaces have the same dimension $p$, the *positive index* $\operatorname{ind}^{+}(V)$ [@OM00, §61A; @Ger08, Def. 2.41].
:::

::: {.definition #def:negative-index title="Negative index of a lattice" defines="negative index of a lattice; negative index; $\operatorname{ind}^{-}(L)$"}

Let $L$ be a lattice and $L_{\bR}=L\otimes\bR$ its real quadratic space.
The *negative index* $\operatorname{ind}^{-}(L)$ is the dimension of a maximal negative definite (@def:definiteness) subspace of $L_{\bR}$, which is the entry $q$ of the signature $(p,q,r)$ (@def:signature) of $L$ [@Ger08, Def. 2.41].
:::

::: {.definition #def:isotropic-basis-hyperbolic-plane title="Isotropic basis of a hyperbolic plane" defines="isotropic basis of a hyperbolic plane; isotropic basis; hyperbolic pair"}

Let $H$ be a hyperbolic plane (@def:hyperbolic-plane) over a field.
An *isotropic basis* of $H$ is a basis $e,f$ with
$$
b(e,e)=b(f,f)=0,\qquad b(e,f)=1,
$$
also called a *hyperbolic pair* [@Cas78, Ch. 2, §2, (2.1)].
The defining basis $e,f$ of the lattice $U$ in @def:hyperbolic-plane is an isotropic basis.
:::

::: {.definition #def:connection-index title="Connection index of a root system" defines="connection index; connection index of a root lattice"}

Let $R$ be a root system (@def:root-system-base) in a real vector space $V$, let $Q(R)\subseteq V$ be the subgroup generated by $R$ (the root lattice), and let $P(R)$ be its weight lattice (@def:coroot-weight).
The quotient $P(R)/Q(R)$ is finite, and its order is the *connection index* of $R$ [@Bou08, Ch. VI, §1, no. 9].
For a root lattice $L$ it is the connection index of the root system $R(L)$ (@def:root-lattice).
:::

::: {.definition #def:coxeter-exponent title="Coxeter elements and exponents" defines="Coxeter element; Coxeter number; Coxeter exponent; exponents of a finite reflection group"}

Let $W$ be an irreducible essential finite reflection group (@def:reflection-group) (@def:coxeter-group) with simple reflections $s_1,\ldots,s_n$.
A *Coxeter element* is a product $s_1\cdots s_n$ in some order; all Coxeter elements are conjugate, and their common order is the *Coxeter number* $h$.
If $\zeta$ is a primitive $h$-th root of unity, the eigenvalues of a Coxeter element are $\zeta^{m_1},\ldots,\zeta^{m_n}$ with $0\le m_1\le\cdots\le m_n<h$.
The integers $m_i$ are the *exponents* of $W$ [@Hum90, §3.16; @Bou08, Ch. V, §6, no. 2, Def. 2].
:::

::: {.definition #def:even-odd-part-reflection-group title="Even and odd parts of a reflection group" defines="even part of a reflection group; odd part of a reflection group; even part; odd part"}

Let $W$ be a finite reflection group (@def:reflection-group) acting on a real vector space.
The *even part* $W^{+}$ is the kernel (@def:kernel-cokernel) of the determinant: the normal subgroup of index (@def:index) $2$ of elements of determinant $1$ (the rotation subgroup), which are the products of an even (@def:form-axioms) number of reflections [@Hum90, §2.12 and §5.2].
The *odd part* is the complementary coset $W\setminus W^{+}$ of elements of determinant $-1$, the products of an odd number of reflections.
:::

::: {.definition #def:nef-cone title="Nef cone" defines="nef cone; $\operatorname{Nef}(X)$; nef class"}

Let $X$ be a smooth projective surface (@def:smooth-projective-surface).
A class $\alpha\in\operatorname{NS}(X)_{\bR}$ is *nef* if $(\alpha\cdot C)\ge0$ for every curve $C\subset X$.
The *nef cone* $\operatorname{Nef}(X)\subseteq\operatorname{NS}(X)_{\bR}$ is the cone (@def:closed-cone) of nef classes; it is convex and is the closure of the ample cone [@Huy16, Ch. 8, Def. 1.1; @Laz04, Def. 1.4.20].
For a K3 surface (@def:k3-surface) the ample cone is that of @def:ample-cone.
:::

::: {.definition #def:lattice-polarization-k3 title="Lattice polarization of a K3 surface" defines="lattice polarization of a K3 surface; lattice polarization; $M$-polarized K3 surface"}

Let $M$ be a lattice of signature (@def:signature) $(1,t)$ and $X$ a K3 surface (@def:k3-surface).
A *lattice polarization* of $X$ by $M$ is a primitive embedding $j\colon M\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(X)$ (@def:primitive-embedding); the pair $(X,j)$ is an *$M$-polarized K3 surface*.
Let $C(M)^{+}$ be the set of vectors of $M$ in the positive cone (@not:reflection-groups-sign-convention) of $M$ that pair positively with a fixed set $\Delta(M)^{+}$ of positive roots, the vectors of norm (@def:lattice-basic-invariants) $-2$.
The polarization is *ample* when $j(C(M)^{+})$ contains an ample class [@Dol96, §1, Definition].
:::

::: {.definition #def:polarization-vector title="Polarization vector" defines="polarization vector"}

Let $X$ be a K3 surface (@def:k3-surface) with a polarization $L$, an ample line bundle that is primitive in $\operatorname{Pic}(X)$ and has degree (@def:graded-object) $2d=(L)^2$ [@Huy16, Ch. 2, Def. 4.1].
The *polarization vector* is the class $h=c_1(L)\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)$, a vector of norm (@def:lattice-basic-invariants) $2d$ in the lattice $\operatorname{Pic}(X)$.
For a rank one lattice polarization $j\colon\bZ\,h_0\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(X)$ (@def:lattice-polarization-k3) it is $j(h_0)$ [@Dol96, §1].
:::
