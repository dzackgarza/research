# Lattices and discriminant forms {#sec-lattices-discriminant}

Let $R$ be a Dedekind domain with fraction field $K$; the main case is $R=\bZ$ and $K=\bQ$.

::: {.definition #def:lattice title="Lattices"}

An $R$-**lattice** is a bilinear module $(L,R,\beta_L)$ whose underlying $R$-module is finitely generated projective.
Write $\mathbf{Lat}_R\hookrightarrow\mathcal B_{R,R}$ for the replete full subcategory of @def:form-categories on these objects, and $\mathbf{Lat}_R^{\mathrm{nd}}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Lat}_R$ for the replete full subcategory cut out by nondegeneracy in @def:polarization.
:::


::: {.example #ex:subobject-base-change title="Subobjects under extension of scalars"}
Let $L$ be an integral lattice and let $\iota_v\colon\generators{v}_{\bZ}\injects L$ be the monomorphism generated by $0\ne v\in L$.
Let $\pi_v$ be its cokernel morphism in $\bZ\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$:
$$
0
\too
\generators{v}_{\bZ}
\xrightarrow{\iota_v}
L
\xrightarrow{\pi_v}
Q_v
\too
0.
$$
Applying the exact functor $(-)\tensor_{\bZ}\bR$ gives
$$
0
\too
\generators{v}_{\bR}
\xrightarrow{\iota_v\tensor1}
L_{\bR}
\xrightarrow{\pi_v\tensor1}
Q_v\tensor_{\bZ}\bR
\too
0,
$$
since $\bR$ is flat over $\bZ$.
:::




::: {.definition #def:even-lattice title="Even lattices"}

An $R$-lattice $L$ is **even** when $\beta_L$ is even in the sense of @def:form-axioms.
Write $\mathbf{EvenLat}_R\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Lat}_R$ for the corresponding replete full subcategory.
:::



## Classification by signature {#sec-lattice-signature}

::: {.definition #def:signature-subcategories title="Signature subcategories"}

For a signature tuple $\underline s=(s_\sigma)_{\sigma\in\Sigma_\infty(R)}$ of @def:signature, let $\mathbf{Lat}_R^{\underline s}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Lat}_R$ be the replete full subcategory of lattices with signature tuple $\underline s$.

For $R=\bZ$, define
$$
\mathbf{Def}_{\bZ}^{+}
\definedas
\coprod_{n\geq1}\mathbf{Lat}_{\bZ}^{(n,0)},
\qquad
\mathbf{Def}_{\bZ}^{-}
\definedas
\coprod_{n\geq1}\mathbf{Lat}_{\bZ}^{(0,n)},
$$
$$
\mathbf{Def}_{\bZ}
\definedas
\mathbf{Def}_{\bZ}^{+}\sqcup\mathbf{Def}_{\bZ}^{-},
\qquad
\mathbf{Indef}_{\bZ}
\definedas
\coprod_{p,q\geq1}\mathbf{Lat}_{\bZ}^{(p,q)},
$$
and
$$
\mathbf{Hyp}_{\bZ}
\definedas
\coprod_{n\geq2}\mathbf{Lat}_{\bZ}^{(1,n-1)}
\hookrightarrow
\mathbf{Indef}_{\bZ}.
$$
The twist functor of @def:form-twist restricts to
$$
(-)(-1)\colon
\mathbf{Def}_{\bZ}^{+}
\isoto
\mathbf{Def}_{\bZ}^{-}.
$$
The root lattices are objects of $\mathbf{Def}_{\bZ}^{-}$.
:::

::: {.definition #def:metric-dual title="Metric $I$-duals"}

Let $L\in\mathbf{Lat}_R^{\mathrm{nd}}$ and let $I\injects K$ be a nonzero fractional ideal.
The coefficient exact sequence is
$$
0\too I\too K\too K/I\too0
$$ {#eq:metric-dual-coefficients}
Applying the exact functor $\Hom_R(L,-)$ to @eq:metric-dual-coefficients gives
$$
0
\too
\Hom_R(L,I)
\too
\Hom_R(L,K)
\too
\Hom_R(L,K/I)
\too
0.
$$
Applying scalar extension $K\tensor_R-$ to $\beta_L$ gives
$$
\beta_{L_K}\colon
L_K\tensor_KL_K
\too
K,
\qquad
\beta_{L_K}^{\sharp}\colon
L_K
\isoto
\Hom_R(L,K).
$$
The **metric $I$-dual** $L^{\# I}$ is the pullback
```tikzcd
L^{\# I} \arrow[r] \arrow[d] &
L_K \arrow[d,"\beta_{L_K}^{\sharp}"] \\
\Hom_R(L,I) \arrow[r] &
\Hom_R(L,K)
```

An **$I$-integral structure** on $L$ is the unique morphism $\eta_{L,I}\colon L\too L^{\# I}$ in $R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$ making
```tikzcd
& L^{\# I} \arrow[d] \\
L \arrow[ur,"\eta_{L,I}"] \arrow[r] & L_K
```

commute; $L$ is **$I$-integral** when this lift exists.
For an $I$-integral lattice define $A_L^{\# I}$ by the exact sequence
$$
0
\too
L
\xrightarrow{\eta_{L,I}}
L^{\# I}
\too
A_L^{\# I}
\too
0.
$$
For $I=R$ write
$$
L^\#\definedas L^{\# R},
\qquad
\iota_L\definedas\eta_{L,R},
\qquad
A_L^\sharp\definedas A_L^{\# R}.
$$
:::

::: {.definition #def:unimodular title="$I$-modular and unimodular lattices"}

An $I$-integral lattice $L$ is **$I$-modular** when $A_L^{\# I}=0$.
Equivalently, its defining exact sequence is
$$
0\too L\xrightarrow{\eta_{L,I}}L^{\# I}\too0.
$$
Write $\mathbf{ModLat}_{R,I}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Lat}_R^{\mathrm{nd}}$ for the replete full subcategory of $I$-modular lattices.
For $I=R$ set $\mathbf{Unimod}_R\definedas\mathbf{ModLat}_{R,R}$.
:::


::: {.definition #def:discriminant title="Bilinear and quadratic discriminant forms"}

Let $L\in\mathbf{Lat}_{\bZ}^{\mathrm{nd}}$ be integral.
The **bilinear denominator** is $N_b(L)\definedas\exp\qty{A_L^\sharp}$.
The restricted pairing has value module $\tfrac1{N_b(L)}\bZ$:
$$
L^\#\tensor_{\bZ}L^\#
\xrightarrow{\beta_{L^\#}}
\tfrac1{N_b(L)}\bZ
\injects
\bQ.
$$
Let $j_{b,L}\colon\bZ\injects\tfrac1{N_b(L)}\bZ$.
Then
$$
\boldsymbol\iota_L
\definedas
(\iota_L,j_{b,L})
$$
is a morphism in $\mathbf{BilMod}_{\bZ}$, and the **bilinear discriminant form** is its cokernel in that abelian category:
$$
(L,\bZ,\beta_L)
\xrightarrow{\boldsymbol\iota_L}
\qty{L^\#,\tfrac1{N_b(L)}\bZ,\beta_{L^\#}}
\twoheadrightarrow
A_L
\too
0.
$$
Thus $A_L$ always denotes the bilinear discriminant object.
The forgetful functor $U\colon\mathbf{BilMod}_{\bZ}\too\bZ\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$ satisfies $U(A_L)=A_L^\sharp$, and the pairing of $A_L$ is the unique morphism
$$
\bar{\beta}_L\colon
A_L^\sharp\tensor_{\bZ}A_L^\sharp
\too
\tfrac1{N_b(L)}\bZ/\bZ
$$
for which, writing $\pi_L\colon L^\#\twoheadrightarrow A_L^\sharp$,
$$
\bar{\beta}_L\circ(\pi_L\tensor\pi_L)
=
\qty{\tfrac1{N_b(L)}\bZ\twoheadrightarrow\tfrac1{N_b(L)}\bZ/\bZ}
\circ\beta_{L^\#}.
$$

Let $N_q(L)$ be the quadratic level of @def:lattice-level; no evenness hypothesis is required.
Define
$$
J_L
\definedas
\generators{2,\ \beta_L(x,x)\mid x\in L}_{\bZ}
\injects
\bZ.
$$
The diagonal of $\beta_{L^\#}$ induces the unique quadratic morphism
$$
q_L\colon
A_L^\sharp
\too
\tfrac2{N_q(L)}\bZ/J_L
$$
satisfying
$$
q_L\circ\pi_L(x)
=
\beta_{L_\bQ}(x,x)+J_L.
$$
The **quadratic discriminant form** is
$$
A_{L,q}
\definedas
\qty{A_L^\sharp,\tfrac2{N_q(L)}\bZ/J_L,q_L}.
$$
If $L$ is even, then $J_L=2\bZ$, so this recovers the usual
$\tfrac2{N_q(L)}\bZ/2\bZ$-valued refinement [@Nik80, §1.1].
:::

::: {.definition #def:discbil title="Discriminant bilinear forms"}

The **discriminant bilinear forms** are the objects of
$\mathbf{BilMod}_{\bZ}$ isomorphic to $A_L$ for some
$L\in\mathbf{Lat}_{\bZ}^{\mathrm{nd}}$.
Write
$$
\mathbf{DiscBil}_{\bZ}^{\simeq}
\hookrightarrow
\mathbf{BilMod}_{\bZ}^{\simeq}
$$
for the full subgroupoid spanned by these cokernels.
:::

::: {.definition #def:discquad title="Discriminant quadratic forms"}

The **discriminant quadratic forms** are the objects isomorphic to
$A_{L,q}$ for some $L\in\mathbf{Lat}_{\bZ}^{\mathrm{nd}}$.
Their bilinearizations are $\operatorname{polar}(A_{L,q})$ in the sense of @def:polarization-functors.
Write $\mathbf{DiscQuad}_{\bZ}^{\simeq}$ for their full subgroupoid in the groupoid of quadratic modules fibred over their value modules.
:::

The two constructions are functors on cores:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\qty{\mathbf{Lat}_{\bZ}^{\mathrm{nd}}}^{\simeq}
&\too
\mathbf{DiscBil}_{\bZ}^{\simeq},
&
L&\longmapsto A_L,
\\
\qty{\mathbf{Lat}_{\bZ}^{\mathrm{nd}}}^{\simeq}
&\too
\mathbf{DiscQuad}_{\bZ}^{\simeq},
&
L&\longmapsto A_{L,q}.
\end{aligned}
$$



## Elementary lattices {#sec-elementary-lattices}

::: {.definition #def:p-elementary title="$p$-elementary lattices"}

Let $p$ be a prime and let $S\in\mathbf{Lat}_{\bZ}^{\mathrm{nd}}$.
The lattice $S$ is **$p$-elementary** when the $\bZ$-module $A_S^\sharp$ is $p$-elementary.
Writing $a\definedas\ell\qty{A_S^\sharp}$, one has
$$
A_S^\sharp\isoto(\bZ/p\bZ)^a,
\qquad
\cardinality{A_S^\sharp}=p^a,
\qquad
\abs{\disc{S}}=p^a.
$$
For $p=2$ this is Nikulin's $2$-elementary condition [@Nik80, §3.6.1].
The condition defines a replete full subcategory of $\mathbf{Lat}_{\bZ}^{\mathrm{nd}}$.

For an even $2$-elementary lattice $S$, the coparity $\delta_S$ is the invariant of $A_{S,q}$ from [@Nik80, §3.6].
Let the signature of $S$ be $(t_{(+)},t_{(-)},0)$.
The genus is determined by $(\delta_S;t_{(+)},t_{(-)},a)$, and when $t_{(+)}>0$ and $t_{(-)}>0$ these invariants determine the integral isometry class [@Nik80, Thm. 3.6.2].
:::

::: {.example #ex:a3-not-two-elementary title="Determinant order does not imply 2-elementarity"}
In the sign convention of @def:definiteness the root lattice $A_3$ has Gram matrix
$$
G=\begin{pmatrix}-2&1&0\\1&-2&1\\0&1&-2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det G=-4.
$$
Write $d_1\mid d_2\mid d_3$ for the invariant factors of $A_{A_3}^\sharp$:
$$
A_{A_3}^\sharp\isoto\bigoplus_i\bZ/d_i\bZ.
$$
The greatest common divisor of the entries of $G$ is $d_1=1$, and the greatest common divisor of its $2\times2$ minors is $d_1d_2=1$.
Since $d_1d_2d_3=\abs{\det G}=4$, one obtains $A_{A_3}^\sharp\isoto\bZ/4\bZ$.
Hence $A_{A_3}^\sharp$ has exponent $4$, whereas a $2$-elementary $\bZ$-module has exponent at most $2$.
:::



## Radical and unimodularity {#sec-radical-unimodularity}

::: {.definition #def:two-witnesses title="Radical and coradical"}

For a symmetric bilinear form $b$ on a finitely generated projective $R$-module $M$, define its radical and coradical by the exact sequence
$$
0
\too
\radic(M)
\too
M
\xrightarrow{b^\sharp}
M^*
\too
\operatorname{corad}_b(M)
\too
0.
$$
Then $b$ is nondegenerate exactly when $\radic(M)=0$, and it is perfect exactly when $\radic(M)=0=\operatorname{corad}_b(M)$.
:::


::: {.theorem #thm:radical-splits title="The radical splits off"}

Let $R$ be a Dedekind domain, let $M$ be a finitely generated projective $R$-module, and let $b$ be a symmetric $R$-valued bilinear form on $M$.
Let
$$
\rho_M\colon\radic(M)\injects M,
\qquad
q_M\colon M\surjects M^{\mathrm{nd}}
$$
be the radical morphism and its cokernel in $\mathbf{BilMod}_R$:
$$
0\too\radic(M)\xrightarrow{\rho_M}M\xrightarrow{q_M}M^{\mathrm{nd}}\too0.
$$
Then there exists a section
$$
s_M\colon M^{\mathrm{nd}}\too M,
\qquad
q_M\circ s_M=\id_{M^{\mathrm{nd}}},
$$
and the orthogonal-sum morphism
$$
\rho_M\perp s_M\colon
\radic(M)\perp M^{\mathrm{nd}}
\isoto
M
$$
is an isomorphism of $R$-valued bilinear modules.
:::

::: {.proof}
The adjoint factors uniquely through $q_M$:
$$
M\xrightarrow{q_M}M^{\mathrm{nd}}
\xrightarrow{\overline b^{\sharp}}
\Hom_R(M,R).
$$
Since $\radic(M)=\ker(b^\sharp)$, the induced sequence
$$
0
\too
M^{\mathrm{nd}}
\xrightarrow{\overline b^\sharp}
\Hom_R(M,R)
$$
is exact.
Thus the underlying $R$-module of $M^{\mathrm{nd}}$ is finitely generated and torsion-free, hence projective over the Dedekind domain $R$.
The underlying short exact sequence of $R$-modules therefore splits; choose
$$
s_M\colon M^{\mathrm{nd}}\too M,
\qquad
q_M\circ s_M=\id_{M^{\mathrm{nd}}}.
$$
The form on $M^{\mathrm{nd}}$ is the unique factorization of $b$ through $q_M\tensor_Rq_M$, so $b(s_Mx,s_My)=b^{\mathrm{nd}}(x,y)$.
Moreover,
$$
b\circ(\rho_M\tensor_Rs_M)=0
=
b\circ(s_M\tensor_R\rho_M).
$$
Hence the split module isomorphism
$$
\radic(M)\oplus M^{\mathrm{nd}}
\xrightarrow{\ \rho_M\oplus s_M\ }
M
$$
is the orthogonal-sum isomorphism
$$
\radic(M)\perp M^{\mathrm{nd}}
\xrightarrow{\ \rho_M\perp s_M\ }
M.
$$
:::

## Localization and comparison {#sec-discriminant}

::: {.theorem #thm:localization-les title="Localization exact sequences for lattices"}
Let
$$
\mathcal E_R\colon 0\too R\too K\too K/R\too0
$$
be the coefficient exact sequence. Tensoring $\mathcal E_R$ with an $R$-module $M$ gives
$$
0
\too
\Tor_1^R(M,K/R)
\too
M
\too
M\tensor_RK
\too
M\tensor_R(K/R)
\too
0.
$$ {#eq:module-localization}
For a lattice $L$, projectivity kills the Tor term:
$$
0
\too
L
\too
L_K
\too
L\tensor_R(K/R)
\too
0.
$$ {#eq:lattice-localization}
Applying $\Hom_R(L,-)$ to $\mathcal E_R$ gives
$$
0
\too
L^*
\too
\Hom_R(L,K)
\too
\Hom_R(L,K/R)
\too
0,
$$ {#eq:dual-localization}
again because $L$ is projective.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:double-complex title="Discriminant comparison via localization"}
Let $L\in\mathbf{Lat}_R^{\mathrm{nd}}$.
The adjoint of the scalar-extended form gives the commutative diagram
```tikzcd
0 \arrow[r] &
L \arrow[r] \arrow[d,"b^\sharp"'] &
L_K \arrow[r] \arrow[d,"b_K^\sharp\;(\sim)"'] &
L\otimes_R(K/R) \arrow[r] \arrow[d,"\bar b"] &
0\\
0 \arrow[r] &
L^* \arrow[r] &
\operatorname{Hom}_R(L,K) \arrow[r] &
\operatorname{Hom}_R(L,K/R) \arrow[r] &
0
```

The snake lemma gives
$$
0
\too
\operatorname{corad}_{b}(L)
\too
L\tensor_R(K/R)
\too
\Hom_R(L,K/R)
\too
0
$$
and the canonical isomorphism
$$
\operatorname{corad}_{b}(L)
\isoto
A_L^\sharp.
$$
For $R=\bZ$, this gives $U(A_L)=A_L^\sharp\isoto\operatorname{corad}_{b}(L)$.
:::
