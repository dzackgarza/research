# Eichler transvections {#sec-eichler-transvections}

## Eichler--Siegel transvections

::: {.definition #def:eichler-siegel title="Eichler--Siegel transvection" defines="Eichler--Siegel transvection"}

Let $L$ be a nondegenerate symmetric lattice, let $e\in L_\QQ$ be an isotropic (@def:orthogonal-complement) vector, and let $a\in \generators{e}^{\perp L_\QQ}$ be a vector orthogonal to $e$, i.e. $ae = 0$.
The **Eichler--Siegel transvection** associated to the pair $(e, a)$ is the isometry $E_{e, a}\in\Orth(L_\QQ)$ defined by
$$
E_{e, a}(v) = v + (ev)\,a - \qty{av + \tfrac{1}{2}(aa)(ev)}e
.
$$
If $a$ is itself isotropic this simplifies to
$$
E_{e, a}(v) = v + (ev)\,a - (av)\,e
.
$$
The construction is due to Eichler [@Eic74].
The pair $(e,a)$ is taken in the rational space $L_\QQ$, so $E_{e,a}$ is an isometry of the rational symmetric bilinear space; it need not preserve $L$. For integral parameters, @prop:eichler-integrality-criterion gives a sufficient condition, which is necessary when $e$ is primitive of divisibility one.
:::

::: {.definition #def:eichler-group title="The Eichler transvection group" defines="Eichler transvection group; $E(L)$"}

For a nondegenerate symmetric integral lattice $L$, the **Eichler transvection group** $E(L)\leq\Orth(L)$ is generated by all transvections $E_{e,a}$ for which
$$
e\in L^{\mathrm{prim}}[0],
\qquad
\div_L(e)=1,
\qquad
a\in\generators{e}^{\perp L},
\qquad
a^2\in 2\ZZ .
$$
The last condition is the integrality condition of @prop:eichler-integrality-criterion.
On an even lattice every $a\in L$ has even square (@def:form-axioms), so it is automatic and the family is the usual one; on an odd lattice it cannot be dropped, since then some transvections satisfying the other three conditions are not isometries of $L$.
:::

::: {.proposition #prop:eichler-integrality-criterion title="When an Eichler--Siegel transvection is integral"}

Let $L$ be a nondegenerate symmetric integral lattice, $e\in L$ isotropic, and $a\in L\cap\generators{e}^{\perp L}$. If $a^2\in2\ZZ$, then $E_{e,a}\in\Orth(L)$. If additionally $e$ is primitive with $\div_L(e)=1$, the converse holds.
Then
$$
E_{e,a}\in\Orth(L)
\iff
a^2\in 2\ZZ .
$$
:::

::: {.proof}

By @def:eichler-siegel, for $x\in L$,
$$
E_{e,a}(x) - x = (e,x)\,a - \qty{(a,x) + \tfrac{1}{2}a^2(e,x)}e .
$$
Both $(e,x)$ and $(a,x)$ are integers, so $(e,x)a\in L$ and $(a,x)e\in L$; the only summand that can fail to be integral is $-\tfrac12 a^2(e,x)e$.
If $a^2$ is even, every summand is integral and $E_{e,a}(L)\subseteq L$.
For $a,b\perp e$, the formula gives $(e,E_{e,b}(x))=(e,x)$ and $(a,E_{e,b}(x))=(a,x)+(e,x)(a,b)$. Substitution yields $E_{e,a}E_{e,b}=E_{e,a+b}$, using symmetry and $(a+b)^2=a^2+2(a,b)+b^2$. Taking $b=-a$ proves the inverse formula.
Since $(-a)^2 = a^2$ is even, $E_{e,-a}(L)\subseteq L$ as well, so $E_{e,a}$ is a bijection of $L$ and lies in $\Orth(L)$.
For necessity, assume $e$ primitive and $\div_L(e)=1$. Then $\div_L(e)=1$ provides $f\in L$ with $(e,f)=1$ (@def:lattice-divisibility), and
$$
E_{e,a}(f) = f + a - \qty{(a,f) + \tfrac12 a^2}e .
$$
If this lies in $L$, then $\tfrac12 a^2 e\in L$, and since $e$ is primitive, $\QQ e\cap L = \ZZ e$ (@def:primitive-embedding), so $\tfrac12 a^2\in\ZZ$.
:::

::: {.remark}

On an even lattice every $a\in L$ has even square, so @def:eichler-group is the generating family of [@GHS08, §3.3], whose lattice is integral and even by hypothesis.
@prop:eichler-integrality-criterion is the sharp form of the integrality condition under the primitive divisibility-one hypotheses: [@GHS08, §3.1, Lem. 3.1, eq. (4)] states the transvection as a rational isometry and licenses no integrality claim beyond the formula itself.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:eichler-criterion title="The Eichler criterion"}

Let $L$ be an even lattice (@def:even-lattice), let $\phi\colon U\perp U_1\perp L_0\isoto L$ be an isometry, where $U_1$ is a second hyperbolic plane (@def:hyperbolic-plane), and let $v_1,v_2\in L$ be primitive (@def:primitive-embedding).
Then there exists $g\in\widetilde{\SO}^+(L)$ with $g(v_1)=v_2$ if and only if
$$
v_1^2=v_2^2,
\qquad
v_1^*=v_2^*\ \text{in }A_L,
$$
where $v_i^*\definedas \frac{v_i}{\div_L(v_i)}+L\in A_L$.
This is Eichler's criterion in the form of [@GHS08, Prop. 3.3(i)], which is stated there for an integral even lattice; the evenness is not removable (@ex:eichler-criterion-fails-odd).
:::

::: {.proof}
Applying [@GHS08, Prop. 3.3(i)] through the inverse isometry $\phi^{-1}\colon L\isoto U\perp U_1\perp L_0$ produces
$$
\tau\in E_U(U_1\perp L_0)
\subseteq
\widetilde{\SO}^+(L)
$$
with $\tau(v_1)=v_2$.
The converse follows because every element of $\widetilde{\SO}^+(L)$ preserves the norm (@def:lattice-basic-invariants) and acts trivially on $A_L$.
:::

::: {.example #ex:eichler-criterion-fails-odd title="The Eichler criterion fails on an odd unimodular lattice"}

Let $L = U\perp U_1\perp\ZZ a$ with $a^2 = -1$, of signature $(2,3)$, let $e,f$ be the hyperbolic basis of the first plane $U$, and let $e_1,f_1$ be that of $U_1$.
Put $v \definedas a$ and $w \definedas a+e$.
Both are primitive of norm $-1$ and of divisibility $1$, and $L$ is unimodular, so $A_L = 0$ and $v^* = w^*$: the two vectors satisfy the hypotheses of @thm:eichler-criterion other than evenness.
Nevertheless no isometry of $L$ carries $v$ to $w$.
An isometry preserves the *characteristic* property $(x,y)\equiv y^2\pmod 2$ for all $y\in L$.
For $y = me+nf+m'e_1+n'f_1+ta$ one has $(v,y) = -t\equiv -t^2 = y^2\pmod 2$, so $v$ is characteristic, while $(w,f) = 1$ and $f^2 = 0$, so $w$ is not.
Hence $v$ and $w$ lie in different $\Orth(L)$-orbits.
:::

::: {.example #ex:eichler-two-hyperbolic-planes title="Even twists contain no hyperbolic plane"}

For every integral lattice $M$,
$$
\Hom_{\mathbf{Lat}_{\bZ}}\qty{U,M(2)}
=
\varnothing.
$$
Indeed, every value of $\beta_{M(2)}$ lies in $2\bZ$, whereas for a hyperbolic basis $e,f$ of $U$ one has $\beta_U(e,f)=1$.
Consequently no isometry $U^{\oplus2}\perp L_0\isoto M(2)$ exists for any lattice $L_0$.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:eichler-U-U title="Eichler transvections on $U\oplus U$"}

For
$$
b_{\det}(X,Y)
\definedas
\det(X+Y)-\det X-\det Y
$$
on $\Mat_{2\times2}(\bZ)$, there is an isometry
$$
\phi\colon
U\perp U
\isoto
\qty{\Mat_{2\times2}(\bZ),b_{\det}}.
$$
Under $\phi$, the group (@def:group) generated by the Eichler--Siegel transvections (@def:eichler-siegel) of $U\perp U$ is the image of
$\SL_2(\bZ)\times\SL_2(\bZ)$ acting by
$$
(A,B)\cdot X\definedas AXB^{-1}.
$$
:::

::: {.proof}

For hyperbolic (@def:hyperbolic-plane) bases (@def:based-module) $e_1,f_1,e_2,f_2$ of $U\perp U$, define
$$
\phi(e_1)=E_{11},
\qquad
\phi(f_1)=E_{22},
\qquad
\phi(e_2)=E_{12},
\qquad
\phi(f_2)=-E_{21}.
$$
Then
$$
b_{\det}(E_{11},E_{22})=1,
\qquad
b_{\det}(E_{12},-E_{21})=1,
$$
and all remaining pairings of distinct basis (@def:based-module) vectors vanish, so $\phi$ is an isometry.
Moreover $b_{\det}(X,X)=2\det X$, hence the matrix lattice is even (@def:form-axioms).

For $A,B\in\SL_2(\bZ)$,
$\det(AXB^{-1})=\det X$, so this action preserves $b_{\det}$.
Under $\phi$, the Eichler--Siegel transvections generate exactly this image [@GHS08, Lem. 3.2].
:::
