# Eichler transvections {#sec:eichler-transvections}

## Eichler--Siegel transvections

::: {.definition #def:eichler-siegel title="Eichler--Siegel transvection"}

Let $L$ be a lattice, let $e\in L_\QQ$ be an isotropic vector, and let $a\in \generators{e}^{\perp L_\QQ}$ be a vector orthogonal to $e$, i.e. $ae = 0$.
The **Eichler--Siegel transvection** associated to the pair $(e, a)$ is the isometry $E_{e, a}\in\Orth(L_\QQ)$ defined by
$$
E_{e, a}(v) = v + (ev)\,a - \qty{av + \tfrac{1}{2}(aa)(ev)}e
.
$$
If $a$ is itself isotropic this simplifies to
$$
E_{e, a}(v) = v + (ev)\,a - (av)\,e
.
$$
The construction is due to Eichler [@Eic74].
:::

::: {.definition #def:eichler-group title="The Eichler transvection group"}

The **Eichler transvection group** $E(L)\leq\Orth(L)$ is generated by all transvections $E_{e,a}$ for which
$$
e\in L^{\mathrm{prim}}[0],
\qquad
\div_L(e)=1,
\qquad
a\in\generators{e}^{\perp L}.
$$
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:eichler-criterion title="The Eichler criterion"}

Let $\phi\colon U\perp U_1\perp L_0\isoto L$ be an isometry, where $U_1$ is a second hyperbolic plane, and let $v_1,v_2\in L$ be primitive.
Then there exists $g\in\widetilde{\SO}^+(L)$ with $g(v_1)=v_2$ if and only if
$$
v_1^2=v_2^2,
\qquad
v_1^*=v_2^*\ \text{in }A_L,
$$
where $v_i^*\definedas \frac{v_i}{\div_L(v_i)}+L\in A_L$.
This is Eichler's criterion in the form of [@GHS08, Prop. 3.3(i)].
:::

::: {.proof}
Applying [@GHS08, Prop. 3.3(i)] through the inverse isometry $\phi^{-1}\colon L\isoto U\perp U_1\perp L_0$ produces
$$
\tau\in E_U(U_1\perp L_0)
\subseteq
\widetilde{\SO}^+(L)
$$
with $\tau(v_1)=v_2$.
The converse follows because every element of $\widetilde{\SO}^+(L)$ preserves the norm and acts trivially on $A_L$.
:::

::: {.example #ex:eichler-two-hyperbolic-planes title="Even twists contain no hyperbolic plane"}

For every integral lattice $M$,
$$
\Hom_{\mathbf{Lat}_{\bZ}}\qty{U,M(2)}
=
\varnothing.
$$
Indeed, every value of $\beta_{M(2)}$ lies in $2\bZ$, whereas for a hyperbolic basis $e,f$ of $U$ one has $\beta_U(e,f)=1$.
Consequently no isometry $U^{\oplus2}\perp L_0\isoto M(2)$ exists for any lattice $L_0$.
:::

::: {.theorem #thm:eichler-U-U title="Eichler transvections on $U\oplus U$"}

For
$$
b_{\det}(X,Y)
\definedas
\det(X+Y)-\det X-\det Y
$$
on $\Mat_{2\times2}(\bZ)$, there is an isometry
$$
\phi\colon
U\perp U
\isoto
\qty{\Mat_{2\times2}(\bZ),b_{\det}}.
$$
Under $\phi$, the group generated by the Eichler--Siegel transvections of $U\perp U$ is the image of
$\SL_2(\bZ)\times\SL_2(\bZ)$ acting by
$$
(A,B)\cdot X\definedas AXB^{-1}.
$$
:::

::: {.proof}

For hyperbolic bases $e_1,f_1,e_2,f_2$ of $U\perp U$, define
$$
\phi(e_1)=E_{11},
\qquad
\phi(f_1)=E_{22},
\qquad
\phi(e_2)=E_{12},
\qquad
\phi(f_2)=-E_{21}.
$$
Then
$$
b_{\det}(E_{11},E_{22})=1,
\qquad
b_{\det}(E_{12},-E_{21})=1,
$$
and all remaining pairings of distinct basis vectors vanish, so $\phi$ is an isometry.
Moreover $b_{\det}(X,X)=2\det X$, hence the matrix lattice is even.

For $A,B\in\SL_2(\bZ)$,
$\det(AXB^{-1})=\det X$, so this action preserves $b_{\det}$.
Under $\phi$, the Eichler--Siegel transvections generate exactly this image [@GHS08].
:::
