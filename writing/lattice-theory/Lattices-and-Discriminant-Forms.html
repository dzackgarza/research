40  Lattices and discriminant forms

Let \(R\) be a Dedekind domain with fraction field \(K\); the main case is \(R=\bZ\) and \(K=\bQ\).

Definition 40.1 (Lattices) An \(R\)-lattice is a bilinear module \((L,R,\beta_L)\) whose underlying \(R\)-module is finitely generated projective. Write \(\mathbf{Lat}_R\hookrightarrow\mathcal B_{R,R}\) for the replete full subcategory of Definition 17.2 on these objects, and \(\mathbf{Lat}_R^{\mathrm{nd}}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Lat}_R\) for the replete full subcategory cut out by nondegeneracy in Definition 17.4.

Example 40.2 (Subobjects under extension of scalars) Let \(L\) be an integral lattice and let \(\iota_v\colon\generators{v}_{\bZ}\injects L\) be the monomorphism generated by \(0\ne v\in L\). Let \(\pi_v\) be its cokernel morphism in \(\bZ\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}\): \[ 0 \too \generators{v}_{\bZ} \xrightarrow{\iota_v} L \xrightarrow{\pi_v} Q_v \too 0. \] Applying the exact functor \((-)\tensor_{\bZ}\bR\) gives \[ 0 \too \generators{v}_{\bR} \xrightarrow{\iota_v\tensor1} L_{\bR} \xrightarrow{\pi_v\tensor1} Q_v\tensor_{\bZ}\bR \too 0, \] since \(\bR\) is flat over \(\bZ\).

Definition 40.3 (Even lattices) An \(R\)-lattice \(L\) is even when \(\beta_L\) is even in the sense of Definition 17.3. Write \(\mathbf{EvenLat}_R\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Lat}_R\) for the corresponding replete full subcategory.

40.1 Classification by signature

Definition 40.4 (Signature subcategories) For a signature tuple \(\underline s=(s_\sigma)_{\sigma\in\Sigma_\infty(R)}\) of Definition 17.19, let \(\mathbf{Lat}_R^{\underline s}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Lat}_R\) be the replete full subcategory of lattices with signature tuple \(\underline s\).

For \(R=\bZ\), define \[ \mathbf{Def}_{\bZ}^{+} \definedas \coprod_{n\geq1}\mathbf{Lat}_{\bZ}^{(n,0)}, \qquad \mathbf{Def}_{\bZ}^{-} \definedas \coprod_{n\geq1}\mathbf{Lat}_{\bZ}^{(0,n)}, \] \[ \mathbf{Def}_{\bZ} \definedas \mathbf{Def}_{\bZ}^{+}\sqcup\mathbf{Def}_{\bZ}^{-}, \qquad \mathbf{Indef}_{\bZ} \definedas \coprod_{p,q\geq1}\mathbf{Lat}_{\bZ}^{(p,q)}, \] and \[ \mathbf{Hyp}_{\bZ} \definedas \coprod_{n\geq2}\mathbf{Lat}_{\bZ}^{(1,n-1)} \hookrightarrow \mathbf{Indef}_{\bZ}. \] The twist functor of Definition 17.18 restricts to \[ (-)(-1)\colon \mathbf{Def}_{\bZ}^{+} \isoto \mathbf{Def}_{\bZ}^{-}. \] The root lattices are objects of \(\mathbf{Def}_{\bZ}^{-}\).

Definition 40.5 (Metric $I$-duals) Let \(L\in\mathbf{Lat}_R^{\mathrm{nd}}\) and let \(I\injects K\) be a nonzero fractional ideal. The coefficient exact sequence is \[ 0\too I\too K\too K/I\too0 \tag{40.1}\] Applying the exact functor \(\Hom_R(L,-)\) to Equation eq-metric-dual-coefficients gives \[ 0 \too \Hom_R(L,I) \too \Hom_R(L,K) \too \Hom_R(L,K/I) \too 0. \] Applying scalar extension \(K\tensor_R-\) to \(\beta_L\) gives \[ \beta_{L_K}\colon L_K\tensor_KL_K \too K, \qquad \beta_{L_K}^{\sharp}\colon L_K \isoto \Hom_R(L,K). \] The metric \(I\)-dual \(L^{\# I}\) is the pullback

L^{\# I} \arrow[r] \arrow[d] &
L_K \arrow[d,"\beta_{L_K}^{\sharp}"] \\
\Hom_R(L,I) \arrow[r] &
\Hom_R(L,K)

An \(I\)-integral structure on \(L\) is the unique morphism \(\eta_{L,I}\colon L\too L^{\# I}\) in \(R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}\) making

& L^{\# I} \arrow[d] \\
L \arrow[ur,"\eta_{L,I}"] \arrow[r] & L_K

commute; \(L\) is \(I\)-integral when this lift exists. For an \(I\)-integral lattice define \(A_L^{\# I}\) by the exact sequence \[ 0 \too L \xrightarrow{\eta_{L,I}} L^{\# I} \too A_L^{\# I} \too 0. \] For \(I=R\) write \[ L^\#\definedas L^{\# R}, \qquad \iota_L\definedas\eta_{L,R}, \qquad A_L^\sharp\definedas A_L^{\# R}. \]

Definition 40.6 ($I$-modular and unimodular lattices) An \(I\)-integral lattice \(L\) is \(I\)-modular when \(A_L^{\# I}=0\). Equivalently, its defining exact sequence is \[ 0\too L\xrightarrow{\eta_{L,I}}L^{\# I}\too0. \] Write \(\mathbf{ModLat}_{R,I}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Lat}_R^{\mathrm{nd}}\) for the replete full subcategory of \(I\)-modular lattices. For \(I=R\) set \(\mathbf{Unimod}_R\definedas\mathbf{ModLat}_{R,R}\).

Definition 40.7 (Bilinear and quadratic discriminant forms) Let \(L\in\mathbf{Lat}_{\bZ}^{\mathrm{nd}}\) be integral. The bilinear denominator is \(N_b(L)\definedas\exp\qty{A_L^\sharp}\). The restricted pairing has value module \(\tfrac1{N_b(L)}\bZ\): \[ L^\#\tensor_{\bZ}L^\# \xrightarrow{\beta_{L^\#}} \tfrac1{N_b(L)}\bZ \injects \bQ. \] Let \(j_{b,L}\colon\bZ\injects\tfrac1{N_b(L)}\bZ\). Then \[ \boldsymbol\iota_L \definedas (\iota_L,j_{b,L}) \] is a morphism in \(\mathbf{BilMod}_{\bZ}\), and the bilinear discriminant form is its cokernel in that abelian category: \[ (L,\bZ,\beta_L) \xrightarrow{\boldsymbol\iota_L} \qty{L^\#,\tfrac1{N_b(L)}\bZ,\beta_{L^\#}} \twoheadrightarrow A_L \too 0. \] Thus \(A_L\) always denotes the bilinear discriminant object. The forgetful functor \(U\colon\mathbf{BilMod}_{\bZ}\too\bZ\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}\) satisfies \(U(A_L)=A_L^\sharp\), and the pairing of \(A_L\) is the unique morphism \[ \bar{\beta}_L\colon A_L^\sharp\tensor_{\bZ}A_L^\sharp \too \tfrac1{N_b(L)}\bZ/\bZ \] for which, writing \(\pi_L\colon L^\#\twoheadrightarrow A_L^\sharp\), \[ \bar{\beta}_L\circ(\pi_L\tensor\pi_L) = \qty{\tfrac1{N_b(L)}\bZ\twoheadrightarrow\tfrac1{N_b(L)}\bZ/\bZ} \circ\beta_{L^\#}. \]

Let \(N_q(L)\) be the quadratic level of Definition 43.1; no evenness hypothesis is required. Define \[ J_L \definedas \generators{2,\ \beta_L(x,x)\mid x\in L}_{\bZ} \injects \bZ. \] The diagonal of \(\beta_{L^\#}\) induces the unique quadratic morphism \[ q_L\colon A_L^\sharp \too \tfrac2{N_q(L)}\bZ/J_L \] satisfying \[ q_L\circ\pi_L(x) = \beta_{L_\bQ}(x,x)+J_L. \] The quadratic discriminant form is \[ A_{L,q} \definedas \qty{A_L^\sharp,\tfrac2{N_q(L)}\bZ/J_L,q_L}. \] If \(L\) is even, then \(J_L=2\bZ\), so this recovers the usual \(\tfrac2{N_q(L)}\bZ/2\bZ\)-valued refinement (Nikulin 1980, sec. 1.1).

Definition 40.8 (Discriminant bilinear forms) The discriminant bilinear forms are the objects of \(\mathbf{BilMod}_{\bZ}\) isomorphic to \(A_L\) for some \(L\in\mathbf{Lat}_{\bZ}^{\mathrm{nd}}\). Write \[ \mathbf{DiscBil}_{\bZ}^{\simeq} \hookrightarrow \mathbf{BilMod}_{\bZ}^{\simeq} \] for the full subgroupoid spanned by these cokernels.

Definition 40.9 (Discriminant quadratic forms) The discriminant quadratic forms are the objects isomorphic to \(A_{L,q}\) for some \(L\in\mathbf{Lat}_{\bZ}^{\mathrm{nd}}\). Their bilinearizations are \(\operatorname{polar}(A_{L,q})\) in the sense of Definition 17.28. Write \(\mathbf{DiscQuad}_{\bZ}^{\simeq}\) for their full subgroupoid in the groupoid of quadratic modules fibred over their value modules.

The two constructions are functors on cores: \[ \begin{aligned} \qty{\mathbf{Lat}_{\bZ}^{\mathrm{nd}}}^{\simeq} &\too \mathbf{DiscBil}_{\bZ}^{\simeq}, & L&\longmapsto A_L, \\ \qty{\mathbf{Lat}_{\bZ}^{\mathrm{nd}}}^{\simeq} &\too \mathbf{DiscQuad}_{\bZ}^{\simeq}, & L&\longmapsto A_{L,q}. \end{aligned} \]

40.2 Elementary lattices

Definition 40.10 ($p$-elementary lattices) Let \(p\) be a prime and let \(S\in\mathbf{Lat}_{\bZ}^{\mathrm{nd}}\). The lattice \(S\) is \(p\)-elementary when the \(\bZ\)-module \(A_S^\sharp\) is \(p\)-elementary. Writing \(a\definedas\ell\qty{A_S^\sharp}\), one has \[ A_S^\sharp\isoto(\bZ/p\bZ)^a, \qquad \cardinality{A_S^\sharp}=p^a, \qquad \abs{\disc{S}}=p^a. \] For \(p=2\) this is Nikulin’s \(2\)-elementary condition (Nikulin 1980, sec. 3.6.1). The condition defines a replete full subcategory of \(\mathbf{Lat}_{\bZ}^{\mathrm{nd}}\).

For an even \(2\)-elementary lattice \(S\), the coparity \(\delta_S\) is the invariant of \(A_{S,q}\) from (Nikulin 1980, sec. 3.6). Let the signature of \(S\) be \((t_{(+)},t_{(-)},0)\). The genus is determined by \((\delta_S;t_{(+)},t_{(-)},a)\), and when \(t_{(+)}>0\) and \(t_{(-)}>0\) these invariants determine the integral isometry class (Nikulin 1980, Thm. 3.6.2).

Example 40.11 (Determinant order does not imply 2-elementarity) In the sign convention of Definition 17.21 the root lattice \(A_3\) has Gram matrix \[ G=\begin{pmatrix}-2&1&0\\1&-2&1\\0&1&-2\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \det G=-4. \] Write \(d_1\mid d_2\mid d_3\) for the invariant factors of \(A_{A_3}^\sharp\): \[ A_{A_3}^\sharp\isoto\bigoplus_i\bZ/d_i\bZ. \] The greatest common divisor of the entries of \(G\) is \(d_1=1\), and the greatest common divisor of its \(2\times2\) minors is \(d_1d_2=1\). Since \(d_1d_2d_3=\abs{\det G}=4\), one obtains \(A_{A_3}^\sharp\isoto\bZ/4\bZ\). Hence \(A_{A_3}^\sharp\) has exponent \(4\), whereas a \(2\)-elementary \(\bZ\)-module has exponent at most \(2\).

40.3 Radical and unimodularity

Definition 40.12 (Radical and coradical) For a symmetric bilinear form \(b\) on a finitely generated projective \(R\)-module \(M\), define its radical and coradical by the exact sequence \[ 0 \too \radic(M) \too M \xrightarrow{b^\sharp} M^* \too \operatorname{corad}_b(M) \too 0. \] Then \(b\) is nondegenerate exactly when \(\radic(M)=0\), and it is perfect exactly when \(\radic(M)=0=\operatorname{corad}_b(M)\).

Theorem 40.13 (The radical splits off) Let \(R\) be a Dedekind domain, let \(M\) be a finitely generated projective \(R\)-module, and let \(b\) be a symmetric \(R\)-valued bilinear form on \(M\). Let \[ \rho_M\colon\radic(M)\injects M, \qquad q_M\colon M\surjects M^{\mathrm{nd}} \] be the radical morphism and its cokernel in \(\mathbf{BilMod}_R\): \[ 0\too\radic(M)\xrightarrow{\rho_M}M\xrightarrow{q_M}M^{\mathrm{nd}}\too0. \] Then there exists a section \[ s_M\colon M^{\mathrm{nd}}\too M, \qquad q_M\circ s_M=\id_{M^{\mathrm{nd}}}, \] and the orthogonal-sum morphism \[ \rho_M\perp s_M\colon \radic(M)\perp M^{\mathrm{nd}} \isoto M \] is an isomorphism of \(R\)-valued bilinear modules.

Proof. The adjoint factors uniquely through \(q_M\): \[ M\xrightarrow{q_M}M^{\mathrm{nd}} \xrightarrow{\overline b^{\sharp}} \Hom_R(M,R). \] Since \(\radic(M)=\ker(b^\sharp)\), the induced sequence \[ 0 \too M^{\mathrm{nd}} \xrightarrow{\overline b^\sharp} \Hom_R(M,R) \] is exact. Thus the underlying \(R\)-module of \(M^{\mathrm{nd}}\) is finitely generated and torsion-free, hence projective over the Dedekind domain \(R\). The underlying short exact sequence of \(R\)-modules therefore splits; choose \[ s_M\colon M^{\mathrm{nd}}\too M, \qquad q_M\circ s_M=\id_{M^{\mathrm{nd}}}. \] The form on \(M^{\mathrm{nd}}\) is the unique factorization of \(b\) through \(q_M\tensor_Rq_M\), so \(b(s_Mx,s_My)=b^{\mathrm{nd}}(x,y)\). Moreover, \[ b\circ(\rho_M\tensor_Rs_M)=0 = b\circ(s_M\tensor_R\rho_M). \] Hence the split module isomorphism \[ \radic(M)\oplus M^{\mathrm{nd}} \xrightarrow{\ \rho_M\oplus s_M\ } M \] is the orthogonal-sum isomorphism \[ \radic(M)\perp M^{\mathrm{nd}} \xrightarrow{\ \rho_M\perp s_M\ } M. \]

40.4 Localization and comparison

Theorem 40.14 (Localization exact sequences for lattices) Let \[ \mathcal E_R\colon 0\too R\too K\too K/R\too0 \] be the coefficient exact sequence. Tensoring \(\mathcal E_R\) with an \(R\)-module \(M\) gives \[ 0 \too \Tor_1^R(M,K/R) \too M \too M\tensor_RK \too M\tensor_R(K/R) \too 0. \] {#eq:module-localization} For a lattice \(L\), projectivity kills the Tor term: \[ 0 \too L \too L_K \too L\tensor_R(K/R) \too 0. \] {#eq:lattice-localization} Applying \(\Hom_R(L,-)\) to \(\mathcal E_R\) gives \[ 0 \too L^* \too \Hom_R(L,K) \too \Hom_R(L,K/R) \too 0, \] {#eq:dual-localization} again because \(L\) is projective.

Proof. The long exact sequence is the snake lemma of Theorem 3.11 applied to the coefficient sequence 0 -> R -> K -> K/R -> 0, with the Tor vanishing of Definition 25.22 killing the left term because the lattice is projective. Those vanishings are what the sequence requires.

Theorem 40.15 (Discriminant comparison via localization) Let \(L\in\mathbf{Lat}_R^{\mathrm{nd}}\). The adjoint of the scalar-extended form gives the commutative diagram

L \arrow[r] \arrow[d,"b^\sharp"'] &
L_K \arrow[r] \arrow[d,"b_K^\sharp\;(\sim)"'] &
L\otimes_R(K/R) \arrow[r] \arrow[d,"\bar b"] &
0\\
0 \arrow[r] &
L^* \arrow[r] &
\operatorname{Hom}_R(L,K) \arrow[r] &
\operatorname{Hom}_R(L,K/R) \arrow[r] &
0

The snake lemma gives \[ 0 \too \operatorname{corad}_{b}(L) \too L\tensor_R(K/R) \too \Hom_R(L,K/R) \too 0 \] and the canonical isomorphism \[ \operatorname{corad}_{b}(L) \isoto A_L^\sharp. \] For \(R=\bZ\), this gives \(U(A_L)=A_L^\sharp\isoto\operatorname{corad}_{b}(L)\).

Proof. The double complex is the one associated with a resolution of each term, and its total complex computes the same homology by the standard spectral sequence argument, and the total complex is exact when the columns are exact (Theorem 25.1).
