# Integral lattices and their arithmetic {#sec-lattice-theory}

This part develops the lattice theory that the Coble and Enriques work evaluates: integral and even lattices with their discriminant forms, primitive embeddings and overlattices, isometries and reflections, the arithmetic invariants that separate a lattice from its genus, the classification of the unimodular and $2$-elementary cases, and the root systems and Coxeter systems built from them.
It assumes the module, form, and duality pages of the first part, in particular @def:module-bilinear-form, @def:form-twist, and @def:polarization; nothing here depends on the geometry of the Coble part, and the Coble-specific lattices are treated after it.

Throughout, $R$ is a Dedekind domain with fraction field $K$, and the main case is $R=\bZ$ with $K=\bQ$; a statement that names $\bZ$ is not available for a general base.
The uniform conventions of the part are the sign convention of @conv:root-sign and the notation $L^\#$ and $A_L^\sharp$ of @def:metric-dual; a page that uses a lattice-specific invariant cites its defining page.

The part is organized in three runs.
The definitions come first: lattices and discriminant forms (@sec-lattices-discriminant), the norm, divisibility, and discriminant class of a vector (@sec-basic-invariants), and the local and level invariants that follow from them.
The second run builds structure on those definitions: embeddings and orthogonal complements, isometries, reflections, spinor norms, Eichler transvections, and the lifting of automorphisms through a gluing.
The third run is arithmetic and classification: the genus and its discriminant forms, the unimodular and $2$-elementary classifications, the Miranda--Morrison obstruction, the definite case, root systems, and Coxeter systems.
One reference points forward, and it is deliberate: the classification of $2$-elementary discriminant forms closes by naming the invariants of @thm:two-elementary-classification, which the part states further on.
The book keeps one registry for the whole volume, so a forward reference resolves; it is recorded here because a reader following the part in order meets the two pages out of sequence.
