                                  1. Introduction
   Let X be a smooth projective K3 surface. An involution ι of X is called nonsym-
plectic if it acts as ι∗ ωX = −ωX on a generator of H 2,0 (X). The (+1)-eigenspace
of the induced involution on H 2 (X, Z) is a hyperbolic lattice S. All the possibilities
for S were found in a classical work [Nik79a] of Nikulin, who proved that there
are 75 cases, given in Fig. 1. The lattices S are uniquely determined by a triple of
invariants (r, a, δ), or an equivalent set of invariants, (g, k, δ).
   For a given lattice S, there is a moduli space FS of K3 surfaces with an involution
and generic Picard lattice S. It is an open subset of DS /Γ, the quotient of a type
IV domain of dimension 20 − rank S by an arithmetic group.
   The K3 surfaces that appear include many interesting ones, for example the
double covers of: Enriques surfaces, smooth del Pezzo surfaces, log del Pezzo sur-
faces of index 2, index 2 Halphen pencils, and rational elliptic surfaces. They have
been the subject of a great deal of research. Here, we are interested in geometric
compactifications of the moduli spaces FS .
   In 50 of the 75 cases, the fixed locus R of the involution contains a smooth curve
Cg of genus g ≥ 2. The divisor Cg is semiample and defines a contraction X → X
to a K3 surface with ADE singularities and an ample Cartier divisor C g .

   It follows that for 0 <   1 the pair (X, C g ) is a KSBA stable pair, see [Kol23]
for their general theory. Stable pairs have complete, projective moduli spaces. One
thus obtains a geometrically meaningful KSBA compactification F S .
Problem 1.1. Describe the compactification F S explicitly.
   In previous collaborations, we solved this problem in two cases: for the degree 2
K3 surfaces [AET19] and for the elliptic degree 2 K3 surfaces [ABE22], which is a
Heegner divisor in the previous case. In this paper, we solve it for all the remaining
cases:
Theorem 1.2. The normalization of F S is a semitoroidal compactification of DS /Γ
for an explicit semifan Fram . In 48 of the 50 cases it is dominated by a toroidal
compactification for a Coxeter reflection fan.
   The precise statement is given in Theorem 9.10. We reduce Problem 1.1 to the
following problem which we solve for all 75 cases:

Problem 1.3. For each cusp of the Baily-Borel compactification F S and each
one-parameter degeneration C \ 0 → FS approaching that cusp, describe explicitly
a Kulikov model X → (C, 0) adapted to the ramification divisor R.
   A Kulikov model is a K-trivial SNC model X → (C, 0) with smooth total space,
and it is adapted to R if Rt ⊂ Xt for t ∈ C \ 0 extends to X0 as a divisor not
containing singular strata, and the limit of any component of positive genus is nef.
   The answer to the last problem can be read off directly from the Coxeter diagrams
of the reflection groups of the hyperbolic lattices T appearing at the 0-cusps of FS .
The reason for this is quite simple. The main tool we use is the mirror symmetry
between degenerations in the S-family and nef line bundles on mirror K3 surfaces
Xb with Picard lattice Sb = T . The nef cone of a K3 surface depends on, and is
described by the reflection group of its Picard lattice.
   The structure of the paper is as follows. In Section 2 we recall the theory
of K3 surfaces with a nonsymplectic involution, of 2-elementary lattices, and the
general facts about the moduli spaces FS of K3 surfaces with group action. We
also recall basic facts about the combinatorial (Baily-Borel, toroidal, semitoroidal)
and functorial (KSBA) compactifications of these moduli spaces.
   In Section 3 we recall Vinberg’s theory of reflection groups in hyperbolic spaces
and the Coxeter-Vinberg diagrams. We don’t need the Coxeter groups for all the
75 of the 2-elementary lattices but only for those that appear at the 0-cusps of FS
for some S. These are the lattices with g ≥ 1, excluding (r, a, δ) = (14, 6, 0). For
the lattices with g ≥ 2 the Coxeter diagrams were computed by Nikulin in [AN06].
A few cases on the g = 1 line were previously known. We complete the job for the
remaining lattices, the answer is given in Figs. 3 and 4.
   In Section 4 we describe K3 surfaces appearing in the 75 families, their quotients
by the involution, and their nef cones. The 75 families are woven together in a web
by certain “Heegner divisor moves,” corresponding to when one moduli space FS 0
is a Heegner divisor in or at the boundary of FS . We describe these moves and
their properties.
   In Section 5 we completely describe the 0- and 1-cusps of FS together with the
incidence relations between them. In particular, the 0-cusps of FS are described
by three kinds of “mirror moves” on the nodes of Fig. 1, making it into a directed
graph in which every vertex has in- and out-degrees equal to 0, 1, 2, or 3.

       2. K3 surfaces with involution and 2-elementary lattices
2A. K3 surfaces with a nonsymplectic involution. Let X be a smooth pro-
jective complex K3 surface. An involution ι of X is called nonsymplectic if it acts
as ι∗ ωX = −ωX on a non-vanishing holomorphic two-form ωX ∈ H 2,0 (X). It is
well known that the quotient Y = X/ι is either a rational or Enriques surface and
that X is algebraic.
   The main invariant of the involution is the (+1) eigenspace S = H 2 (X, Z)+ , a
hyperbolic lattice of some rank r. Its orthogonal complement T = S ⊥ = H 2 (X, Z)−
in H 2 (X, Z) is a lattice of signature (2, 20 − r). There is a canonical isomorphism
AS = AT between the discriminant lattices AS = S ∗ /S and AT = T ∗ /T . The
involution acts by multiplication by ±1 on AS and AT respectively. This implies
that AS = Za2 for some a ≥ 0. Such lattices are called 2-elementary.
   Conversely, if S ⊂ LK3 = II3,19 = U ⊕3 ⊕ E8⊕2 is a primitive 2-elementary lattice
and T = S ⊥ then the involution ρ of LK3 ⊗ Q acting as ±1 on S and T = S ⊥
respectively is an involution of LK3 . If X is a K3 surface whose Picard lattice SX
equals S via some marking H 2 (X, Z) → LK3 then by the Torelli theorem, there
exists a unique involution ι of X such that ι∗ = ρ.

2B. 2-elementary lattices. A lattice H is a free finite rank Z-module together
with a nondegenerate Z-valued bilinear form. It is called even if x2 ≡ 0 (mod 2Z)
for all x ∈ H and odd otherwise. The discriminant lattice is AH = H ∗ /H, where
H ∗ ⊂ HQ is the dual lattice. It comes with the discriminant form qH : AH → Q/Z,
q(x) = x2 . Moreover, if H is even then qH takes well-defined values in Q/2Z. One
also has the associated bilinear form bH : AH × AH → Q/Z.
   If L is a unimodular lattice, S ⊂ L a primitive (i.e. saturated) sublattice and
T = S ⊥ then (AS , qS ) = (AT , −qT ) in a canonical way.
2.2. If S and T are, respectively, the (±1)-eigenspaces of an involution ι on L then
ι acts as identity on AS and as (−1) on AT . Therefore, AS ≃ Za2 for some a.
Lattices H with AH ≃ Za2 are called 2-elementary. Thus, S and T are 2-elementary
in this case.
Definition 2.3. We define an additional invariant, coparity δH as follows: δ = 0
if for all x ∈ AH one has qH (x) ≡ 0 (mod Z) and δ = 1 otherwise. We will call
lattices with δH = 0 co-even and lattices with δH = 1 co-odd.
  This notation is explained by the following. Recall that for any lattice K, K(n)
denotes the lattice with the bilinear product scaled by n, i.e. (x, y)K(n) = n·(x, y)K .
Definition 2.4. For a 2-elementary (not necessarily even) lattice H, the doubled
dual is H † = H ∗ (2). The assignment H → H † is an involution since
                                          ∗
                       (H † )† = (H ∗ (2)) (2) = H( 21 )(2) = H.

2C. Moduli of K3 surfaces with an involution. The K3 surfaces with a non-
symplectic involution corresponding to a given 2-elementary lattice S come with a
natural moduli space. One way to approach it is using the moduli of S-polarized
K3 surfaces following [Dol96], as in [DK07]. The construction is a little delicate.
Another, more direct approach applies to K3 surfaces with any finite automorphism
group that is not totally symplectic, see [AEH21, Sec. 2A].
   Fix an involution ρ of LK3 with the (±1) eigenspaces S and T . A ρ-marking
of a K3 surface with an involution ι is an isometry φ : H 2 (X, Z) → LK3 such that
ι∗ = φ−1 ◦ ρ ◦ φ. Let DS := P{x ∈ T ⊗ C | x · x = 0, x · x̄ > 0} be the period domain.
Then ρ-marked K3 surfaces with involution have a period φ(CωX ) ∈ DS .
   One defines the discriminant locus ∆ = (∪δ δ ⊥ ) ∩ DS , with δ ranging over the
(−2)-vectors in S. The ρ-markings of a K3 surface with involution are a torsor over
                             Γρ := {γ ∈ O(LK3 ) | γ ◦ ρ = ρ ◦ γ}.
Then FS = (DS \ ∆)/Γρ is the coarse space of K3 surfaces that admit a ρ-marking.
   Recall that for the 2-elementary indefinite lattices S and T the homomorphisms
O(S) → O(AS , qS ) and O(T ) → O(AT , qT ) are surjective and one of course has
O(AS , qS ) = O(AT , qT ). Thus, DS /Γρ = DS /O(T ) is the quotient by the full group
of isometries of T , and FS is the complement of finitely many divisors in it.
   Note that for the surfaces parameterized by FS the Picard lattice SX could be

2D. Baily-Borel, toroidal, and semitoroidal compactifications. This mate-
rial is well known, so we refer to [AMRT75, Loo03] for details. Let D = DS as above,
and let Γ ⊂ O(T ) be a finite index subgroup. The Baily-Borel compactification
                                                BB
                                   D/Γ ,→ D/Γ
is a projective variety whose boundary consists of finitely many points (0-cusps) and
modular curves (1-cusps). The “Type III” 0-cusps (resp. “Type II” 1-cusps) are in
a bijection with Γ-orbits of primitive isotropic lines I ⊂ T (resp. primitive isotropic
planes J ⊂ T ). The “Type” terminology arises from the Kulikov-Persson-Pinkham
classification of K3 degenerations [Kul77, PP81].
                                     F
   A toroidal compactification D/Γ is a combinatorially defined normal variety
specified by the data F = {FI } of a compatible system of admissible fans for
each cusp. For a Type IV domain, the data for the 1-cusps is trivial, so the only
important fans are for the 0-cusps and they are always compatible. The fan FI is a
rational polyhedral decomposition of the rational closure C I,Q of the positive cone
CI ⊂ I ⊥ /I ⊗ R. It is required to satisfy the usual fan axioms, and additionally be
Γ-invariant with only finitely many orbits of cones.
                                                         F
   A semitoroidal (or semitoric) compactification D/Γ of Looijenga is a general-
ization in which the cones of FI are locally polyhedral, but not necessarily finitely
generated. The data for the 1-cusps and the compatibility condition may be non-
trivial. By [AE21, Thm. 5.14], a semitoroidal compactification is the same as a
normal compactification which may be sandwiched between the Baily-Borel and a
toroidal compactification.

                              3. Reflection groups
   One of the main tools in the study of K3 surfaces is reflection groups. In this
paper we apply it in two ways: to determine the nef cones in Section 4D and to
describe certain toroidal compactifications of Section 2D.
3A. Vinberg’s theory. We refer to [Vin75, Vin72] for Vinberg’s theory of reflec-
tion groups of hyperbolic lattices. We briefly describe it below.
   Let H be a hyperbolic lattice. Let C the component of the set {v ∈ HR | v 2 > 0},
containing a fixed class h with h2 > 0. Let H = PC be the corresponding hyperbolic
              COMPACTIFICATIONS OF MODULI SPACES OF K3 SURFACES                        9


space. A vector v 6= 0 with v 2 = 0 in the closure of C is a point on the sphere at
infinity of H.
   There are two kinds of closures of C, and it is always clear from the context
which one we have in mind. When H = Pic X for some surface X, the nef cone
Nef(X) is naturally a subset of the round cone C = {v ∈ HR | v 2 ≥ 0}, so here
we add all infinite points of H. When C is used to define a (semi)fan F for some
(semi)toroidal compactification, one considers the rational closure C Q instead, with

Definition 4.9 (Heegner moves). The (−1, −1)-move goes from a node (r, a, 1) of

5A. Isotropic vectors in 2-elementary discriminant groups. For a nonzero
vector T its divisibility div(v) ∈ N is defined by v · T = div(v)Z. Since T is 2-
elementary, div(v) = 1 or 2. Define v ∗ = v/ div(v) ∈ AT ; one has v ∗ = 0 iff
div(v) = 1. If e is a primitive isotropic vector then one certainly has qT (e∗ ) = 0.
Thus, the first step in classifying the 0-cusps is to understand the isotropic vectors
in the finite discriminant group AT . For this, one has the following result of Nikulin.
Definition 5.1. For an 2-elementary lattice H the form q (mod Z) : AH → 12 Z/Z is
linear. A nonzero vector v ∗ ∈ AH is called characteristic if q(x) = (x, v ∗ ) (mod Z)
for all x ∈ AT . It is called ordinary otherwise. Note that if the lattice H is co-even
then q (mod Z) ≡ 0 and there are no characteristic vectors.
