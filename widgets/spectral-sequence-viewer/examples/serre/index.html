The order of π₄(S²)

The Hopf fibration S¹ → S³ → S² identifies π₄(S³) with π₄(S²): both adjacent homotopy groups of the circle vanish. We will compute the former group. The answer is ℤ/2, and the integer 2 comes from the cup square of a degree-two class.

Turn the question into homology

Serre’s thesis detects the first 2-torsion above π₃(S³) in degree four. His general calculation modulo 2 leaves its exponent open. To determine the group integrally, we use the homotopy-fiber calculation in Theorem 4.11 of the worked account, taking the last step in homology.

Choose a map S³ → K(ℤ,3) representing the integral fundamental cohomology class, and call its homotopy fiber F. The map induces an isomorphism on π₃. Since K(ℤ,3) has no other nonzero homotopy groups, the long exact sequence makes F 3-connected and identifies π₄(F) with π₄(S³). Hurewicz now identifies π₄(F) with H₄(F; ℤ).

Pulling back the based path fibration of K(ℤ,3) gives a fibration ℂP∞ → F → S³, since ΩK(ℤ,3) ≃ K(ℤ,2) ≃ ℂP∞. We read its integral Serre spectral sequence twice, first to compute a coefficient and then to take its cokernel.

The cup square supplies the coefficient

In cohomology the fiber contributes ℤ[x], with |x| = 2. The sphere contributes the unit and a class u in degree three. Hence only columns 0 and 3 occur, and only d₃ can act. Since H²(F) and H³(F) vanish, d₃ must carry x isomorphically to u. Choose the sign of u accordingly. The product rule gives d₃(x²) = 2ux, since x has even degree.

Read total degree four in homology

Write bk for the homology class dual to xk, and s for the fundamental class dual to u. The E₃ groups are free abelian, and the homological and cohomological differentials are adjoint under evaluation, up to orientation sign. Thus the homological d₃ takes sb₁ to 2b₂. Its target (0,4) has no outgoing differential.

The only other possible column in total degree four is (3,1), and H₁(ℂP∞) = 0. Thus the filtration of H₄(F) has a single nonzero quotient. Returning through Hurewicz and the two homotopy exact sequences gives π₄(S²) ≅ π₄(S³) ≅ H₄(F) ≅ ℤ/2.

For Serre’s original loop-space and universal-cover method, see J.-P. Serre, Homologie singulière des espaces fibrés. Applications (1951), Chapter V, §§1 and 5, Proposition 4 and its example.
