The group \pi_4(S^2)

Serre proved that the 2-primary subgroup of \pi_4(S^3) is cyclic of order 2^k for some k\geq 1. The integral calculation below gives the whole group. By the exact sequence of the Hopf fibration S^1\to S^3\to S^2, it also gives \pi_4(S^2), since \pi_4(S^1)=\pi_3(S^1)=0.

The homotopy fiber

Let F be the homotopy fiber of a map S^3\to K(\mathbb Z,3) representing a generator of H^3(S^3;\mathbb Z). This map induces an isomorphism on \pi_3, and the target has no other homotopy groups. The homotopy exact sequence therefore gives \pi_i(F)=0 for 1\leq i\leq 3 and \pi_4(F)\cong\pi_4(S^3). By the Hurewicz theorem, \pi_4(F)\cong H_4(F;\mathbb Z).

The projection F\to S^3 has fiber \Omega K(\mathbb Z,3)\simeq\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}. In the figures, p is the degree on the base and q the degree on the fiber. All coefficients are integral.

The transgression

Write x for the degree-two generator of H^*(\mathbb{CP}^{\infty})=\mathbb Z[x]. Only columns 0 and 3 occur, so the sole possible differential is d_3. In particular, its kernel on E_3^{0,2} gives H^2(F) and its cokernel in E_3^{3,0} gives H^3(F). Both vanish. Thus u=d_3(x) generates H^3(S^3), and the derivation rule in even degree gives d_3(x^2)=d_3(x)x+xd_3(x)=2ux.

The fourth homology group

Let b_j be dual to x^j and let s be dual to u. The homological differential is adjoint to the cohomological differential under the integral evaluation pairing. Hence d_3(s\otimes b_1)=\pm2b_2. The sign depends on the choice of orientations. The image is 2\mathbb Z\,b_2.

In total degree four the other possible term is E^2_{3,1}=0, since H_1(\mathbb{CP}^{\infty})=0. Thus E^\infty_{0,4} accounts for all of H_4(F), and the isomorphisms above give \pi_4(S^2)\cong\pi_4(S^3)\cong H_4(F)\cong\mathbb Z/2.
